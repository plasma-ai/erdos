**Source.** The paragraph closing §II on p. 214 of P. Erdős and P. Turán,
*On a problem of Sidon in additive number theory, and on some related
problems*, J. London Math. Soc. 16 (1941), 212--215,
doi:10.1112/jlms/s1-16.4.212. The copy read is identified on the
[[additive_bases/erdos_1941_problem_sidon_additive_number_theory_related/_index|source card]].

## Statement

Setting (p. 212). For a $B_2$ sequence $a_1<a_2<\cdots$ of positive
integers (all sums $a_i+a_j$ with $i\le j$ different), $\phi(n)$ is the
number of its terms not exceeding $n$.

**Remark** (p. 214). The paper makes two assertions, neither with a proof:

1. For every infinite $B_2$ sequence,
   $\liminf_{n\to\infty}\phi(n)/\sqrt n=0$; the paper calls this easy to see
   and italicizes *infinite*.
2. There is a $B_2$ sequence with
   $\limsup_{n\to\infty}\phi(n)/\sqrt n>0$; the paper says such an example
   is not difficult to give. Such a sequence is necessarily infinite.

The print writes the lower and upper limits as an underlined and an
overlined $\lim$.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the two sentences were read on the page
image of p. 214. The paper gives no argument for either, and none is
supplied here.

## Proof pointer

None in the paper.
