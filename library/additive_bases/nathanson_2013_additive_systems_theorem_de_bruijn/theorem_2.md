## Statement

Notation as on the
[[additive_bases/nathanson_2013_additive_systems_theorem_de_bruijn/theorem_1|Theorem 1 page]]:
$[a,b)=\{n\in\mathbf Z:a\le n<b\}$, $g*A=\{ga:a\in A\}$, and an additive
system is a family of sets each containing $0$ and at least two elements
whose finite-support sums are exactly the nonnegative integers, each with
exactly one representation.

**Theorem 2** (p. 5). Let $(g_i)_{i\in\mathbf N}$ be an infinite sequence of
integers with $g_i\ge2$ for all $i\ge1$. Put $G_0=1$ and, for
$i\in\mathbf N$, $G_i=\prod_{j=1}^ig_j$ and

$$
A_i=\{0,G_{i-1},2G_{i-1},\ldots,(g_i-1)G_{i-1}\}=G_{i-1}*[0,g_i).
$$

Then $\mathcal A=(A_i)_{i\in\mathbf N}$ is an additive system.

The system so constructed is the *British number system* generated by
$(g_i)_{i\in\mathbf N}$ (p. 5). With $g_i=2$ for all $i$ it is the binary
number system, and with $g_i=g$ for all $i$ the $g$-adic system (Example 2,
p. 2).

**Lemma 6** (p. 5). If $(g_i)_{i\in\mathbf N}$ generates the British number
system $\mathcal A=(A_i)_{i\in\mathbf N}$ and $(g'_i)_{i\in\mathbf N}$
generates the British number system $\mathcal A'=(A'_i)_{i\in\mathbf N}$,
then $\mathcal A=\mathcal A'$ if and only if $g_i=g'_i$ for all
$i\in\mathbf N$. So British number systems correspond one to one with
integer sequences $(g_i)_{i\in\mathbf N}$ with all $g_i\ge2$ (p. 5).

**Source.** Melvyn B. Nathanson, Additive systems and a theorem of de Bruijn,
Amer. Math. Monthly 121 (2014), no. 1, 5--17,
doi:10.4169/amer.math.monthly.121.01.005, read in the arXiv version
1301.6208v2 (12 April 2013) identified on the
[[additive_bases/nathanson_2013_additive_systems_theorem_de_bruijn/_index|source card]],
whose pages are numbered 1 to 12; labels and pages here are that version's.
Theorem 2, its proof, the definition of a British number system and Lemma 6
with its proof are all on p. 5.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the statements of Theorem 2 and Lemma 6 and
both proofs were read clause by clause on the page image of p. 5. Nothing

## Proof pointer

P. 5. Every $n\in\mathbf N_0$ lies in some $[0,G_r)$, where
[[additive_bases/nathanson_2013_additive_systems_theorem_de_bruijn/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
gives a unique representation using $A_1,\ldots,A_r$; since every nonzero
element of a later set $A_i$, $i>r$, is at least $G_r$, no representation of
$n$ can use those sets, and uniqueness holds in the whole family. Lemma 6
follows by induction on $i$: equal first sets give $g_1=g'_1$, and once
$G_{r-1}=G'_{r-1}$, equal $r$th sets give $g_r=g'_r$.

  source of exact complementary pairs only. Splitting the digit positions of a
  British number system into two infinite classes and applying
  [[additive_bases/nathanson_2013_additive_systems_theorem_de_bruijn/lemma_2|Lemma 2]]
  gives sets $U,V$ with $\mathbf N_0=U\oplus V$: every sum $u+v$ with

  is such a sum in exactly one way. The paper does not mention the problem and proves nothing
  about the ratio of the $n$th elements of $U$ and $V$, which is what the
  problem's hypothesis $a_n/b_n\to1$ concerns.
