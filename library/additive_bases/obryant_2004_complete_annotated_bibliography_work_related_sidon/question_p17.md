## Statement

**Question** (p. 17). "If $\mathcal A^*$ is bounded, is it necessarily 0
infinitely often?"

Here $\mathcal A^*(k)$ is the number of ordered pairs
$(a_1,a_2)\in\mathcal A\times\mathcal A$ with $a_1+a_2=k$
([[additive_bases/obryant_2004_complete_annotated_bibliography_work_related_sidon/definition_1|Definition 1]]
and p. 1). The survey states that the question is equivalent to a USD 500
question of Erdős, its entry [43]: if every positive integer is a sum of two
elements of $\mathcal B\subseteq\mathbb N$, so that $\mathcal B$ is a basis,
must the number of such representations tend to infinity?

The second question of §9, an "anti-Freiman theorem" asking whether
$|\mathcal A+\mathcal A|/|\mathcal A|^2>c$ forces a large $B_2^*[g]$ subset
with $g$ and "large" depending only on $c$, is not recorded on this page.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the question and the stated equivalence were
read on the page image of p. 17; the survey gives no argument for the
equivalence, and none was checked here.

## Proof pointer

An open question; the survey proves nothing about it.

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/additive_bases/E0028/_index|Problem 28]]: for
  $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathbb N$, $\mathcal A^*=1_{\mathcal A}\ast1_{\mathcal A}$,
  which vanishes at every negative integer, so the question is read with the
  zeros of $\mathcal A^*$ counted at positive integers, as the survey's
  equivalence with Erdős's question on bases indicates. In that reading, a
  set with $\mathcal A^*$ bounded and $0$ at only finitely many positive
  integers would be a set whose $\mathcal A+\mathcal A$ contains all but
  finitely many positive integers with
  $\limsup 1_{\mathcal A}\ast1_{\mathcal A}(n)<\infty$. So, for subsets of
  $\mathbb N$, a yes answer to the question is the problem's statement
  (an observation of this page). The survey records the question as open and
  does not cite the problem's number.
