## Statement

Setting (pp. 1722--1723). For a set $\mathcal E$ in an abelian group,
$k\mathcal E$ is the $k$-fold sumset $\mathcal E+\cdots+\mathcal E$. A
subset of a finite abelian group $G$ is *dégénéré* (periodic) when it is a
union of cosets of some nonzero subgroup of $G$; $G$ itself is periodic.

**Théorème 3** (p. 1721). Let $h$ and $n$ be positive integers and let
$\mathcal E$ be a subset of $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ with at least two
elements such that

$$
\mathcal E\cup2\mathcal E\cup\cdots\cup h\mathcal E=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z .
$$

Then $\left(\frac{h(h+1)}{2}+\left\lceil\frac{h-1}{3}\right\rceil\right)\mathcal E$
is periodic: it is a union of cosets of some nonzero subgroup. (The print
says "sous-groupe non nul de $G$"; the group meant is
$\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$.)

The paper says (p. 1721) that the theorem is not needed for Théorème 1 and
is recorded for its generality, and thanks (p. 1765) an anonymous referee
for suggesting it.

## Proof pointer

Pp. 1754--1756. Lemme 25 (p. 1751) gives the same conclusion under the
stronger hypothesis that some $i\mathcal E$ is the whole group
(*foncièrement générateur*). The proof of Théorème 3 treats $h=1$ as
evident, passes to the subgroup $H$ generated by $\mathcal E-e$, applies
Lemme 25 there to $l\mathcal E$, where $l$ is the order of $e$ modulo $H$,
and checks an inequality between the resulting coefficient and
$h(h+1)/2+\lceil(h-1)/3\rceil$.
