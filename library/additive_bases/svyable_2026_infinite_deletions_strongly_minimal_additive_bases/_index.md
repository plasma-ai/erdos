# additive_bases/svyable_2026_infinite_deletions_strongly_minimal_additive_bases

[[additive_bases/svyable_2026_infinite_deletions_strongly_minimal_additive_bases/lemma_8|lemma_8]]: The manuscript's lemma that for k >= 2, a finite S in {2,3,4,...}, c in S
and M >= 1, every large U admits a finite D and an integer p, both in (M,U), with
p a sum of k elements of S, D and 1 and a sum of k+1 elements of S and D,
but not a sum of k+1 elements of S, D and 1 that avoids c.

[[additive_bases/svyable_2026_infinite_deletions_strongly_minimal_additive_bases/lemma_9|lemma_9]]: The manuscript's lemma that for k >= 2, a finite S in {2,3,4,...} and
M >= 1, every large U admits a finite D and an integer q, both in (M,U), with q a
sum of k elements of S, D and 1 but not a sum of k elements of S and D.

[[additive_bases/svyable_2026_infinite_deletions_strongly_minimal_additive_bases/main_theorem_6|main_theorem_6]]: The manuscript's claimed answer to its Problem 5: yes for order one, and for
every k >= 2 a set that is an asymptotic basis of order k, minimal at order
k both for single and for infinite deletions, with no infinite deletion an
asymptotic basis of order k+1, and with at most O_k(x^(1/k)) elements up to x.

[[additive_bases/svyable_2026_infinite_deletions_strongly_minimal_additive_bases/proposition_12|proposition_12]]: The manuscript's proposition that no set of nonnegative integers is both an
asymptotic basis of order 2 and a minimal asymptotic basis of order 3.

[[additive_bases/svyable_2026_infinite_deletions_strongly_minimal_additive_bases/proposition_7|proposition_7]]: The manuscript's case k = 1 of its deletion question: every asymptotic
basis of order 1 has an infinite subset whose removal leaves an asymptotic
basis of order 2.

[[additive_bases/svyable_2026_infinite_deletions_strongly_minimal_additive_bases/theorem_11|theorem_11]]: The manuscript's finite-booster normal form: if A is an asymptotic basis of
order k >= 1 and no infinite deletion from A is an asymptotic basis of order
k+1, then removing some finite F from A leaves a minimal asymptotic basis of
order k+1.

***

*Infinite Deletions from Strongly Minimal Additive Bases*, manuscript (2026),
17 pp., no author printed; posted by Svyable in the thread of Erdős Problem
881 on erdosproblems.com on 2026-05-03. No notice is printed in the file; the
hosting service's terms (https://www.overleaf.com/legal, read 2026-10-02) say
"We don't claim any ownership of your stuff" and grant readers of a shared
project no license; the term is unstated. The manuscript is not refereed.

The manuscript poses Erdős Problem 881 as its Problem 5 (p. 3), with a basis
of order $k$ meaning that every large integer is a sum of exactly $k$
elements of $A$ and with $0\in\mathbb N$ (p. 2): if $A$ is an asymptotic
basis of order $k$ that is strongly minimal under infinite deletions at order
$k$, must some infinite $B\subset A$ leave $A\setminus B$ an asymptotic basis
of order $k+1$? Main Theorem 6 (p. 3) states the answer yes for $k=1$
(Proposition 7, p. 4) and no for every $k\ge2$, through a set $A=C\cup\{1\}$
that is a basis of order $k$, ordinarily minimal and strongly minimal at order
$k$, with no infinite deletion a basis of order $k+1$, and with
$|A\cap[0,x]|=O_k(x^{1/k})$. The construction (Section 7, pp. 9--11) gives each
element of $C$ witnesses private at order $k+1$ (Lemma 8, p. 5), gives the
element $1$ witnesses at order $k$ (Lemma 9, p. 7), and covers successive
intervals by thin digit blocks (Lemma 10, p. 8); Section 8 (pp. 12--14)
argues that it works. Theorem 11 (p. 14) states a normal form: every set
answering the question no is a minimal basis of order $k+1$ plus a finite set.
Proposition 12 (p. 15) states that no basis of order $2$ is a minimal basis of
order $3$.

The site's curator wrote in the thread that the manuscript appears to have
been generated by GPT, and a reader's AI check posted there reports gaps,
among them that the "Moreover" clauses of Lemmas 8 and 9 are false as stated
and that Theorem 11 is not proved; the
[[../wiki/problems/additive_bases/E0881/claims/2026_05_03_svyable|claim page]]
records them.

Read status: claims checked. The statements below were read clause by clause
on the print; the proofs were read for their structure only and no step is
checked here.

**Bears on.**

- [[../wiki/problems/additive_bases/E0881/_index|#881]]: the manuscript's
  Problem 5 is the problem's question with a basis of order $k$ read as sums
  of exactly $k$ elements (the site's definitions allow sums of at most $k$
  elements); Main Theorem 6 states the answer yes for $k=1$ and
  no for each $k\ge2$ in that reading.

**Results.**

- [[additive_bases/svyable_2026_infinite_deletions_strongly_minimal_additive_bases/main_theorem_6|Main Theorem 6 (p. 3)]]: yes for $k=1$; for every
  $k\ge2$ a set $A$ that is an asymptotic basis of order $k$, ordinarily
  minimal and strongly minimal under infinite deletions at order $k$, with
  $A\setminus B$ not a basis of order $k+1$ for every infinite $B\subset A$,
  and with $A(x)=O_k(x^{1/k})$.
- [[additive_bases/svyable_2026_infinite_deletions_strongly_minimal_additive_bases/proposition_7|Proposition 7 (p. 4)]]: every asymptotic basis of order
  $1$ has an infinite $B$ with $A\setminus B$ an asymptotic basis of order $2$.
- [[additive_bases/svyable_2026_infinite_deletions_strongly_minimal_additive_bases/lemma_8|Lemma 8 (p. 5)]]: for $k\ge2$, finite
  $S\subset\{2,3,4,\ldots\}$, $c\in S$ and $M\ge1$, every large $U$ gives a
  finite $D\subset(M,U)$ and $p\in(M,U)$ with $S'=S\cup D$,
  $p\in k(S'\cup\{1\})\cap(k+1)S'$ and
  $p\notin(k+1)\bigl((S'\setminus\{c\})\cup\{1\}\bigr)$, with a
  "Moreover" clause placing $p$ and $D$ in any prescribed subinterval of
  $(M,U)$ whose length tends to infinity with $U$.
- [[additive_bases/svyable_2026_infinite_deletions_strongly_minimal_additive_bases/lemma_9|Lemma 9 (p. 7)]]: for $k\ge2$, finite
  $S\subset\{2,3,4,\ldots\}$ and $M\ge1$, every large $U$ gives a finite
  $D\subset(M,U)$ and $q\in(M,U)$ with $q\in k(S'\cup\{1\})$ but
  $q\notin kS'$, $S'=S\cup D$, with the same "Moreover" clause.
- Lemma 10 (p. 8), not given a page: for every $L\ge1$ a finite
  $Q_L\subset[0,O_k(L)]$ with $|Q_L|=O_k(L^{1/k})$ and $[0,L]\subset kQ_L$;
  and, for $T>kM$, a finite $D\subset(M,O_k(T+L))$ with
  $[T,T+L]\subset kD$ and $|D|=O_k(L^{1/k})$.
- [[additive_bases/svyable_2026_infinite_deletions_strongly_minimal_additive_bases/theorem_11|Theorem 11 (p. 14)]]: for $k\ge1$, if $A$ is an
  asymptotic basis of order $k$ and no infinite deletion from $A$ is an
  asymptotic basis of order $k+1$, then $A=C\cup F$ with $F\subset A$ finite
  and $C=A\setminus F$ a minimal asymptotic basis of order $k+1$.
- [[additive_bases/svyable_2026_infinite_deletions_strongly_minimal_additive_bases/proposition_12|Proposition 12 (p. 15)]]: no $A\subset\mathbb N$ is
  both an asymptotic basis of order $2$ and minimal as an asymptotic basis of
  order $3$.
