# additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes

[[additive_combinatorics/_index|..]]

[[additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes/proposition_1_2|proposition_1_2]]: Alon, Nathanson and Ruzsa's bound for sums of one element from each of
k + 1 nonempty subsets of the integers modulo a prime with all summands
distinct: when the sets have pairwise distinct sizes and the sizes sum to
at most p + C(k+2, 2) - 1, there are at least the sum of the sizes minus
C(k+2, 2) plus 1 such sums.

[[additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes/theorem_1_3|theorem_1_3]]: The Erdős–Heilbronn conjecture as the paper's Theorem 1.3, attributed to
Dias da Silva and Hamidoune: a nonempty subset A of the integers modulo a
prime p has at least min(p, 2|A| - 3) sums of two distinct elements, derived
from the case k = 1 of Proposition 1.2 and again as the case s = 2 of
Theorem 3.3.

[[additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes/theorem_2_1|theorem_2_1]]: Alon, Nathanson and Ruzsa's coefficient criterion for restricted sumsets
modulo a prime: when |A_i| = c_i + 1 and m is the sum of the c_i minus the
degree of h, a nonzero coefficient of the monomial with exponents c_i in
(x_0 + ... + x_k)^m h forces at least m + 1 sums a_0 + ... + a_k with
a_i in A_i and h(a_0, ..., a_k) nonzero.

[[additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes/theorem_3_2|theorem_3_2]]: Alon, Nathanson and Ruzsa's main theorem: for nonempty subsets of the
integers modulo a prime with sizes b_0 >= ... >= b_k, the sums of one
element from each set with all summands distinct number at least the
minimum of p and the sum of the trimmed sizes b'_i minus C(k+2, 2) plus 1,
whenever b'_k is positive, and the bound is sharp.

[[additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes/theorem_3_3|theorem_3_3]]: The Dias da Silva–Hamidoune theorem as the paper's Theorem 3.3, proved from
Theorem 3.2 by the polynomial method: the sums of s distinct elements of a
nonempty subset A of the integers modulo a prime p fill at least
min(p, s|A| - s^2 + 1) residues.

## Contents

- Abstract and § 1, Introduction (pp. 404--405, page images). The abstract
  announces "a simple and general algebraic technique for obtaining results
  in Additive Number Theory" and lists its applications as new extensions
  of the Cauchy--Davenport theorem, the main one being a tight lower bound,
  in terms of the cardinalities $|A_i|$ alone, on the number of sums
  $a_0+a_1+\cdots+a_k$ with $a_i\in A_i\subseteq Z_p$ and the summands
  pairwise distinct. Theorem 1.1 is the Cauchy--Davenport theorem,
  $|A+B|\ge\min\{p,|A|+|B|-1\}$, cited to Davenport 1935 and proved in [1]
  by the method described here. Proposition 1.2 (p. 405, quoted): "Let $p$
  be a prime, and let $A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_k$ be nonempty subsets of the cyclic
  group $Z_p$. If $|A_i|\ne|A_j|$ for all $0\le i<j\le k$ and
  $\sum_{i=0}^k|A_i|\le p+\binom{k+2}2-1$ then
  $|\{a_0+a_1+\cdots+a_k: a_i\in A_i,\ a_i\ne a_j\text{ for all }i\ne j\}|
  \ge\sum_{i=0}^k|A_i|-\binom{k+2}2+1$." The remark after it specializes
  to $k=1$, $A_0=A$ and $A_1=A-\{a\}$ for any $a\in A$: when $A\subset Z_p$
  and $2|A|-1\le p+2$, the sums $a_1+a_2$ with $a_1,a_2\in A$ and
  $a_1\ne a_2$ number at least $2|A|-3$, from which the paper says Theorem
  1.3 follows easily. The same remark dates the conjecture to Erdős and
  Heilbronn in 1964, cites [5] for it, and credits its recent proof to Dias
  da Silva and Hamidoune [4] by linear-algebraic and
  representation-theoretic tools. Theorem 1.3 ([4]) (p. 405, quoted): "If
  $p$ is a prime, and $A$ is a nonempty subset of $Z_p$, then
  $|\{a+a': a,a'\in A,\ a\ne a'\}|\ge\min\{p,2|A|-3\}$." Paged at
  [[additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes/theorem_1_3|theorem_1_3]].
- § 2, The General Theorem (pp. 406--408). For a polynomial
  $h$ over $Z_p$ in $k+1$ variables, $\bigoplus_h\sum_{i=0}^kA_i$ is the
  set of sums $a_0+\cdots+a_k$ with $a_i\in A_i$ and
  $h(a_0,\ldots,a_k)\ne0$. Theorem 2.1: with $|A_i|=c_i+1$ and
  $m=\sum_{i=0}^kc_i-\deg(h)$, whenever the coefficient of
  $\prod_{i=0}^kx_i^{c_i}$ in $(x_0+\cdots+x_k)^mh(x_0,\ldots,x_k)$ does
  not vanish in $Z_p$, the restricted sumset has at least $m+1$ elements,
  $|\bigoplus_h\sum_{i=0}^kA_i|\ge m+1$, so that $m<p$. Lemma 2.2 is the
  vanishing lemma for polynomials of bounded degree in each variable on a
  product of sets (cited to Alon--Tarsi [2], reproved in half a page), and
  the proof of Theorem 2.1 multiplies $h$ by $\prod_{e\in E}(x_0+\cdots+x_k-e)$
  over a multiset $E$ of $m$ elements covering the sumset, reduces each
  $x_i^{c_i+1}$ through $\prod_{a\in A_i}(x_i-a)$ and reads off the
  surviving top coefficient. The section closes with the derivation of the
  Cauchy--Davenport theorem ($h\equiv1$, $k=1$, coefficient $\binom m{c_0}$).
  Paged at
  [[additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes/theorem_2_1|theorem_2_1]].
- § 3, Adding Distinct Residues (pp. 408--411). Lemma 3.1: if
  $\sum_{i=0}^kc_i=m+\binom{k+1}2$, the coefficient of $\prod_ix_i^{c_i}$ in
  $(x_0+\cdots+x_k)^m\prod_{k\ge i>j\ge0}(x_i-x_j)$ is
  $\frac{m!}{c_0!\cdots c_k!}\prod_{k\ge i>j\ge0}(c_i-c_j)$, proved from the
  Vandermonde determinant. $\bigoplus_{i=0}^kA_i$ denotes the sums
  $a_0+\cdots+a_k$ with $a_i\in A_i$ and all $a_i$ distinct, the case
  $h=\prod_{i>j}(x_i-x_j)$; Proposition 1.2 follows from Lemma 3.1 and
  Theorem 2.1 since $m<p$ and the $c_i$ are pairwise distinct (paged at
  [[additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes/proposition_1_2|proposition_1_2]]).
  Theorem 3.2 (p. 410, quoted): "Let $p$ be a prime, and let $A_0,\ldots,A_k$ be nonempty
  subsets of $Z_p$, where $|A_i|=b_i$, and suppose $b_0\ge b_1\cdots\ge
  b_k$. Define $b'_0,\ldots,b'_k$ by $b'_0=b_0$ and
  $b'_i=\min\{b'_{i-1}-1,b_i\}$, for $1\le i\le k$. (2) If $b'_k>0$ then
  $|\bigoplus_{i=0}^kA_i|\ge\min\{p,\sum_{i=0}^kb'_i-\binom{k+2}2+1\}$.
  Moreover, the above estimate is sharp for all possible values of
  $p\ge b_0\ge\cdots\ge b_k$." (The print omits the $\ge$ after $b_1$ in
  the hypothesis.) Its proof passes to subsets $A'_i\subseteq
  A_i$ of the pairwise distinct sizes $b'_i$, applies Proposition 1.2 when
  $\sum b'_i\le p+\binom{k+2}2-1$ and otherwise trims the sizes by an
  operator $T$ to a sequence $b''_0>\cdots>b''_k\ge1$ with
  $\sum b''_i=p+\binom{k+2}2-1$; sharpness is the intervals
  $A_i=\{1,2,\ldots,b_i\}$, whose distinct-summand sums lie in
  $\{\binom{k+2}2,\ldots,\sum b'_i\}$ (paged at
  [[additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes/theorem_3_2|theorem_3_2]]). The paper then
  derives the theorem of Dias da Silva and Hamidoune [4] from a special case of Theorem 3.2.
  Theorem 3.3 ([4]) (p. 411, quoted): "Let $p$ be a
  prime and let $A$ be a nonempty subset of $Z_p$. Let $s^\wedge A$ denote
  the set of all sums of $s$ distinct elements of $A$. Then
  $|s^\wedge A|\ge\min\{p,s|A|-s^2+1\}$." The proof takes $A_i=A$ for
  $s=k+1$ sets, so $b'_i=|A|-i$, and computes
  $\sum_{i=0}^k(|A|-i)-\binom{k+2}2+1=(k+1)|A|-(k+1)^2+1$. The section
  ends with the sentence "The case $s=2$ of the last theorem settles a
  problem of Erdős and Heilbronn" and a list of the earlier partial results
  on the conjecture, [12], [9], [13], [11] and [6] (Rickert's 1976 thesis,
  Mansfield 1981, Rødseth 1994, Pyber's personal communication and the
  Freiman--Low--Pitman preprint of 1992). Paged at
  [[additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes/theorem_3_3|theorem_3_3]].
- § 4, Further Examples (pp. 411--414). Proposition 4.1 (from
  [1]): $|\{a+b: a\in A,\,b\in B,\,ab\ne1\}|\ge\min\{p,|A|+|B|-3\}$.
  Proposition 4.2: sums $a_0+\cdots+a_k$ with $\prod a_i\ne g$ number at
  least $\min\{p,\sum|A_i|-2k-1\}$ (the print's sum in that minimum runs
  to $p$, a misprint for $k$). Proposition 4.3: sums with
  $a_ia_j\ne1$ for all $i<j$ number at least
  $\min\{p,\sum|A_i|-(k+1)^2+1\}$ when every $|A_i|\ge k+1$, not sharp;
  Proposition 4.4 (a probabilistic argument): the set is nonempty when the
  $A_i\subseteq Z_p-\{1,-1\}$ each have $s>\log_2(k+1)$ elements,
  tight for $s\le(p-3)/2$. Proposition 4.5: for $|A|>|B|$ and any $e$,
  $|\{a+b: a\in A,\,b\in B,\,ab\ne e,\,a\ne b\}|\ge\min\{p,|A|+|B|-4\}$,
  tight for $|A|>|B|>1$ by an arithmetic-progression example.
- § 5, Concluding Remarks and Open Problems (pp. 414--416). 1.
  All results hold over any field of characteristic $p$. 2. Theorem 3.3
  gives $s^\wedge A=Z_p$ when $|A|\ge(p+s^2-1)/s$, which the paper uses for
  an explicit scheme for write-once memories (a notion of Rivest and
  Shamir [14]; $w(\langle v\rangle^t)$ is the fewest write-once bits that
  can hold one of $v$ values through $t$ updates) that improves one of
  their schemes and gives $w(\langle p\rangle^{0.35p})\le p-1$ for every
  prime $p$; the conjectured asymptotic
  $w(\langle v\rangle^t)=(1+o(1))\max\{t,t\log v/\log t\}$ as
  $t,v\to\infty$ is refuted separately, by a counting argument giving
  $w(\langle v\rangle^{\varepsilon v})\ge2\varepsilon v$ for every fixed
  $0<\varepsilon<0.5$ (p. 415). 3. The
  difficulty in further applications of Theorem 2.1 is computing the
  coefficient; for sums with $a_i-a_j\notin E$ the relevant coefficients
  connect to Dyson's conjecture. 4. Vosper's characterization of equality
  in Cauchy--Davenport; the analogous problem for Proposition 1.2, Theorem
  1.3 and § 4 is posed. 5. Non-prime analogs are posed.
- References (pp. 416--417), nineteen items, including [1] the authors'
  1995 Monthly paper, [2] Alon and Tarsi 1992, [3] Davenport 1935, [4] Dias
  da Silva and Hamidoune 1994, [5] Erdős and Graham 1980, [6] Freiman, Low
  and Pitman 1992 (preprint), [13] Rødseth 1994, [14] Rivest and Shamir
  1982 and [16], [17] Vosper 1956.

## Compiled scope

The paper is compiled at statement depth for its main results and the
results Problem 476 consumes, each with a result page: Theorem 2.1 (p. 406),
the general coefficient criterion; Proposition 1.2 (p. 405) and Theorem 3.2
(p. 410), the bounds for sums with distinct summands; and Theorem 1.3
(p. 405) and Theorem 3.3 (p. 411), the results Problem 476 consumes. All
were read on the page images; their proofs were followed for structure,
except that of Theorem 3.3, read in full. Lemmas 2.2 and 3.1 and § 4 are

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/additive_combinatorics/E0476/_index|#476]]: Theorem 1.3
(printed p. 405, PDF p. 2), "If $p$ is a prime, and $A$ is a nonempty
subset of $Z_p$, then $|\{a+a': a,a'\in A,\ a\ne a'\}|\ge\min\{p,2|A|-3\}$",
is the problem's displayed inequality $|A\hat{+}A|\ge\min(2|A|-3,p)$ (the
problem's $A\hat{+}A$ is the paper's set of sums of two distinct elements,
its $2^\wedge A$; for $|A|\le1$ both sides are trivial). The paper labels
it "([4])", Dias da Silva and Hamidoune, and gives two derivations by the
polynomial method: from the case $k=1$ of Proposition 1.2 (p. 405), and as
the case $s=2$ of Theorem 3.3 (p. 411), "The case $s=2$ of the last theorem
settles a problem of Erdős and Heilbronn." Theorem 3.3 (printed p. 411,
PDF p. 8), $|s^\wedge A|\ge\min\{p,s|A|-s^2+1\}$ for the sums of $s$
distinct elements, is the general theorem of Dias da Silva and Hamidoune,
here stated and proved in a refereed paper from Theorem 3.2; in the form
"sums of exactly $s$ distinct elements" it also gives the general
conjecture (73) of Erdős's 1965 lectures, $\min(p,rk-r^2+1)$ distinct sums
of at most $r$ distinct residues out of $k$, since every sum of exactly $r$
distinct elements is a sum of at most $r$ (a filing observation, not a
claim the paper makes). The original paper of Dias da Silva and Hamidoune
is filed separately (reference 4 above), and this paper's proofs of
Theorems 1.3 and 3.3 are its own, not that paper's. The paper's own results
behind those proofs bear on the problem only through them: Proposition 1.2
(p. 405) with $k=1$, $A_0=A$, $A_1=A-\{a\}$ gives the problem's bound
$2|A|-3$ when $2|A|-1\le p+2$, the paper's first route to Theorem 1.3;
Theorem 3.2 (p. 410) with $k=1$ and $A_0=A_1=A$, $|A|\ge2$, gives
$\min(p,2|A|-3)$, the route through Theorem 3.3, and its sharpness example
$\{1,\ldots,b\}$ attains the problem's bound; Theorem 2.1 (p. 406) is the
criterion from which both are derived and does not mention the problem.

**Results.**

- [[additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes/proposition_1_2|Proposition 1.2]]
  (p. 405): at least $\sum_i|A_i|-\binom{k+2}2+1$ sums of pairwise
  distinct summands $a_i\in A_i$, for nonempty $A_i\subseteq Z_p$, when
  the $|A_i|$ are pairwise distinct and $\sum_i|A_i|\le p+\binom{k+2}2-1$.
- [[additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes/theorem_1_3|Theorem 1.3]]
  (p. 405): $|\{a+a': a,a'\in A,\ a\ne a'\}|\ge\min\{p,2|A|-3\}$ for a
  nonempty $A\subseteq Z_p$; the Erdős--Heilbronn conjecture, from the case
  $k=1$ of Proposition 1.2.
- [[additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes/theorem_2_1|Theorem 2.1]]
  (p. 406): a nonzero coefficient of $\prod_ix_i^{c_i}$ in
  $(x_0+\cdots+x_k)^mh$, with $|A_i|=c_i+1$ and $m=\sum_ic_i-\deg(h)$,
  gives $|\bigoplus_h\sum_{i=0}^kA_i|\ge m+1$.
- [[additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes/theorem_3_2|Theorem 3.2]]
  (p. 410): $|\bigoplus_{i=0}^kA_i|\ge\min\{p,\sum_ib'_i-\binom{k+2}2+1\}$
  for the trimmed sizes $b'_i$ when $b'_k>0$, sharp for all
  $p\ge b_0\ge\cdots\ge b_k$.
- [[additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes/theorem_3_3|Theorem 3.3]]
  (p. 411): $|s^\wedge A|\ge\min\{p,s|A|-s^2+1\}$ for the sums of $s$
  distinct elements of a nonempty $A\subseteq Z_p$, from Theorem 3.2 with
  $A_i=A$.
