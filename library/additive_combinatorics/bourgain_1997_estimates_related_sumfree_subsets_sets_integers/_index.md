# additive_combinatorics/bourgain_1997_estimates_related_sumfree_subsets_sets_integers

[[additive_combinatorics/_index|..]]

[[additive_combinatorics/bourgain_1997_estimates_related_sumfree_subsets_sets_integers/display_8_4|display_8_4]]: Bourgain's § 8 fact that no constant fraction of a finite set of positive
integers can always be kept k-sum-free for every k: if s_k(A) > delta_k |A|
holds for all finite A, then delta_k tends to 0, shown by sets built from
blocks of multiples of factorials and an unproved circle-method lemma.

[[additive_combinatorics/bourgain_1997_estimates_related_sumfree_subsets_sets_integers/proposition_1_3|proposition_1_3]]: Bourgain's bound S(B) ≥ (|B| + 2)/3 for the largest sum-free subset of a
set B of positive integers, proved for |B| ≥ 3 by a case analysis on the
three smallest elements with Erdős's rotation argument written as a Fourier
minorization; the (n + 2)/3 in the chain of lower bounds for Problem 792.

[[additive_combinatorics/bourgain_1997_estimates_related_sumfree_subsets_sets_integers/proposition_1_4|proposition_1_4]]: Bourgain's lower bound S(B) >= |B|/3 + c_1 (log |B|)^{-1} times the L^1
norm of the sum of cos 2 pi k theta over k in B, for the largest sum-free
subset of a finite set B of positive integers; the L^1 route to Problem 792
that Bedert's 2025 bound develops.

[[additive_combinatorics/bourgain_1997_estimates_related_sumfree_subsets_sets_integers/proposition_1_7|proposition_1_7]]: Bourgain's bound S_3(B) > |B|/4 + c log |B| / log log |B| for the largest
subset A of a finite set B of positive integers with (A + A + A) ∩ A
empty, proved with two asymmetric arcs about 1/4 and -1/4 and the paper's
version of the McGehee-Pigno-Smith L^1 estimate.

## Contents

- Abstract and § 1, Introduction (pp. 71--72, page images). The abstract
  opens with the definition, quoted: "A subset $A$ of the positive integers
  $\mathbb Z_+$ is called sumfree provided $(A+A)\cap A=\emptyset$." It
  then announces the paper's results: every finite $B\subset\mathbb Z_+$
  has a sumfree subset $A$ with $|A|\ge\frac13(|B|+2)$, a slight
  improvement on Erdős [Erd] and Alon and Kleitman [A-K], proved by
  harmonic analysis refining Erdős's original approach; with $s_k(B)$ the
  maximum size of a $k$-sumfree subset $A$ of $B$, one whose $k$-fold sum
  $(A)_k=A+\cdots+A$ is disjoint from $A$, the same techniques give for
  instance $s_3(B)>\frac{|B|}4+c\frac{\log|B|}{\log\log|B|}$, improving the
  $s_k(B)>\frac{|B|}4$ that Erdős's argument yields; and any inequality
  $s_k(B)>\delta_k|B|$ valid for all finite $B\subset\mathbb Z_+$ forces
  $\delta_k\to0$ as $k\to\infty$, which the author says had been unclear.
  The abstract closes by calling the methods and the harmonic analysis
  questions they raise the most interesting part of the paper. The
  introduction recalls (1.1), Erdős's observation in [Erd] that any finite
  $B\subset\mathbb Z$ has a sumfree subset of size at least $\frac13|B|$,
  and (1.2), Alon and Kleitman's remark in [A-K] that the same argument
  gives strict inequality, hence a sumfree subset of size at least
  $\frac13(|B|+1)$; the paper's stated aim is to treat this and similar
  problems by harmonic analysis, bounding discrepancies with
  trigonometric-sum estimates, and its first illustration is a slight
  improvement of (1.2). Proposition 1.3 (p. 72, quoted):
  "$S(B)\ge\frac13(|B|+2)$, for any $B\subset\mathbb Z_+$. $S(B)$ denotes
  the maximum size of a sumfree subset of $B$." Proposition 1.4 (p. 72,
  quoted): "$S(B)\ge\frac{|B|}3+c_1(\log|B|)^{-1}\bigl\|\sum_{k\in
  B}\cos2\pi k\theta\bigr\|_1$. Here $c_1$ is some fixed constant", with
  (1.5), from the solution of Littlewood's conjecture, $\bigl\|\sum_{k\in
  B}e^{2\pi ik\theta}\bigr\|_1\equiv\int_0^1\bigl|\sum_{k\in B}\cos2\pi
  k\theta\bigr|\,d\theta>c_2\log|B|$, cited to [M-P-S] for its proof; the
  paper remarks that in many cases Proposition 1.4 gives a larger gain over
  $|B|/3$. $S_3(B)$ is the size of the largest $A\subset B$ with (1.6)
  $(A+A+A)\cap A=\emptyset$, and Proposition 1.7 (p. 72, quoted):
  "$S_3(B)>\frac{|B|}4+c\frac{\log|B|}{\log\log|B|}$", presented as an
  improvement on the inequality $S_3(B)>|B|/4$, which the paper calls
  obvious, and as the technically most interesting part of the paper.
- § 2, Harmonic analysis formulation (p. 73, page image). $f$ is the
  indicator of the arc $J=\,]\tfrac12-\tfrac16,\tfrac12+\tfrac16[$ on
  $\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$; a set $A\subset\mathbb Z$ all of whose
  dilates $nx$, $n\in A$, fall in $J$ for one $x\in\mathbb T$ is sumfree,
  since $J+J$ misses $J$, hence (2.1)
  $S(B)\ge\max_x\sum_{m\in B}f(mx)=\frac{|B|}3+\max_x\sum_{m\in B}(f-\tfrac13)(mx)$,
  Erdős's rotation argument. The Fourier expansion
  (2.2) writes $f-\frac13$ as $-\frac{\sqrt3}\pi\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\chi(n)}n$
  times the cosine at frequency $n$, where (2.3) $\chi(n)$ is $0,1,-1$ for
  $n\equiv0,1,2\pmod3$; a Möbius sieve over the primes up to $P$
  ((2.4)--(2.6)) rewrites the combination of the sums
  $\sum_{m\in B}(f-\frac13)(mkx)$ over $k\mid P!$ with the signed weights
  $\mu(k)\chi(k)/k$ as $-\frac{\sqrt3}\pi\sum_{m\in B}\cos mx$
  plus a sum over $n>1$ coprime to the primes up to $P$.
- § 3, Proof of Proposition 1.3 (pp. 74--76, page images). The quantity
  minorized is (3.1), $\max_x\sum_{m\in B}(f-\frac13)(mx)
  =\frac{\sqrt3}\pi\max_xF(x)$ with $F(x)=-\sum_{n\ge1,m\in
  B}\frac{\chi(n)}n\cos nmx$ (3.2). The elements of $B$ are
  $0<m_1<m_2<m_3<\cdots<m_N$, and $\gcd(B)=1$ is assumed without loss of
  generality. Case (I), $m_1>1$: with $j$
  the least index such that $m_j\notin m_1\mathbb Z$ and the test function
  $G(x)=(1-\cos m_1x)(1-\cos m_jx)$ ($G\ge0$, $\int G=1$), (3.4)--(3.7) give
  (3.8) $\max F\ge\frac12+\frac12-\frac14=\frac34$ and (3.9) $(3.1)\ge
  \frac{\sqrt3}\pi\cdot\frac34=0{,}41\ldots>\frac13$. Case (II), $m_1=1$:
  if $m_2>2$, $G=1-\frac43\cos x+\frac13\cos2x$ gives (3.12) the same
  $\frac{\sqrt3}\pi\cdot\frac34>\frac13$; if $m_2=2$, $G=(1-\cos x)(1-\cos
  m_3x)$ and six subcases on $m_3$ ($m_3=3,4,5$; $m_3\ge6$ in each residue
  class modulo 3) give (3.15)--(3.23), each a numerical bound between
  $0{,}35\ldots$ and $0{,}39\ldots$, all $>\frac13$. Conclusion (p. 76):
  the case bounds (3.9), (3.12), (3.15), (3.16), (3.17), (3.19), (3.21) and
  (3.23) together give, in the paper's words, "for any $B\subset\mathbb
  Z_+$, $|B|\ge3$", the inequality (3.24)
  $\max_{x\in\mathbb T}\bigl[\sum_{m\in B}(f-\frac13)(mx)\bigr]>\frac13$,
  so by (2.1) $S(B)>\frac{|B|}3+\frac13$, and since $3S(B)$ is an integer,
  $S(B)\ge\frac{|B|}3+\frac23$, which is Proposition 1.3. The result page
  records a filing observation on the hypothesis $|B|\ge3$, which the
  statement on p. 72 omits.
- § 4, Proof of Proposition 1.4 (p. 77, text layer). Since $\int F=0$,
  (4.1) $\max F\ge\frac12\|F\|_{L^1}$; in the sieved form (2.6) the second
  term is bounded (4.3) by $C|B|P^{-1/2}$, and $P=|B|^2$ gives (4.4)
  $\|F\|_1>c(\log P)^{-1}\bigl\|\sum_{m\in B}\cos mx\bigr\|_1$.
- § 5, Further estimates on (2.6) (pp. 77--82, text layer). Lemma 5.1: for
  a finite $S\subset\mathbb Z_+$ and $P>(\log|S|)^2$, for every $K>1$
  the sum of $|S\cap kS|$ over the $k\le K$ coprime to the primes up to $P$,
  divided by $K$ (not by the number of such $k$), is less than
  $CP^{-1/2}|S|\log|S|$, by a partition of these $k$ according to the
  quotient $k/q(k)$, $q(k)$ the largest prime divisor of $k$; a remark
  that the count of such $k$ alone "does
  not imply (5.2)"; Lemma 5.25 (an $\ell^2$ version for $f\in
  L^2(\mathbb T)$, $\|f\|_2\le1$, $P>(\log N)^4$) and Lemma 5.35 (a dyadic
  consequence for the sieved sum over $B$).
- § 6, The Littlewood conjecture revisited (pp. 83--86, text layer). The
  section reproduces the [M-P-S] proof of the Littlewood conjecture (6.1),
  $\|\sum_{n=1}^Ne^{im_nx}\|_{L^1(\mathbb T)}>c\log N$ for $m_1<\cdots<m_N$,
  with the adjustments the later sections need, ending in Lemma 6.22 and
  Lemma 6.27 ($P>(\log|B|)^{100}$, coefficients $|a_n|,|b_n|\le1$: an $L^1$
  lower bound $c\log|B|$ for the sieved exponential sum).
- § 7, Proof of Proposition 1.7 (pp. 87--88, text layer). The approach is
  the one used to minorize $s_2(B)$, with the indicators $f_+$ and $f_-$ of
  two short arcs about $\frac14$ and $-\frac14$ (7.1) in place of $f$, and
  the $L^1$ estimates of § 6. Two remarks (p. 88). Remark 1 explains why
  the same device does not improve the lower bound for $s_2(B)$: a set
  $\Omega\subset\mathbb T$ with $|\Omega|=\frac13$ and
  $(\Omega+\Omega)\cap\Omega=\emptyset$, taken in place of the arc
  $]\frac12-\frac16,\frac12+\frac16[$, satisfies
  $\Omega=\mathbb T\setminus(\Omega-\Omega)$ by Kneser's theorem and is
  therefore symmetric. Remark 2 notes that the proof of Proposition 1.7
  resembles [B].
- § 8, Further remarks (pp. 89--91; p. 89 and p. 90 on the page images, the
  rest in the text layer). $A\subset\mathbb Z_+$ is $k$-sum free if (8.1)
  $(A)_k\cap A=\emptyset$, $s_k(A)$ the largest size of a $k$-sum-free
  subset; (8.2) $s_k(A)>\frac1{k+1}|A|$ and an infinite version (8.3) both
  follow from the analogue of (2.1) for the arc
  $J=\bigl[\frac1{2(k-1)}-\frac1{2(k+1)},\frac1{2(k-1)}+\frac1{2(k+1)}\bigr]$,
  averaged over $x\in\mathbb T$. The section's main point is that an
  estimate (8.4) $s_k(A)>\delta_k|A|$ holding for every finite
  $A\subset\mathbb Z_+$ forces $\delta_k\to0$ as $k\to\infty$, shown by
  explicit examples. Lemma 8.5 ("an exercise on the circle method (we omit
  the proof)"): a set $A\subset\mathbb Z\cap[0,M]$
  with $|A|>\delta M$ has integers $r,q<C(\delta)$ and an interval
  $I\subset[M,rM]$ of length $M$ with $q(I\cap\mathbb Z)\subset(A)_r$. The
  construction (pp. 90--91): $A_j=\{j!\,n\mid\frac N2\le n\le N\}$,
  $A=\bigcup_{j\le J}A_j$, and for $B\subset A$ with $|B|>\delta|A|$ three
  indices $j_0<j_1<j_2$ with $B$ dense in $A_{j_0}$ and $A_{j_1}$ and
  meeting $A_{j_2}$, so that for $k$ large depending on $\delta$ the
  $k$-fold sum $(B)_k$ covers $A_{j_2}$ and $(B)_k\cap B\ne\emptyset$.
- References (p. 92, page image), four items: [A-K], [B], [Erd] and
  [M-P-S], as listed above.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/additive_combinatorics/E0792/_index|#792]]: Proposition
1.3 (printed p. 72, PDF p. 2) is the bound $f(n)\ge(n+2)/3$ the site
attributes to the paper, paged on
[[additive_combinatorics/bourgain_1997_estimates_related_sumfree_subsets_sets_integers/proposition_1_3|proposition_1_3]]:
"$S(B)\ge\frac13(|B|+2)$, for any
$B\subset\mathbb Z_+$", where "$S(B)$ denotes the maximum size of a sumfree
subset of $B$" and sumfree means $(A+A)\cap A=\emptyset$ (p. 71), the
problem's convention that forbids $a+b=c$ with $a=b$ allowed. The proof
(pp. 74--76) concludes (3.24) "for any $B\subset\mathbb Z_+$, $|B|\ge3$"
(p. 76, PDF p. 6), the range under which
[[additive_combinatorics/eberhard_2014_sets_integers_no_large_sum_free/_index|Eberhard, Green and Manners]]
quote it ("for $n\ge3$", p. 1), while
[[additive_combinatorics/bedert_2025_large_sum_free_subsets_sets_integers/_index|Bedert]]
states the bound with no size condition (p. 2); the set $\{1,2\}$ has
$S=1<\frac43$, so the restriction is needed. The paper states the bound
for positive integers, where
[[additive_combinatorics/alon_1990_sum_free_subsets/proposition_1_1|Alon and Kleitman]]
state theirs for nonzero integers.
[[additive_combinatorics/bourgain_1997_estimates_related_sumfree_subsets_sets_integers/proposition_1_4|Proposition 1.4]]
(p. 72) is a lower bound for $S(B)$ whose excess over $|B|/3$ is
$c_1\|\sum_{k\in B}\cos2\pi k\theta\|_1/\log|B|$; the paper draws from it
no bound on $f(n)$ beyond Proposition 1.3, and it is the $L^1$ route that
Bedert's paper develops into the $c\log\log n$ bound.
Remark 1 of § 7 (p. 88, PDF p. 18, read on the page image) records why
the asymmetric-interval method of Proposition 1.7 does not reach $S(B)$: any
$\Omega\subset\mathbb T$ with $|\Omega|=\frac13$ and
$(\Omega+\Omega)\cap\Omega=\emptyset$ satisfies
$\Omega=\mathbb T\setminus(\Omega-\Omega)$ by Kneser's theorem and so is
symmetric, which rules out the asymmetric two-arc device of § 7 for $S(B)$.
The problem page reads Proposition 1.3 on the page image at statement depth
and its proof for structure; no proof was checked.

**Results.**

- [[additive_combinatorics/bourgain_1997_estimates_related_sumfree_subsets_sets_integers/proposition_1_3|Proposition 1.3]]
  (p. 72): $S(B)\ge\frac13(|B|+2)$ for $B\subset\mathbb Z_+$, proved for
  $|B|\ge3$ (p. 76). Bears on #792.
- [[additive_combinatorics/bourgain_1997_estimates_related_sumfree_subsets_sets_integers/proposition_1_4|Proposition 1.4]]
  (p. 72; proof p. 77): $S(B)\ge\frac{|B|}3+c_1(\log|B|)^{-1}
  \|\sum_{k\in B}\cos2\pi k\theta\|_1$ with $c_1$ a fixed constant. Bears
  on #792 as a lower bound for $S(B)$ in terms of an $L^1$ norm.
- [[additive_combinatorics/bourgain_1997_estimates_related_sumfree_subsets_sets_integers/proposition_1_7|Proposition 1.7]]
  (p. 72; proof pp. 87--88):
  $S_3(B)>\frac{|B|}4+c\frac{\log|B|}{\log\log|B|}$ for the largest
  $A\subset B$ with $(A+A+A)\cap A=\emptyset$. No Erdős problem in this
  corpus concerns $S_3$; its Remark 1 (p. 88) records why the method does not
  reach $S(B)$.
- [[additive_combinatorics/bourgain_1997_estimates_related_sumfree_subsets_sets_integers/display_8_4|Display (8.4)]]
  (p. 89; construction pp. 90--91): a bound $s_k(A)>\delta_k|A|$ valid for
  every finite $A\subset\mathbb Z_+$ forces $\delta_k\to0$ as
  $k\to\infty$, by examples resting on Lemma 8.5, stated without proof. No
  Erdős problem in this corpus concerns $s_k$ for $k\ge3$.
