## Statement

Setting (pp. 1--2). For a finite subset $A$ of an abelian group $(G,+)$ and
an ordering $\omega=(x_{j_1},\ldots,x_{j_k})$ of its elements,
$s_i(\omega)=x_{j_1}+\cdots+x_{j_i}$. **Conjecture 1.1** (Alspach; p. 1):
"Given an abelian group $(G,+)$ and a subset $A$ of $G\setminus\{0_G\}$ of
size $k$ such that $\sum_{z\in A}z\neq0_G$, it is possible to find an
ordering $\omega$ of the elements of $A$ such that $s_i(\omega)\neq0_G$ and
$s_i(\omega)\neq s_j(\omega)$ for all $1\le i<j\le k$." The paper calls $A$
*nice* when $0_G\notin A$ and its sum is not $0_G$, and says that Alspach's
conjecture is true in $G$ for any subset of size $k$ when it holds for every
nice subset of $G$ of size $k$ (p. 2).

**Theorem 2.4** (p. 3, quoted). "Let $k$ be a positive integer and suppose
that, for infinitely many primes $p$, Alspach's conjecture holds in
$\mathbb Z_p$ for any subset of size $k$. Then Alspach's conjecture holds for
any subset of size $k$ in any torsion-free abelian group $G$."

**Source.** S. Costa and M. A. Pellegrini, *Some new results about a
conjecture by Brian Alspach*, Arch. Math. (Basel) 115 (2020), no. 5,
479--488, DOI 10.1007/s00013-020-01507-7, read in the arXiv version
arXiv:2003.05939v2 (23 April 2020; 9 pp.), whose pagination is used here,
as identified on the
[[additive_combinatorics/costa_2020_new_results_about_conjecture_brian_alspach/_index|source card]]:
Conjecture 1.1 on p. 1, the definitions and Lemma 2.1 on p. 2, Propositions
2.2 and 2.3 and Theorem 2.4 on p. 3.

## Proof pointer

Pp. 2--3. For a nice $A$ the paper sets
$\Upsilon(A)=A\cup\Delta(A)\cup\{\sum_{z\in A}z\}$, where $\Delta(A)$ is the
set of differences of distinct elements of $A$. Lemma 2.1 (p. 2): if
Alspach's conjecture holds in $G_2$ for every subset of size $k$, $A$ is a
nice subset of $G_1$ of size $k$, and some homomorphism
$\varphi\colon G_1\to G_2$ has $\ker(\varphi)\cap\Upsilon(A)=\emptyset$, then
the conjecture holds for $A$; the image of $A$ is a nice set of size $k$, and
an ordering of it pulls back. Proposition 2.2 (p. 3) passes from infinitely
many primes to $\mathbb Z$ by reducing modulo a prime larger than every
$|z|$ with $z\in\Upsilon(A)$. Proposition 2.3 (p. 3) passes from $\mathbb Z$
to $\mathbb Z^n$, $n\ge2$, through $\varphi(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\sum_ix_iM^{i-1}$
with $M$ larger than every $n|z_j|$ over the coordinates of the elements of
$\Upsilon(A)$, whose kernel misses $\Upsilon(A)$ by uniqueness of base-$M$
expansions. For the theorem, the subgroup generated by $A$ is finitely
generated and torsion-free, so by the structure theorem it is isomorphic to
a subgroup of $\mathbb Z^k$, and the two propositions apply.
