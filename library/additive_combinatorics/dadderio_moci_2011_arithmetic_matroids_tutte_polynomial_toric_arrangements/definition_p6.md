## Statement

**The main example** (Section 1.4, p. 6). Let $X$ be a finite list of
elements of a finitely generated abelian group $G\cong G_f\oplus G_t$, with
$G_t$ the (finite) torsion subgroup and $G_f$ free abelian. For a sublist
$A\subseteq X$ let $\langle A\rangle$ be the subgroup generated by its
underlying set.

- The rank $rk(A)$ is the maximal rank of a free abelian subgroup of
  $\langle A\rangle$; this defines a matroid on $X$.
- $G_A$ is the maximal subgroup of $G$ with $\langle A\rangle\le G_A$ and
  $|G_A:\langle A\rangle|<\infty$, and the multiplicity is
  $m(A):=|G_A:\langle A\rangle|$.

The paper assumes throughout that $\langle X\rangle$ has finite index in $G$,
replacing $G$ by $G_X$ otherwise, and notes that $m(\emptyset)=|G_t|$, so
$m(\emptyset)=1$ if and only if $G$ is free abelian (p. 6). That $m$ satisfies
the five axioms of
[[additive_combinatorics/dadderio_moci_2011_arithmetic_matroids_tutte_polynomial_toric_arrangements/definition_p4|an arithmetic matroid]]
is completed on p. 11: axiom (1) directly, axiom (3) by Lemma 1.4 (p. 6),
axiom (4) by Lemma 4.1 (p. 16), and axioms (2) and (5) by duality through
[[additive_combinatorics/dadderio_moci_2011_arithmetic_matroids_tutte_polynomial_toric_arrangements/theorem_2_2|Theorem 2.2]].

**Representability** (Section 1.5, p. 8). An arithmetic matroid is
representable if it is realized, with rank and multiplicity defined as above,
by a list of elements of a finitely generated abelian group. It is
0-representable if its underlying matroid is realized by vectors in
$\mathbb R^n$, torsion-free if $m(\emptyset)=1$, and GCD if $m(A)$ equals the
greatest common divisor of $m(B)$ over the sublists $B\subseteq A$ with
$|B|=rk(B)=rk(A)$. Remark 1.6 (p. 8) states that a representable arithmetic
matroid is 0-representable, and GCD when it is also torsion-free. Example 1.7
(p. 9) takes a matroid that is not 0-representable, such as the Fano matroid,
with any multiplicity function, and Example 1.8 (p. 9) takes the matroid on
$\{a,b,c\}$ whose bases are the three pairs, with the bases of multiplicity 2
and all other sublists of multiplicity 1, which is not GCD since
$m(X)=1\ne2$; neither is representable.

**Computing multiplicities** (Remark 2.3, p. 10). For a list of elements of
$\mathbb Z^m$, the multiplicity is the greatest common divisor of the minors
of order equal to the rank of the matrix whose columns are the elements.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: Sections 1.4 and 1.5 were read clause by
clause on pp. 6--9, and Remark 2.3 on p. 10.

## Proof pointer

The axioms for the main example are proved in Lemma 1.4 (pp. 6--8), Lemma
4.1 (p. 16, stated as a consequence of Pontryagin duality) and Theorem 2.2
(pp. 9--11).
