# additive_combinatorics/dias_da_silva_hamidoune_1994_cyclic_spaces_grassmann_derivatives_additive_theory

[[additive_combinatorics/dias_da_silva_hamidoune_1994_cyclic_spaces_grassmann_derivatives_additive_theory/corollary_3_3|corollary_3_3]]: Dias da Silva and Hamidoune's bound for the degree of the minimal
polynomial of the derivative Df on the mth Grassmann space in terms of
that of f; it is printed without the min with the characteristic p, and
the proof of Theorem 4.1 uses it with the min.

[[additive_combinatorics/dias_da_silva_hamidoune_1994_cyclic_spaces_grassmann_derivatives_additive_theory/corollary_4_3|corollary_4_3]]: Dias da Silva and Hamidoune's corollary for nonzero residues: for a prime
p, every set A of ⌊√(4p-7)⌋ nonzero residues modulo p represents every
element of Z_p as the sum of a subset of A of cardinality ⌊(|A|-1)/2⌋ or
⌊(|A|+1)/2⌋, a threshold Example 4.2 shows best possible for infinitely
many primes.

[[additive_combinatorics/dias_da_silva_hamidoune_1994_cyclic_spaces_grassmann_derivatives_additive_theory/theorem_3_2|theorem_3_2]]: Dias da Silva and Hamidoune's cyclic-subspace bound for Grassmann
derivatives: for a linear operator f on a finite-dimensional space V and a
vector v, the cyclic subspace of the derivative Df on the mth Grassmann
space generated by v ∧ f(v) ∧ ... ∧ f^{m-1}(v) has dimension at least
min(p, (dim C_f(v) - m)m + 1), with p the characteristic.

[[additive_combinatorics/dias_da_silva_hamidoune_1994_cyclic_spaces_grassmann_derivatives_additive_theory/theorem_4_1|theorem_4_1]]: The m-fold Erdős–Heilbronn theorem as the paper's Theorem 4.1: for a
finite subset A of a field of characteristic p (infinity in characteristic
zero) and a positive integer m, the sums of the m-subsets of A number at
least min(p, m|A| - m^2 + 1); the case m = 2 in Z_p is the Erdős–Heilbronn
conjecture.

[[additive_combinatorics/dias_da_silva_hamidoune_1994_cyclic_spaces_grassmann_derivatives_additive_theory/theorem_4_2|theorem_4_2]]: Dias da Silva and Hamidoune's subset-sum theorem: for a prime p, every
set A of ⌊√(4p-7)⌋ + 1 residues modulo p, the residue 0 allowed,
represents every element of Z_p as the sum of a subset of A of
cardinality ⌊(⌊√(4p-7)⌋ + 1)/2⌋.

## Contents

- Abstract and § 1, Introduction (pp. 140--141, page images). $F$ is a
  field, $V$ a vector space of finite dimension $d$ over $F$, and $p$ the
  characteristic of $F$ when it is nonzero and $\infty$ otherwise; an
  $m$-set is a set of $m$ elements; for $A\subseteq F$, $\wedge^mA$ is the
  set of sums of the $m$-subsets of $A$; $\wedge^mV$ is the $m$th Grassmann
  space of $V$, and every linear operator $f$ on $V$ induces its derivative
  $Df$ on $\wedge^mV$, display (1), which applies $f$ to one factor of a
  decomposable element at a time and sums the results (the printed display
  writes the upper limit and the last factor with $n$ where the context has
  $m$). The two announced results: the derivative $Df$ has a cyclic
  subspace of dimension at least $\min\{p,m(n-m)+1\}$, where $n$ is the
  largest dimension of a cyclic subspace of $f$, sharp when $f$ has $d$
  distinct eigenvalues in arithmetic progression; and, for a finite
  $A\subseteq F$ with $|A|=a$, $|\wedge^mA|\ge\min\{p,m(a-m)+1\}$, sharp when
  $A$ is an arithmetic progression. Then (p. 140): "The case $m=2$ of this
  formula was conjectured by Erdös and Heilbronn", followed by a quotation
  of the monograph passage [4, p. 95, lines 11--14] (a filing observation:
  the paper's quotation reads "residues modulo $p$" and "the pairs of sums"
  where the monograph's page image, recorded on the problem page, reads
  "distinct residues modulo $p$" and "the pair sums"). The second
  application (p. 141): Erdős and Heilbronn [5] proved that
  $A\subseteq Z_p\setminus\{0\}$ with $|A|\ge3\sqrt{6p}$ has every element
  of $Z_p$ as a subset sum, Olson [10] lowered the threshold to
  $\lfloor\sqrt{4p-3}\rfloor+1$, and the paper lowers it to
  $\lfloor\sqrt{4p-7}\rfloor+1$ without excluding $0$, with the subset's
  size prescribed (Theorem 4.2 and Corollary 4.3, stated in advance).
- § 2, A combinatorial formula (pp. 141--142, page images, structure).
  Partitions are written increasing, $\lambda=(\lambda_1\le\cdots\le\lambda_t)$,
  with $\deg\lambda=\sum\lambda_i$; $\mathcal P_s$ is the set of partitions
  of length at most $s$ and $\mathcal P_{k,s}$ those of degree $k$;
  $\mu\to\lambda$ means that $\lambda$ is $\mu$ with one part increased by
  one; $[\lambda]$ is the Young diagram and $h^\lambda_{ij}$ the hook length
  of its $(i,j)$-box. Theorem 2.1 ([7, Theorem 2.3.21]) is the hook-length
  formula for the degree $\xi_\lambda(\mathrm{id})=k!/\prod_{ij}h^\lambda_{ij}$
  of the irreducible character of $S_k$ indexed by $\lambda$, and
  Theorem 2.2 ([7, Theorem 2.4.3]) the branching rule
  $\xi_\lambda=\sum_{\mu\to\lambda}\xi_\mu$ on restriction to $S_k$.
  Corollary 2.3: for $\lambda\in\mathcal P_{k+1,m}$,
  $(k+1)/\prod_{ij}h^\lambda_{ij}=\sum_{\mu\to\lambda}1/\prod_{ij}h^\mu_{ij}$,
  by dividing the branching rule evaluated at the identity by $k!$. The
  authors write that they know no direct proof of this identity.
- § 3, Derivatives in the Grassmann space (pp. 142--144; Theorem 3.2 and
  Corollary 3.3 on the page images, the rest on the page images for
  structure). With a basis $e^0,\ldots,e^{d-1}$ of $V$, the standard basis
  $\mathcal I$ of $\wedge^mV$ is indexed by the partitions
  $\lambda\in\mathcal P_m$ with $\lambda_m\le d-m$ through
  $e^{\lambda_1}\wedge e^{1+\lambda_2}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{m-1+\lambda_m}$,
  and for a linear operator $f$ and a vector $v$,
  $\wedge(f^\lambda)(v)=f^{\lambda_1}(v)\wedge f^{\lambda_2+1}(v)\wedge\cdots\wedge f^{\lambda_m+m-1}(v)$.
  Theorem 3.1 (p. 143): the $k$th power of the derivative applied to
  $v\wedge f(v)\wedge\cdots\wedge f^{m-1}(v)$ equals
  $\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal P_{k,m}}\bigl(k!/\prod_{ij}h^\lambda_{ij}\bigr)\wedge(f^\lambda)(v)$,
  by induction on $k$ from Corollary 2.3. $\mathcal C_g(x)$ denotes the
  cyclic subspace spanned by $g^i(x)$, $i\ge0$, and $\mathcal P_m$ is
  ordered by degree and then lexicographically. Theorem 3.2 (p. 143): for a
  linear operator $f$ on $V$ and $v\in V$, the cyclic subspace of $Df$
  generated by $v\wedge f(v)\wedge\cdots\wedge f^{m-1}(v)$ has dimension at
  least $\min\{p,(\dim\mathcal C_f(v)-m)m+1\}$. Its proof (pp. 143--144),
  read for structure: for $k\le\min\{p-1,m(n-m)\}$ with $n=\dim\mathcal C_f(v)$
  (the proof's opening line prints $\max$ where its conclusion has $\min$),
  the partition $\omega\in\mathcal P_{k,m}$ that is largest in the ordering
  has a nonzero coefficient $k!/\prod_{ij}h^\omega_{ij}$ modulo $p$, since
  $k<p$, and every other term of Theorem 3.1 is a combination of basis
  elements below $\omega$, so the vectors $(Df)^k(v\wedge\cdots\wedge f^{m-1}(v))$,
  $0\le k\le\min\{p-1,(n-m)m\}$, are linearly independent. The spectrum
  formula $\sigma(Df)=\wedge^m\sigma(f)$, the sums of $m$ distinct
  eigenvalues counted with their indices, is quoted as well known [2, 6].
  Corollary 3.3 (p. 144), as printed: $\deg P_{Df}\ge(\deg P_f-m)m+1$,
  where $P_T$ is the minimal polynomial of $T$; proved from Theorem 3.2 and
  the fact that the largest dimension of an $f$-cyclic subspace is
  $\deg P_f$ [8, Theorem 6, p. 397]. A filing observation, not a review
  verdict: the printed corollary omits the $\min$ with $p$ that Theorem 3.2
  carries and that the proof of Theorem 4.1 uses when it cites the
  corollary; as printed it fails for the diagonal operator on $Z_p^{\,p}$
  with spectrum $Z_p$ and $m=2$, $p\ge5$, where $\deg P_{Df}=p<2p-3$. It is
  read here as $\deg P_{Df}\ge\min\{p,(\deg P_f-m)m+1\}$. Paged at
  [[additive_combinatorics/dias_da_silva_hamidoune_1994_cyclic_spaces_grassmann_derivatives_additive_theory/theorem_3_2|theorem_3_2]] and
  [[additive_combinatorics/dias_da_silva_hamidoune_1994_cyclic_spaces_grassmann_derivatives_additive_theory/corollary_3_3|corollary_3_3]].
- § 4, Some additive problems (pp. 144--145, page images). Theorem 4.1
  (p. 144): for a finite subset $A$ of a field $F$ and a positive integer
  $m$, $|\wedge^mA|\ge\min\{p,m|A|-m^2+1\}$. Proof: take the diagonal
  operator $f$ on $F^n$ with spectrum $A$; then $\sigma(Df)=\wedge^mA$, $Df$
  is diagonal, so $\deg P_{Df}$ is the number of its distinct eigenvalues,
  and Corollary 3.3 gives the bound. The remark after the proof states the
  case $m=2$ for $A\subseteq Z_p$, $|A\wedge A|\ge\min\{p,2|A|-3\}$, as the
  result "conjectured by Erdös and Heilbronn [4]", analogous to the
  Cauchy--Davenport theorem with the addition of an element to itself
  excluded [9]. Example 4.1: for $S=\{1,\ldots,a\}$ the sums of the
  $m$-subsets are the integers from $m(m+1)/2$ to $ma-m(m-1)/2$, and the
  image $A$ of $S$ in $Z_p$ has $|\wedge^mA|=m(|A|-m)+1$ when $m\le|A|$
  and $m(|A|-m)+1\le p$ (conditions the print leaves implicit), so the
  bound is best possible for $Z_p$. Paged at
  [[additive_combinatorics/dias_da_silva_hamidoune_1994_cyclic_spaces_grassmann_derivatives_additive_theory/theorem_4_1|theorem_4_1]].
  Then the subset-sum application (p. 145). Theorem 4.2: a set
  $A\subseteq Z_p$ of cardinality $\lfloor\sqrt{4p-7}\rfloor+1$ has every
  element of $Z_p$ as the sum of a subset of cardinality
  $\lfloor(\lfloor\sqrt{4p-7}\rfloor+1)/2\rfloor$. Proof: the identity
  $\lfloor\sqrt{4p-7}\rfloor=\lceil\sqrt{4p-4}\rceil-1$, which holds for
  every prime since $4p-6\equiv2$ and $4p-5\equiv3\pmod4$ are not
  squares, gives $|A|=\lceil\sqrt{4p-4}\rceil$ and so
  $\lfloor|A|^2/4\rfloor\ge p-1$; with $m=\lfloor|A|/2\rfloor$, Theorem 4.1
  then gives $|\wedge^mA|\ge\min\{p,\lfloor|A|^2/4\rfloor+1\}=p$. The first
  remark: best possible, by Example 4.1 with $|A|<\lfloor\sqrt{4p-7}\rfloor$,
  where the print writes
  $|\wedge^mA|=m(|A|-m)+1\le\lfloor|A|^2/4\rfloor\le p-1$ (a filing
  observation: at $m=\lfloor|A|/2\rfloor$,
  $m(|A|-m)+1=\lfloor|A|^2/4\rfloor+1$, so the first inequality fails, but
  $|A|^2<4p-7$ gives $\lfloor|A|^2/4\rfloor\le p-2$, so
  $|\wedge^mA|\le p-1$ still holds). The second remark:
  $0\in A$ is not excluded and only subsets of cardinality
  $\lfloor|A|/2\rfloor$ are used, "However, our bound is sharper than
  Olson's bound." Corollary 4.3: $A\subseteq Z_p\setminus\{0\}$ with
  $|A|=\lfloor\sqrt{4p-7}\rfloor$ has every element of $Z_p$ as the sum of a
  subset of cardinality $\lfloor(|A|-1)/2\rfloor$ or $\lfloor(|A|+1)/2\rfloor$,
  by applying Theorem 4.2 to $A\cup\{0\}$. The third remark: Theorem 4.2
  stays valid with subsets of cardinality
  $\lceil(\lfloor\sqrt{4p-7}\rfloor+1)/2\rceil$ in place of
  $\lfloor(\lfloor\sqrt{4p-7}\rfloor+1)/2\rfloor$ (only the outer floor
  becomes a ceiling), and it adds that a similar statement holds for
  Corollary 4.3. Example 4.2, attributed to Erdős and Heilbronn: when
  $\lfloor\sqrt{4p-7}\rfloor=2s+1$ is odd,
  $A=\{-s,\ldots,-1\}\cup\{1,\ldots,s\}$ has $s^2+s+1\le p-1$ subset sums
  modulo $p$, a set of cardinality $\lfloor\sqrt{4p-7}\rfloor-1$ whose sums
  do not cover $Z_p$, so the bound of Corollary 4.3 is best possible; by a
  theorem of Balog [1] infinitely many primes have this parity. Applied to
  the element $0$, Corollary 4.3 says that, for $p\ge5$ (where both subset
  sizes are positive), $\lfloor\sqrt{4p-7}\rfloor$ distinct nonzero
  residues modulo $p$ have a nonempty subset with sum $0$, the prime case
  of the question of Problem 540 with a threshold below Olson's
  $s>(4p-3)^{1/2}$ (a filing observation; that problem page does not cite
  this paper). Paged at
  [[additive_combinatorics/dias_da_silva_hamidoune_1994_cyclic_spaces_grassmann_derivatives_additive_theory/theorem_4_2|theorem_4_2]] and
  [[additive_combinatorics/dias_da_silva_hamidoune_1994_cyclic_spaces_grassmann_derivatives_additive_theory/corollary_4_3|corollary_4_3]].

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/additive_combinatorics/E0476/_index|#476]]: Theorem 4.1
(printed p. 144, PDF p. 5), for a finite subset $A$ of a field $F$ and a
positive integer $m$, $|\wedge^mA|\ge\min\{p,m|A|-m^2+1\}$ with $p$ the
characteristic of $F$ ($\infty$ in characteristic zero), and the remark
after its proof, which states the case $m=2$ for $A\subseteq Z_p$,
$|A\wedge A|\ge\min\{p,2|A|-3\}$, as the result "conjectured by Erdös and
Heilbronn [4]", is the problem's displayed inequality
$|A\hat{+}A|\ge\min(2|A|-3,p)$: the paper's $A\wedge A$, the sums of the
$2$-subsets of $A$, is the problem's restricted sumset. The problem page
had recorded the theorem second-hand through the attributions of Alon,
Nathanson and Ruzsa
([[additive_combinatorics/alon_1995_adding_distinct_congruence_classes_modulo_prime/theorem_2|Theorem 2]]
of their 1995 paper and
[[additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes/theorem_3_3|Theorem 3.3]]
of their 1996 paper) and the site. The paper's own proof is by linear
algebra and the representation theory of the symmetric group, not the
polynomial method of the reproofs: the diagonal operator with spectrum $A$
has a derivative on the $m$th Grassmann space whose spectrum is
$\wedge^mA$, and Corollary 3.3 bounds the degree of that derivative's
minimal polynomial from below through the cyclic-subspace bound of Theorem
3.2 and the hook-length identity of Corollary 2.3. Example 4.1 (p. 144),
the image of $\{1,\ldots,a\}$ in $Z_p$, shows the bound is best possible
with an arithmetic progression, as does the problem page's sharpness
example. The $m$-fold statement, read as sums
of exactly $m$ distinct elements, also covers the general conjecture (73)
of Erdős's 1965 lectures that the problem page records, since every sum of
exactly $r$ distinct elements is a sum of at most $r$ (a filing
observation; the paper does not state that conjecture).

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/integer_sequences/E0540/_index|#540]]:
[[additive_combinatorics/dias_da_silva_hamidoune_1994_cyclic_spaces_grassmann_derivatives_additive_theory/corollary_4_3|Corollary 4.3]]

page does not cite this paper.
