## Statement

$F$ is a field and $p$ is its characteristic when that is nonzero and
$\infty$ otherwise (p. 140). For $A\subseteq F$ and a positive integer $m$,
$\wedge^mA$ is the set of sums of the $m$-subsets of $A$ (p. 140), the sums
of $m$ distinct elements of $A$.

**Theorem 4.1** (printed p. 144). "Let $A$ be a finite subset of a field $F$
and $m$ be a positive integer. Then

$$
|\wedge^mA|\ge\min\{p,\,m|A|-m^2+1\}.
$$"

When $|A|<m$ the set $\wedge^mA$ is empty and the right side is at most
$1-m\le0$, so the content is the case $|A|\ge m$. The remark after the
proof states the case $m=2$ for $A\subseteq Z_p$: $|A\wedge A|\ge\min\{p,2|A|-3\}$,
the result "conjectured by Erdös and Heilbronn [4]", which the remark calls
the analogue of the Cauchy--Davenport theorem when the addition of an
element to itself is excluded. Example 4.1 (p. 144) shows the bound is best
possible for $Z_p$: for $S=\{1,\ldots,a\}$ the sums of the $m$-subsets are
exactly the integers from $m(m+1)/2$ to $ma-m(m-1)/2$, and the image $A$ of
$S$ in $Z_p$ has $|\wedge^mA|=m(|A|-m)+1$ when $m\le|A|$ and
$m(|A|-m)+1\le p$ (conditions the print leaves implicit).

## Proof pointer

Page 144. Let $n=|A|$ and let $f$ be the diagonal linear operator on $F^n$
whose spectrum $\sigma(f)$ is $A$. The derivative $Df$ of $f$ on the $m$th
Grassmann space $\wedge^mF^n$ is diagonal in the standard basis with
spectrum $\sigma(Df)=\wedge^m\sigma(f)=\wedge^mA$ (p. 144, quoted as well
known from [2, 6]: the eigenvalue on $e^{\alpha(0)}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{\alpha(m-1)}$
is the sum of the $m$ eigenvalues of $f$ with those indices). For a
diagonal operator the degree of the minimal polynomial is the number of
distinct eigenvalues, so $|\wedge^mA|=\deg P_{Df}$, and Corollary 3.3 gives
$\deg P_{Df}\ge\min\{p,(\deg P_f-m)m+1\}$ with $\deg P_f=|A|$.

Corollary 3.3 (p. 144) follows from Theorem 3.2 (p. 143), since the largest
dimension of an $f$-cyclic subspace equals $\deg P_f$ [8, Theorem 6,
p. 397]: Theorem 3.2 says that for any linear operator $f$ on $V$ and any
$v\in V$, the cyclic subspace of $Df$ generated by
$v\wedge f(v)\wedge\cdots\wedge f^{m-1}(v)$ has dimension at least
$\min\{p,(\dim\mathcal C_f(v)-m)m+1\}$, where $\mathcal C_f(v)$ is the
cyclic subspace of $f$ generated by $v$. Its proof (pp. 143--144, read for
structure) expands $(Df)^k(v\wedge f(v)\wedge\cdots\wedge f^{m-1}(v))$ by
Theorem 3.1 as a sum over the partitions $\lambda$ of $k$ into at most $m$
parts of $\bigl(k!/\prod_{ij}h^\lambda_{ij}\bigr)\,f^{\lambda_1}(v)\wedge f^{\lambda_2+1}(v)\wedge\cdots\wedge f^{\lambda_m+m-1}(v)$,
with $h^\lambda_{ij}$ the hook lengths of $\lambda$, and shows that for
$k\le\min\{p-1,m(n-m)\}$, $n=\dim\mathcal C_f(v)$, the leading term in the
degree-then-lexicographic order has a coefficient that is nonzero modulo
$p$ (it divides $k!$ and $k<p$) and is not canceled, so these powers are
linearly independent. Theorem 3.1 is proved by induction on $k$ from
Corollary 2.3 (p. 142), the identity
$(k+1)/\prod_{ij}h^\lambda_{ij}=\sum_{\mu\to\lambda}1/\prod_{ij}h^\mu_{ij}$
over the partitions $\mu$ obtained from $\lambda$ by removing one box,
which the paper deduces from the hook-length formula and the branching
rule for the irreducible characters of the symmetric group ([7], Theorems
2.3.21 and 2.4.3).

A filing observation, not a review verdict: Corollary 3.3 is printed
without the $\min$ with $p$ that Theorem 3.2 carries and that the proof of
Theorem 4.1 uses when it cites the corollary; it is read here with the
$\min$, as the digest records.

## Bears on

  with $F=Z_p$, stated in the remark after the proof, is the problem's
  inequality $|A\hat{+}A|\ge\min(2|A|-3,p)$, the paper's $A\wedge A$ being
  the problem's restricted sumset. The general case is the $m$-fold
  theorem the problem page had recorded second-hand through
  [[additive_combinatorics/alon_1996_polynomial_method_restricted_sums_congruence_classes/theorem_3_3|Theorem 3.3]]
  of Alon, Nathanson and Ruzsa 1996, whose polynomial-method proof is
  independent of this one. Read as sums of exactly $r$ distinct elements,
  it also covers the general conjecture (73) of Erdős's 1965 lectures,
  which asks for $\min(p,rk-r^2+1)$ distinct sums of at most $r$ distinct
  residues out of $k$, since every sum of exactly $r$ distinct elements is
  a sum of at most $r$; the paper does not state that conjecture.
