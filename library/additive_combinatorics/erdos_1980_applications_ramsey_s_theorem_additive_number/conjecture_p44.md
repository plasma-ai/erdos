## Statement

**Conjecture** (p. 44). "For every $k$ there is a $B_2^{(k)}$ sequence $A$
so that if $A=\bigcup_{r=1}^T A_r$ then at least one of the $A_r$'s is a
$B_2^{(k)}$ sequence."

Here a $B_2^{(k)}$ sequence is one in which every integer has at most $k$
representations as a sum of two or fewer terms and some integer has exactly
$k$ (p. 43). The paper proves the cases of
[[additive_combinatorics/erdos_1980_applications_ramsey_s_theorem_additive_number/theorem_1_prime|Theorem 1']]
and states, also on p. 44, the more general conjecture that for every $k$
and $r$ there is a sequence with property $B_r^{(k)}$ every decomposition of
which into finitely many subsequences has a part with property $B_r^{(k)}$,
which it says the same methods prove for every $r$ and infinitely many $k$.

The note added in proof (p. 46) records that the conjecture "has recently
been proved for every $k$ by J. Nešetřil and V. Rödl."

**Source.** P. Erdős, *Some applications of Ramsey's theorem to additive
number theory*, European J. Combin. 1 (1980), no. 1, 43--46,
doi:10.1016/S0195-6698(80)80020-5; the conjecture on p. 44, the note added
in proof on p. 46. The edition is recorded on the
[[additive_combinatorics/erdos_1980_applications_ramsey_s_theorem_additive_number/_index|source card]].
