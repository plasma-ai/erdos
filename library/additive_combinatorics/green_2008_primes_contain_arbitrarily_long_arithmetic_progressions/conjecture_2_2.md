## Statement

**Conjecture 2.2** (Erdős conjecture on arithmetic progressions; p. 3,
quoted). "Suppose that $A=\{a_1<a_2<\dots\}$ is an infinite sequence of
integers such that $\sum1/a_i=\infty$. Then $A$ contains arbitrarily long
arithmetic progressions."

The paper adds that the conjecture would imply
[[additive_combinatorics/green_2008_primes_contain_arbitrarily_long_arithmetic_progressions/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]],
calls it very open, and relates it to the hypothetical bound
$N_0(\delta,k)\le2^{c_k\delta^{-1}}$ (2.1) in the finitary Szemerédi theorem
(Proposition 2.1, p. 3), a bound that would also give Theorem 1.1 at once.
It states that it makes no progress on these questions (p. 3).

## Scope

The paper records the conjecture and proves nothing about it; its argument
is a transference principle that does not pass through quantitative bounds
in Szemerédi's theorem (pp. 3--4).

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the statement and the surrounding remarks
were read clause by clause on p. 3 of the print.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/additive_combinatorics/E0003/_index|#3]]:
the conjecture is the problem's question asserted in the affirmative, stated
for an infinite sequence of integers where the problem takes
$A\subseteq\mathbb N$. The paper proves only the case $A$ the primes, as
Theorem 1.1; that the primes have divergent reciprocal sum is the classical
fact the problem page's claim relies on, not a statement of the paper.
