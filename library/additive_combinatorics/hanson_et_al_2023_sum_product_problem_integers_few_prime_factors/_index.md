## Main result for integers with few prime factors

Write $\omega(a)$ for the number of distinct prime divisors of $a$. **Theorem
1.1 (p. 429)** states that if $0<\varepsilon<1/6$, $A$ is a sufficiently
large finite set of integers, and

$$
\omega(a)\leq k\quad(a\in A),
\qquad
k\leq(\log|A|)^{1-6\varepsilon},
$$

then some $\widetilde A\subseteq A$ satisfies

$$
|\widetilde A|\geq\frac{|A|}{2^{k+3}k!}
$$

and

$$
|AA|+\frac{|\widetilde A|^4}
{E_+(\widetilde A,\widetilde A)}
\geq
|A|^{5/3}
\exp\!\left(- (\log|A|)^{1-\varepsilon}\right).
$$

The remark immediately after the theorem gives the useful dichotomy: one may
instead state that either $|AA|\geq|A|^{5/3}$ or

$$
E_+(\widetilde A,\widetilde A)
\leq |\widetilde A|^{7/3}
\exp\!\left((\log|\widetilde A|)^{1-\varepsilon}\right).
$$

By $|\widetilde A+\widetilde A|E_+(\widetilde A,\widetilde A)
\geq|\widetilde A|^4$, **Corollary 1.2 (p. 429)** concludes

$$
\max\{|A+A|,|AA|\}\gg_{\varepsilon,k}|A|^{5/3-o(1)}.
$$

For fixed $k$ this is the clean restricted-class consequence. The theorem is
quantitative enough to permit $k$ to grow as a power of $\log|A|$; after
renaming the loss parameter, the abstract summarizes the range as
$k\leq(\log|A|)^{1-\eta}$ for every fixed $\eta>0$.

## Why few prime factors yield structure

The elementary starting point is **Lemma 1.3 (p. 430)**. If every $a\in A$
has at most $k$ prime divisors and every $b\in B$ has at most $\ell$, then a
fixed $q\in AB$ has at most $2^{k+\ell}$ coprime representations $q=ab$.
Indeed, coprimality assigns every prime divisor of $q$ wholly to one factor.
This uniform multiplicity bound makes a prime-factor decomposition usable
combinatorially.

Put $K=|AA|/|A|$. Under the nontrivial small-product hypothesis
$K<2^{-2k-1}|A|$, **Theorem 4.2 (p. 436)** passes to a subset of the same
minimum size as above and finds primes $P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_r\}$ with $r\leq k$
such that

$$
\widetilde A
=\bigsqcup_{\mathbf v\in V}\mathbf p^{\mathbf v}B_{\mathbf v}
=\bigsqcup_{b\in B}b\Gamma_b,
\qquad
|B|\leq 8^{k+1}k!K.
$$

Here each $B_{\mathbf v}$ is coprime to $p_1\cdots p_r$ and each
$\Gamma_b$ lies in the rank-$r$ group generated by $P$. The iteration in
Lemma 4.1 (p. 436) repeatedly selects a prime common to many elements until at
least half of the remaining pairs have no common prime outside $P$. Lemma 1.3
then controls product multiplicities, and the popularity pruning on p. 437
retains only sufficiently large fibres. Thus a set with few prime divisors and
small product growth is forced into a generalized Balog--Wooley form: a
low-rank multiplicative component over a small collection of base points.

## Energy mechanism and quantitative closure

Two further inputs turn that structure into an additive-energy estimate.

- **Theorem 5.1 (p. 438)** proves a Littlewood--Paley square-function bound
  along the $p_1$-adic through $p_r$-adic scales, with norm loss $C_q^r$.
  Its $q=4$ consequence, **Corollary 5.2 (pp. 438--439)**, decouples
  $E_+(\widetilde A,\widetilde A)$ into energies between the fibres
  $\mathbf p^{\mathbf v}B_{\mathbf v}$. Burkholder's martingale transform
  theorem supplies the rank-one estimate; Lemma 5.5 (pp. 440--441) iterates it
  through the $r$ prime scales.

- **Lemma 6.3 (p. 443)**, taken from Lemma 2.1 of Roche-Newton and Zhelezov
  (2015) and proved from the Amoroso--Viada quantitative $S$-unit estimate in
  Theorem 6.2 (p. 443), says that for $0<\varepsilon<1/6$, sufficiently large
  $B\subset\mathbb Q$ and a group $\Gamma$ generated by
  $r\leq(\log|B|)^{1-6\varepsilon}$ rational primes,

  $$
  |\{(b_1,b_2)\in B^2:b_1-b_2\in\Gamma\}|
  \leq |B|\exp\!\left((\log|B|)^{1-\varepsilon}\right).
  $$

In the proof of Theorem 1.1, equations **(3)--(4) (p. 444)** record the subset
and base-set bounds. The square-function reduction converts an energy solution
to the base-difference equation

$$
b_1-b_2=\gamma^{-1}\gamma'(b_3-b_4),
\tag{5}
$$

where $\gamma,\gamma'$ lie in the low-rank group. Applying Lemma 6.3 gives on
**p. 444**

$$
E_+(\widetilde A,\widetilde A)
\ll C^k|\widetilde A|^2
+K^2|\widetilde A|
\exp\!\left((\log|\widetilde A|)^{1-\varepsilon}\right).
$$

After harmless cases are removed, the remaining optimization is the last
display on p. 445:

$$
K|A|+\frac{|A|^3}{K^2}\gg |A|^{5/3},
$$

whose two terms balance at $K\asymp|A|^{2/3}$.

## Sharpness of this route and limits for E0052

The Balog--Wooley construction on **pp. 428--429** takes
$A=\Gamma B$, where $\Gamma$ is a geometric progression and $B$ an arithmetic
progression. Choosing the generator of $\Gamma$ and the members of $B$ to be
primes keeps $\omega(a)\leq2$. With $|\Gamma|^2=|B|$, it can have

$$
|AA|\ll|A|^{5/3-o(1)},
\qquad
E_+(A,A)\gg|A|^{7/3}.
$$

Consequently the standard energy inequality
$|A+A|E_+(A,A)\geq|A|^4$ yields only the exponent $5/3$. Theorem 1.1 is
therefore best possible up to subpower losses for its
product-size/additive-energy formulation, already when every element has at
most two distinct prime factors.

This does **not** show that such sets have small sumsets, and the paper notes
on p. 429 that Balog--Wooley sets do not contradict the full
Erdős--Szemerédi conjecture. The loss comes from using ordinary additive energy
as a predictor for $|A+A|$. E0052 asks for the nearly quadratic bound
$\max\{|A+A|,|AA|\}\geq|A|^{2-o(1)}$ for arbitrary finite integer sets; this
paper proves only $5/3-o(1)$ for the restricted few-prime-factor class. It
neither reaches exponent $2$ in that class nor treats integers with unrestricted
prime support. The discussion on p. 431 identifies an asymmetric refinement as
a possible way around the energy barrier but supplies no such result.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/additive_combinatorics/E0052/_index|#52]]: proves the
$5/3-o(1)$ sum-product lower bound when every integer has few distinct prime
factors, and isolates both the low-rank arithmetic mechanism and the ordinary
energy barrier that prevent it from resolving the full integer conjecture.
