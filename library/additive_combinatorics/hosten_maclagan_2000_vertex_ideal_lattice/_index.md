# The vertex ideal of a lattice

[[additive_combinatorics/_index|..]]

[[additive_combinatorics/hosten_maclagan_2000_vertex_ideal_lattice/corollary_4_7|corollary_4_7]]: States that the radicals of the product ideal and the vertex ideal of a
lattice coincide and that the top-dimensional part of the product ideal
is contained in that of the vertex ideal.

[[additive_combinatorics/hosten_maclagan_2000_vertex_ideal_lattice/corollary_4_8|corollary_4_8]]: States that for every lattice of dimension two the top-dimensional parts
of the product ideal and the vertex ideal are equal, and records that this
fails in dimension three.

[[additive_combinatorics/hosten_maclagan_2000_vertex_ideal_lattice/definition_4_1|definition_4_1]]: Defines the product ideal of a lattice as the monomial ideal generated by
the products x^u x^v over the Graver basis elements u - v, and records
that it is contained in the vertex ideal, sometimes strictly.

[[additive_combinatorics/hosten_maclagan_2000_vertex_ideal_lattice/proposition_2_1|proposition_2_1]]: States that lowering a positive coordinate of a vertex of a lattice fiber
gives a vertex of its own fiber, so the non-vertices of all fibers are the
exponents of a monomial ideal, the vertex ideal of the lattice.

[[additive_combinatorics/hosten_maclagan_2000_vertex_ideal_lattice/proposition_4_2|proposition_4_2]]: States that if L = ker(A) intersected with Z^n for a unimodular matrix A,
the product ideal equals the vertex ideal and both equal the
Stanley–Reisner ideal of the matroid complex of the lattice.

[[additive_combinatorics/hosten_maclagan_2000_vertex_ideal_lattice/proposition_4_3|proposition_4_3]]: States that the product ideal equals the vertex ideal for every
two-dimensional lattice in Z^2, and records the paper's examples showing
that this fails for a two-dimensional lattice in Z^3 and in dimension three.

[[additive_combinatorics/hosten_maclagan_2000_vertex_ideal_lattice/theorem_2_10|theorem_2_10]]: States that the radical of the vertex ideal of a rank m lattice is the
intersection of the primes generated by the variables indexed by the
linearly independent m-subsets of rows of a lattice-basis matrix, that is
the Stanley–Reisner ideal of a matroid complex.

[[additive_combinatorics/hosten_maclagan_2000_vertex_ideal_lattice/theorem_2_3|theorem_2_3]]: States that the vertex ideal of a lattice equals the intersection of the
initial ideals of its lattice ideal over all generic weight vectors, which
gives a first, finite algorithm for computing it.

[[additive_combinatorics/hosten_maclagan_2000_vertex_ideal_lattice/theorem_2_8|theorem_2_8]]: States that the vertex ideal of a lattice is generated by the monomials
lcm of x^alpha_j over the supports of the positive circuits of its ordered
Graver basis, so it can be computed from the Graver basis alone.

[[additive_combinatorics/hosten_maclagan_2000_vertex_ideal_lattice/theorem_3_11|theorem_3_11]]: States that for a codimension two lattice ideal with L = ker(A) meeting
the nonnegative orthant only at 0, an embedded prime P_tau of the vertex
ideal has cone{a_i : i in tau} not a face of the cone of all columns, so
the irrelevant maximal ideal is not associated.

[[additive_combinatorics/hosten_maclagan_2000_vertex_ideal_lattice/theorem_3_14|theorem_3_14]]: States that some toric ideal of codimension three has a Gröbner cone with
five facets, refuting a conjecture of Sturmfels and Thomas that such cones
have at most four facets; the witness is A = [15, 247, 248, 345].

[[additive_combinatorics/hosten_maclagan_2000_vertex_ideal_lattice/theorem_3_8|theorem_3_8]]: States that the vertex ideal is the intersection of the irreducible ideals
generated by the powers x_i^(u_i+1), i outside tau, over all critical
polyhedra Q_u^tau-bar, after characterizing its standard monomials and
standard pairs through vertices at the origin of lattice-point hulls.

## Overview

**Question and construction.** Hoşten and Maclagan associate to an arbitrary
rank-$m$ sublattice $\mathcal L\subseteq\mathbf Z^n$ a monomial ideal encoding
all vertices of all lattice fibers. For $u\in\mathbf N^n$, Definition 1.1 sets

$$
P_u=\operatorname{conv}\{v\in\mathbf N^n:u-v\in\mathcal L\}.
$$

Proposition 2.1 proves that the union of the sets $\operatorname{Vert}(P_u)$ is
an order ideal in $\mathbf N^n$; consequently there is a monomial ideal
$V_{\mathcal L}\subseteq k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ such that $x^v\notin V_{\mathcal L}$
exactly when $v$ is a vertex of its fiber. The rational-polyhedral finiteness of
the vertex sets is cited background from Theorem 16.1 of reference [9], not a
result proved here.

**Descriptions and computation of the vertex ideal.** After defining the lattice
ideal

$$
I_{\mathcal L}=\langle x^u-x^v:u-v\in\mathcal L\rangle
$$

in Definition 2.2, Theorem 2.3 identifies

$$
V_{\mathcal L}=\bigcap_{\omega\ \mathrm{generic}}\operatorname{in}_{\omega}(I_{\mathcal L}).
$$

The proof uses the fact that standard monomials of a generic initial ideal are
the unique minimizers of the corresponding weight functional on their fibers.
This gives a finite but potentially very inefficient algorithm; Example 2.4
exhibits a family with at least $n!$ initial ideals, while Remark 2.13 obtains
the required intersection from only $n2^{n-1}$ of them.

Definition 2.5 introduces the Graver basis $\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathcal L}$ as
the union of the Hilbert bases in all orthants. Lemma 2.6 converts a positive
dependence $\sum_i c_i(\alpha_i-\beta_i)=0$ among Graver elements into a
monomial $\operatorname{lcm}_i(x^{\alpha_i})\in V_{\mathcal L}$. Corollary 2.7
proves that every minimal generator arises this way. Theorem 2.8 sharpens this
to the generating formula

$$
V_{\mathcal L}=\left\langle\operatorname{lcm}_{j\in\tau}x^{\alpha_j}:\tau\text{ supports a positive circuit of }\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathcal L}\right\rangle.
$$

Thus the main constructive method is to compute the Graver basis and its
positive circuits, rather than the entire Gröbner fan.

**Radical, matroid, and enumeration.** For a lattice-basis matrix
$B\in\mathbf Z^{n\times m}$ with rows $b_i$, Proposition 2.9 records the cited
Gröbner-theoretic fact that the radical of a generic initial ideal is the
Stanley–Reisner ideal of the corresponding simplicial complex $\Delta_\omega$.
Theorem 2.10 then proves

$$
\sqrt{V_{\mathcal L}}=\bigcap_{\substack{\sigma\subseteq[n],\ |\sigma|=m\\\{b_i:i\in\sigma\}\text{ linearly independent}}}
\langle x_i:i\in\sigma\rangle.
$$

Corollary 2.12 reformulates this as the Stanley–Reisner ideal of the matroid
complex $\Delta(\mathcal M(\mathcal L))$. Proposition 2.14 identifies the
multigraded Hilbert series of $S/V_{\mathcal L}$ with the generating series for
vertices of all fibers. For $\mathcal L=\ker(A)\cap\mathbf Z^n$ with
$A=[a_1,\ldots,a_n]$ a single row of natural numbers, Proposition 2.15 deduces
that the number of vertices in the fiber of degree $b$ is eventually periodic,
with period dividing $\operatorname{lcm}(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$.

**Associated primes and standard pairs.** Lemma 3.1 shows that every associated
prime of $V_{\mathcal L}$ occurs among the associated primes of some generic
initial ideal, and that the minimal primes are exactly the union of the minimal
primes of those initial ideals; Example 3.2 shows strictness for associated
primes in general. Proposition 3.3 gives codimension at most $\min(n,2^m-1)$.
Proposition 3.5 characterizes standard pairs directly through persistence of the
vertex property. With

$$
Q_u=\{z\in\mathbf R^m:Bz\leq u\},\qquad R_u=\operatorname{conv}(Q_u\cap\mathbf Z^m),
$$

Theorem 3.6 says that $x^u$ is standard precisely when the origin is a vertex of
$R_u$, and characterizes a standard pair $(x^u,\tau)$ by retaining this property
after the inequalities indexed by $\tau$ are removed, but losing it when any
further inequality is removed. Definition 3.7 calls $Q_u$ critical when the
origin is a vertex of $R_u$ but of no $R_{u+e_i}$ (the print writes the range
as $i=1,\ldots,k$), and Theorem 3.8 indexes the irreducible primary
decomposition, called irredundant in the text before Definition 3.7, by the
critical polyhedra $Q_u^{\bar\tau}$:

$$
V_{\mathcal L}=\bigcap_{Q_u^{\bar\tau}\ \mathrm{critical}}
\langle x_i^{u_i+1}:i\in\bar\tau\rangle.
$$

For a codimension two lattice ideal $I_{\mathcal L}$ with
$\mathcal L=\ker(A)\cap\mathbf Z^n$ and $\mathcal L\cap\mathbf N^n=\{0\}$,
the planar polygon argument of Lemma 3.9 yields Theorem 3.11: if
$\mathcal P_\tau=\langle x_i:i\notin\tau\rangle$ is an embedded prime of
$V_{\mathcal L}$, then the cone generated by the columns $a_i$, $i\in\tau$, of
$A$ is not a face of the cone generated by all the columns; so
$\langle x_1,\ldots,x_n\rangle$ is not an associated prime of $V_{\mathcal L}$.
Remark 3.12 and Example 3.13 show that this fails in higher codimension. The
explicit lattice in Example 3.13 is then used in Theorem 3.14 to prove the
existence of a codimension-three toric ideal having a Gröbner cone with five
facets, refuting a conjecture of Sturmfels and Thomas that every such cone has
at most four (numbered 6.1 on p. 2 and 6.2 on p. 14). Example 3.15 is a
separate computer-produced example with six facets; it is presented as
computation, not as a general theorem.

**The product ideal.** Definition 4.1 introduces the more readily computed ideal

$$
P_{\mathcal L}=\langle x^ux^v:u-v\in\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathcal L}\rangle,
$$

and immediately gives $P_{\mathcal L}\subseteq V_{\mathcal L}$; the example
following the definition shows that containment can be strict. Proposition 4.2
proves equality, with both ideals equal to the relevant matroid ideal, when
$\mathcal L=\ker(A)\cap\mathbf Z^n$ and $A$ is unimodular. Proposition 4.3
proves equality for a rank-two lattice in the ambient lattice $\mathbf Z^2$,
while Example 4.4 shows failure in rank three. Lemma 4.5 and Proposition 4.6
establish compatibility of Graver bases, vertex ideals, and product ideals with
coordinate projection/localization when lattice rank is preserved. Corollary 4.7
proves $\sqrt{P_{\mathcal L}}=\sqrt{V_{\mathcal L}}$ and
$\operatorname{Top}(P_{\mathcal L})\subseteq\operatorname{Top}(V_{\mathcal L})$;
Corollary 4.8 upgrades the latter to equality for rank-two lattices. These
statements concern radicals or top-dimensional components and do not assert
$P_{\mathcal L}=V_{\mathcal L}$ in general.

## Relation to E963

Let $X=\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\subset\mathbf R$ be the set in
[[../wiki/problems/number_theory/E0963/_index|E963]], with a fixed ordering, and put

$$
\mathcal L_X=\left\{z\in\mathbf Z^n:\sum_{i=1}^n z_i a_i=0\right\}.
$$

This is a saturated sublattice of $\mathbf Z^n$. A subset indexed by
$S\subseteq[n]$ is dissociated exactly when

$$
\mathcal L_X\cap\{z\in\{-1,0,1\}^n:\operatorname{supp}(z)\subseteq S\}=\{0\};
$$

indeed, a nonzero such vector is precisely an equality between two distinct
subset sums supported on $S$.

The radical results discard precisely the bounded-coefficient information
central to E963. Corollary 4.7 and Theorem 2.10 identify
$\sqrt{P_{\mathcal L_X}}=\sqrt{V_{\mathcal L_X}}$ with a matroid Stanley–Reisner
ideal. Its faces avoid arbitrary integer relations, whereas dissociation only
forbids relations with coefficients in $\{-1,0,1\}$. Thus the matroid complex
yields only subsets of $X$ that are linearly independent over $\mathbf Q$; for
a set of integers these have at most one element, so it cannot by itself imply
a logarithmic bound. Likewise, Proposition 2.14 counts all fiber vertices
but supplies no estimate for squarefree vertices of a prescribed degree. The
paper's ideals therefore give computational machinery for finite examples, but
the paper proves neither $f(n)\geq\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor$ nor a construction
violating it.
