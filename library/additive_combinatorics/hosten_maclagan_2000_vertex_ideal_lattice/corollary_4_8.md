## Statement

**Corollary 4.8** (p. 18). If $\dim(\mathcal L)=2$, then
$\operatorname{Top}(P_{\mathcal L})=\operatorname{Top}(V_{\mathcal L})$,
where $P_{\mathcal L}$ is the
[[additive_combinatorics/hosten_maclagan_2000_vertex_ideal_lattice/definition_4_1|product ideal]]
and $\operatorname{Top}$ the intersection of the top-dimensional primary
components (p. 17).

The paper notes (p. 18) that the corollary fails when
$\dim(\mathcal L)\ge3$: for the three-dimensional lattice in $\mathbf Z^3$ of
Example 4.4 (p. 17), $\operatorname{Top}(V_{\mathcal L})=V_{\mathcal L}$ and
$\operatorname{Top}(P_{\mathcal L})=P_{\mathcal L}$, but
$P_{\mathcal L}\ne V_{\mathcal L}$.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the statement was read clause by clause on
p. 18, with the proof.

## Proof pointer

Page 18. At a minimal prime $\mathcal P_\sigma$ the localized lattice
$\mathcal L_\sigma$ is a two-dimensional lattice in $\mathbf Z^2$, so
[[additive_combinatorics/hosten_maclagan_2000_vertex_ideal_lattice/proposition_4_3|Proposition 4.3]]
gives $P_{\mathcal L_\sigma}=V_{\mathcal L_\sigma}$; Proposition 4.6 and
[[additive_combinatorics/hosten_maclagan_2000_vertex_ideal_lattice/corollary_4_7|Corollary 4.7]]
finish the argument.
