## Statement

**Definition 4.1** (p. 15). For a lattice $\mathcal L\subseteq\mathbf Z^n$
with Graver basis $\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathcal L}$ (Definition 2.5, p. 4, on
the
[[additive_combinatorics/hosten_maclagan_2000_vertex_ideal_lattice/theorem_2_8|Theorem 2.8 page]]),
the *product ideal* is the monomial ideal

$$
P_{\mathcal L}=\left\langle x^ux^v:u-v\in\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathcal L}\right\rangle .
$$

Here $u,v\in\mathbf N^n$. Every such $u,v$ dominate the positive and negative
parts $g^+,g^-$ of $g=u-v$, so the same ideal is generated by the monomials
$x^{g^++g^-}$, $g\in\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathcal L}$.

The paper records (p. 15) that $P_{\mathcal L}\subseteq V_{\mathcal L}$,
because every generic initial ideal $\operatorname{in}_\omega(I_{\mathcal L})$
contains $x^u$ or $x^v$ whenever $u-v\in\mathcal L$, and that the containment
can be strict: for $\mathcal L=\ker(A)\cap\mathbf Z^3$ with $A=[3\ 4\ 5]$,
$P_{\mathcal L}=\langle ab^2c,a^2bc^2,a^3bc,a^4b^3,a^5c^3,b^5c^4\rangle$ is
strictly contained in
$V_{\mathcal L}=\langle ab^2c,a^2bc,a^4b^3,a^5c^3,b^5c^4\rangle$.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the definition, the containment and its
one-line justification, and the example were read on p. 15; the example's
ideals were not recomputed.

  only. The paper does not mention dissociated sets, subset sums or the
  problem. The source card's section on E963 derives from this definition
  that, for the lattice $\mathcal L_X$ of integer relations among the
  elements of a finite set $X\subset\mathbf R$, a subset $S$ is dissociated
  if and only if the squarefree monomial $x_S$ is not in
  $P_{\mathcal L_X}$, so the largest dissociated subset has the largest
  degree of a squarefree standard monomial of $P_{\mathcal L_X}$. That is a
  reformulation; the paper gives no bound on that degree, and neither the
  problem's lower bound nor a counterexample follows from it.
