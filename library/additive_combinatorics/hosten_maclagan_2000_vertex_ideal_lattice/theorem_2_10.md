## Setting

Let $\mathcal L\subseteq\mathbf Z^n$ be a lattice of dimension $m$ with
vertex ideal $V_{\mathcal L}$, and let $B\in\mathbf Z^{n\times m}$ be a matrix
whose columns form a basis of $\mathcal L$, with rows $b_1,\ldots,b_n$ (p. 6).

- **Matroid** (p. 7): the complements of the bases of the rows of $B$ (the
  linearly independent subsets of size $m$) are the maximal independent sets
  of a matroid $\mathcal M(\mathcal L)$; its matroid complex
  $\Delta(\mathcal M(\mathcal L))$ has the independent sets as simplices
  (Definition 2.11). When $\mathcal L=\ker(A)\cap\mathbf Z^n$,
  $\mathcal M(\mathcal L)$ is the matroid of linearly independent subsets of
  the columns of $A$.

## Statement

**Theorem 2.10** (p. 6). The radical of $V_{\mathcal L}$ is

$$
\sqrt{V_{\mathcal L}}=\bigcap_{\sigma}\langle x_i:i\in\sigma\rangle,
$$

the intersection taken over all $\sigma\subseteq[n]$ of size $m$ for which
$\{b_i:i\in\sigma\}$ is linearly independent.

**Corollary 2.12** (p. 7). The radical of $V_{\mathcal L}$ is the
Stanley–Reisner ideal of $\Delta(\mathcal M(\mathcal L))$.

Proposition 2.9 (p. 6), cited from Hoşten and Thomas, is the ingredient: for
generic $\omega$, the radical of $\operatorname{in}_\omega(I_{\mathcal L})$ is
the Stanley–Reisner ideal of a pure $(n-m)$-dimensional simplicial complex
$\Delta_\omega$, the regular triangulation of $A$ with respect to $\omega$
when $\mathcal L=\ker(A)\cap\mathbf Z^n$.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the statements and the definitions they use
were read clause by clause on pp. 6--7, with the proofs.

## Proof pointer

Pages 6--7. Take radicals in
[[additive_combinatorics/hosten_maclagan_2000_vertex_ideal_lattice/theorem_2_3|Theorem 2.3]],
apply Proposition 2.9 to each generic initial ideal, and note that for every
full-dimensional simplicial cone on $m$ of the $b_i$, the complement of the
indices of its generators is involved in some $\Delta_\omega$.

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/number_theory/E0963/_index|Problem 963]]: background
  only. The paper does not mention dissociated sets or the problem. As the
  source card's section on E963 explains, for the lattice of integer
  relations among the elements of $X$ the faces of this matroid complex are
  the subsets of $X$ linearly independent over $\mathbf Q$, which forbids
  every integer relation rather than only those with coefficients in
  $\{-1,0,1\}$; for a set of integers these faces have at most one element,
  so the theorem gives no bound for the problem.
