# A non-trivial bound for 3AP-intersecting families

Peter Keevash\*

## Abstract

A family $F$ of subsets of $[n]$ is *3AP-intersecting* if every two members have intersection containing a non-trivial three-term arithmetic progression. We prove that there is an absolute constant $c>0$ such that any such $F$ has size at most $(\frac{1}{2}-c)2^n$. This is the first non-trivial progress towards a conjecture of Simonovits and Sós that the maximum possible size is $2^{n-3}$. More generally, we show the same bound for $H$-intersecting families whenever $H$ is a $3$-graph on $[n]$ with bounded codegrees. A clique shows that this is sharp, in that the bounded codegree assumption cannot be removed.

## 1 Introduction

Given a hypergraph $H$ and a family $F$ of subsets of a common vertex set $V$, we say $F$ is $H$-intersecting if $A\cap B$ contains an edge of $H$ for every $A,B\in F$. We say that $F$ on $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ is 3AP-intersecting if it is $H$-intersecting with $H$ being the $3$-graph on $[n]$ whose edges are all non-trivial $3$-term arithmetic progressions (3APs). A conjecture of Simonovits and Sós (personal communication to the authors of [1]) states that any 3AP-intersecting family has size at most $2^{n-3}$. This would clearly be sharp, as shown by the family of all sets containing some fixed 3AP. They also made the analogous conjecture for triangle-intersecting families of graphs, which can be viewed as an $H$-intersecting problem in the auxiliary $3$-graph on $V=E(K_n)$ whose edges are all triples $\{ab,bc,ca\}$. This conjecture was proved using spectral methods by Ellis, Filmus and Friedgut [3]. However, it seems that their methods cannot work for the 3AP problem.

There is a rich field of related theorems and conjectures on intersection problems, see the survey [2]. A long-standing conjecture of Alon stated that there is an absolute constant $c>0$ such that for any graph $H$ that is not a star-forest, any $H$-intersecting family of graphs on $[n]$ contains at most $\frac{1}{2}-c$ proportion of all graphs. This was recently proved by Han and Wang [5], building on a new concentration of measure inequality due to Gillott [4]. In this note we show that a similar method gives the same type of bound for the Simonovits-Sós 3AP-intersection conjecture. More generally, the argument applies to any $3$-graph $H$ with bounded codegrees. We write $\Delta_2(H):=\max_{\{x,y\}\subseteq V(H)}|\{e\in E(H):\{x,y\}\subseteq e\}|$,

**Theorem 1.1.** For every $\Delta\in\mathbb{N}$ there is $c_\Delta>0$ such that for any $3$-graph $H$ with $\Delta_2(H)\leq\Delta$ and $H$-intersecting family $F$ of subsets of $V(H)$ we have $|F|\leq(\frac{1}{2}-c_\Delta)2^{|V(H)|}$.

**Corollary 1.2.** There is an absolute constant $c>0$ such that every 3AP-intersecting family on $[n]$ has size at most $(\frac{1}{2}-c)2^n$.

To see sharpness of Theorem 1.1 suppose that $H$ is obtained from a complete $3$-graph on $m$ vertices by adding some isolated vertices. Let $F$ consist of all subsets that intersect the clique in at least $(m+3)/2$ vertices. Then $F$ has density $1/2-o_m(1)$ as $m\to\infty$. If a symmetric example is desired one could let $H$ be a disjoint union of cliques.

## 2 Proof strategy

Let $F$ be an $H$-intersecting family on $V(H)$. We identify subsets of $V(H)$ with indicator vectors in $\mathbb{F}_{2}^{V(H)}$. We write $\mathbf{1}$ for the all-ones vector and let $\mathcal{I}(H)$ be the family of all independent sets in $H$. Then the sumsets $F+\mathcal{I}(H)$ and $F+\{\mathbf{1}\}=\{V(H)\setminus A:A\in F\}$ are disjoint, as if we had $A+I=B+\mathbf{1}$ for some $A,B$ in $F$ then $A\cap B\subseteq I$, which contradicts $F$ being $H$-intersecting and $I$ being independent. This observation reduces the proof to an isoperimetric statement in the Cayley graph on $\mathbb{F}_{2}^{V(H)}$ generated by $\mathcal{I}(H)$. It suffices to show that if $F$ has density $p$ close to $1/2$ then it expands to a set $F+\mathcal{I}(H)$ of density more than $1-p$. This is forced to intersect $F+\mathbf{1}$ simply by size considerations, so such $F$ cannot be $H$-intersecting.

The above viewpoint was well-known to experts, but did not lead to any progress until recently due to the lack of a suitable isoperimetric statement. The key new ingredient is the following concentration inequality of Gillott, which he applied to obtain several new bounds, including the version of Corollary 1.2 with 3AP replaced by 4AP. The following is a slight adjustment of [4, Theorem 3.1] that follows from the same proof. For convenient comparison we retain the nonstandard notation used there, writing $Y_{P}$ for the binomial random subset of $[m]$ where each $\mathbb{P}(i\in Y_{P})=p_{i}$ independently.

**Lemma 2.1 (Gillott).** *For any sufficiently small $K>0$ there is $\eta=\eta(K)>0$ such that if $F\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}$ with $|F|\geq(\frac{1}{2}-\eta)2^{m}$ then for at least $(\frac{1}{2}+\eta)2^{m}$ points $x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}$ there exists $P=(p_{i})\in[0,1]^{m}$ such that $\sum_{i}p_{i}^{2}\leq K$ and $\mathbb{P}(x+Y_{P}\in F)>K^{3/2}$.*

To deduce that a family $F$ of density $\frac{1}{2}-\eta$ for small enough $\eta>0$ cannot be 4AP-intersecting, one applies this lemma and size considerations to see that there is $x^{\prime}\in F$ such that $x=x^{\prime}+\mathbf{1}$ has $P$ as in the lemma. By Cauchy-Schwarz (see [4, Theorem 3.3]), the probability that $Y_{P}$ contains a 4AP is at most $K^{2}<K^{3/2}$, so there is nonzero probability that $x^{\prime\prime}=x+Y_{P}\in F$ and $Y_{P}$ contains no 4AP. But then $x=x^{\prime}+\mathbf{1}=x^{\prime\prime}+Y_{P}$ belongs to $F+\mathbf{1}$ and $F+\mathcal{I}(H)$, so $F$ cannot be $H$-intersecting.

This argument does not handle 3AP-intersecting families or Alon’s graph intersection conjecture. To resolve the latter, Han and Wang [5] employ an additional trick based on Plünnecke’s inequality, which allows one to replace $\mathcal{I}(H)$ by the sumset $\mathcal{I}(H)+\mathcal{I}(H)$; the following is a slight adaptation of their argument. For convenient notation we write $\mu$ for uniform measure.

**Lemma 2.2.** *Suppose that for some $0<\eta<1/4$, every $A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{V(H)}$ with $\mu(A)\geq\frac{1}{2}-\eta$ satisfies $\mu(A+\mathcal{I}(H)+\mathcal{I}(H))\geq\frac{1}{2}+\eta$. Then every $H$-intersecting family has density at most $\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\eta}{9}$.*

*Proof.* Let $F$ be a nonempty $H$-intersecting family of density $\alpha$. As noted above, we have $(F+\mathcal{I}(H))\cap(F+\mathbf{1})=\varnothing$. As $\varnothing\in\mathcal{I}(H)$ we have $\alpha\leq 1/2$ and $\frac{|F+\mathcal{I}(H)|}{|F|}\leq\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}$. By Plünnecke’s inequality, $F$ has a nonempty subset $X$ with $|X+\mathcal{I}(H)+\mathcal{I}(H)|\leq\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}\right)^{2}|X|$. By [5, Lemma 2.2], which can be applied as $\mathcal{I}(H)+\mathcal{I}(H)$ generates $\mathbb{F}_{2}^{V(H)}$, we deduce $|X+\mathcal{I}(H)+\mathcal{I}(H)|\geq(1+2\eta)|X|$. We deduce $\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\eta}{9}$. $\square$

Our final ingredient is an estimate for $\mathbb{P}(Y_{P}\notin\mathcal{I}(H)+\mathcal{I}(H))$, which plays the same role in our proof as the estimate of the probability that $Y_{P}$ contains a 4AP plays in Gillott’s proof. For fixed $\Delta$ our bound is $O_{\Delta}(K^{2})<K^{3/2}$ for small $K$.

**Lemma 2.3.** *Let $H$ be a 3-graph with $\Delta_{2}(H)\leq\Delta$. Write $\mathcal{I}(H)$ for the family of independent sets in $H$. Let $Y_{P}\subseteq V(H)$ retain the vertices independently with probabilities $(p_{v})$ and write $K=\sum_{v\in V(H)}p_{v}^{2}$. If $\Delta K<1$ then $\mathbb{P}(Y_{P}\notin\mathcal{I}(H)+\mathcal{I}(H))\leq\frac{(\Delta K)^{2}}{1-\Delta K}.$*

We prove this lemma in the next section. First we complete the deduction of Theorem 1.1, which is as above.

*Proof of Theorem 1.1.* Fix $\Delta$ and choose $0<K<1/\Delta$ such that $\frac{(\Delta K)^2}{1-\Delta K}<K^{3/2}$. Let $\eta$ be as in Lemma 2.1. Let $H$ be a $3$-graph with $\Delta_2(H)\leq\Delta$. Let $F\subseteq\mathbb{F}_2^{V(H)}$ with $\mu(F)\geq 1/2-\eta$. Let $A$ be the set of $x$ such that $P$ as in Lemma 2.1 exists. Then $\mu(A)\geq 1/2+\eta$. By Lemma 2.3 we have $\mathbb{P}(Y_P\notin\mathcal{I}(H)+\mathcal{I}(H))<K^{3/2}$. Thus the events $\{x+Y_P\in F\}$ and $\{Y_P\in\mathcal{I}(H)+\mathcal{I}(H)\}$ intersect, so $x\in F+\mathcal{I}(H)+\mathcal{I}(H)$. We deduce that $\mu(F+\mathcal{I}(H)+\mathcal{I}(H))\geq\mu(A)\geq 1/2+\eta$. The theorem now follows by Lemma 2.2. $\square$

## 3 Proof of the lemma

It remains to prove Lemma 2.3. Our first observation is that it suffices to bound the probability that $H[Y_P]$ contains a cycle in its incidence graph. Indeed, if $H[Y_P]$ is a forest then it is 2-colourable (by a greedy algorithm), so $Y_P\in\mathcal{I}(H)+\mathcal{I}(H)$.

To estimate this probability, we consider a shortest cycle in $H[Y_P]$ and write it as $x_1,e_1,x_2,e_2,\ldots,x_\ell,e_\ell,x_1$, where each $e_i=\{x_i,z_i,x_{i+1}\}$, reading indices modulo $\ell$. Minimality implies that the $x_i$ and $z_i$ are all distinct.

We bound the probability of seeing such a cycle by $\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\operatorname{tr} M^\ell$, where $M$ is the symmetric matrix with $M_{xx}=0$ and $M_{xy}=\sqrt{p_xp_y}\sum_{z:xyz\in E(H)}p_z$.

Let $\lambda_j$ be the eigenvalues of $M$. Then $\operatorname{tr} M^\ell=\sum_j\lambda_j^\ell\leq\left(\sum_j\lambda_j^2\right)^{\ell/2}$, where $\sum_j\lambda_j^2=\operatorname{tr} M^2=\sum_{x\ne y}p_xp_y\left(\sum_{z:xyz\in E(H)}p_z\right)^2\leq 2\Delta\sum_zp_z^2\sum_{xy:xyz\in E(H)}p_xp_y\leq\Delta^2K^2$ by the codegree bounds and Cauchy-Schwarz. The last inequality uses $p_xp_y\leq(p_x^2+p_y^2)/2$ and the codegree bound. The lemma follows.
