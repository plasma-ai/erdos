# Mapping Incidences

[[additive_combinatorics/_index|..]]

[[additive_combinatorics/vu_et_al_2007_mapping_incidences/theorem_1_1|theorem_1_1]]: For a finite subset S of a characteristic-zero integral domain and a finite
set L of nonzero elements of the ring Z[S], there is a sequence of primes of
positive relative density for each of which some ring homomorphism from Z[S]
to Z/pZ sends no element of L to zero.

[[additive_combinatorics/vu_et_al_2007_mapping_incidences/theorem_2_3|theorem_2_3]]: For at most N points and at most N lines in D x D, where D is any
characteristic-zero integral domain, the number of incidences is at most
c N^(3/2-delta) for positive absolute constants c and delta, those of the
finite-field bound the paper quotes as its Theorem 2.1.

[[additive_combinatorics/vu_et_al_2007_mapping_incidences/theorem_3_2|theorem_3_2]]: For every finite subset A of a characteristic-zero integral domain, the
larger of the sumset and the product set has size at least
C|A|^(14/13)(log|A|)^alpha, for positive absolute constants C and alpha
that the paper says are those of the Katz-Shen bound for Z/pZ it quotes as
its Theorem 3.1.

[[additive_combinatorics/vu_et_al_2007_mapping_incidences/theorem_4_2|theorem_4_2]]: For a finite subset A of SL_2(D), with D a characteristic-zero integral
domain, if the group generated by A is infinite and not metabelian, then
|AAA| > c|A|^(1+delta) for absolute constants c > 0 and delta > 0.

[[additive_combinatorics/vu_et_al_2007_mapping_incidences/theorem_5_1|theorem_5_1]]: For every rho < 1 there is delta < 1 such that an n by n matrix with iid
entries, each a finitely supported random variable in a characteristic-zero
integral domain taking each value with probability at most rho, is singular
with probability at most delta^n for all n large in terms of rho and the
support size.

## The mapping theorem

[[additive_combinatorics/vu_et_al_2007_mapping_incidences/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]]
(p. 2; proved in Section 7, pp. 11--13) says: if $S$ is a finite subset of
a characteristic-zero integral domain $D$, and $L$ is a finite set of nonzero
elements of the subring $\mathbb Z[S]$, then there is an infinite sequence of
primes of positive relative density such that, for every prime $p$ in the
sequence, there is a unital ring homomorphism

$$
\phi_p:\mathbb Z[S]\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z
$$

with $\phi_p(\ell)\ne0$ for every $\ell\in L$. The theorem is qualitative: it
does not bound the least usable prime in terms of $S$ and $L$ (the discussion
after Theorem 1.1 on p. 2, and Remark 7.2 on p. 11).

The proof first specializes to an algebraic-number ring without killing $L$
(Lemma 7.1, p. 11), then uses the Frobenius Density Theorem (Theorem 6.1, p. 10) to choose a
positive-density set of primes for which the required defining polynomial
splits; the final quotient construction is in the proof of Theorem 1.1 in
Section 7.

This preserves any prescribed *finite* zero/nonzero pattern of
integer-coefficient polynomial expressions in elements of $S$. True polynomial
equalities are preserved because $\phi_p$ is a ring homomorphism; putting each
nonzero residual into $L$ prevents false equalities from becoming true. In
particular, suitable choices of $L$ preserve:

- distinctness and hence cardinality, by protecting $s-t$ for distinct
  elements;
- additive incidences $a_1+a_2=a_3+a_4$ and multiplicative incidences
  $a_1a_2=a_3a_4$;
- point-line incidences $y=mx+b$
  ([[additive_combinatorics/vu_et_al_2007_mapping_incidences/theorem_2_3|Theorem 2.3]],
  p. 3); and
- vanishing of determinants, in both directions, for all $n\times n$ matrices
  with entries in $S$, for a given $n$ (Lemma 5.4, p. 9, the step behind
  [[additive_combinatorics/vu_et_al_2007_mapping_incidences/theorem_5_1|Theorem 5.1]]).

Theorem 2.3 applies this device to the finite-field incidence estimate of
Theorem 2.1 (p. 3), which the paper quotes as Theorem 6.2 of Bourgain, Katz and Tao,
valid for all $N\le q$ once later sum-product estimates replace theirs
(Remark 2.2), obtaining the same $cN^{3/2-\delta}$ point-line incidence bound
in every characteristic-zero integral domain. Its proof protects the differences
needed to keep the point and line parameter sets distinct, while the equation
$y=mx+b$ passes through the homomorphism.

## Sum-product application

For a finite $A\subset D$, the proof of
[[additive_combinatorics/vu_et_al_2007_mapping_incidences/theorem_3_2|Theorem 3.2]]
(p. 5) takes $L$ to
contain every nonzero expression of the forms

$$
a_1-a_2,\qquad a_1+a_2-(a_3+a_4),\qquad
a_1a_2-a_3a_4
$$

with $a_i\in A$. It may then choose $p>|A|^2$ and obtains exactly

$$
|\phi_p(A)|=|A|,\qquad
|\phi_p(A)+\phi_p(A)|=|A+A|,\qquad
|\phi_p(A)\phi_p(A)|=|AA|.
$$

Applying the Katz--Shen finite-field estimate quoted as Theorem 3.1 (p. 4),
which assumes $|A|<p^{1/2}$, therefore
gives absolute $C,\alpha>0$ such that every finite subset of every
characteristic-zero integral domain satisfies

$$
C|A|^{14/13}(\log|A|)^\alpha
 \le \max\{|A+A|,|AA|\}.
$$

More generally, the same argument transfers any finite-field lower bound whose
hypotheses can be expressed by finitely many protected algebraic incidences;
the availability of arbitrarily large usable primes handles conditions such as
$|A|<p^{1/2}$.

## Direction of transfer

The theorem maps a fixed finite characteristic-zero configuration **to** a
finite field. It does not map a finite-field or real configuration into
$\mathbb Q$ or $\mathbb Z$, and it does not realize the generated
characteristic-zero ring inside either one. Thus, even if one maps each member
of a family of real sum-product constructions to some finite field while
preserving $|A|$, $|A+A|$, and $|AA|$, the output is only a family in varying
finite fields.

This is decisive for constructions made in totally real number fields whose
degrees grow with $|A|$: Theorem 1.1 chooses a separate prime and specialization
for each finite input, but supplies no bounded-degree model, embedding, or
specialization in $\mathbb Q$ or $\mathbb Z$. Consequently it cannot turn such
a growing-degree real counterexample into an integer counterexample to Problem
52. Its relevance to the integer problem is instead the forward transfer of
finite-field *lower bounds*, exemplified by the exponent $14/13$ above.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/additive_combinatorics/E0052/_index|#52]]:
Theorem 3.2 gives $\max(|A+A|,|AA|)\ge C|A|^{14/13}(\log|A|)^\alpha$ for
finite sets of integers, by transferring a finite-field bound through Theorem
1.1. The exponent $14/13$ is far below the $2-\epsilon$ the problem asks
about, and the paper does not compare it with exponents already known over
the reals. The paper neither settles the problem nor transfers growing-degree
real constructions into $\mathbb Q$ or $\mathbb Z$.
