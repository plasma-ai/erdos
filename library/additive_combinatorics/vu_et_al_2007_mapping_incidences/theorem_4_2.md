## Statement

For a finite $A\subseteq\mathrm{SL}_2(D)$, $\langle A\rangle$ is the subgroup
of $\mathrm{SL}_2(D)$ generated by $A$ and $AAA=\{a_1a_2a_3:a_i\in A\}$
(p. 5). A group $G$ is metabelian if it has an abelian normal subgroup $N$
with $G/N$ abelian (p. 6).

**Theorem 4.2** (p. 6). Let $A$ be a finite subset of $\mathrm{SL}_2(D)$,
where $D$ is a characteristic-zero integral domain. If $\langle A\rangle$ is
infinite and not metabelian, then

$$
|AAA|>c|A|^{1+\delta},
$$

where $c>0$ and $\delta>0$ are absolute constants.

The paper compares this with Chang's product theorem in
$\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb C)$ (its Theorem 4.3, p. 6), whose hypothesis is that
$\langle A\rangle$ is not virtually abelian. The paper notes that neither
property, metabelian or virtually abelian, implies the other, with an example
of each kind (p. 6).

*Mapping incidences*, J. London Math. Soc. (2) 84 (2011), no. 2, 433--445,
doi:10.1112/jlms/jdr017; read in the arXiv version arXiv:0711.4407v2, whose

## Proof pointer

Pages 7--8. Let $G=\langle A\rangle$ and let $N_0$ be the intersection of the
normal subgroups of $G$ with abelian quotient; as $G$ is not metabelian, $N_0$
contains non-commuting $B_1,B_2$. Pick $121$ distinct elements of $G$. Theorem
1.1, applied to the matrix entries with $L$ protecting the entrywise
differences among elements of $A$ and among the $121$ chosen elements and the
non-identity of the commutator of $B_1$ and $B_2$, gives a prime $p>|A|$ and a
reduction $\Phi_p:\mathrm{SL}_2(D)\to\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)$
that is injective on $A$ and whose image of $G$ has more than $120$ elements.
Suzuki's classification of proper subgroups of
$\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)$ for $p\ge5$ (Theorem 4.5, p. 8) then
forces $\Phi_p(A)$ to generate all of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)$,
and Helfgott's theorem (Theorem 4.1, p. 6) gives the growth.
