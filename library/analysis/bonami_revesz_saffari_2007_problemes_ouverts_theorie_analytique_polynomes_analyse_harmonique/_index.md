## Summary

This volume records the open-problem session held on 28 June 2007 at the end of the 2006–2007 Institut Henri Poincaré working group on analytic polynomial theory and harmonic analysis. It is a proceedings-style collection rather than a single article: each contribution supplies enough background to isolate one or more questions, and several also record the sharpest result then available. The opening contribution, Vincent Nesme’s “Multivariate polynomials and complexity lower bounds” (pp. 2–3), passes from the univariate Nisan–Szegedy estimate, stated as Lemma 1, to Problem 2, which asks for asymptotic degree lower bounds for a real polynomial that is near $1$ at the barycenter of a simplex and near $0$ at the points of the simplex with coordinates in $\frac1{n+1}\mathbb Z$ and at least one coordinate zero. Máté Matolcsi’s “Problems on covering the plane by rotations of certain small sets” (pp. 3–4) poses Problem 3: whether, for $0<\varepsilon<1/4$, finitely many rotations of the $\varepsilon$-neighborhood of the vertical integer-line arrangement cover the plane, after recalling that the authors of the cited paper showed the analogous lattice-disk arrangement to be a negative example, and explaining why a natural reduction to covering a line by dilates does not decide the stated case.

The next group concerns polynomial inequalities and extremal geometry. Norman Levenberg’s “Questions on multivariate polynomials, distances and pseudo-metrics” (pp. 4–6) introduces the Baran and Markov pseudometrics and their integrated pseudodistances, then asks about upper semicontinuity and existence beyond symmetric convex bodies (Problems 4–5), equality of the Dubiner and Markov distances on centrally symmetric convex bodies (Problem 6), degree-one extremality on the planar simplex (Problem 7), and equality of the Markov and Baran pseudometrics on a simplex or on arbitrary convex bodies (Problems 8–9). Aline Bonami and Szilárd Gy. Révész’s “Integral Concentration of idempotents modulo a prime” (pp. 6–9) formulates toral and finite-cyclic $p$-concentration in Definitions 10 and 12, recalls the $p>1$ toral theorem as Theorem 11, and gives in Theorem 14 the finite-group dichotomy: uniform concentration holds for $p>1$ and fails for $p\leq1$. Within that discussion, Problem 13 asks about the $p=1$ endpoint before the negative result is supplied, while Problem 15 asks for the logarithmic asymptotics of the best fixed-$q$ constant $c_1(q)$.

Two unnumbered contributions by Hervé Queffelec follow on pp. 9–10. “Flat polynomials with weakly lacunary spectra” asks whether every analytic-type periodic function with absolutely convergent Taylor series factors as a convolution of two continuous $H^2$ functions; “Convolution squares and rectangles” asks for optimal separation between the coefficient $\ell^1$-norm and supremum norm on concrete sparse spectra, especially the squares, and whether the squares form a stationary set. Révész then contributes four related sections. “Bernstein inequality for multivariate polynomials” (pp. 10–11) asks in Problem 16 for the minimax norm of degree-$n$ polynomial extensions from an inscribed circle to its triangle. “Rearrangement of Fourier series” (pp. 11–12) presents Ulyanov’s uniform-rearrangement question as Problem 17, a subsequential convergence theorem as Theorem 18, its finite rearrangement constant as Problem 19, the equivalence in Proposition 20, and Konyagin’s $O(\log\log N)$ bound in Theorem 21. “Coefficient estimates and extremal problems for nonnegative and positive definite functions” (pp. 12–14) asks for the domain of the Landau-type extremal function $\alpha$ in Problem 22, brackets its endpoint growth in Theorem 23, states the sharp nonnegative-measure coefficient bound of Lemma 24, and seeks its positive-definite refinement in Problem 25. “Turán–Erőd type reverse Markov inequalities on convex domains on the plane in $L^p$” (pp. 14–15) moves from the sup-norm results of Theorems 26–28 (Turán's disk bound, the lower bound of order $n$ on bounded convex domains, and the matching upper construction on compact connected sets) and the supporting-disk estimate of Lemma 29 to the $L^p$ oscillation question in Problem 30.

Stéphane Jaffard’s “Quelques questions ouvertes concernant l’irrégularité des séries trigonométriques lacunaires” (pp. 16–18) defines pointwise Hölder regularity and first- and second-order frequency gaps in Definitions 31–32, gives coefficient obstructions to local smoothness in Proposition 33, and asks for sharp higher-order versions, for the largest possible Hölder exponent of quadratic-frequency series with coefficients comparable to $n^{-2}$, and for lacunarity hypotheses implying Hölder homogeneity as defined in Definition 34. The collection closes with Myriam Déchamps’s “Quelques questions ouvertes sur les racines de l’unité et les ensembles de Sidon” (pp. 18–21). That final contribution studies the maximal cardinality $\psi(n)$ of a quasi-independent subset of the $n$-th roots of unity, poses Questions 35–39, and records the Graham–Ramsey structure theorem as Theorem A together with arithmetic properties of $\psi$ in Theorems B–C. The roots-of-unity discussion is thus one substantial endpoint of a collection whose broader subject also includes complexity lower bounds, geometric polynomial inequalities, idempotent concentration, Fourier rearrangement, reverse Markov inequalities, and local regularity of lacunary series.

## Relation to E0774

The relevant contributor is **Myriam Déchamps** (pp. 18–21). She works in the additive group $\mathbb R^2\cong\mathbb C$ and calls a set quasi-independent when it admits no nontrivial finite relation with coefficients in $\{-1,0,1\}$; this is the notion called dissociated in E0774. For $T_n$, the set of $n$-th roots of unity, she defines $\psi(n)$ to be the largest size of a quasi-independent subset. Question 35 asks whether $\psi(n)/\varphi(n)$ is bounded, Question 36 whether $n/\psi(n)$ is bounded, and Question 37 asks for $\psi(n)$ itself. After recalling Pisier’s characterization—$\Lambda$ is Sidon exactly when every finite $A\subseteq\Lambda$ contains a quasi-independent $B$ with $|B|\geq|A|/K$ for one uniform $K$—Déchamps asks in Question 38 whether the set $e^{2\pi i\mathbb Q}$ of all roots of unity is a finite union of quasi-independent sets, and in Question 39 whether that set is Sidon.

These last two questions isolate the two sides of E0774 on a concrete torsion-free additive set. Through Pisier's characterization, Question 39 asks whether all roots of unity satisfy the proportional-dissociation hypothesis; Question 38 asks whether they satisfy the finite-union conclusion. In particular, an affirmative answer to Question 39 together with a negative answer to Question 38 would make this set a counterexample to the analogue of E0774 in the additive group $\mathbb C$; it would not by itself settle the integer question. Question 38 is therefore the roots-of-unity version of the present covering problem: its “finite union” is a cover by finitely many quasi-independent subsets, and because quasi-independence is hereditary, such a cover can be made into a partition by assigning each root to one covering set. The source also records how Questions 35 and 36 relate to them: boundedness in Question 35 would force negative answers to Questions 38 and 39, whereas boundedness in Question 36 could support positive answers.

The dates matter. This collection reports Questions 35–39 as open at the 2007 working-group session and supplies historical evidence for that status only. The current problem page for E0774 records the integer problem as open; the known bounded-torsion decomposition theorem does not settle the torsion-free setting. That present status should not be read backward into the collection, nor does the 2007 text alone establish the current individual status of every roots-of-unity subquestion.
