## Statement

Setting as for
[[analysis/bonami_revesz_saffari_2007_problemes_ouverts_theorie_analytique_polynomes_analyse_harmonique/question_35|Question 35]]:
for $n\ge2$, $\psi(n)$ is the largest cardinality of a quasi-independent subset
of the $n$-th roots of unity in the additive group $\mathbb C$.

**Question 36** (p. 19, quoted). "La suite $(\frac{n}{\psi(n)})_{n\geq1}$
est-elle bornée ?" That is: is the sequence $n/\psi(n)$ bounded?

The author notes (p. 20) that a positive answer "pourrait être une étape pour
avoir des réponses positives aux Questions 38 et 39" (quoted): it could be a
step toward positive answers to
[[analysis/bonami_revesz_saffari_2007_problemes_ouverts_theorie_analytique_polynomes_analyse_harmonique/question_38|Question 38]]
and
[[analysis/bonami_revesz_saffari_2007_problemes_ouverts_theorie_analytique_polynomes_analyse_harmonique/question_39|Question 39]].
She does not say that it implies them.

**Source.** Myriam Déchamps, "Quelques questions ouvertes sur les racines de
l'unité et les ensembles de Sidon," pp. 18--21 of *Problèmes ouverts: Théorie
analytique des polynômes et analyse harmonique*, open problems of the Institut
Henri Poincaré working group organized by Aline Bonami, Szilárd Révész and
Bahman Saffari, 2006--2007; Question 36 on p. 19. The edition is identified on
the
[[analysis/bonami_revesz_saffari_2007_problemes_ouverts_theorie_analytique_polynomes_analyse_harmonique/_index|source card]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the question and the remark on p. 20 were read
clause by clause on the printed pages. A question; the collection proves nothing
about it.

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/integer_sequences/E0774/_index|Problem 774]]: only through
  Questions 38 and 39. A positive answer to Question 36 is the case of the
  proportional-dissociation hypothesis of E0774, posed for roots of unity, in
  which the finite set is a full group of $n$-th roots of unity; the hypothesis
  asks for the same extraction from every finite set of roots of unity (an
  observation of this page). Question 36 says nothing about sets of natural
  numbers.
