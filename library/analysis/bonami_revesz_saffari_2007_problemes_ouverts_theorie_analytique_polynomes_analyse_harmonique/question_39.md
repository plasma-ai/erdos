## Statement

Setting (pp. 18--19). $e^{2\pi i\mathbb Q}$ is the set of all complex roots of
unity, a subset of the additive group $\mathbb C$, and quasi-independence is as
defined on p. 19 (see
[[analysis/bonami_revesz_saffari_2007_problemes_ouverts_theorie_analytique_polynomes_analyse_harmonique/question_38|Question 38]]).
The author recalls a characterization due to Pisier, with a combinatorial
version by Bourgain (p. 19): a subset $\Lambda$ of a discrete group $\Gamma$ is
a Sidon set if and only if there is a positive integer $K$ such that every
finite $A\subseteq\Lambda$ contains a quasi-independent subset $B$ with
$|B|\ge|A|/K$.

**Question 39** (p. 20, quoted). "L'ensemble $e^{2\pi i\mathbb Q}$ est-il un
ensemble de Sidon ?" That is: is the set of all roots of unity a Sidon set?

A positive answer to
[[analysis/bonami_revesz_saffari_2007_problemes_ouverts_theorie_analytique_polynomes_analyse_harmonique/question_38|Question 38]]
gives a positive answer to Question 39, since a finite union of
quasi-independent sets is Sidon (pp. 19--20). The author notes (p. 20) that a
positive answer to
[[analysis/bonami_revesz_saffari_2007_problemes_ouverts_theorie_analytique_polynomes_analyse_harmonique/question_35|Question 35]]
would give negative answers to Questions 38 and 39, and that a positive answer
to
[[analysis/bonami_revesz_saffari_2007_problemes_ouverts_theorie_analytique_polynomes_analyse_harmonique/question_36|Question 36]]
could be a step toward positive answers.

**Source.** Myriam Déchamps, "Quelques questions ouvertes sur les racines de
l'unité et les ensembles de Sidon," pp. 18--21 of *Problèmes ouverts: Théorie
analytique des polynômes et analyse harmonique*, open problems of the Institut
Henri Poincaré working group organized by Aline Bonami, Szilárd Révész and
Bahman Saffari, 2006--2007; Question 39 on p. 20. The edition is identified on
the
[[analysis/bonami_revesz_saffari_2007_problemes_ouverts_theorie_analytique_polynomes_analyse_harmonique/_index|source card]].
