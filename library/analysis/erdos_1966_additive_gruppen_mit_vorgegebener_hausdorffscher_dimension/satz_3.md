## Statement

Notation (p. 205). $\{M\}^\beta$ is the $\beta$-dimensional Hausdorff
measure of $M$, and $\dim$ is Hausdorff dimension.

**Satz 3** (p. 207, quoted). "Unter Annahme der Kontinuumhypothese gibt es
eine additive Gruppe $H$ von reellen Zahlen mit $\{H\}^1>0$ (also
$\dim H=1$) derart, daß keine Untergruppe $U$ mit $0<\dim U<1$ existiert."
That is, assuming the continuum hypothesis, there is an additive group $H$
of real numbers with $\{H\}^1>0$, hence $\dim H=1$, having no subgroup $U$
with $0<\dim U<1$.

**Sharper form** (p. 207). The proof constructs such an $H$, of cardinality
$2^{\aleph_0}$, all of whose subgroups $U$ with $\{U\}^1=0$ are countable,
so that $\dim U<1$ forces $\dim U=0$. Footnote 5 (p. 207) conjectures that
the weaker statement of Satz 3 can be proved without the continuum
hypothesis, which the authors did not investigate.

**Section 6, first paragraph** (p. 208). The paper states that the same
method gives a complex, algebraically closed field of cardinality
$2^{\aleph_0}$ whose intersection with every set of measure zero is
countable, by using at each step of the construction the smallest
algebraically closed field containing the given set in place of the
additively generated group. No separate proof is given, and the paper
draws no conclusion about Hausdorff dimension from it.

## Proof pointer

Pp. 207--208. A modification of Sierpiński's construction, under
$2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_1$, of a set of positive measure meeting every null set
in an at most countable set: well-order the null $G_\delta$ sets as
$A_\alpha$, $1\le\alpha<\Omega_1$, put $B_\alpha=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}A_\beta$,
and choose $x_\alpha$ outside the union of the translates of $B_\alpha$ by
the countable group $E_\alpha$ generated by the earlier $x_\beta$; $H$ is
the group generated by all the $x_\alpha$. A null subgroup lies in some
$B_\alpha$ and hence in $E_\alpha$; and $\{H\}^1>0$ since otherwise the same
argument would make $H$ countable.
