# Hollom--Sorkin 2025: independent repair of the higher-dimensional chain

This is a private mathematical reconstruction of arXiv:2510.05044v1. It is
not an author erratum, a corpus proof, or a claim about a later version. All
13 PDF pages were inspected as images; the native text was used only to pin
navigation spans.

The source defines an $r$-approximating sequence by a **squared** error at
most $r$. This distinction matters below. The reconstructed argument proves
the stated Theorem 1.5 bound

$$
\left\|\sum_i\eta_i v_i\right\|^2\le d-\varepsilon,
\qquad
\varepsilon=2^{-100}d^{-80},
$$

for $d\ge3$ and $n\not\equiv d\pmod2$. The source's lower-dimensional
inputs are outside this reconstruction.

## Printed issues and their exact effect

1. On physical page 1, the proposed even-$n$ counterexample with $n/2$
   copies on each of two axes works only when $n/2$ is odd. For every even
   $n\ge2$, one copy of $e_1$ and $n-1$ copies of $e_2$ gives the
   intended lower bound $\sqrt2$. This is introductory context and does not
   enter Theorem 1.5.
2. In the physical-page-4 sketch of Lemma 2.5, the plus convention in (2.1)
   requires $\eta_i=-\lambda_i$ at an endpoint, rather than
   $\eta_i=\lambda_i$.
3. Lemma 2.6's second display needs
   $p+\operatorname{Conv}(S(W))$, not $p+\operatorname{Conv}(W)$. In its
   proof, the moving-coefficient point belongs to the convex hull of the
   fixed-sign cell, not generally to the vertex set itself. The translated
   cell formula remains valid for repeated vectors and is the interface used
   below.
4. The polar-decomposition estimate in Lemma 2.8 does not transparently
   track the matrix norm and its factor of $\sqrt d$. The singular-value
   argument below proves the stronger column bound actually used in the
   repair.
5. Lemma 4.2's second bullet depends on the displayed coefficients
   $\lambda_i$. It is a conclusion for that fixed target, not the uniform
   assertion that the vector sequence is $d-\delta$-approximating. At
   $\lambda=0$, an orthonormal basis has minimum squared error $d$, which
   directly disproves the printed uniform reading.
6. In the physical-page-8 bound after (4.3), the planar target is visibly
   printed as $p_2$; it must be $p_1$. The preceding and following
   orthogonal decomposition makes the correction unambiguous.
7. The physical-page-9 cluster calculation uses the wrong powers of
   $\zeta$. With $\alpha=\zeta^{1/4}$, a paired difference has norm at
   most $\sqrt{2\alpha}$, so its balanced squared contribution is at most
   $2d\alpha$, not $2d\zeta$.
8. On physical page 10, the chord parameters give
   $r^2=1+(1-\lambda_1)^2\cos^2\theta$. Both the printed
   $1-\lambda_1^2$ expression and the following minus sign are wrong.
9. On physical page 11, the existence of one oblique pair supports one
   intermediate coordinate of $y$, not every coordinate. For example,
   $y=(e_1+e_2)/\sqrt2$ has an oblique basis pair and zero later
   coordinates. The one-coordinate proof below is enough.
10. The page-11 change from $x_i$ to $e_i$ moves both the chosen signed
    sum and the target point in its zonotope. The displayed comparison moves
    only the signed sum. The total transfer bound is therefore
    $2d^2\sqrt\varepsilon$, not a one-sided sum bound.
11. In the first main case, the initial use of Lemma 2.5 is unavailable at
    $n=d+2$, because the removable set has only $d$ members. At that
    endpoint one simply takes the resulting $d+2$-term sequence to be the
    original sequence. For $n\ge d+3$, Lemma 2.5 applies as intended.
12. The source begins the main proof without the case $n\le d-1$. The
    elementary expectation argument below supplies it. At the end of the
    cluster case, the construction proves the required zero-target theorem,
    not the stronger uniform approximation assertion stated in the final
    sentence.

The physical-page-12 rearrangement was also checked independently. It is
not a source error. If $a=6\varepsilon^{1/4}d^3$,
$B=36\varepsilon^{1/2}d^7$, and
$q=\sum_i(2y_i-1)e_i$, then

$$
\|q\|^2=d+4-4\sum_i y_i.
$$

Thus $\|q\|^2\le(\sqrt{d-a}-\sqrt B)^2$ is exactly equivalent to

$$
4+a-B+2\sqrt{B}\sqrt{d-a}\le4\sum_i y_i.
$$

The displayed sufficient condition (5.3) follows from
$6d^3+12d^4\le18d^4$. The earlier private allegation of a missing factor
four is superseded by this computation.

## Two corrected preliminary lemmas

Let $X$ be the matrix whose columns are unit vectors $x_1,\ldots,x_d$,
and suppose

$$
|\langle x_i,x_j\rangle|\le\delta\qquad(i\ne j).
$$

Take a singular-value decomposition $X=A\Sigma B^T$ and put
$U=AB^T$, extending singular-vector bases if $X$ is singular. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|X-U\|_F^2
 &=\sum_i(\sigma_i-1)^2\\
 &\le\sum_i(\sigma_i^2-1)^2\\
 &=\|X^TX-I\|_F^2\\
 &\le d^2\delta^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $|\sigma-1|\le|\sigma^2-1|$ for $\sigma\ge0$. Consequently the
$i$-th column $e_i$ of $U$ satisfies

$$
\|x_i-e_i\|\le d\delta.
$$

For the corrected Lemma 4.2, first retain the source's elementary induction
that $m$ unit vectors have uniform squared error at most $m$. If two of
the $d$ unit vectors have inner product of magnitude $c\ge\delta$, orient
the second vector and assume $\lambda_1\ge0$. Choose
$\eta_1=-1$, set $\rho=1-\lambda_1$, and use

$$
a^2=1-\rho^2\sin^2\theta,
\qquad
r^2=1+\rho^2\cos^2\theta,
$$

where $c=|\sin\theta|$. Then $r^2-a^2=\rho^2$, the chord in Claim 5.1
has length two, and

$$
r^2\le1+\cos^2\theta=2-\sin^2\theta\le2-\delta^2.
$$

The degenerate endpoints follow directly or by continuity. Rounding the
remaining $d-2$ vectors gives uniform squared error at most
$d-\delta^2$. Separately, for a fixed coefficient target with
$|\lambda_i|\ge\delta$, choosing the opposite sign at that coordinate
leaves squared error at most $1-\delta$ there; rounding the other
coordinates gives squared error at most $d-\delta$ for that target.

## Repaired Lemma 4.1

Fix $d\ge3$, set

$$
\varepsilon=2^{-100}d^{-80},\qquad
\zeta=18\varepsilon^{1/4}d^4,\qquad
\tau=8d^3\sqrt\varepsilon,
$$

and suppose a sequence of $d+1$ unit vectors contains a
$\zeta$-oblique pair. If every $d$-subsequence has a pair with inner
product magnitude at least $\sqrt\varepsilon$, the uniform pair clause and
Lemma 2.5 give squared error at most $d-\varepsilon$.

Otherwise write the sequence as $X=(x_1,\ldots,x_d,y)$, where

$$
|\langle x_i,x_j\rangle|<\sqrt\varepsilon.
$$

The singular-value lemma gives an orthonormal basis $E=(e_1,\ldots,e_d)$
with $\|x_i-e_i\|\le d\sqrt\varepsilon$. The oblique pair must involve
$y$, since $\sqrt\varepsilon<\zeta$. Orient each pair $(x_i,e_i)$
together so the coordinates $y_i=\langle y,e_i\rangle$ are nonnegative;
this preserves both the signed-sum set and the column-error bound. For at
least one index $j$,

$$
b\le y_j\le1-b,\qquad b=\zeta/2,
$$

because $d\sqrt\varepsilon\le\zeta/2$. No condition on the other
coordinates is asserted.

Consider any $d$-subsequence of $(E,y)$ containing $y$. If it retains
$e_j$, it contains a pair whose squared inner product is at least $b^2$.
If it omits $e_j$, then

$$
\sum_{i\ne j}y_i^2=1-y_j^2\ge1-(1-b)^2\ge b,
$$

so one retained coordinate has square at least $b^2/d$. Thus every such
subsequence has uniform squared error at most

$$
d-\frac{b^2}{d}
=d-\frac{\zeta^2}{4d}
\le d-\tau,
$$

because $\zeta^2/(4d)=81d^7\sqrt\varepsilon\ge\tau$.

The corrected translated-cell form of Lemma 2.6 now leaves only the two
cells obtained by omitting $y$. By symmetry, translate the cell
$Z(E)-y$ by $+y$. It suffices to approximate each
$p=\sum_i\lambda_i e_i\in Z(E)$ by

$$
S(E)\cup(S(E)+2y).
$$

If some $|\lambda_i|\ge\tau$, use the fixed-target clause of Lemma 4.2.
Otherwise $\|p\|\le\sqrt d\,\tau$. The point

$$
q=2y-\sum_i e_i\in S(E)+2y
$$

satisfies

$$
\sum_i y_i
\ge y_j+\sum_{i\ne j}y_i^2
=1+y_j(1-y_j)
\ge1+\zeta/4,
$$

and therefore $\|q\|^2=d+4-4\sum_i y_i\le d-\zeta$. Since
$\tau\le1$ and $\zeta\ge4d\tau$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|q-p\|^2
&\le(\sqrt{d-\zeta}+\sqrt d\,\tau)^2\\
&\le d-\zeta+3d\tau\\
&\le d-\tau.
\end{aligned}
$$

It remains to transfer both target and signed sum back from $E$ to $X$.
For the same coefficient vector $\lambda$ and sign vector $\eta$, the
additional error is

$$
\left\|\sum_i(\eta_i-\lambda_i)(x_i-e_i)\right\|
\le2d^2\sqrt\varepsilon.
$$

Hence the squared error in the original sequence is at most

$$
d-\tau+4d^{5/2}\sqrt\varepsilon+4d^4\varepsilon
\le d-\varepsilon.
$$

After division by $\sqrt\varepsilon$, the last inequality follows from

$$
\sqrt\varepsilon+4d^{5/2}+4d^4\sqrt\varepsilon\le8d^3.
$$

For $d\ge3$, the middle term is at most
$(4/\sqrt3)d^3$, while the two terms involving $\sqrt\varepsilon$ are
less than $d^3$. All other sufficient estimates above follow from
$\varepsilon^{1/4}\le9/16$. This proves the needed dichotomy with the
source's stated constant.

## Completion of Theorem 1.5 for $d\ge3$

For independent signs,

$$
\mathbb E\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_i v_i\right\|^2=n.
$$

Thus $n\le d-1$ is immediate, and parity excludes $n=d$. Assume
$n\ge d+1$. Put $\alpha=\zeta^{1/4}$. The explicit constant gives

$$
\zeta<2^{-20}d^{-16},\qquad
\alpha<\frac1{32d^4}.
$$

### An oblique pair

Suppose some pair $u,w$ has inner-product magnitude in
$(\alpha,1-\alpha)$. If $n=d+1$, use the repaired Lemma 4.1. If
$n=d+2$, take $X=V$. If $n\ge d+3$, Lemma 2.5 reduces to an arbitrary
$X=Y\cup\{u,w\}$ with $|Y|=d$.

The second elimination in the source is valid: unless one of the two
$(d+1)$-sets $Y\cup\{u\}$, $Y\cup\{w\}$ is already uniformly good,
both are $\zeta$-almost orthogonal. The source's metric argument then gives

$$
|\langle y,u\rangle|,|\langle y,w\rangle|\le\zeta
\qquad(y\in Y).
$$

After orienting $w$, the $2\times2$ Gram matrix has least eigenvalue at
least $\alpha$; this verifies all sign cases in the source's projection
argument and in particular gives the stated bound

$$
\|\operatorname{proj}_{\operatorname{span}(u,w)}y\|
\le2\zeta^{3/4}.
$$

Using $p_1$ in the physical-page-8 planar line, the squared error is at
most

$$
R=d-2+
\left(\sqrt{2-\sqrt\zeta}+4d\zeta^{3/4}\right)^2.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
R=d-\sqrt\zeta\left(
1-8d\zeta^{1/4}\sqrt{2-\sqrt\zeta}-16d^2\zeta
\right).
$$

The bracket exceeds $1/2$: its two deducted terms are at most
$(\sqrt2/4)d^{-3}$ and $2^{-16}d^{-14}$. Moreover
$\sqrt\zeta/2\ge\varepsilon$. Hence $R\le d-\varepsilon$.

### No oblique pair

Suppose every pair has inner-product magnitude at most $\alpha$ or at
least $1-\alpha$. Near parallelism is transitive because, after sign
orientation, two successive close pairs force the endpoint inner product to
be at least $1-4\alpha>\alpha$. There are at most $d$ clusters: $d+1$
representatives would have a Gram matrix with diagonal one and off-diagonal
magnitudes at most $\alpha<1/d$, hence a positive-definite matrix of rank
$d+1$ in $\mathbb R^d$.

Orient each cluster consistently and pair its vectors. The number $L$ of
unpaired vectors satisfies $L\equiv n\pmod2$, so $L\le d-1$. Beck's
rounding interface gives a signed long sum with squared norm at most $d-1$.
Each paired difference has squared norm at most $2\alpha$, so the short
differences have a signed sum with squared norm at most $2d\alpha$.
Reverse all short-pair signs, if necessary, so its inner product with the
long sum is nonpositive. The total squared norm is at most

$$
d-1+2d\alpha\le d-\varepsilon.
$$

This last branch is a zero-target argument, exactly what Theorem 1.5 needs.
It does not establish uniform $d-\varepsilon$-approximation.

## Scope boundary

The reconstruction uses finite-dimensional SVD and the source's Beck
rounding/elimination interfaces. It establishes that the same overall proof
method can retain $\varepsilon=2^{-100}d^{-80}$ for $d\ge3$ after the
listed corrections. It gives zero canonical proof credit, makes no claim of
an author-issued correction, and does not certify a published version or the
source's separate $d=1,2$ route.
