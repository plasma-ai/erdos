***

**Source.** Laczkovich (1984), printed p. 110
([PDF p. 2](laczkovich_1984_kemperman_s_inequality.pdf#page=2)),
the answer to Lawrence's Question 7.

## Statement

If $f:G_{\sqrt2}\to[0,\infty)$ satisfies

$$
2f(x)\le\max\{f(x+h),f(x+2h)\}
\qquad(x,h\in G_{\sqrt2},\ h>0),
\tag{1}
$$

then $f$ vanishes identically.

**Dependencies.**
[[analysis/laczkovich_1984_kemperman_s_inequality/theorem_2|Theorem 2]]
and [[analysis/laczkovich_1984_kemperman_s_inequality/positive_increments|density of the subgroup]].

**Source scope.** The source first passes to one-sided limits of the
nondecreasing function and then doubles its values along a finite
chain. The argument below supplies the same doubling comparison
directly with a small positive subgroup step, so no undeclared
continuity or existence of a finite real-line extension is needed.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/analysis/E1125/_index|Problem 1125]], as a distinct
stronger-inequality consequence. Compare the
[[analysis/laczkovich_1984_kemperman_s_inequality/rational_counterexample|rational counterexample]],
which does not extend to this subgroup.

## Proof

Nonnegativity makes the maximum in (1) at most the sum of the two
later values. Thus $f$ satisfies (K), and Theorem 2 makes it
nondecreasing on $G_{\sqrt2}$.

For any $x<y$ in this subgroup, choose a positive subgroup element
$h<(y-x)/2$, using density. Both $x+h$ and $x+2h$ are below $y$,
so monotonicity and (1) give

$$
2f(x)\le\max\{f(x+h),f(x+2h)\}\le f(y).
\tag{2}
$$

Fix $x$. For every positive integer $m$, density permits a chain
of $m$ points

$$
x<x_1<\cdots<x_m<x+1
$$

in the subgroup, for example by choosing one in each of $m$
disjoint ordered subintervals of $(x,x+1)$.
Applying (2) successively, and then monotonicity at the last point,
gives

$$
0\le f(x)\le2^{-m}f(x_m)\le2^{-m}f(x+1).
$$

The value $f(x+1)$ is finite and independent of $m$.
Letting $m$ tend to infinity proves $f(x)=0$.
