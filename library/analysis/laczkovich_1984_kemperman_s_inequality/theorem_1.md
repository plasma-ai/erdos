***

**Source.** Laczkovich (1984), Theorem 1, printed p. 109, and its
deduction from Theorem 2 on p. 110
([PDF pp. 1–2](laczkovich_1984_kemperman_s_inequality.pdf#page=1)).

## Statement

If $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ satisfies

$$
2f(x)\le f(x+h)+f(x+2h)
\qquad(x\in\mathbb R,\ h>0),
$$

then $f$ is nondecreasing. No measurability, continuity, or local
boundedness hypothesis is required.

**Dependencies.**
[[analysis/laczkovich_1984_kemperman_s_inequality/theorem_2|Theorem 2]]
and the bounded continued fraction of $\sqrt2$ recorded in
[[analysis/laczkovich_1984_kemperman_s_inequality/continued_fraction_inputs|the classical input page]].
The preceding source-specific proof chain is part of this compilation;
general continued-fraction theory is the stated external input.

**Bears on.** This is the affirmative real-function conclusion in
[[../wiki/problems/analysis/E1125/_index|Problem 1125]]. It does not assert strict
increase or a theorem on arbitrary rational-domain functions.

## Proof

Choose any real $a<b$ and define

$$
g(t)=f(a+(b-a)t).
$$

For every real $t$ and $s>0$, the original inequality applies with
base point $a+(b-a)t$ and positive step $(b-a)s$.
Hence $g$ satisfies the same inequality. Restrict $g$ to
$G_{\sqrt2}=\mathbb Z\sqrt2+\mathbb Z$ and apply Theorem 2.
Since $0,1\in G_{\sqrt2}$,

$$
f(a)=g(0)\le g(1)=f(b).
$$

This proves nondecreasing monotonicity on all of $\mathbb R$.
