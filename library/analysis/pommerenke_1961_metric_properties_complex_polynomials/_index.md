  Answers to 1958 problems of Erdős, Herzog and Piranian on the set where a
  monic polynomial has modulus at most one: unboundedly many components of
  diameter near 4 (Theorem 1), the z^n - r^n example (p. 98), a disk of
  radius (2e)^{-1} n^{-2} (Theorem 4), every projection above 2.386

  (Theorem 14) and the discriminant bound 2^{4(n-1)} n^n (Theorem 16).

# analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials

[[analysis/_index|..]]

[[analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials/example_p103|example_p103]]: A lemniscate set whose projection onto every line has measure greater
than 2.386, obtained from the "5-Stern" through the approximation theorem,
with Theorem 7's complementary bound that some projection of a capacity-1
set has measure less than 3.30; the negative answer to Problem 1043.

[[analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials/example_p98|example_p98]]: For 1 < r < 2 the set where |z^n - r^n| is at most 1 has n components
whose common diameter tends to 0 as n grows, so no component has diameter
at least 2 - r; the negative half of the answer to Problem 1048.

[[analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials/theorem_1|theorem_1]]: For every l below 4 and every k there is a monic polynomial whose
sublevel set has at least k components of diameter at least l; the
negative answer to Problem 511 and to Problems 8 and 9 of the 1958 paper.

[[analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials/theorem_10|theorem_10]]: With the centroid of the zeros at 0, E is connected when the zeros lie
in the disk of radius 1/sqrt 2 or in [-1, 1], and a connected E lies in
the disk of radius 2 about the centroid, with the zeros inside it and
sigma below sqrt 2; the connected case of Problem 509.

[[analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials/theorem_14|theorem_14]]: The polynomial z^p (z - a), for large p and a slightly above
(1 + 1/p) p^{1/(p+1)}, has a sublevel set with two components one of which
is not convex; the negative answer to Grunsky's question, Problem 1047.

[[analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials/theorem_16|theorem_16]]: The maximum of the ordered product of the distances among n points of
diameter at most 2 is at most 2^{4(n-1)} n^n, from Theorem 15 on convex
continua of capacity 1; the first general upper bound for Problem 1045,
with the remark that the hull of a maximal system is nearly a disk.

[[analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials/theorem_3|theorem_3]]: When the zeros lie in the disk of radius r at most 1, the component of E
containing 0 has diameter at least 2, more than 1/r, or more than 2 - r^2
in three ranges of r, in every case at least 2 - r; the affirmative half of
the answer to Problem 1048, for the closed set.

[[analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials/theorem_4|theorem_4]]: When the zeros lie in the closed unit disk, E contains a disk of radius
(2e)^{-1} n^{-2}, so its area is at least pi (2e)^{-2} n^{-4}; the
polynomial lower bound of Problem 116 and the n^{-2} inradius bound of
Problem 1039.

[[analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials/theorem_9|theorem_9]]: The lemniscate |f(z)| = 1 of a monic polynomial of degree n has length
less than 74 n^2, the first polynomial upper bound toward the length
question of Problem 114.

## Contents

Throughout, $f(z)=\prod_{\nu=1}^n(z-z_\nu)=z^n+\cdots$ and
$E=\{|f(z)|\le1\}$, the closed set the paper calls the lemniscate domain
(p. 97); the open set $\{|f(z)|<1\}$ is written $\mathring E$ where the
paper distinguishes it (p. 102). Several site problems (#116, #511,
#1038, #1039, #1048) are posed for the open set; where the transfer from
the closed set matters, the result page says so as a filing observation.

- Introduction and § 1, the diameters of the components of $E$
  (pp. 97--100). The paper "will give (at least partial) answers to some
  problems raised by Erdös, Herzog and Piranian [2]" and derive "some
  metric properties of continua of capacity 1" (p. 97). Every $E$ has
  logarithmic capacity 1 ([4]) and, quoted (p. 97), "the following
  approximation theorem holds [5]: Let $F$ be a closed bounded set with
  $\operatorname{cap}F=1$. Given any $\varepsilon>0$ and $\eta>0$, there
  exists a $\rho$ ($1<\rho<1+\eta$) and a polynomial $f(z)=z^n+\cdots$
  such that the lemniscate $\{|f(z)|=\rho^n\}$ contains $F$ in its
  interior and is contained in an $\varepsilon$-neighborhood of $F$."
  Problem 8 of [2] asks whether $\sum\max(0,d_j-1)$ over the component
  diameters $d_j$ is bounded, and Problem 9 (revised form, [2, p. 148])
  whether the number of components of diameter greater than a fixed $l>1$
  is bounded. The paper states that both answers are negative, and that
  they stay negative when Problem 8 is asked with $d_j-l$, $l<4$, in place
  of $d_j-1$, and when Problem 9 is asked with any $l<4$ (p. 98).
  Theorem 1 (p. 98, quoted): "For each $0<l<4$ and
  $k=1,2,\cdots$, one can find a polynomial $f(z)=z^n+\cdots$ such that
  $E=\{|f(z)|\le1\}$ has at least $k$ different components of diameter
  greater than or equal to $l$." Proof: $F$ the union of the $k$ segments
  $[i\mu\delta,\,l+i\mu\delta]$, $\mu=1,\ldots,k$; a segment of length $l$
  has capacity $l/4<1$, so $\operatorname{cap}F<1$ for small $\delta$, and
  the approximation theorem gives $f$ with $E\supset F$ inside a
  $\delta/3$-neighborhood of $F$. A filing observation, not a review
  verdict: the approximation theorem is quoted for $\operatorname{cap}F=1$
  and a level $\rho^n>1$, and the proof applies it to a set of capacity
  below 1 at level 1 without spelling out the rescaling; the step was not
  reconstructed here. Theorem 2 (p. 98, quoted): "If $z^*\in E$ lies on a
  line of support of $E$, there exists a point $z\in E$ with
  $|z-z^*|>3\sqrt3/4\approx1.299$. The constant $3\sqrt3/4$ is best
  possible", answering Problem 10b of [2], which asked for such a $z$ with
  $|z-z^*|\ge2$; the paper cites its [9] for the failure of the constant
  $2$.
  Proof: an explicit map shows the half-disk of radius $3\sqrt3/4$ has
  capacity 1, and a lemniscate set inside it would equal it. Problem 7 of
  [2] (p. 98), quoted: "Let the zeros $z_\nu$ of $f(z)$ belong to the disk
  $|z|\le r$. Erdös, Herzog and Piranian [2, Problem 7] raised the question
  whether there is always a component of $E$ with diameter at least $2-r$
  ($r<2$). The answer is negative for $r>1$." The example is
  $f(z)=z^n-r^n$, $r>1$: $E$ has $n$ components and, for $z$ in the
  component of the zero $r$, $|z-r|\le n^{-1}r^{-n+1}(1+O(n^{-1}))\to0$,
  so "the (common) diameter of the components of $E$ tends to $0$ as
  $n\to\infty$." Lemma 1 (pp. 98--99): with $z_0=\frac1n\sum z_\nu$ and
  $\sigma^2=\frac1n\sum|z_\nu|^2$, if $\sigma^2-|z_0|^2\le1$ the disk
  $|z-z_0|\le(1-\sigma^2+|z_0|^2)^{1/2}$ lies in $E$ (by the
  arithmetic-geometric mean inequality). Lemma 2 (p. 99): if a continuum
  $A\subset E$ contains all the zeros, $E$ is connected. Theorem 3 (p. 99,
  quoted): "Let $f(z)=\prod(z-z_\nu)$, $|z_\nu|\le r\le1$, and let $d_0$
  be the diameter of the component $E_0$ of $E$ that contains $0$. Then
  $d_0\ge2$ for $0\le r\le1/2$, $d_0>1/r$ for $1/2<r\le(\sqrt5-1)/2$,
  $d_0>2-r^2$ for $(\sqrt5-1)/2\le r\le1$." Remarks (p. 99): $0\in E$
  since $|f(0)|=\prod|z_\nu|\le1$, and the centroid $z_0$ lies in $E_0$;
  $d_0\ge2$ is sharp for $r\le1/2$ ($f=z^n$), and the 1958 polynomial
  $(z^n+1)(z-1)^2(z-e^{i\pi/n})^{-1}(z-e^{-i\pi/n})^{-1}$ has
  $d_0<1+\varepsilon$, so $d_0>1$ is best possible for $r=1$; Remark 3
  observes that each of the three bounds $2$, $r^{-1}$ and $2-r^2$ is at
  least $2-r$, so Theorem 3 gives the affirmative answer to Problem 7 of
  [2] for $0<r\le1$. Proof (p. 100), four steps: $E_0$ meets
  $|z|>1$ unless $f=z^n$ (the polynomial $\prod(1-\bar z_\nu z)$ and the
  minimum principle); for $r\le1/2$, $E$ is connected and a continuum of
  capacity 1 has diameter at least 2; for the middle range, the disk of
  Lemma 1 and a case split on $|z_0|$ against $(1-2r^2)/(2r)$; for the
  last range, the point outside the unit disk and the disk of Lemma 1.
- § 2, the largest disk contained in $E$ (pp. 101--102). For zeros in a
  given compact set $A$, let $\rho$ be the radius of the largest disk
  contained in $E$ (p. 101). The paper recalls that $\rho$ has a positive
  lower bound $\rho_0=\rho_0(A)$ when $\operatorname{cap}A<1$
  [2, Theorem 6], and that no lower bound independent of the degree $n$
  exists when $A$ is a radius-$1$ disk or a length-$4$ segment, both of
  capacity $1$. Erdös, Herzog and Piranian asked whether
  $\rho\ge\mathrm{const}\cdot n^{-1}$ when $|z_\nu|\le1$ [2, Problem 3];
  the paper calls Theorem 4 "a weaker estimate" and points also to
  [2, Problem 2]. Theorem 4 (p. 101, quoted): "If $|z_\nu|\le1$, the
  lemniscate domain $E$ contains a disk of radius $(2e)^{-1}n^{-2}$."
  Proof: $d_0>1$ by Theorem 3, $d_0\le4\operatorname{cap}E_0$ [6, p. 42],
  so $\operatorname{cap}E_0>1/4$; the derivative bound of [11],
  $|f'(z)|\le en^2/(2\operatorname{cap}E_0)<2en^2$ on $E_0$; integrating
  from a zero $z_\mu\in E_0$ to the nearest boundary point $z^*$ gives
  $1=|f(z^*)|<|z^*-z_\mu|\cdot2en^2$, so the disk
  $|z-z_\mu|\le1/(2en^2)$ lies in $E_0\subset E$. Lemma 3 (p. 101):
  for $\xi=z+z^{-1}$, $\xi'=z'+z'^{-1}$ on the upper unit semicircle,
  $|\xi-\xi'|\ge|z-z'|^2$. Theorem 5 (p. 101, quoted): "Let
  $-2\le z_\nu=\xi_\nu\le2$. Then the set $E\cap X$ contains a segment of
  length $1/8e^2n^4$ ($X$ denotes the real axis)", introduced with "I want
  to establish the conjecture of Erdös, Herzog and Piranian [2, p. 132]
  that $\rho\ge n^{-\gamma}$, where $\gamma$ denotes an absolute constant",
  and the introduction (p. 97) announces "In Section 2 it will be proved
  that $E$ contains a disk of radius const$\cdot n^{-4}$, if
  $z_\nu\in[-2,+2]$"; the theorem as printed gives a segment of $E\cap X$,
  not a disk. The conjecture printed on [2, p. 132] itself reads
  $|E\cap L|>n^{-c}$ for zeros on $[-2,2]$, which a real segment of length
  of order $n^{-4}$ in $E$ already gives. Proof (pp. 101--102):
  $g(z)=z^nf(z+z^{-1})$ of degree $2n$ with zeros on $|z|=1$; Theorem 4's
  proof gives a disk of radius $1/(2en^2)$ in $\{|g|\le1\}$ centered on
  $|z|=1$; an arc $B$ of the circle "of diameter $1/4en^2$" with $|g|\le1$
  on $B$ and $\Im z\ge0$; $B^*=\{z+z^{-1}:z\in B\}$ "has length at least
  $(4en^2)^{-2}$, by Lemma 3", and $|f(\xi)|\le1$ on $B^*$. A filing
  observation, not a review verdict: the proof's last estimate as printed
  gives $(4en^2)^{-2}=1/(16e^2n^4)$, half the constant stated in the
  theorem; the discrepancy was not resolved here.
- § 3, upper bounds for geometric quantities associated with $E$
  (pp. 102--105). Pólya [8]: the projection of a compact set of capacity 1
  onto a line has linear measure $d\le4$. Theorem 6 (p. 102, quoted):
  "Let $f(z)=\prod_{\nu=1}^n(z-z_\nu)$, let $P$ be the projection of $E$
  onto the real axis $X$, and let $d$ be the linear measure of $P$. Then
  $\operatorname{cap}P\le2^{-1/n}$, $d\le4\cdot2^{-1/n}$, with
  $\operatorname{cap}P=2^{-1/n}$ exactly if all $z_\nu$ lie on a parallel
  to $X$ and if $\mathring E=\{|f(z)|<1\}$ has $n$ components. (The
  result concerning $d$ was already known to P. Erdös and Bl. Sendov; see
  the remark after Problem 102, Wisk. Opgaven 20/3 (1957), p. 22.)"
  Remark: $d=4\cdot2^{-1/n}$ holds exactly when $P$ is one segment of
  capacity $2^{-1/n}$, which "can be shown" to happen if and only if
  $f(z)=T_n(2^{-1+1/n}z+c)$, $T_n$ the Chebyshev polynomial. Proof
  (pp. 102--103): $P\subset E^*\cap X$ for the polynomial $f^*$ with the
  real parts of the zeros, Fekete's theorem gives capacity $2^{-1/n}$ for
  $\{f^*\text{ real},\,|f^*|\le1\}$, and Pólya's $d\le4\operatorname{cap}P$.
  Then (p. 103): since $d\le4$ holds for every direction, the largest of
  the projection measures of $E$ is at most $4$. Writing $b$ for the
  smallest projection measure of $E$, the paper applies the approximation
  theorem to the "5-Stern" of [10, p. 73] and obtains a lemniscate domain
  with $b>2.386$, pointing to [2, Problem 10a], and then turns to an upper
  bound for $b$. Theorem 7 (p. 103, quoted): "Let
  $F$ be a closed bounded set with $\operatorname{cap}F=1$. Then the
  projection of $F$ onto a certain straight line has measure less than
  $3.30$." Proof: $F$ is enclosed by closed convex curves $L_\mu$ of
  total length below $10.36$ [12, Theorem 2]; with $b_\mu(\theta)$ the
  width of $L_\mu$ in direction $\theta$ and
  $\Lambda_\mu=\frac12\int_0^{2\pi}b_\mu(\theta)\,d\theta$ [1, p. 48],
  some $\theta_0$ has $\sum_\mu b_\mu(\theta_0)<10.36/\pi<3.30$, and the
  projection of $F$ onto a line perpendicular to the direction $\theta_0$
  has measure at most that sum. Theorem 8 (p. 104): if $f\not\equiv z^n$
  then $\operatorname{meas}[E\cap\{|z|=1\}]\le2\pi n/(n+1)$, by averaging
  $|f|^2$ over the $(n+1)$st roots of unity. Then (p. 104): "Problem 12a
  in [2] asks whether $\Lambda$ is greatest for $f(z)=z^n-1$. An
  affirmative answer would imply that $\Lambda\le2n+o(n)$." Theorem 9
  (p. 104, quoted): "If $f(z)=z^n+\cdots$ and $\Lambda$ is the length of
  $C=\{|f(z)|=1\}$, then $\Lambda<74n^2$." Proof (pp. 104--105): $C$ is
  the real part of an algebraic curve of order $2n$, assumed by continuity
  to have only simple singularities and no real double points; at most
  $2n(2n-2)$ real inflection points [7] and, by Bézout, at most $2n(2n-1)$
  points with horizontal tangent; fewer than $8n^2$ marked points cut $C$
  into $m<8n^2$ arcs $C_k$ of constant curvature sign, each of capacity at
  most $\operatorname{cap}C=1$, whose convex hulls have perimeter below
  $9.2$ [10, Theorem 5]; so $\Lambda<9.2m<9.2\cdot8n^2<74n^2$.
- § 4, the connectedness of $E$ (pp. 105--110). Lemma 4 (p. 105): for
  $C(r)=\{|f(z)|=r^n\}$ and
  $\lambda(r)=\frac1{2\pi r^n}\int_{C(r)}|z|^2|f'(z)|\,|dz|$,
  $\lambda(r)>\sum|z_\nu|^2$ for $r>0$ ($\lambda$ is strictly increasing
  with limit $\sum|z_\nu|^2$ at $0$). Theorem 10 (pp. 106--107, quoted):
  "Let $z_0=\frac1n\sum_{\nu=1}^nz_\nu=0$ and
  $\sigma^2=\frac1n\sum_{\nu=1}^n|z_\nu|^2$. Then the following best
  possible results hold: (a) If $|z_\nu|\le\sqrt2/2$ or if
  $z_\nu\in[-1,+1]$, then $E$ is connected. (b) If $E$ is connected, then
  $|z_\nu|<2$ and $\sigma<\sqrt2$." Proof of (a): Lemma 1 puts the disk
  $|z|\le\sqrt2/2$ in $E$, or [2, Theorem 1] puts $[-1,0]$ and $[0,1]$ in
  $E$, and Lemma 2 applies; $(z^2-1/2)^m(z^2+a^2)$ with $a>\sqrt2/2$ and
  $z^2-a^2$ with $a>1$ show sharpness. Proof of (b) (p. 107): with
  $z_0=0$, $w=f(z)^{1/n}=z+a_2^*z^{-1}+\cdots$ is univalent outside $E$,
  its inverse $\phi(w)=w+\sum b_\mu w^{-\mu}$ is univalent in $|w|>1$, so
  $E$ lies in the closed disk $|z|\le2$ [6, p. 42], and each zero, an
  interior point of $E$, has $|z_\nu|<2$; Lemma 4 and the area theorem
  $\sum\mu|b_\mu|^2\le1$ give $\sigma^2<2$. The polynomial $T_n(2^{1/n-1}z)$,
  whose $E$ is connected, shows the bounds in (b) cannot be improved
  (pp. 107--108). Lemma 5
  (p. 108): $f(z)^{1/n}$ is univalent outside the convex hull of the
  zeros. Theorem 11 (p. 108): if $A$ is a closed bounded convex set of
  capacity $\kappa\le1/2$ with conformal center $0$, the zeros lie in $A$
  and their centroid is $0$, then $E$ is connected and contains $A$. Then
  (p. 109), for a continuum $F$ of capacity 1 with width $b$ and diameter
  $d$, the paper recalls Problem 15 of [2] (bounds for $b$, $d$, $bd$),
  its own $b<2.920$ [10, Theorem 6], and completes the proof of the
  inequality asserted in [9], whose proof "was not correctly formulated,
  as Prof. Herzog kindly pointed out": the sentence prints the assertion
  as $b^2+d^2\le63/3$, and the completed proof derives
  $b^2+d^2\le64/3$, "which is the asserted inequality"; "Probably
  $b^2+d^2\le16$ holds (with equality for a segment of length 4)", proved
  in [10, Theorem 9] when $F$ is convex or contains a segment of length
  $d$. Theorem 12 (p. 109): for a continuum $F$ of capacity 1 symmetric
  about $0$, either (I) $b\le2\sqrt2$, $b^2+d^2\le16$ and $bd\le8$, or
  (II) $b\le2$, $b^2+d^2\le18$ and $bd\le4\sqrt3$; remarks give a
  symmetric continuum with $b>2.18$ and one with $bd>6.15$ [9], and the
  sharp $b^*\le2\sqrt2$, $b^*d\le8$ for the width $b^*$ measured parallel
  to a diameter (equality for two perpendicular segments of length
  $2\sqrt2$).
- § 5, convexity (pp. 110--115). Erdős, Herzog and Piranian [2,
  Theorem 11] proved $E$ convex when
  $|z_\nu|\le\sin(\pi/8)/(1+\sin(\pi/8))\approx0.277$. Theorem 13
  (p. 110, quoted): "If one of the conditions (a) $|z_\nu|\le0.320$,
  (b) $|z_\nu|\le0.424$ and $z_0=0$ is satisfied, then $E$ is convex."
  Proof (pp. 110--111) by the area theorem and a convexity criterion for
  the level curve of the exterior map (Hilfssatz 4b of [13]). Then
  (pp. 111--112), for $f(z)=\prod_{k=1}^m(z-z_k)^{p_k}$ with distinct
  $z_k$ and positive integer exponents $p_k$, and $E=\{|f(z)|\le1\}$ with
  the maximal number $m$ of components, the paper recalls that "H. Grunsky
  (see [2, Problem 16]) raised the question whether all components must be
  convex" and announces a counterexample. Theorem 14 (p. 112, quoted):
  "Let $f(z)=z^p(z-a)$. If $a-(1+p^{-1})\cdot p^{1/(p+1)}$ is positive and
  sufficiently small,
  and $p$ is sufficiently large, then the set $E=\{|f(z)|\le1\}$ has two
  components, one of which is not convex." Proof: with
  $\xi=p^{1/(p+1)}$ and $f_p(z)=z^p(z-(1+p^{-1})\xi)$, $f_p(\xi)=-1$ and
  $f_p'(\xi)=0$, so $\xi$ is a double point of $\{|f_p|=1\}$ whose two
  branches have tangents $y=\pm(x-\xi)$, and near $\xi$ the set
  $E_p=\{|f_p|\le1\}$ lies in $S=\{x+iy:|y|\le1.1|\xi-x|\}$; the point
  $z^*=0.5+0.6i$ has $f_p(z^*)\to0$, so $z^*\in E_p$ for large $p$, while
  $z^*\notin S$ since $\xi\to1$; the segment from $z^*$ to $\xi$ leaves
  $E_p$, and increasing $a$ slightly separates the two components and
  keeps the component of $0$ nonconvex. Then (p. 112) the paper turns to
  Problem 13 of Erdös, Herzog and Piranian [2], quoted: "Let $z_\nu$ be
  $n$ complex numbers which satisfy $|z_\mu-z_\nu|\le2$
  ($\mu,\nu=1,\cdots,n$). Is $\prod_{\nu=1}\prod_{\mu\ne\nu}|z_\mu-z_\nu|$
  maximal if the $z_\nu$ are the vertices of a regular $n$-gon of diameter
  2?" It writes $\Delta_n$ for the maximum, display (10)
  $\Delta_n=\max_{|z_\mu-z_\nu|\le2}\prod_{\nu=1}^n\prod_{\mu\ne\nu}|z_\mu-z_\nu|$,
  and notes that the conjecture would give $\Delta_n=n^n$ for even $n$ and
  $\Delta_n=n^n(\cos\pi/2n)^{-n(n-1)}$ for odd $n$, the latter equal to
  $n^n(1-\pi^2/8n^2+\cdots)^{-n(n-1)}\sim n^ne^{\pi^2/8}$. Lemma 6
  (pp. 112--113): for a convex continuum $K$ of capacity 1 there are
  monic polynomials $f_n$ of every degree with zeros in $K$ and
  $\max_K|f_n|\le4$ (the zeros are the images of the $n$th roots of unity
  under the exterior map, and a starlike univalent function bounds the
  product). Theorem 15 (p. 113, quoted): "Let $K$ be a convex continuum
  of capacity 1. Then, for $z_\nu\in K$ ($\nu=1,\cdots,n$),
  $\prod_{\nu=1}^n\prod_{\mu\ne\nu}|z_\mu-z_\nu|\le2^{4(n-1)}n^n$." Proof
  (pp. 113--114): the product is the squared Vandermonde determinant,
  whose columns may be replaced by $1,f_1(z_j),\ldots,f_{n-1}(z_j)$;
  Hadamard's determinant theorem gives
  $n^n\prod_k\max_K|f_k|^2$ (the paper attributes this inequality to
  Szegö, citing footnote 7 on p. 236 of [3]), and Lemma 6 bounds each
  factor by 16.
  Theorem 16 (p. 114, quoted): "If $\Delta_n$ is defined by (10), then
  $\Delta_n\le2^{4(n-1)}\cdot n^n$." Proof: for $K$ the convex hull of a
  maximal system, Theorem 15 scaled gives
  (12) $\Delta_n\le2^{4(n-1)}n^n(\operatorname{cap}K)^{n(n-1)}$, and
  $2\operatorname{cap}K\le\operatorname{diam}K\le2$. Remark
  (pp. 114--115), "in favor of the conjecture", quoted: "The convex hull
  $K_n$ of a maximal system $\{z_n^{(n)},\cdots,z_n^{(n)}\}$ [sic] is nearly a
  disk, for large $n$" (Remark, p. 114), since a subsequence of hulls
  converging to a non-disk convex set $K_0$ of diameter at most 2
  would have $\operatorname{cap}K_{n_k}\le1-\delta<1$, and (12) would
  give $\Delta_{n_k}<1$, against $\Delta_{n_k}\ge n_k^{n_k}$.

## Compiled scope

The paper is compiled at statement depth for the results the citing
problems consume, each with a result page: Theorem 1 (p. 98), the
$z^n-r^n$ example (p. 98), Theorem 3 (p. 99), Theorem 4 (p. 101), the
projection example with Theorem 7 (p. 103), Theorem 9 (p. 104), Theorem 10
(pp. 106--107), Theorem 14 (p. 112) and Theorems 15--16 (pp. 113--114).
Theorems 2, 5, 6, 8, 11, 12 and 13 and Lemmas 1--6 are recorded above as
statements read on the page images. The short proofs named in the read
status were followed; no proof was checked line by line, and nothing here
is independently reviewed.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/analysis/E1043/_index|#1043]]: the passage on p. 103,
"By applying the approximation theorem to the "5-Stern" [10, p. 73] we
obtain a lemniscate domain with $b>2.386$ (compare [2, Problem 10a])",
where $b$ is "the minimum of the measures of the projections of $E$", is
the negative answer to the problem's question (a line onto which
$\{|f|\le1\}$ projects to measure at most 2): for that polynomial every
projection has measure above $2.386$. The construction rests on the
approximation theorem and on the 5-Stern of the author's [10, p. 73], not
held, and not on the 1959 note [9], which the paper cites only for
Problem 10b (Theorem 2, p. 98) and for the width and diameter bounds of
p. 109. Theorem 7 (p. 103) bounds the other side, some projection of any
capacity-1 set has measure below $3.30$, and Theorem 6 (p. 102) gives
$d\le4\cdot2^{-1/n}$ for the projection onto any fixed line.
[[../wiki/problems/analysis/E1045/_index|#1045]]: display (10) on p. 112 is the problem's
ordered product $\prod_{i\ne j}|z_i-z_j|$ under the same diameter
constraint, and Theorem 16 (p. 114), "$\Delta_n\le2^{4(n-1)}\cdot n^n$",
is a general upper bound, from Theorem 15 (p. 113) for points in a convex
continuum of capacity 1; the remark on pp. 114--115 shows that for large
$n$ the convex hull of any maximizing configuration is close to a disk.
The paper does not determine $\Delta_n$ or decide the regular-polygon
question; the values it records for the conjecture are $n^n$ for even $n$
and $n^n(\cos\pi/2n)^{-n(n-1)}$ for odd $n$, the even value later refuted
for every even $n\ge4$ by
[[analysis/danzer_pommerenke_1967_ber_die_diskriminante_von_mengen_gegebenen_durchmessers/_index|Danzer and Pommerenke (1967)]].
[[../wiki/problems/analysis/E1047/_index|#1047]]: Theorem 14 (p. 112), quoted above,
the set $E=\{|f(z)|\le1\}$ of $f(z)=z^p(z-a)$ with two components, one of
which is not convex, answers Grunsky's question, the problem's, in the
negative with $m=2$ distinct roots at $c=1$ for the closed sublevel set the
problem uses;
the counterexample of high degree with a multiple root that
[[analysis/goodman_1966_convexity_level_curves_polynomial/_index|Goodman (1966)]]
attributes to the author, before his quartic with simple roots.
[[../wiki/problems/analysis/E1048/_index|#1048]]: the example on p. 98, $f(z)=z^n-r^n$
with $1<r<2$, whose $n$ components have common diameter tending to $0$,
is the negative answer for $1<r<2$ ("The answer is negative for $r>1$"),
and Theorem 3 (p. 99) with its Remark 3 is the affirmative answer for
$0<r\le1$ for the closed set, which carries over to the open set for
$0<r\le1/2$ and is left undecided by the theorem for $1/2<r\le1$; the
problem is posed for the open set $\{|f|<1\}$ and a strict inequality, and
the example transfers since the open set's components lie inside the
closed set's.
[[../wiki/problems/polynomials/E0114/_index|#114]]: Theorem 9 (p. 104), "$\Lambda<74n^2$"
for the length of $\{|f(z)|=1\}$, is an upper bound only; the paper records
that an affirmative answer to Problem 12a of [2], the problem's question,
"would imply that $\Lambda\le2n+o(n)$", and decides nothing about it.
[[../wiki/problems/polynomials/E0116/_index|#116]]: Theorem 4 (p. 101), a disk of radius
$(2e)^{-1}n^{-2}$ inside $E$ when $|z_\nu|\le1$, gives
$|\{|f|<1\}|\ge\pi(2e)^{-2}n^{-4}$, the polynomial lower bound the problem
asks for (the open disk of that radius lies in the interior of $E$, which
is the open set); the paper says nothing about a $(\log n)^{-O(1)}$ bound.
[[../wiki/problems/polynomials/E0509/_index|#509]]: the paper states no result on covering
$E$ by disks with radii summing to at most 2. Its connected case is the
proof of Theorem 10(b) (p. 107): for a connected $E$ with the centroid of
the zeros at $0$, "$E$ is contained in $|z|\le2$", one disk of radius 2
about the centroid; Theorem 2 (p. 98) and Theorem 6 (p. 102) bound related
quantities.
[[../wiki/problems/analysis/E0511/_index|#511]]: Theorem 1 (p. 98), at least $k$
components of diameter at least $l$ for every $l<4$ and every $k$, is the
negative answer to the problem's question (boundedly many components of
diameter above a fixed $c>1$, independent of the degree), stated by the
paper as the negative answer to Problems 8 and 9 of [2]; the problem is
posed for the open set, and the approximation theorem puts the segments in
the interior of $E$, which is that open set. The 2025 rediscovery is filed
as
[[analysis/huang_2025_many_lemniscates_large_diameter/_index|huang_2025_many_lemniscates_large_diameter]].
[[../wiki/problems/analysis/E1038/_index|#1038]]: the paper states no result on the
infimum or the supremum of $|\{x:|f(x)|<1\}|$ for real zeros in $[-1,1]$;
Theorem 5 (p. 101) gives a real segment of length $1/8e^2n^4$ in $E$ for
real zeros in the wider interval $[-2,2]$, with the printed-constant
observation above, and the proof of Theorem 10(a) (p. 107) records that
for zeros in $[-1,1]$ with centroid $0$ both $[-1,0]$ and $[0,1]$ lie in
$E$ by [2, Theorem 1].
[[../wiki/problems/polynomials/E1039/_index|#1039]]: Theorem 4 (p. 101) is the bound
$\rho\ge(2e)^{-1}n^{-2}$ on the problem's $\rho(f)$, introduced (p. 101)
as "a weaker estimate" than the $\rho\ge\mathrm{const}\cdot n^{-1}$ of
[2, Problem 3], the problem's question, which the paper leaves open.

**Results.**

- [[analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
  (p. 98): for each $0<l<4$ and each $k$, a monic polynomial whose $E$ has
  at least $k$ components of diameter at least $l$.
- [[analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials/example_p98|Example, p. 98]]:
  $f(z)=z^n-r^n$, $1<r<2$: $n$ components of common diameter tending to
  $0$; no component of diameter at least $2-r$ for large $n$.

  (p. 99): for $|z_\nu|\le r\le1$ the component of $0$ has diameter
  $\ge2$, $>1/r$ or $>2-r^2$ in three ranges, in every case at least $2-r$.
- [[analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials/theorem_4|Theorem 4]]
  (p. 101): for $|z_\nu|\le1$, $E$ contains a disk of radius
  $(2e)^{-1}n^{-2}$.
- [[analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials/example_p103|Example, p. 103]]:
  a lemniscate set with every projection of measure above $2.386$; with
  Theorem 7, some projection of any capacity-1 set has measure below
  $3.30$.
- [[analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials/theorem_9|Theorem 9]]
  (p. 104): the length of $\{|f(z)|=1\}$ is less than $74n^2$.
- [[analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials/theorem_10|Theorem 10]]

  $|z|\le\sqrt2/2$ or in $[-1,1]$; a connected $E$ lies in $|z|\le2$, with
  $|z_\nu|<2$ and $\sigma<\sqrt2$.
- [[analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials/theorem_14|Theorem 14]]
  (p. 112): $z^p(z-a)$ with two components, one not convex.
- [[analysis/pommerenke_1961_metric_properties_complex_polynomials/theorem_16|Theorem 16]]
  (p. 114): $\Delta_n\le2^{4(n-1)}n^n$, from Theorem 15 (p. 113) for
  points in a convex continuum of capacity 1.
