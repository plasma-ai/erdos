# Planar Sidonicity and quasi-independence for multiplicative subgroups of the roots of unity

[[analysis/_index|..]]

[[analysis/ramsey_graham_2006_planar_sidonicity_quasi_independence_multiplicative_subgroups_roots_unity/corollary_2_11|corollary_2_11]]: If a prime p_s divides n exactly once and a set of n-th roots of unity
misses the subgroup H of index p_s, the set is (quasi-)independent exactly
when its intersection with each nonzero coset of H is.

[[analysis/ramsey_graham_2006_planar_sidonicity_quasi_independence_multiplicative_subgroups_roots_unity/definition_1_1|definition_1_1]]: Ramsey and Graham's vocabulary: a relation on the n-th roots of unity is an
integer-valued function in the kernel of the evaluation map, an N-relation
has values in [-N, N], and a set is quasi-independent exactly when it
supports no nonzero relation with values in {0, ±1}.

[[analysis/ramsey_graham_2006_planar_sidonicity_quasi_independence_multiplicative_subgroups_roots_unity/example_7_3|example_7_3]]: Ramsey and Graham list, in seven layers, a set of 52 of the 105th roots of
unity that two computer programs found quasi-independent, so that
Ψ(105) ≥ 52 = φ(105) + 4.

[[analysis/ramsey_graham_2006_planar_sidonicity_quasi_independence_multiplicative_subgroups_roots_unity/proposition_1_2|proposition_1_2]]: For every n ≥ 2 the largest independent subset of the n-th roots of unity,
in the additive group of the complex numbers, has exactly φ(n) elements,
and the roots e^(2πik/n) with 0 ≤ k < φ(n) form one.

[[analysis/ramsey_graham_2006_planar_sidonicity_quasi_independence_multiplicative_subgroups_roots_unity/proposition_7_5|proposition_7_5]]: For a product n of three distinct odd primes, the n-th roots of unity are a
union of two independent sets except when n is 105, 165 or 195; those three
need three independent sets, while 165 and 195 are unions of two
quasi-independent sets by a computer-found cover and 105 is not.

[[analysis/ramsey_graham_2006_planar_sidonicity_quasi_independence_multiplicative_subgroups_roots_unity/theorem_1_4|theorem_1_4]]: For a set P of primes, the roots of unity whose orders are products of
primes from P form a finite union of independent sets in the complex
numbers exactly when the sum of 1/p over P converges; then the least
number of sets needed is the ceiling of the product of p/(p - 1), and the
set is Sidon.

[[analysis/ramsey_graham_2006_planar_sidonicity_quasi_independence_multiplicative_subgroups_roots_unity/theorem_2_12|theorem_2_12]]: A set of n-th roots of unity is (quasi-)independent exactly when its
intersection with each multiplicative coset of the group of ñ-th roots of
unity is, where ñ is the product of the distinct primes dividing n.

[[analysis/ramsey_graham_2006_planar_sidonicity_quasi_independence_multiplicative_subgroups_roots_unity/theorem_3_1|theorem_3_1]]: A set of roots of unity is Sidon in the complex numbers exactly when one
integer k gives every finite part of the set lying in one coset of the
n-th roots of unity, n > 1 square-free, a quasi-independent subset of at
least 1/k of its size.

[[analysis/ramsey_graham_2006_planar_sidonicity_quasi_independence_multiplicative_subgroups_roots_unity/theorem_4_1|theorem_4_1]]: Four rules for Ψ(n): Ψ(pn) = pΨ(n) when the prime p divides n,
Ψ(p^k) = φ(p^k), Ψ(2n) = Ψ(n) for odd n, and Ψ(pn) ≥ (p - 1)Ψ(n) when
the prime p does not divide n.

[[analysis/ramsey_graham_2006_planar_sidonicity_quasi_independence_multiplicative_subgroups_roots_unity/theorem_5_1|theorem_5_1]]: For square-free n = r1·r2 and E inside H1 × H2, if in every row H1 × {c}
either the point (a, c) lies in E or adding it to the row's part of E
destroys quasi-independence, then E is not quasi-independent.

[[analysis/ramsey_graham_2006_planar_sidonicity_quasi_independence_multiplicative_subgroups_roots_unity/theorem_5_5|theorem_5_5]]: When the prime p_j divides n exactly once, β(E) is at most the least β of
the nonzero layers, and β(E) < N ≤ M exactly when some nonzero function
on the zeroth layer with values in [-N, N] gives a set of spikes that
every nonzero layer can complete to a function supported on its part of
E with values in [-N, N].

[[analysis/ramsey_graham_2006_planar_sidonicity_quasi_independence_multiplicative_subgroups_roots_unity/theorem_6_3|theorem_6_3]]: If n has exactly two distinct prime factors, a largest quasi-independent
subset of the n-th roots of unity has φ(n) elements, no more than a
largest independent one.

[[analysis/ramsey_graham_2006_planar_sidonicity_quasi_independence_multiplicative_subgroups_roots_unity/theorem_6_4|theorem_6_4]]: For distinct odd primes p and q, a set of pq-th roots of unity is
quasi-independent exactly when its intersections with all cosets of Z_p
and of Z_q are, and no nonempty set of points in one coset has its spikes
shadowed by the set; such a set is then independent.

[[analysis/ramsey_graham_2006_planar_sidonicity_quasi_independence_multiplicative_subgroups_roots_unity/theorem_7_4|theorem_7_4]]: For every prime p ≥ 7, a largest quasi-independent subset of the 15p-th
roots of unity has exactly φ(15p) + 4 elements; the lower bound rests on
the computer-verified 52-element example for n = 105.

## Research digest

The paper asks which sets of roots of unity are Sidon, and which are
quasi-independent, as subsets of the discrete additive group $\mathbb C$
(p. 326). It writes the $n$-th roots of unity as the product of the cyclic
groups of the prime-power factors of $n$ and studies relations
coordinate direction by coordinate direction.

## Useful machinery

- The square-free theorem (Theorem 2.12) reduces (quasi-)independence to
  intersections with cosets of $T_{\tilde n}$, $\tilde n$ the product of the
  distinct primes dividing $n$. Theorem 3.1 similarly reduces the Pisier
  proportional-extraction test to finite subsets of cosets of $T_n$ with $n>1$
  square-free.
- The spike and shadow results of Section 5 (Theorems 5.1 and 5.5) analyze a
  set one prime-coordinate direction at a time. The empty-floor criterion
  (Corollary 2.11) glues sets layer by layer when one layer is empty; Theorem
  5.5 handles a nonempty zeroth layer through sets of spikes that the other
  layers shadow or block.
- The paper determines or bounds $\Psi(n)$, the largest quasi-independent
  subset of $T_n$, in several families: $\phi(n)\le\Psi(n)<n$, rules for
  prime powers and for multiplying by a prime (Theorem 4.1), $\Psi(n)=\phi(n)$
  for two prime factors (Theorem 6.3) and $\Psi(15p)=\phi(15p)+4$ for primes
  $p\ge7$ (Theorem 7.4). Its explicit configurations, a 52-element
  quasi-independent subset of $T_{105}$ (Example 7.3) and covers of
  $T_{165}$ and $T_{195}$ by two quasi-independent sets (Appendix), are
  computer-verified finite test cases for any proposed coloring, rank, or
  circuit argument.
- The paper notes (p. 327) that $\inf_n\Psi(n)/n>0$ "suggests" that the
  set of all roots of unity is Sidon, while $\sup_n\Psi(n)/\phi(n)<\infty$
  would prove that it is not; it leaves the question open.

## Relation to E0774

The paper supplies a structured finite model in which both extraction density
and covering by quasi-independent classes can be studied. Quasi-independence
of a set of complex numbers is the problem's dissociation (distinct subset
sums), transplanted from the natural numbers to the roots of unity. The paper
does not produce unbounded quasi-independent covering numbers with a uniform
extraction constant, and it does not mention Erdős or study sets of natural numbers. Its
additive relations are in $\mathbb C$, not in the cyclic exponent group, so
a transfer to integer dissociation still needs a carry-free or
relation-preserving embedding.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/integer_sequences/E0774/_index|E0774]]: the
result pages above concern the roots-of-unity analogue of the problem, in
which quasi-independence in $\mathbb C$ plays the part of dissociation;
Theorem 3.1 localizes the problem's proportional-extraction hypothesis for
roots of unity, Theorem 1.4 and Proposition 7.5 give finite covers by
independent or quasi-independent sets in special cases, and Theorems 4.1,
6.3 and 7.4 determine or bound $\Psi(n)$, the size of a largest
quasi-independent subset of $T_n$, which measures extraction density there. None of them concerns
sets of natural numbers, and none settles the problem in either direction.
