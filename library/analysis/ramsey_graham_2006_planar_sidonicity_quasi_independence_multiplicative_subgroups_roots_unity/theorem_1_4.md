**Source.** Theorem 1.4, stated on p. 327 and again on p. 331 with its proof
on pp. 331--332, of L. Thomas Ramsey and Colin C. Graham, *Planar Sidonicity and
quasi-independence for multiplicative subgroups of the roots of unity*,
Pacific J. Math. 225 (2006), no. 2, 325--360, doi:10.2140/pjm.2006.225.325;
see the [[analysis/ramsey_graham_2006_planar_sidonicity_quasi_independence_multiplicative_subgroups_roots_unity/_index|source card]].

## Statement

Independence is in the additive group $\mathbb C$
([[analysis/ramsey_graham_2006_planar_sidonicity_quasi_independence_multiplicative_subgroups_roots_unity/definition_1_1|Definition 1.1]]).

**Theorem 1.4** (p. 331, quoted). "For any set of positive primes $P$, let
$W$ be the be [sic] multiplicative subset of $\mathbb Z$ generated by $P$.
Then

$$
E=\{e^{i2\pi a/m}:a\in\mathbb Z\text{ and }m\in W\}
$$

is a finite union of independent sets if and only if
$\sum_{p\in P}1/p<\infty$, in which case

$$
M=\Bigl\lceil\prod_{p\in P}\frac{p}{p-1}\Bigr\rceil
$$

independent sets will suffice (and no fewer) and $E$ is Sidon in
$\mathbb C$."

Here "Sidon in $\mathbb C$" means a Sidon subset of the discrete additive
group $\mathbb C$ (p. 325). Since $\sum_p1/p$ diverges over all primes, the
full set $e^{2\pi i\mathbb Q}$ of roots of unity is not a finite union of
independent sets (p. 327).

**Read depth.** Claims checked: statement and proof read clause by clause on
pp. 327 and 331--332. Nothing here is independently reviewed.

## Proof pointer

Pp. 331--332. Lemma 1.5 (p. 331) covers $T_n$ by $\lceil n/\phi(n)\rceil$
rotated copies of the independent block of
[[analysis/ramsey_graham_2006_planar_sidonicity_quasi_independence_multiplicative_subgroups_roots_unity/proposition_1_2|Proposition 1.2]], and Remark 1.6 notes that fewer sets
cannot cover $T_n$. With $r_n=(p_1\cdots p_n)^n$, convergence of
$\sum1/p$ bounds $\lceil r_n/\phi(r_n)\rceil$ by $M$, and a diagonal
subsequence argument passes the covers of the $T_{r_n}$ to a cover of $E$
by $M$ independent sets; a finite union of independent sets is Sidon. If
$\sum1/p$ diverges, $r_n/\phi(r_n)\to\infty$.
