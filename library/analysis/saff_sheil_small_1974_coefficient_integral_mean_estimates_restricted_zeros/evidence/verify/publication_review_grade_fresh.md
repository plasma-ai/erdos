## Rederivations by the grader

The grader rederived the following steps from the frozen extraction and the
source renders before reading the record's versions closely, then compared.

1. **Zero transfer and exact degree** (`theorem_2.md` lines 67--95). With
   $P_{2n}(z)=\sum_{k=-n}^nb_kz^{k+n}$ one has
   $T_n(\theta)=e^{-in\theta}P_{2n}(e^{i\theta})$ for every complex $\theta$.
   The factor $e^{-in\theta}$ never vanishes and $\theta\mapsto e^{i\theta}$
   is a local biholomorphism, injective on $[0,2\pi)$, so each real zero of
   $T_n$ of multiplicity $m$ is a zero of $P_{2n}$ of multiplicity exactly
   $m$ on the circle, at distinct points for distinct $\theta$. $T_n$ has
   finitely many zeros in a period, so $T_n\not\equiv0$ and $P_{2n}\neq0$. A
   nonzero polynomial of degree at most $2n$ with at least $2n$ zeros counted
   with multiplicity has degree exactly $2n$ and no further zero; hence
   $b_n\neq0$, $b_{-n}=P_{2n}(0)\neq0$, and every zero of $P_{2n}$ lies on
   the circle. Since $|e^{-in\theta}|=1$ for real $\theta$, the maxima and
   the $q$-th power integrals of $T_n$ and $P_{2n}(e^{i\theta})$ coincide, so
   Theorem 1 at degree $2n\geq2$ gives (8). Agrees with the record's step 1.
2. **Equality transfer** (lines 97--120). From
   $P_{2n}=\tfrac M2(e^{i\alpha}z^{2n}+e^{i\beta})$,
   $T_n(\theta)=\tfrac M2(e^{i(\alpha+n\theta)}+e^{i(\beta-n\theta)})
   =\tfrac M2e^{i(\alpha+\beta)/2}\bigl(e^{i(n\theta+(\alpha-\beta)/2)}
   +e^{-i(n\theta+(\alpha-\beta)/2)}\bigr)
   =Me^{i(\alpha+\beta)/2}\cos(n\theta+(\alpha-\beta)/2)$. Conversely
   $Me^{i\phi}\cos(n\theta+\tau)$ vanishes exactly at
   $n\theta+\tau\in\pi/2+\pi\mathbb Z$, which is $2n$ simple real zeros per
   period, so it is admissible; its $P_{2n}$ is
   $\tfrac M2(e^{i(\phi+\tau)}z^{2n}+e^{i(\phi-\tau)})$ with unimodular
   coefficients and maximum $M$, an equality case of Theorem 1. The clause
   is two-sided. Agrees with the record's step 2.
3. **Theorem 1's equality clause** (`theorem_1.md` lines 209--267).
   Equality in (4) forces equality in
   $\int|P|^q\leq(M/2)^q\int|1+w|^q$; the integrand difference is continuous
   and nonnegative on the circle, so it vanishes identically, giving
   $|P'|=Mn/2$ wherever $1+w\neq0$. Since $w$ is a finite Blaschke product
   times $z$, $1+w$ is analytic across the circle and equals $1$ at $0$, so
   it has finitely many zeros on the circle, and continuity of $|P'|$ extends
   the identity to every $\theta$. With $R=P'$ of exact degree $d=n-1$
   (leading coefficient $na_n\neq0$) and $R^*(z)=z^d\overline{R(1/\bar z)}$,
   on the circle $R^*=z^d\overline R$, so $RR^*=(Mn/2)^2z^d$ there and hence
   as polynomials; the right side vanishes only at $0$, so $R=cz^d$ with
   $|c|=Mn/2$ (evaluate at $z=1$). Integrating,
   $P=\tfrac M2(\lambda z^n+\mu)$ with $|\lambda|=1$, and the zeros solve
   $z^n=-\mu/\lambda$; $n\geq1$ gives at least one zero, on the circle, so
   $|\mu|=1$. Agrees with the record's step 3.
4. **Subordination for every $q>0$ and the boundary passage**
   (`external_inputs.md` lines 62--81). For $G$ analytic on the disk,
   $|G|^q=\exp(q\log|G|)$ is subharmonic for every $q>0$. Fix $r<1$ and let
   $U$ be the Poisson integral of $|G(re^{it})|^q$ over $|z|<r$, harmonic
   there, continuous on $|z|\leq r$, with $|G|^q\leq U$. For $\omega$
   analytic with $\omega(0)=0$ and $|\omega(z)|\leq|z|$ (Schwarz), $\omega$
   maps $|z|<r$ into itself, $U\circ\omega$ is harmonic there, and
   $|G\circ\omega|^q\leq U\circ\omega$; the mean of the harmonic function
   over $|z|=\rho<r$ is $U(\omega(0))=U(0)$, the mean of $|G|^q$ over
   $|z|=r$; let $\rho\uparrow r$ by continuity. With $G=1+z$ and
   $\omega=w$, which is continuous on the closed disk, both integrands
   converge uniformly as $r\uparrow1$, giving the boundary form (S). Agrees
   with the record; the record's derivation is correct as written.
5. **The constant.** $|1+e^{i\theta}|=2|\cos(\theta/2)|$, so
   $A_q=2^q\int_0^{2\pi}|\cos(\theta/2)|^q\,d\theta
   =2^{q+2}\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^qx\,dx$, and $t=\sin^2x$ gives
   $\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos^qx\,dx=\tfrac12B(\tfrac12,\tfrac{q+1}2)$, hence
   $A_q=2^{q+1}\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma((q+1)/2)/\Gamma(q/2+1)$; $A_1=8$,
   $A_2=4\pi$. A throwaway double-precision quadrature at
   $q\in\{1/2,1,2,3\}$ agreed to nine digits, $\int_0^{2\pi}|M\cos(3\theta
   +\tau)|\,d\theta$ returned $4M$, and a degree-$4$ admissible
   non-extremal example returned $\int|T|/M\approx2.02<4$. This check is the
   grader's sanity check, not evidence, and is not retained.
6. **The witnesses.** For $n=0$, $\int_0^{2\pi}|c|^q=2\pi M^q$ while
   $A_q(M/2)^q=M^q\cdot2\int_0^\pi|\cos x|^q\,dx<2\pi M^q$ for every $q>0$;
   $T=e^{in\theta}$ has no real zero and integral $2\pi>4$; $f=e^{in\theta}$
   has $P=z^n$ with all roots at $0$ and integral $2\pi>4$. Each is excluded
   by a clause written on the frozen pages ($n\geq1$; the $2n$ count; the
   full-root reading), as the record says.
7. **The transfer and the record's remark.** Theorem 1 at $q=1$, $M=1$,
   degree $n\geq1$, all $n$ roots on the circle, gives $\int|f|\leq A_1/2=4$.
   The record's remark that Theorem 2 also reaches the display is correct:
   $f(2\psi)=e^{in\psi}T(\psi)$ with $T(\psi)=\sum_kc_ke^{i(2k-n)\psi}$ of
   degree $n$ ($c_n\neq0$, and $c_0\neq0$ since no root is $0$), whose zeros
   in $[0,2\pi)$ are $\theta_j/2$ and $\theta_j/2+\pi$, $2n$ in all, and
   $\int_0^{2\pi}|T(\psi)|\,d\psi=\tfrac12\int_0^{4\pi}|f|=\int_0^{2\pi}|f|$.
