**Source.** Dartyge--Maynard, arXiv:2212.03381v1, 7 December 2022,
Theorem 1.1 on
[physical and numbered p. 3](dartyge_maynard_2025_largest_prime_factor_quartic_polynomial_values_cyclic_dihedral.pdf#page=3).
The selected PDF is the preprint; its theorem text has not been compared
with the journal version of record.

**Statement.** Fix a monic irreducible quartic polynomial
$P\in\mathbb Z[X]$ whose Galois group over $\mathbb Q$ is isomorphic to
$C_4=\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$ or to the dihedral group $D_4$ of order eight.
There are constants $c_P>0$, $b_P>0$, and $x_0(P)$ such that for every
$x>x_0(P)$,

$$
\#\{n\in\mathbb Z:x<n\leq2x,\ P^+(P(n))\geq x^{1+c_P}\}
\geq b_Px.
$$

The source writes the lower bound as $\gg x$ with $P$ fixed; $b_P$
exposes that implicit constant. No common positive exponent for all
polynomials is asserted. We read $P^+(a)$ on $|a|$ and set $P^+(1)=1$.
Irreducibility in degree four excludes integer roots, so the polynomial
values in the products below are nonzero.

**Proof pointer.** Section 1.1, pp. 4--6, first reduces the question to a
positive proportion of values with a sufficiently large friable part.
It then uses principal ideals in the field generated by a root of $P$
and the $q$-analog of van der Corput's method, reducing the task to
suitable factorizations of a ternary sextic form $q$. For $C_4$ or $D_4$,
the form is a product of a quadratic and a quartic incomplete norm form.
The final step adapts Type II estimates to count the required
factorizations with additional congruence conditions.

The paper makes this reduction via Lemma 3.1 on p. 7 followed by
Propositions 3.2 and 3.3 on p. 8. With the auxiliary parameter $\theta_0$
sufficiently small and the ideal set chosen in Section 6, these give the
main-term bound $S_0\gg1$ and the error bound $S_1=o(X)$.
The paper assigns their proofs to Sections 8 and 7, respectively.
The main-term argument uses Theorem 4.1 on p. 10, with the setup on
pp. 8--9, for localized factors of incomplete norm forms. Section 4.1 on
p. 11 identifies the relevant algebraic structure and points to Lemmas
5.9--5.10 and Proposition 5.11. These locators describe the route; those
later proofs are not reconstructed here.

The external inputs identified in this part of the source include
Heath-Brown's *The largest prime factor of $x^3+2$*, Lemma 2, and de la
Bretèche's *Plus grand facteur premier de valeurs de polynômes aux
entiers*, Lemme 4.1, for the initial reduction (source references [8] and
[1]); the Rosser--Iwaniec lower-bound sieve (references [11]--[12]); and
methods from Maynard's work on primes represented by incomplete norm
forms (reference [14]). Their proofs are not included or independently
checked here. Theorem 4.1 is a same-paper adaptation, not an unqualified
application of Maynard's theorem.

**Elementary initial-product transfer.** This is a compilation consequence
of Theorem 1.1, not its printed conclusion. Define, for a positive integer
$N$,

$$
F_P(N)=P^+\!\left(\prod_{m=1}^{N}P(m)\right).
$$

For every sufficiently large $N$, apply the theorem with $x=N/2$.
The positive lower bound for the count gives an integer $n$ with
$N/2<n\leq N$ and a prime divisor of $P(n)$ at least $(N/2)^{1+c_P}$.
Since $P(n)$ is a factor of the initial product, that prime divides the
product too. Thus

$$
F_P(N)\geq 2^{-(1+c_P)}N^{1+c_P},
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
F_P(N)\gg_P N^{1+c_P}.
$$

**Relation to E976.** This is an unconditional fixed-power result for the
stated quartic subclass. It does not establish the bound for every
irreducible quartic or every irreducible integer polynomial, and it does
not give $F_P(N)\gg_P N^4$.
