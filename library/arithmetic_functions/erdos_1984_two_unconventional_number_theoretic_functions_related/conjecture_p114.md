**Source.** Displays (5) and (6), p. 114, of P. Erdős, *On two unconventional
number theoretic functions and on some related problems*, Calcutta
Mathematical Society, Diamond-cum-platinum jubilee commemoration volume
(1908--1983), Part I, pp. 113--121, Calcutta Math. Soc., Calcutta, 1984
(MR 87k:11007), the edition named on the
[[arithmetic_functions/erdos_1984_two_unconventional_number_theoretic_functions_related/_index|source card]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: both displays and the sentences around them
were read clause by clause on the page image of p. 114, and the remark on
p. 117 on that of p. 117. Nothing here is independently reviewed.

## Statement

Setting (pp. 113--114). With $f$ and $F$ as on
[[arithmetic_functions/erdos_1984_two_unconventional_number_theoretic_functions_related/conjecture_p113|the conjectures (1) and (2) page]],

$$
m(x)=\max_{n<x}f(n),\qquad M(x)=\max_{n<x}F(n).
$$

**Conjecture (5).** Erdős writes "I conjecture but can not prove that"

$$
m(x)=(1+o(1))\frac{x\log x}{\log\log x}, \tag{5}
$$

and calls (5) much stronger than (4), the upper-limit statement proved as
[[arithmetic_functions/erdos_1984_two_unconventional_number_theoretic_functions_related/theorem_4|Theorem 4]].
On p. 117 he says again that he could not prove (5).

**Question (6).** He writes that he "can not decide whether for infinitely many
$x$"

$$
m(x)=M(x), \tag{6}
$$

and that perhaps (6) holds for every sufficiently large $x$, or even for all
$x$.

## Proof pointer

None; these are open statements in the paper.

## Dependencies

None in the corpus.

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/arithmetic_functions/E0878/_index|Problem 878]]: the
  second question, whether $\max_{n\le x}f(n)\sim x\log x/\log\log x$, is
  conjecture (5), with the maximum over $n\le x$ in place of $n<x$. The third
  question, whether $\max_{n\le x}f(n)=\max_{n\le x}F(n)$ for all $x$ or for
  all large $x$, takes the two stronger forms Erdős suggests after (6); his
  question (6) itself asks only for infinitely many $x$.
