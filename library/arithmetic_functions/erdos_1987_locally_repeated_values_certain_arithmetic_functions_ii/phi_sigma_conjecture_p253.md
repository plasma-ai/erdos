***

**Source.** Erdős, Pomerance, and Sárközy (1987), the paragraph following
Theorem 2 on printed p. 253
(PDF, physical p. 3).

**Statement.** For every $\varepsilon>0$, the authors conjecture that, once
$x\geq x_0(\varepsilon)$, each of

$$
\varphi(n)=\varphi(n+1)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\sigma(n)=\sigma(n+1)
$$

has at least $x^{1-\varepsilon}$ solutions $n\leq x$. They then state that
they cannot prove that either equation has infinitely many solutions.

**Locator guard.** This is the totient unknown-infinitude statement on
printed p. 253. The superficially similar sentence on printed p. 251 concerns
$\nu(n)=\nu(n+1)$ and is not a source for the totient claim.

**Proof pointer.** This is an unnumbered conjecture, so the paper supplies no
proof. The preceding proved input is [[arithmetic_functions/erdos_1987_locally_repeated_values_certain_arithmetic_functions_ii/theorem_2|Theorem 2]], whose proof is
outlined in Section 4.

**Relation to E1003.** The statement is the historical conjectural direction
behind [[../wiki/problems/arithmetic_functions/E1003/_index|Problem 1003]]. This page
transcribes the 1987 source and does not by itself certify present-day status.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/arithmetic_functions/E1003/_index|#1003]].
