## Statement

Notation: $\sigma_k(n)$ is the $k$-fold iterate of the sum-of-divisors
function $\sigma$ (p. 169).

**Statements** (p. 169, unnumbered list). The authors write that it "seems
very difficult to prove anything non-trivial" about the iterates
$\sigma_k(n)$ and list six statements:

- (i) for every $n>1$, $\sigma_{k+1}(n)/\sigma_k(n)\to1$ as $k\to\infty$;
- (ii) for every $n>1$, $\sigma_{k+1}(n)/\sigma_k(n)\to\infty$ as
  $k\to\infty$;
- (iii) for every $n>1$, $\sigma_k(n)^{1/k}\to\infty$ as $k\to\infty$;
- (iv) for every $n>1$, there is some $k$ with $n\mid\sigma_k(n)$;
- (v) for every $n$, $m>1$, there is some $k$ with $m\mid\sigma_k(n)$;
- (vi) for every $n$, $m>1$, there are some $k$, $\ell$ with
  $\sigma_k(m)=\sigma_\ell(n)$.

They add (p. 169, quoted): "We can neither prove nor disprove any of these
statements."

Statements (i) and (ii) cannot both hold.

**Source.** Paul Erdős, Andrew Granville, Carl Pomerance and Claudia Spiro,
*On the Normal Behavior of the Iterates of Some Arithmetic Functions*, in
*Analytic Number Theory: Proceedings of a Conference in Honor of Paul T.
Bateman*, Progress in Mathematics 85, Birkhäuser (1990), 165--204; the list
on p. 169. The edition is identified on the [[arithmetic_functions/erdos_1990_normal_behavior_iterates_arithmetic_functions/_index|source card]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the six statements and the authors' comment
were read clause by clause on the print (p. 169). Open statements; nothing
here is independently reviewed.

## Proof pointer

None; the paper proves none of the six and disproves none.
