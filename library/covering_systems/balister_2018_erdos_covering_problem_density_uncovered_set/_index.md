## Results and complete proof chains

For a finite family of progressions, the uncovered set is periodic modulo
the least common multiple of the moduli. Its natural density is therefore
the proportion of uncovered residues in a finite cyclic group.

- [[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]] proves the density lower bound
  $e^{-4C}/2$ for sufficiently large distinct moduli, where
  $C=\sum_d\mu(d)/d$ and
  $\mu(p^a)=1+(\log p)^{3+\varepsilon}/p$ for fixed $\varepsilon>0$.
  Its minimum-modulus threshold is uniform in the family; the density bound
  depends on the weighted sum. The complete proof uses the exact classical
  prime estimates recorded in [[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/external_inputs|External analytic inputs]].
- [[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/theorem_5_1|Theorem 5.1]] derives a positive density bound for
  moduli in $[n,Kn]$, with an absolute minimum threshold independent of
  $K$. The original interval conjecture had already been proved by
  Filaseta–Ford–Konyagin–Pomerance–Yu (2007), as the introduction records.
- [[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/theorem_1_2|Theorem 1.2, restated as Theorem 9.1]], proves
  Schinzel's conjecture for proper moduli: some pair has one modulus
  dividing the other. The full proof includes the three smooth-antichain
  lemmas and their finite case classification.
- [[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/theorem_7_1|Theorem 7.1]] first excludes a distinct-modulus cover
  coprime to six. [[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/theorem_1_4|Theorem 1.4]] strengthens this to
  $2\mid Q$, $9\mid Q$, or $15\mid Q$ for its period $Q$. The last
  alternative does not require one modulus divisible by $15$.
- [[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/theorem_8_1|Theorem 8.1]] proves that distinct moduli all at
  least $616000$ do not cover. The full rational certificate supplies
  sufficient numerical bounds for this theorem; this is the source's
  quantitative result, not a current-best assertion.
- [[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/theorem_10_1|Theorem 10.1]] constructs arbitrarily small
  uncovered density using arbitrarily large distinct square-free moduli
  with reciprocal sum below one. [[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/theorem_10_2|Theorem 10.2]] uses
  a different branching construction to obtain bounded cost even for
  $\mu(p^a)=1+\lambda/p$. Both constructions are proved completely;
  neither gives a covering.

The sieve's canonical definitions are in
[[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/sieve_construction|Prime-stage distortion measures]]. Lemmas 2.1–2.2
preserve earlier marginals and bound the change of measure; Lemmas 3.3–3.7
bound removed mass, progression probabilities, average distortion, and
moments. [[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/theorem_3_1|Theorem 3.1]] converts those estimates to
noncoverage and ordinary density. Its explicitly justified indexed-family
extension permits repeated moduli, counting multiplicity in the reciprocal
sum. The universal Euler-product bounds of [[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/theorem_3_2|Theorem 3.2]]
retain the distinct-modulus hypothesis.

The complete survival recurrence and prime-tail induction live once at
[[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/lemma_6_2|Lemma 6.2]] and [[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/theorem_6_1|Theorem 6.1]], respectively.
They also support the separate
[[covering_systems/balister_2021_erdos_selfridge_problem_square_free_moduli/_index|square-free paper]],
whose theorem is an external input at [[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/theorem_1_3|Theorem 1.3]] here.
The square-free proof is not duplicated in this source folder.

## Numerical evidence and source precision

The [portable verifier](evidence/verify_bbmst_density.py)
uses only integer and rational arithmetic, with explicitly directed
intervals. [[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/smooth_tail_sums|The finite smooth-tail reduction]] proves
that it includes all infinite prime-power and lcm sums.
[[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/numerical_bounds|The numerical record]] documents the exact legal
parameter schedule, complete $51000$-stage calculation, and finite backward
witness leading to the external Dusart termination bound. The witnessed
thresholds $1.26$, $3.007$, and $5800000$ suffice for every application here.
The stronger, near-critical digits in the printed Table 1 have not all been
reproduced, and no maximal-threshold attainment or optimality is inferred.
No local proof-assistant verification is claimed.

The full proofs make several source details explicit: zero-distortion and
zero-mass conventions; a fixed absolute prime cutoff for Theorem 1.1;
its normalized Claim 2 loss sum; induction over real cutoffs in Theorem 5.1;
proper moduli in the antichain theorem; and the $0<\lambda<1$ case of
Theorem 10.2 by domination with $\max\{1,\lambda\}$. The printed Section 8
parameter formula is implicit in its current-stage mass; the certified
explicit rational implementation gives a slightly different final bound
while proving the same $616000$ conclusion. Each clarification is explained
on the relevant page and is not presented as an author-issued erratum.

The termination proof needs no correction: both versions give
$\lambda_i-\lambda_{i-1}\ge\log(i/(i-1))>1/i$ (v1 p. 18, published p. 399), as
[[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/theorem_6_1|Theorem 6.1]] records.

The introductions' other cited theorems, historical comparisons, and
Schinzel's polynomial irreducibility implication are not independently
reconstructed here. They are not implicit prerequisites of the source
proof chains above.

## Problem relationships

For [[../wiki/problems/integer_sequences/E0025/_index|Problem 25]], the paper gives a precise
finite periodic model and strong information about how small its survivor set
can be, but it does not address the problem's delayed activation directly.
Section 2, printed pp. 382–385 (PDF pp. 6–9), equations (3)–(5), fixes a
finite set $D$ of moduli, puts every progression $A_d$ in the common cyclic
space $\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z$ for $Q=\operatorname{lcm}(D)$, and exposes the
progressions in prime-factor stages $B_i$. The order of those stages records
which prime coordinates have been processed; it is not the numerical cutoff
$n\ge d$ in Problem 25. At every integer represented in the cyclic space, all
members of the chosen finite family are fully active.

The prime-stage distortion mechanism is explicit in
[[covering_systems/balister_2018_erdos_covering_problem_density_uncovered_set/sieve_construction|Section 2]]. Lemmas 2.1–2.2, printed p. 385
(PDF p. 9), preserve every earlier-coordinate marginal while increasing any
point mass by at most $(1-\delta_i)^{-1}$ and never increasing mass on the
new forbidden set. Lemma 3.3, printed p. 387 (PDF p. 11), bounds the mass
lost at stage $i$ by the better of a first-moment and a second-moment bound.
Lemmas 3.6–3.7, printed pp. 390–392 (PDF pp. 14–16), and Theorem 3.2,
stated on printed pp. 386–387 (PDF pp. 10–11) and proved on printed
pp. 392–393 (PDF pp. 16–17), turn those fiber moments into weighted
least-common-multiple sums and uniform Euler-product bounds.
Theorem 3.1, stated on printed p. 386 (PDF p. 10), equations (9)–(10), and
proved on printed pp. 389–390 (PDF pp. 13–14), sums those losses: if their
total is $\eta<1$, then the finite family does not cover and its ordinary
uncovered density is at least

$$
(1-\eta)\exp\left(-\frac{2}{1-\eta}
                    \sum_{d\in D}\frac{\nu(d)}d\right).
$$

The return from the distorted measure to ordinary density is the weighted
mechanism most relevant here. Lemma 3.5, printed pp. 388–389
(PDF pp. 12–13), bounds the mean logarithmic distortion by
$2\sum_{d\in D_i}\nu(d)/d$. Theorem 1.1, stated on printed p. 380
(PDF p. 4) and proved in Section 4, printed pp. 393–396
(PDF pp. 17–20), takes
$\delta_p=u_p/(1+u_p)$ for large primes, where
$u_p=(\log p)^{3+\varepsilon}/p$. This makes
$\nu(d)\le\mu(d)$ for the multiplicative weight
$\mu(p^a)=1+u_p$, while the first- and second-moment losses total at most
$1/2$. Consequently a finite distinct-modulus family above a fixed threshold
has uncovered density at least $e^{-4C}/2$ when
$C=\sum_d\mu(d)/d$.

Section 10 gives complementary finite constructions. Theorem 10.1, printed
pp. 407–410 (PDF pp. 31–34), takes disjoint prime blocks and moduli
$pQ_j$, chooses each residue greedily, and obtains

$$
\varepsilon_{j+1}
 \le \varepsilon_j
 \exp\left(-\frac{\delta e^{-\delta j}}{\varepsilon_j}\right),
\qquad \varepsilon_j\le e^{-\delta j}.
$$

After deleting progressions to make the reciprocal sum strictly below one,
it still produces, for every $M$ and $\varepsilon>0$, a finite family of
distinct square-free moduli at least $M$ with positive uncovered density
less than $\varepsilon$. Theorem 10.2, stated on printed p. 408
(PDF p. 32) and proved on printed pp. 410–413 (PDF pp. 34–37), uses a
branching version. A fresh prime block at each surviving branch reduces its
conditional survivor proportion by $q=e^{2-t}$, so the total survivor
density satisfies $\rho_i\le q^i$, while the total weighted cost remains
bounded by a constant (the reconstructed proof gives $C<2t^2$) for
$\mu(p^a)=1+\lambda/p$. Thus even bounded reciprocal-type cost does not
give a uniform positive lower bound for the densities of arbitrary finite
families.

These statements do not settle Problem 25. For its increasing sequence
$n_1<n_2<\cdots$, put

$$
R_k=\mathbb Z\setminus\bigcup_{i\le k}(a_i+n_i\mathbb Z).
$$

On the moving interval $[n_k,n_{k+1})$, the problem's set is
$A\cap[n_k,n_{k+1})=R_k\cap[n_k,n_{k+1})$. The paper computes or bounds
the full-period density of each fixed $R_k$ modulo
$Q_k=\operatorname{lcm}(n_1,\ldots,n_k)$. It gives no uniform comparison
between that density and the harmonic mass of $R_k$ in the particular
moving interval $[n_k,n_{k+1})$; this interval may be much shorter than
$Q_k$ and can occupy an exceptional part of the period. Nor does it give
convergence or a summable error estimate as $k$ changes. Theorem 1.1's
bound may also decay with the prefix cost $C_k$, which is unrestricted in
Problem 25. Conversely, Theorems 10.1–10.2 choose a different finite
fully active periodic family for each target density; they do not produce
one infinite increasing sequence whose delayed-prefix harmonic averages
fail to converge. Passing from those finite densities to the logarithmic
density

$$
\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac1{\log X}
 \sum_{\substack{n\le X\\n\in A}}\frac1n
$$

therefore requires new control of the diagonal sequence of moving windows,
not merely the paper's control of each finite periodic model.

For [[../wiki/problems/covering_systems/E0273/_index|Problem 273]], the general covering
restrictions are background for the proposed moduli $p-1$ with primes $p\ge5$.
All these moduli are even, so the alternative $2\mid Q$ in Theorem 1.4 is
automatic. Neither that condition nor Schinzel's divisibility condition
constructs or excludes the required family. The connection concerns possible
necessary conditions; no resolution of Problem 273 is established here.

For [[../wiki/problems/integer_sequences/E0688/_index|Problem 688]], a positive density of
uncovered integers does not guarantee an uncovered integer in the particular
interval $[1,n]$. This distinction persists even when all moduli exceed the
threshold in Theorem 1.1. Indeed, for any finite set $P$ of distinct primes
and one forbidden residue $a_p$ for each $p\in P$, the Chinese remainder
theorem gives the exact uncovered density

$$
\prod_{p\in P}\left(1-\frac1p\right)>0.
$$

There are $\prod_{p\in P}(p-1)$ allowed residues modulo $\prod_{p\in P}p$,
regardless of the choices of the $a_p$. This elementary observation is a scope
check, not an additional theorem of this paper. It already establishes global
noncoverage for the finite prime window in Problem 688, but supplies no
location of an uncovered integer in $[1,n]$. No $\varepsilon^{4+o(1)}$ density
consequence or corresponding “$\mathbb Z$-analogue” for Problem 688 is
established here or stated as a theorem in the paper. In particular, the
minimum-modulus threshold alone does not explain the finite-window obstacle.
No estimate for the problem's $\varepsilon_n$ is extracted here.

## Source inspection

The statements, labels and pages on the result pages were checked clause by
clause against the published article; the arXiv v1 preprint is cited only
where a page names it. Where a result page's statement departs from the
print (moduli at least $2$ in Theorems 1.2, 1.4, 3.1 and 7.1, which the
print leaves implicit; square-free moduli in Theorem 10.1, which the paper
remarks on after the statement; witnessed thresholds in place of $g_k$ in
Corollary 6.3; the renamed bound in Theorem 5.1), the page gives the
printed form and says what it adds or changes. The contextual
applications to Problems 25, 273 and 688 remain distinct from the paper's
proved results, as explained above.
