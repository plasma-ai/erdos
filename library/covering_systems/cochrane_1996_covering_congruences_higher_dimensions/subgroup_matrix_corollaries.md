**Source.** The definition of an $m$-cover and the question on p. 78
(physical p. 2 of the scan), which the paper says its Theorem settles in the
affirmative: whether there is a finite $2$-cover with no element of

absolute value, equivalently whether $\mathbf Z\oplus\mathbf Z$ is a finite

and matrix-basis calculations below are written here.

Rocky Mountain J. Math. **26** (1996), no. 1, 77–81,
doi:10.1216/rmjm/1181072104; the edition read and its page mapping are named on

## Statement

The group $\mathbb Z^2$ is the union of finitely many proper subgroups whose
indices are pairwise distinct.

Equivalently, there is a finite set $\mathcal A$ of $2\times2$ integer
matrices such that every integer row vector $h$ can be written

$$
h=kA
$$

for an integer row vector $k$ and some $A\in\mathcal A$, while every
$|\det A|>1$ and the determinant magnitudes are pairwise distinct. In the
paper's terminology, $\mathcal A$ is a finite $2$-cover with neither a
unimodular matrix nor repeated absolute determinants.

## Proof

Take the homogeneous cover from the
[[covering_systems/cochrane_1996_covering_congruences_higher_dimensions/theorem|main theorem]].
For one of its triples $(a,b,m)$, define

$$
\phi:\mathbb Z^2\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z,
\qquad \phi(x,y)=ax-by\pmod m.                            \tag{1}
$$

The condition $\gcd(a,b,m)=1$ gives integers $r,s,t$ with
$ar-bs+mt=1$, so $\phi(r,s)=1$. Thus $\phi$ is onto, and its kernel

$$
H(a,b,m)=\{(x,y):ax-by\equiv0\pmod m\}                  \tag{2}
$$

is a proper subgroup of index $m$. The main theorem says that these kernels
cover $\mathbb Z^2$; their indices are distinct because their moduli are.

It remains to produce the matrices without hiding the lattice calculation.
Let $d=\gcd(a,b)>0$, put $a'=a/d$ and $b'=b/d$, and note that
$\gcd(d,m)=1$. Hence (2) is equivalent to

$$
a'x-b'y\equiv0\pmod m.                                   \tag{3}
$$

Choose $u,v\in\mathbb Z$ such that $a'u-b'v=1$, and set

$$
A(a,b,m)=
\begin{pmatrix}
b'&a'\\
mu&mv
\end{pmatrix}.                                           \tag{4}
$$

Both rows satisfy (3). Conversely, if $(x,y)$ satisfies (3), define

$$
q=\frac{a'x-b'y}{m},
\qquad \lambda=uy-vx.
$$

Direct substitution using $a'u-b'v=1$ gives

$$
(x,y)=\lambda(b',a')+q(mu,mv).                            \tag{5}
$$

Therefore the integer row lattice generated by $A(a,b,m)$ is exactly
$H(a,b,m)$. Moreover,

$$
\det A=m(b'v-a'u)=-m.                                    \tag{6}
$$

Use one matrix (4) for each congruence in the homogeneous cover. Equation
(5) proves the matrix-cover property, and (6) proves that the absolute
determinants are the distinct indices $m>1$.
