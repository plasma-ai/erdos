**Source.** Theorem 2, printed p. 4 (PDF p. 5) of the 22 May 2001 author manuscript.

## Statement

Let $d$ be an odd positive integer. Suppose there is an
$f(x)\in\mathbb Z[x]$ such that

$$
f(1)\neq-d
$$

and $f(x)x^n+d$ is reducible for every integer $n\geq0$ (over the rationals,
as the paper's problem is posed on p. 1).
Then an odd covering of the integers exists: a finite covering whose moduli
are distinct odd integers greater than $1$.

**Proof pointer.** The manuscript describes this as a variation of Schinzel's
argument and proves it in Section 5, printed pp. 19--22 (PDF pp. 20--23). That proof was not
reconstructed or independently checked here.

**Bears on.** This is a conditional implication toward
[[../wiki/problems/covering_systems/E0007/_index|Problem 7]], not an existence theorem for
the required polynomial or covering.
