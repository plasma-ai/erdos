**Source.** Conjecture 1, PDF p. 1 of arXiv:2601.10296v2.

## Statement

Let $a,b\geq2$ be integers, neither of which is a power of an integer; that
is, neither can be written as $A^k$ with integers $A$ and $k\geq2$.
Then, as $m,n$ range over the positive integers,

$$
|a^m-b^n|
$$

takes infinitely many distinct prime values, unless there is a nonzero
integer $Q$ such that

$$
\gcd(a^m-b^n,Q)>1
$$

for every pair of positive integers $m,n$.

This asserts infinitely many prime **values** of the displayed expression,
not merely infinitely many prime divisors among its values.

**Proof scope.** This is a conjecture, not a proved result in the source.
No exact numbered Erdős-problem relationship is assigned here.
