**Source.** Conjecture 2, PDF p. 2 of arXiv:2601.10296v2.

## Statement

Let $a,b\geq2$ be integers. Choose $k$ and $\ell$ maximal such that

$$
a=A^k,\qquad b=B^{\ell}
$$

for integers $A,B$. Then, as $m,n$ range over the positive integers,

$$
|a^m-b^n|
$$

takes infinitely many distinct prime values, unless there is a nonzero
integer $Q$ such that

$$
\gcd(a^m-b^n,Q)>1
$$

for all positive $m,n$ satisfying

$$
\gcd(m,\ell)=\gcd(n,k)=1.
$$

**Proof scope.** This is a conjecture, not a proved result in the source.
No exact numbered Erdős-problem relationship is assigned here.
