**Source.** Section 4, printed pp. 1743--1745, physical PDF pp. 5--7.

## Statement recorded by the construction

There exists a finite covering system with distinct moduli greater than $1$
such that every integer satisfies at least three of its congruences.

More specifically, the source constructs three covering systems
$C_1,C_2,C_3$. Each has distinct moduli greater than $1$, and no modulus used
in one $C_i$ is used in another. Their union is therefore a distinct-modulus
$3$-covering.

**Proof pointer.** Section 4 lists the congruences and chooses its auxiliary
primes to prevent repeated moduli; the final paragraph takes
$p_1=11,p_2=13,p_3=17,p_4=19$. The full list and coverage check were not
reconstructed or independently verified here.

**Bears on.** This proves the lower bound in
[[covering_systems/harrington_2015_two_questions_covering_systems/question_1_4|Question 1.4]].
It is motivated by [[../wiki/problems/covering_systems/E0002/_index|Problem 2]] but does not
alter that problem's status.
