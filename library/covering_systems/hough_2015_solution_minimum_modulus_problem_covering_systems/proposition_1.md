***

**Source.** Hough, Proposition 1, printed pp. 369–371 of the
published paper.
Use the notation and definitions of goodness and well distribution in
[[covering_systems/hough_2015_solution_minimum_modulus_problem_covering_systems/sieve_setup|the sieve setup]].

**Statement.** For every $i\ge0$ and every $\lambda\ge0$, every
$\lambda$-good fiber is $\lambda$-well distributed.

**Complete proof.** Fix a good fiber $r$ and give
$F_r=(r\bmod Q_i)\subseteq\mathbb Z/Q_{i+1}\mathbb Z$ its uniform
probability measure $\mathbb P_r$. Translation by $-r$ and division by
$Q_i$ identifies it with $\mathbb Z/(Q_{i+1}/Q_i)\mathbb Z$.
The Chinese remainder theorem decomposes this quotient into independent
prime-power coordinates. Each event $A_{n,r}$ depends only on coordinates
at primes dividing $n$, and

$$
\mathbb P_r(A_{n,r})=\frac{a_n(r)}n.
$$

Thus $A_{n,r}$ is independent of the sigma-algebra of all events with
moduli coprime to $n$, not merely pairwise independent of those events.
In the dependency graph join different $n,n'$ exactly when
$\gcd(n,n')>1$.

If $\mathcal N_{i+1}=\varnothing$, the fiber survives in full. If
$\lambda=0$, goodness forces every $a_n(r)=0$: each $n>1$ has a prime
divisor and occurs with a positive coefficient in that prime's sum.
Again the full fiber survives. In either case uniform CRT counting
gives (4), including $\lambda=0$, directly. Hence assume $\lambda>0$.

Set $x_n=e^{\lambda\omega(n)}a_n(r)/n$. For every prime in the band,
$\sum_{p\mid n}x_n\le1-e^{-\lambda}$, so
$0\le x_n\le1-e^{-\lambda}<1$. Concavity of $\log(1-x)$ puts its
graph above the chord joining $x=0$ to $x=1-e^{-\lambda}$; therefore

$$
1-x_n\ge\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda x_n}{1-e^{-\lambda}}\right).
$$

For any $n\in\mathcal N_{i+1}$, repeated factors between $0$ and $1$
can only decrease a product. Hence

$$
\prod_{\substack{n'\in\mathcal N_{i+1}\\(n,n')>1}}(1-x_{n'})
\ge\prod_{p\mid n}\prod_{\substack{n'\in\mathcal N_{i+1}\\p\mid n'}}(1-x_{n'})
\ge e^{-\lambda\omega(n)}.
\tag{*}
$$

The last inequality follows by applying the exponential bound and each
prime's goodness condition. Dropping the possible self-factor from
the first product only increases it. Multiplication by $x_n$ now gives
the criterion of the
[[covering_systems/hough_2015_solution_minimum_modulus_problem_covering_systems/relative_local_lemma|relative local lemma]],
so the event $B=\bigcap_{n'}A_{n',r}^c$ has positive probability.

Fix $b\pmod n$, and let $U=\{n'\in\mathcal N_{i+1}:(n,n')=1\}$.
The event $b\pmod n$ has probability $1/n$ and is independent of the
sigma-algebra generated by $(A_{n',r})_{n'\in U}$. Consequently

$$
\mathbb P_r(B\cap(b\bmod n))
\le\frac1n\mathbb P_r\left(\bigcap_{n'\in U}A_{n',r}^c\right).
$$

The relative lemma, including its proved empty-$U$ case, bounds
$\mathbb P_r(B)$ below by the last avoidance probability multiplied by
$\prod_{(n,n')>1}(1-x_{n'})$. Divide the two inequalities and use (*)
to obtain $\mathbb P_r(b\bmod n\mid B)\le e^{\lambda\omega(n)}/n$.
This is precisely (4), and $B$ is the required surviving fiber.

**Source precision.** The separate $\lambda=0$ argument avoids the
printed proof's division by $1-e^{-\lambda}$ at its stated endpoint.
The empty collection and empty relative subcollection are also included.
