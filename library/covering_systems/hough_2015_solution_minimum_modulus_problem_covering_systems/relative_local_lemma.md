***

**Source.** Hough, the unnumbered lemma on printed pp. 369–370 and its
complete proof in Appendix B, pp. 379–381 of the
published paper.

**Statement.** Let $V$ be finite and let $(A_u)_{u\in V}$ be events in a
probability space. For each $u$, let $\Gamma(u)\subseteq V\setminus\{u\}$,
and assume $A_u$ is independent of the sigma-algebra generated by
$\{A_v:v\in V\setminus(\Gamma(u)\cup\{u\})\}$.
If $0\le x_u<1$ and

$$
\mathbb P(A_u)\le x_u\prod_{v\in\Gamma(u)}(1-x_v)
\qquad(u\in V),
$$

then, writing $B_S=\bigcap_{u\in S}A_u^c$, for every $U\subseteq V$,

$$
\mathbb P(B_V)\ge\mathbb P(B_U)\prod_{v\in V\setminus U}(1-x_v).
\tag{11}
$$

In particular $\mathbb P(B_V)\ge\prod_{v\in V}(1-x_v)>0$.

**Complete proof.** Induct on the number of conditioning events to show
both positivity of every $\mathbb P(B_S)$ and

$$
\mathbb P(A_u\mid B_S)\le x_u\qquad(u\notin S).
\tag{*}
$$

For $S=\varnothing$, the assumption implies
$\mathbb P(A_u)\le x_u$, and $\mathbb P(B_\varnothing)=1$.
Suppose (*) has been proved for conditioning sets smaller than $S$.
By writing $B_S$ as a product of conditional avoidance probabilities
and using those smaller cases, $\mathbb P(B_S)\ge
\prod_{v\in S}(1-x_v)>0$. Put $S_1=S\cap\Gamma(u)$ and
$S_2=S\setminus S_1$. If $S_1=\varnothing$, joint independence gives
$\mathbb P(A_u\mid B_S)=\mathbb P(A_u)\le x_u$.
Otherwise

$$
\mathbb P(A_u\mid B_S)
=\frac{\mathbb P(A_u\cap B_{S_1}\mid B_{S_2})}
{\mathbb P(B_{S_1}\mid B_{S_2})}.
$$

The numerator is at most $\mathbb P(A_u\mid B_{S_2})=
\mathbb P(A_u)$. Order $S_1$ and expand the denominator by successive
conditioning. Each factor conditions on fewer than $|S|$ events,
so the inductive hypothesis bounds the denominator below by
$\prod_{v\in S_1}(1-x_v)$. Consequently the displayed quotient is at most

$$
x_u\frac{\prod_{v\in\Gamma(u)}(1-x_v)}
{\prod_{v\in S_1}(1-x_v)}\le x_u.
$$

This closes the induction. Finally order $V$ with $U$ first and expand
$\mathbb P(B_V)$ starting from $\mathbb P(B_U)$. Each subsequent
conditional avoidance factor is at least $1-x_v$ by (*), proving (11).
The empty $U$ and empty $V$ cases follow from the same convention.

**Source precision.** The printed dependency-graph wording must exclude
$u$ itself from the nonneighbors; otherwise it would demand that a
nontrivial event be independent of itself. This is the usual convention,
and matches the graph actually used on p. 370. The source states (11)
for nonempty $U$, but its proof also gives $U=\varnothing$, which is
needed when a modulus has no coprime companion events. Appendix B's
last product has suppressed $j_\ell$ subscripts; the proof above keeps
the actual indexed events. No external local-lemma theorem is assumed.
