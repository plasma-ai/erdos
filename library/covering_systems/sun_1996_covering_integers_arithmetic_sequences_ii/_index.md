# Covering the integers by arithmetic sequences II

[[covering_systems/_index|..]]

[[covering_systems/sun_1996_covering_integers_arithmetic_sequences_ii/theorem_1|theorem_1]]: Covering as many consecutive terms of a+nZ as there are fractional parts of
the subset sums of m_s/n_s, each at least m times, makes the system an
m-cover of a+nZ.

[[covering_systems/sun_1996_covering_integers_arithmetic_sequences_ii/theorem_i|theorem_i]]: Collects the paper's central consequences for an m-cover of the integers:
integer subset sums of m_s/n_s, fractional parts when a sequence is
essential, and a lower bound on how often the largest modulus repeats.

[[covering_systems/sun_1996_covering_integers_arithmetic_sequences_ii/theorem_ii|theorem_ii]]: Collects the paper's central consequences for an exact m-cover of the
integers: residues of subset sums through a prescribed index, residues
modulo the largest modulus, and binomial coefficients as sums of
denominators.

***

Zhi-Wei Sun, *Covering the integers by arithmetic sequences II*, Transactions
of the American Mathematical Society **348** (1996), no. 11, 4279–4320,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-96-01674-1).

This digest records the paper's place in Sun's program, its main results, and
the context its introduction gives for Problem 7. Theorems I and II
(pp. 6–7 of the copy read) collect the paper's central results for m-covers
and exact m-covers in their simplest forms; Theorem 1 (pp. 10–11) is the
finite-block criterion behind several of them. Each has a result page stating
it and pointing to the paper's proof; no proof has been reconstructed or
independently reviewed here. The read status of the three statements is
claims checked: their hypotheses and conclusions were read clause by clause
against the copy.

The copy read for this card is the author's 48-page TeX copy,
internally numbered 1–48. Its first-page header carries the published
Transactions citation **348** (1996), no. 11, 4279–4320; the copy itself
does not use that 42-page journal pagination. Its PDF metadata identifies
a TeX/pdfTeX production, so the digest cites this copy without describing it
as a publisher scan or a publisher facsimile. It prints no copyright or license
line, and no hosting terms are recorded for it; the term is unstated.

The paper explicitly continues the program of
[[covering_systems/sun_1995_covering_integers_arithmetic_sequences/_index|Sun's 1995 paper]],
and its introduction cites Simpson's regular-covering lower bound
[[covering_systems/simpson_1985_regular_coverings_integers_arithmetic_progressions/_index|from 1985]].

## Results

- [[covering_systems/sun_1996_covering_integers_arithmetic_sequences_ii/theorem_i|Theorem I]]
  (pp. 6–7): for an $m$-cover, at least $m$ positive integers are subset sums
  of $m_s/n_s$; integer subset sums avoiding a given index when $m>1$;
  fractional parts of reciprocal subset sums when a sequence is essential;
  and, under the paper's condition (7), $l\ge n_k/n_{k-l}$ or
  $\sum_{s=1}^{k-l}1/n_s\ge m$.
- [[covering_systems/sun_1996_covering_integers_arithmetic_sequences_ii/theorem_ii|Theorem II]]
  (p. 7): for an exact $m$-cover, residues of subset sums of
  $(n,n_s)/n_s$ and $n_k/n_s$, every $r/n_t$ as an exact subset sum of
  $1/n_s$ with $s\ne t$ when $m=1$, and binomial coefficients
  $\binom l\lambda$ as sums of denominators.
- [[covering_systems/sun_1996_covering_integers_arithmetic_sequences_ii/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
  (pp. 10–11): when $(m_s,n_s)=(n,n_s)$ for every $s$, covering at least
  $m$ times each of as many consecutive terms of $a+n\mathbb Z$ as there are
  fractional parts of the sums of $m_s/n_s$ over subsets of the indices with
  $(n,n_s)\mid a_s-a$ forces an $m$-cover of $a+n\mathbb Z$; its part (ii) bounds the
  number of such fractional parts when a sequence is essential. Its proof
  uses Lemma 3, quoted from Sun's 1995 paper.

The paper's Theorems 2 (p. 17) and 3 (pp. 31–33) are technical general forms
from which the corollaries behind Theorems I and II follow; they have no
result page. Section 6 (pp. 45–46) poses eight open problems.

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/covering_systems/E0275/_index|Problem 275]]: Theorem 1(i)
  with $n=1$, $m=1$ and every $m_s=1$ gives the problem's statement, since
  the number of fractional parts is at most $2^k$. This derivation is the
  corpus's; the paper credits that statement to Crittenden and Vanden
  Eynden and presents its Lemma 3, quoted from Sun's 1995 paper, as a
  stronger result (p. 10).
- [[../wiki/problems/covering_systems/E0947/_index|Problem 947]]: the first
  assertion of Theorem I(iv) with $m=1$ excludes an exact cover by at least
  two sequences with distinct moduli, by the derivation on the result page;
  the paper credits that theorem to Davenport, Mirsky, Newman and Radó.
- [[../wiki/problems/covering_systems/E0007/_index|Problem 7]]: context only;
  the introduction states the Erdős–Selfridge conjecture as its
  Conjecture II (p. 2), and no result of the paper decides it.
