# diophantine_problems/bennett_2024_computing_four_term_arithmetic_progressions_powerful_numbers

[[diophantine_problems/_index|..]]

[[diophantine_problems/bennett_2024_computing_four_term_arithmetic_progressions_powerful_numbers/evidence/_index|evidence/]]: Exact integer check of the published 111-digit four-term progression of
powerful numbers.

[[diophantine_problems/bennett_2024_computing_four_term_arithmetic_progressions_powerful_numbers/section_4_record_example|section_4_record_example]]: Records and directly verifies the published Bennett-Walsh record example
of signature [1,3,5,7].

***

Michael A. Bennett and P. G. Walsh, "Computing four-term arithmetic
progressions of powerful numbers," *INTEGERS* **24A** (2024), article A3,
5 pp. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.11352598).

The paper searches for coprime four-term progressions with a fixed
signature $[\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta]$, meaning that the terms are
respectively $\alpha^3x^2,\beta^3y^2,\gamma^3z^2,\delta^3w^2$ for positive
integers $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta$; the search takes $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$
odd and squarefree. It parametrizes the first three terms by a
ternary quadratic form, obtains a genus-one quartic from the fourth, maps
that curve to an elliptic curve, and searches a computed subgroup of its
rational points.

Section 4 publishes a 111-digit example of signature $[1,3,5,7]$, improving
the 190-digit example of Bajpai, Bennett and Chan (Int. J. Number Theory 20
(2024), 19–45). The abstract calls it the current "record" example, and
Section 4 reports that "the smallest quadruple with signature $[1, 3, 5, 7]$
has integers with only 111 digits" (p. 4). It is not a proof that the example
is least possible: the authors say only that it may be minimal and that a
proof would need a careful analysis of the quadratic forms and of generators
for the elliptic curves.
