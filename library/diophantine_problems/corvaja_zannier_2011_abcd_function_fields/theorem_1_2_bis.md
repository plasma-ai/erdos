## Statement

**Theorem 1.2 bis** (p. 441). Let $a,c$ be positive integers with $c\ge2$ and
$a\ge10^4$, and let $\mathcal S\subset\mathbf A^3$ be the surface

$$
x^a+y^a+z^c=1
$$

over the algebraically closed field $\kappa$ of characteristic zero that the
paper fixes on p. 438. Then $\mathcal S$ contains only finitely many (affine)
curves of geometric genus at most $1$.

The paper states that this confirms Bogomolov's conjecture, which predicts
finitely many curves of geometric genus at most $1$ on every surface of
general type, for this surface (p. 442). It adds that, under the same
assumptions, the same method bounds the degree in $\mathbf P_3$ of the curves
on $\mathcal S$ of any fixed genus, and that the proof, especially inequality
(5), implies the bound $10^4(2g-1)$ for curves of genus $g$ (p. 442). In the
proof it notes that for large genus algebraic families of curves of bounded
degree exist, while in genus one finiteness can be proved (p. 448).

**Source.** Pietro Corvaja and Umberto Zannier, An abcd theorem over function
fields and applications, Bull. Soc. Math. France 139 (2011), no. 4, 437-454:
Theorem 1.2 bis on p. 441, the remarks after it on p. 442, the proof on
p. 448, and the Appendix on pp. 449-454 with Proposition A and its Corollary
on p. 450. The edition read is identified on the
[[diophantine_problems/corvaja_zannier_2011_abcd_function_fields/_index|source card]].

## Proof pointer

Page 448, with the Appendix. A curve on $\mathcal S$ is a solution of its
equation in a function field $\kappa(\mathcal C)$.
[[diophantine_problems/corvaja_zannier_2011_abcd_function_fields/theorem_1_2|Theorem 1.2]]
gives finiteness in genus $0$, and inequalities (5) and (6) of its proof bound
the degree of curves of each fixed genus. For genus $1$, infinitely many
elliptic curves of bounded degree with Zariski dense union would, through
their $j$-invariants, give a dominant map from an elliptic surface to
$\mathcal S$. Proposition A (p. 450) shows that $\mathcal S$ is of general
type when $c\ge2$ and $a>3c/(c-1)$, and its Corollary (p. 450) that no
elliptic or ruled surface maps dominantly to it.
