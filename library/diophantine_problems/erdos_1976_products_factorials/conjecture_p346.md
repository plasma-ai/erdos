## Statement

Notation as on
[[diophantine_problems/erdos_1976_products_factorials/fact_1|the page of the sets F_k and D_k]].

**Examples and question** (pp. 345--346). Both families of elements of
$D_3$ given on p. 342 have $a_2\ge a_1-2$. The paper gives the examples
$10!\,7!\,6!$, $50!\,47!\,3!$ and $50!\,47!\,4!$, all squares, with
$a_2=a_1-3$, and asks whether there are others, and whether
$a_1!a_2!a_3!$ can ever be a square when $a_3<a_2<a_1-3$.

**Remark** (p. 346). The paper says it is not difficult to show that if
$a_1\in D_3$ and $a_1=2p$ for an odd prime $p$, then $a_1$ is $6$ (with
$6!\,5!\,3!$ a square) or $10$ (with $10!\,7!\,6!$ a square); no proof is
given.

**Conjecture** (p. 346, quoted). "We are sure now that
$D_3(x)=(c+o(1))x^{1/2}$ but we cannot prove it."

## Proof pointer

None: the paper states the conjecture, the question and the remark
without proof.
