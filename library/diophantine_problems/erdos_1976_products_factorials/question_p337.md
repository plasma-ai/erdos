## Statement

**Question** (p. 337, abstract and introduction). Motivated by the
Erdős--Selfridge theorem that a product of two or more consecutive
positive integers is never a perfect power, the paper asks when a product
of two or more disjoint blocks of consecutive integers can be a square or
a higher power. As an example it suggests that if $A_1,\ldots,A_n$ are
disjoint intervals, each consisting of at least $3$ integers, then perhaps
$\prod_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{a_k\in A_k}a_k$ is a square (the abstract says a
nonzero square) in only a finite number of cases.

The number $n$ of intervals and their lengths are not fixed in the
suggestion. The paper proves nothing about it.

## Proof pointer

None: the paper poses the question in its abstract and introduction and
does not return to it.
