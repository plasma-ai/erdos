## Statement

Setting (p. 1). Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
$0$, let $\Gamma$ be a subgroup of $(K^*)^n$, the group of $n$-tuples of
nonzero elements of $K$ under coordinatewise multiplication, and let
$(a_1,\dots,a_n)\in(K^*)^n$. Equation (1.1) is

$$
a_1x_1+\dots+a_nx_n=1,\qquad \mathbf{x}=(x_1,\dots,x_n)\in\Gamma .
$$

A solution is nondegenerate when $\sum_{i\in I}a_ix_i\neq0$ for every
nonempty subset $I$ of $\{1,\dots,n\}$, and $A(a_1,\dots,a_n;\Gamma)$ is the
number of nondegenerate solutions. The group $\Gamma$ has rank $r$ when it has
a finitely generated subgroup $\Gamma_0$ of rank $r$ with $\Gamma/\Gamma_0$ a
torsion group.

**Theorem 1.1** (p. 2). If $\Gamma$ has finite rank $r$, then

$$
A(a_1,\dots,a_n;\Gamma)\le A(n,r)=\exp\bigl((6n)^{3n}(r+1)\bigr). \tag{1.2}
$$

The bound depends only on $n$ and $r$, not on the coefficients $a_i$, the
field $K$ or the group beyond its rank. The paper's abstract states the
setting for any field of characteristic $0$.

The paper notes (pp. 2--3) that any admissible bound must depend on both $n$
and $r$, that for $n=2$ the bound $2^{9(r+1)}$ of Beukers and Schlickewei is
much better, and (p. 4) that for $n\ge3$ and $r\ge1$ the theorem is new. A
remark on p. 8 says that a sharpening by David and Philippon improves the
bound to $\exp((r+1)\exp(c_1n))$ with an absolute constant $c_1$.

## Proof pointer

Sections 3 and 4, pp. 10--14. A specialization argument (Lemma 3.2, p. 12)
reduces to $K=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$. Section 4 then argues by induction on
$n$: with $\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{a}*\mathbf{x}$ in the group generated by
$\mathbf{a}$ and $\Gamma$, of rank at most $r+1$,
[[diophantine_problems/evertse_schlickewei_schmidt_2002_linear_equations_multiplicative_group/theorem_2_1|Theorem 2.1]] places all solutions of
$y_1+\dots+y_n=1$ in at most $B(n,r+1)$ proper linear subspaces, and the
solutions in each subspace, sorted by a vanishing subsum of the subspace's
equation, are counted by two equations in fewer variables, to which the
induction hypothesis applies.

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/diophantine_problems/E0407/_index|Problem 407]]: the
  paper does not mention the problem. A representation
  $n=2^a+3^b+2^c3^d$ is a solution of
  $\tfrac1n x_1+\tfrac1n x_2+\tfrac1n x_3=1$ with
  $(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(2^a,3^b,2^c3^d)$ in the subgroup of $(\mathbb{Q}^*)^3$
  generated by $(2,1,1)$, $(1,3,1)$, $(1,1,2)$ and $(1,1,3)$, of rank $4$,
  and it is nondegenerate because every term is positive. Theorem 1.1 with
  $K=\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ therefore bounds the number of representations by
  a constant independent of $n$. The problem's site credits the proof that
  the count is bounded to Evertse, Győry, Stewart and Tijdeman (1988),
  before this paper.
