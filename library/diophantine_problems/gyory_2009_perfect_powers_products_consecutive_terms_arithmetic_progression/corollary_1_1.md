## Statement

**Corollary 1.1** (printed p. 848), quoted: "Suppose that $n\geq2$,
$1<k<35$ and $(k,n)\neq(2,2)$. Then (2) has no solutions in positive
rational numbers $u,v$."

Equation (2), on the same page, is

$$
u(u+1)\cdots(u+k-1)=v^n .
$$

**Source.** K. Győry, L. Hajdu and Á. Pintér, *Perfect powers from
products of consecutive terms in arithmetic progression*, *Compositio
Mathematica* **145** (2009), 845--864, DOI
[10.1112/S0010437X09004114](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X09004114);
Corollary 1.1 and equation (2) on printed p. 848.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the statement was read against the print.
The paper gives no proof of its own to check.

## Proof pointer

Printed p. 848. For $k\le11$ the paper attributes the corollary to
Bennett, Bruin, Győry and Hajdu (2006); for $k>11$ it calls it a
straightforward consequence of [[diophantine_problems/gyory_2009_perfect_powers_products_consecutive_terms_arithmetic_progression/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]] and refers
to that paper and to Győry, Hajdu and Saradha (2004) for the argument,
which it does not repeat.
