## Statement

Setting (p. 2). For a positive integer $n$ let
$B_n=\{\binom n1,\binom n2,\ldots,\binom n{n-1}\}$. The paper recalls Ram's
theorem that $\gcd(B_n)=p$ when $n=p^m$ is a prime power and $\gcd(B_n)=1$
otherwise, and defines $S(B_n)$ as the numerical semigroup generated by
$B_n$ when $n$ is not a prime power, and by
$\frac1p\binom n1,\ldots,\frac1p\binom n{n-1}$ when $n=p^m$. For a
numerical semigroup $S$, $F(S)$ is the largest integer not in $S$, and
$\mathrm{Ap}(S,x)$ is the Apéry set of $S$ with respect to $x$.

**Theorem 0.1** (pp. 2-3).

1. Let $n=p_1^{k_1}\cdots p_t^{k_t}$ with primes $p_1<\cdots<p_t$ and
   $t\ge2$. Then
   (a) the Apéry set of $S(B_n)$ with respect to $n$ is
   $$
   \mathrm{Ap}(S(B_n),n)=\Bigl\{\sum_{i=1}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{k_i}c_{i,j}\binom{n}{p_i^{\,j}}\ :\ c_{i,j}\in\mathbb Z,\ 0\le c_{i,j}\le p_i-1\Bigr\};
   $$
   (b) the Frobenius number is
   $$
   F(S(B_n))=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\sum_{j=1}^{k_i}(p_i-1)\binom{n}{p_i^{\,j}}-n.
   $$
2. Let $n=p^m$ with $p$ prime and $m\ge1$ an integer. Then
   (a) the Apéry set of $S(B_n)$ with respect to $p^{m-1}$ is
   $$
   \mathrm{Ap}(S(B_n),p^{m-1})=\Bigl\{\frac1p\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}c_i\binom{n}{p^i}\ :\ c_i\in\mathbb Z,\ 0\le c_i\le p-1\Bigr\};
   $$
   (b) the Frobenius number is
   $$
   F(S(B_n))=\frac{p-1}{p}\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\binom{p^m}{p^i}-p^{m-1}.
   $$

The print calls the exponents $k_i$ in part (1) "nonnegative integers".
With a zero exponent among them $n$ could be a prime power, the case part
(2) treats under a different normalisation, so part (1) is read with every
$k_i\ge1$, which is the case where $n$ has $t\ge2$ distinct prime factors.
In part (2) with $m=1$ both sums are empty: the Apéry set is $\{0\}$ and
$F(S(B_p))=-1$, so $S(B_p)$ is all of $\mathbb N$.

**Remark 0.2** (p. 3, quoted). "Theorem 0.1-(1)-(b) solves a problem of
Erdos [1]." The paper's reference [1] is T. F. Bloom, Erdős Problem #435,
erdosproblems.com/435, accessed 2025-10-01; the acknowledgments (p. 16)
say the authors learned of the problem after the paper was written.

## Proof pointer

The paper (p. 3) computes the Apéry set from an explicit generating set of
$S(B_n)$ and then reads off $F$ from the standard relation
$F(S)=\max\mathrm{Ap}(S,x)-x$ (its Lemma 1.8(a), p. 4). For the proof it
points to
[[diophantine_problems/hwang_2024_frobenius_problem_numerical_semigroups_generated_binomial/lemma_2_2|Lemma 2.2]],
[[diophantine_problems/hwang_2024_frobenius_problem_numerical_semigroups_generated_binomial/theorem_3_2|Theorem 3.2]]
and Theorem 3.7 (p. 3); the statements carrying the conclusions are
[[diophantine_problems/hwang_2024_frobenius_problem_numerical_semigroups_generated_binomial/corollary_3_3|Corollary 3.3]]
(part (1)) and
[[diophantine_problems/hwang_2024_frobenius_problem_numerical_semigroups_generated_binomial/corollary_3_7|Corollary 3.7]]
(part (2)), the latter printed as a corollary although the text calls it
Theorem 3.7. The paper omits the proofs of the prime-power case (p. 8)
"because of the similarity of the proof".

## Bears on

  problem asks, for $n$ not a prime power, for the largest integer that is
  not a nonnegative integer combination of $\binom n1,\ldots,\binom n{n-1}$.
  That integer is $F(S(B_n))$, and part (1)(b) gives it in closed form for
  every $n$ with at least two distinct prime factors, which are exactly the
  $n>1$ that are not prime powers. Remark 0.2 states that part (1)(b)
  solves the problem.
