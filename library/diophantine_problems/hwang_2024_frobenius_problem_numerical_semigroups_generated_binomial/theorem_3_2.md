## Statement

**Theorem 3.2** (p. 6). Let $n=p_1^{k_1}\cdots p_t^{k_t}$ with primes
$p_1<\cdots<p_t$ and $t\ge2$, and let $S(B_n)$ be the numerical semigroup
generated by $\binom n1,\ldots,\binom n{n-1}$. Then

(a) $S(B_n)$ is generated by
$$
\binom n1,\ \binom n{p_1},\binom n{p_1^2},\ldots,\binom n{p_1^{k_1}},\ \ldots,\ \binom n{p_t},\binom n{p_t^2},\ldots,\binom n{p_t^{k_t}};
$$

(b) the embedding dimension of $S(B_n)$ (the size of its minimal system of
generators) is
$$
e(S(B_n))=1+\sum_{i=1}^{t}k_i.
$$

The proof (p. 7) concludes that the generators listed in (a) form the
minimal system of generators, which is what (b) counts. As with
[[diophantine_problems/hwang_2024_frobenius_problem_numerical_semigroups_generated_binomial/theorem_0_1|Theorem 0.1]],
the print calls the $k_i$ "nonnegative integers"; the statement is read
with every $k_i\ge1$.

## Proof pointer

Pp. 6-7. The listed coefficients lie in $S(B_n)$ and none is a combination
of the others, which the paper argues by comparing sizes and $p$-adic
valuations. Every other $\binom nm$ is shown to be a nonnegative integer
combination of the listed ones: when $m$ is prime to $n$, $n$ divides $\binom nm$; for the
remaining $m\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ the paper invokes its Theorem 3.1
(p. 6), which as printed takes $m=p_1^{s_1}\cdots p_t^{s_t}$ and writes
$\binom nm$ as a nonnegative integer combination of $\binom n1$ and the
$\binom n{p_i^{s_i}}$ by a Frobenius bound for several generators (its
Proposition 2.3); larger $m$ follow by $\binom nm=\binom n{n-m}$.
