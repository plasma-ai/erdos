## Statement

Setting (p. 5). Let $\mathbb F$ be a field and
$f\in\mathbb F[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$. For $i=1,\ldots,n$ let $D_i$ and $S_i$
be finite nonempty subsets of $\mathbb F$ with $D_i\subset S_i$, and put

$$
g_i(X_i)=\prod_{s\in S_i}(X_i-s),\qquad l_i(X_i)=\prod_{d\in D_i}(X_i-d).
$$

Multiplicity, $T(n,t)$ and the sums over $i\in\tau$ are as on the
[[discrete_geometry/ball_2009_punctured_combinatorial_nullstellensatze/theorem_3_1|Theorem 3.1]] page.

**Theorem 4.1** (p. 5). Suppose $f$ has a zero of multiplicity at least
$t$ at every point of $S_1\times\cdots\times S_n$ except at at least one
point of $D_1\times\cdots\times D_n$, where its multiplicity is less than
$t$. Then there are polynomials $h_\tau\in\mathbb F[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$
with $\deg h_\tau\le\deg f-\sum_{i\in\tau}\deg g_i$, and a nonzero
polynomial $u$ with
$\deg u\le\deg f-\sum_{i=1}^n(\deg g_i-\deg l_i)$, such that

$$
f=\sum_{\tau\in T(n,t)}g_{\tau(1)}\cdots g_{\tau(t)}h_\tau+u\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{g_i}{l_i}.
$$

If moreover $f$ is nonzero at some point of $D_1\times\cdots\times D_n$,
then

$$
\deg f\ge(t-1)\max_j\bigl(|S_j|-|D_j|\bigr)+\sum_{i=1}^n\bigl(|S_i|-|D_i|\bigr).
$$

**Reading.** The print writes $\deg(h_i)$ for the coefficients
$h_\tau$. The hypothesis is read as multiplicity at least $t$ at every
point of $(S_1\times\cdots\times S_n)\setminus(D_1\times\cdots\times D_n)$
and multiplicity less than $t$ at some point of
$D_1\times\cdots\times D_n$; the proof uses it in this form. For $t>1$
the degree bound needs the stronger hypothesis that $f$ is nonzero at a
point of $D_1\times\cdots\times D_n$.

## Proof pointer

Pp. 5–6. Reduce $f$ modulo the ideal generated by the products
$g_{\tau(1)}\cdots g_{\tau(t)}$ to a remainder $w$, which is nonzero
because $f$ is not in that ideal. For each $i$, $fl_i^t$ and hence
$wl_i^t$ have a zero of multiplicity $t$ on the whole grid, and
Theorem 3.1 together with the form of $w$ shows that $g_i$ divides $wl_i^t$;
since $g_i/l_i$ is coprime to $l_i$, it divides $w$. For the degree
bound the paper fixes an index $j$ attaining the maximum and a point
$d$ of the smaller grid where $f$ is nonzero, and shows that
$(g_j/l_j)^{t-1}$ divides the nonzero one-variable restriction of $u$
through $d$.
