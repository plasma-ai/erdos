***

**Source.** ArXiv v3, pp. 10–11, Questions 6.1–6.5
([canonical PDF](behague_2025_nearly_all_known_euclidean_ramsey_sets_subsoluble.pdf#page=10)).
These are questions posed by the 5 December 2025 manuscript. This page
preserves that source scope and does not certify that each question remains
open at a later date.

1. **Question 6.1.** If a finite Euclidean configuration $X$ is Ramsey, must
   an isometric copy of $X$ be contained in a finite configuration $Y$ on
   which a soluble isometry group acts transitively?
2. **Question 6.2.** Let $X$ be finite and transitive. If some soluble
   isometry group acts on $X$ with exactly two orbits, must $X$ embed in a
   finite soluble configuration? A positive answer would turn Kříž's
   two-orbit hypothesis into a transitive soluble enclosure, but
   [[discrete_geometry/behague_2025_nearly_all_known_euclidean_ramsey_sets_subsoluble/lemma_4_2|Lemma 4.2]]
   proves this only under its additional orbit-stabilizer ratio bound.
3. **Question 6.3.** Is the vertex set of every finite convex regular
   polytope subsoluble? In particular, are the 120-cell and 600-cell
   subsoluble? The source notes that a suitable copy of the 600-cell lies in
   the 120-cell, so an enclosure of the latter would cover both. It also
   reports that the direct inscribed-polytope attempt to use Lemma 4.2 fails
   at least one of solubility, two-orbit structure, or the ratio bound in
   every case checked there. That is a limitation of the attempted method,
   not a negative theorem.
4. **Question 6.4.** Given real numbers
   $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_s$, not necessarily distinct, and integers
   $i_1,\ldots,i_s\ge0$, for which multiplicity patterns is the set of all
   coordinate permutations of

   $$
   (\alpha_1^{i_1},\ldots,\alpha_s^{i_s})
   $$

   subsoluble for every choice of the real parameters? Each exponent denotes
   coordinate repetition. The source singles out the five-singleton pattern
   $(1,1,1,1,1)$. Its
   [[discrete_geometry/behague_2025_nearly_all_known_euclidean_ramsey_sets_subsoluble/theorem_1_6|Theorem 1.6]]
   settles only the four families stated there. The
   block-set conjecture would give Ramsey consequences for the broader
   coordinate sets; Ramsey-ness alone would not supply subsoluble
   enclosures without an answer to Question 6.1.
5. **Question 6.5.** Is every finite transitive Euclidean configuration
   subsoluble?

The source further explains why its affine-group method does not directly
extend to three or more distinguished coordinate positions: it uses the
soluble $2$-transitive group $\operatorname{AGL}(1,p)$, whereas its cited
classification discussion rules out a soluble $3$-transitive group of
sufficient degree. That observation limits this method; it does not answer
Question 6.4.

**Scope.** The source's statement that a positive answer to Question 6.2
would handle every Ramsey example then known is a dated literature claim.
Likewise, the displayed implication diagram records the author's 2025 view.
Neither is used here as current exhaustive-status evidence. These questions
do not alter [[../wiki/problems/discrete_geometry/E0174/_index|Problem 174]].
