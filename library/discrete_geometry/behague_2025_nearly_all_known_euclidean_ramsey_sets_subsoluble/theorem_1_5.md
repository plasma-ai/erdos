***

**Source.** ArXiv v3, p. 2, Theorem 1.5
([canonical PDF](behague_2025_nearly_all_known_euclidean_ramsey_sets_subsoluble.pdf#page=2)).

**Statement.** The following finite Euclidean configurations are
subsoluble:

1. for all $\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb R$ and integers $i,j\ge0$, the set of
   coordinate permutations of
   $(\alpha^i,\beta^j,\gamma)$, where exponents denote repetition;
2. the vertex sets of all finite convex regular polytopes except possibly
   the 120-cell and 600-cell;
3. every nondegenerate convex symmetric isosceles trapezium, including
   rectangles.

Consequently every configuration in these three classes is Ramsey.

**Proof.** Item 1 is the second family of
[[discrete_geometry/behague_2025_nearly_all_known_euclidean_ramsey_sets_subsoluble/theorem_1_6|Theorem 1.6]]. Item 2 is
[[discrete_geometry/behague_2025_nearly_all_known_euclidean_ramsey_sets_subsoluble/theorem_4_1|Theorem 4.1]], and item 3 is
[[discrete_geometry/behague_2025_nearly_all_known_euclidean_ramsey_sets_subsoluble/theorem_5_1|Theorem 5.1]]. Each page gives the
complete same-paper deduction and identifies its external inputs.
Subsoluble configurations are Ramsey by the exact Kříž theorem and subset
closure recorded in
[[discrete_geometry/behague_2025_nearly_all_known_euclidean_ramsey_sets_subsoluble/definitions|the definitions page]]. $\square$

**Scope.** Behague also records rectangular parallelepipeds as soluble
(p. 2, citing Section 2.2) and, as their Theorem 1.3 cited from Karamanlis, all
nondegenerate simplexes as subsoluble. Neither is an item of Theorem 1.5.
The source's phrase “nearly all known examples” describes its 2025
comparison. It does not certify a current exhaustive list, settle Graham's
spherical-set conjecture or the Leader--Russell--Walters transitive-set
conjecture, or prove that the 120-cell and 600-cell are subsoluble.
