Source: [published paper](conlon_2019_lines_euclidean_ramsey_theory.pdf#page=2),
printed p. 219, the unnumbered unit-sphere observation.

## Statement

If $K$ is any subset of a sphere of radius one in $\mathbb R^n$, then
$\mathbb E^n\to(\ell_2,K)$. The set $K$ need not be finite.

## Full proof

Assume there is no red unit-distance pair. If there are no red points, the
whole space is blue and contains $K$. Otherwise choose a red point $p$.
Every point of the sphere of radius one about $p$ is blue. Translate the
sphere containing $K$ to that sphere; the corresponding translate of $K$
is blue. This proves the assertion.

This observation explains why cardinality alone cannot upper-bound the size
of every configuration forced against a red unit pair. The separation and
diameter conditions in the main theorem have substantive roles.
