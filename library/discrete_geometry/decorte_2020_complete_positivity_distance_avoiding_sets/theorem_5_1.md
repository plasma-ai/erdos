## Statement

Setting. A topological graph $G=(V,E)$ is *locally independent* when every
compact independent set is contained in an open independent set
(Definition 2.1, p. 6). For a Borel measure $\omega$ on $V$,
$\alpha_\omega(G)$ is the supremum of $\omega(I)$ over measurable independent
sets $I$ (p. 6). For compact $V$ and a convex cone $\mathcal K(V)$ of
symmetric kernels in $L^2(V\times V)$, $\vartheta(G,\mathcal K(V))$ is the
supremum of $\langle J,A\rangle$, with $J$ the constant kernel $1$, over
continuous $A\in\mathcal K(V)$ with $\int_VA(x,x)\,d\omega(x)=1$ and
$A(x,y)=0$ for $(x,y)\in E$ (problem (6), p. 8). The completely positive cone
$\mathcal C(V)$ is the closure in the norm of $L^2(V\times V)$ of the cone
generated by the kernels $f\otimes f^*$ with $f\in L^2(V)$ nonnegative almost
everywhere ((7), p. 8). Section 5 normalizes Haar measure on a compact group
to total mass $1$ (p. 16). For a compact group $\Gamma$ acting continuously
and transitively on $V$, the pushforward of its Haar measure $\mu$ is
$X\mapsto\mu(\{\sigma:\sigma x\in X\})$ for a fixed $x\in V$ (p. 16). A metric
is a *density metric* for a measure that is positive on open sets when almost
every point of each measurable set is a density point of it, and it is
*bi-invariant* on $\Gamma$ when
$d(\lambda\sigma\gamma,\lambda\tau\gamma)=d(\sigma,\tau)$ for all
$\lambda,\gamma,\sigma,\tau\in\Gamma$ (p. 17).

**Theorem 5.1** (p. 17, quoted). "Let $G=(V,E)$ be a locally independent
graph where $V$ is a compact Hausdorff space, $\Gamma\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(G)$
be a compact group that acts continuously and transitively on $V$, and
$\omega$ be a multiple of the pushforward of the Haar measure on $\Gamma$. If
$\Gamma$ is metrizable via a bi-invariant density metric for the Haar
measure, then $\vartheta(G,\mathcal{C}(V))=\alpha_\omega(G)$."

The paper calls this "the main theorem of the paper" (p. 17, quoted). As an
instance it gives
$\vartheta(G(S^{n-1},\{\theta\}),\mathcal C(S^{n-1}))=\alpha_\omega(G(S^{n-1},\{\theta\}))$
for every angle $\theta>0$, with $\Gamma=\mathrm O(n)$ and $\omega$ the
surface measure (p. 17).

## Proof pointer

The inequality $\vartheta(G,\mathcal C(V))\ge\alpha_\omega(G)$ is Theorem 3.1
(p. 8). The reverse inequality is Lemma 5.6 (p. 21), proved in Section 5.3
(pp. 21-23): the problem is moved to a Cayley graph on $\Gamma$ (Lemmas
5.2-5.3, pp. 17-18), a feasible kernel of trace one is written by Choquet's
theorem as a barycenter of extreme points of the tip of $\mathcal C(\Gamma)$,
which are rank-one kernels $f\otimes f^*$ with $f\ge0$ (Theorems 4.8-4.9,
p. 15), and the density-metric hypothesis is used to trim the support of such
an $f$ to an independent set of the same measure.

## Dependencies

Theorem 3.1, Lemmas 5.2-5.6 and Theorems 4.8-4.9 of the same paper;
Choquet's theorem.
