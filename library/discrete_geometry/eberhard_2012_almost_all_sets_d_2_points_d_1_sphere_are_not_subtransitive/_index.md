# Eberhard: Almost all sets of d+2 points on the (d-1)-sphere are not subtransitive

[[discrete_geometry/_index|..]]

[[discrete_geometry/eberhard_2012_almost_all_sets_d_2_points_d_1_sphere_are_not_subtransitive/theorem_p1|theorem_p1]]: Eberhard's unnumbered Theorem: for points x_0, ..., x_{d+1} chosen
uniformly at random from the sphere S^{d-1} in R^d, almost surely no
nonconstant affine map carries them into a finite transitive set in any R^n.

***

Sean Eberhard, "Almost all sets of d+2 points on the (d-1)-sphere are not
subtransitive," Mathematika 59 (2013), no. 2, 267-268. DOI
10.1112/S0025579313000053. arXiv:1212.1803 (2012). The copy read for this card
is the arXiv preprint arXiv:1212.1803v1. The arXiv record names arXiv's
non-exclusive distribution license (arXiv:1212.1803), every other right
reserved.

## Overview

The paper concerns whether finite spherical configurations must be
**subtransitive**, meaning contained in a finite transitive set in some possibly
higher-dimensional Euclidean space. Leader, Russell and Walters had answered
this negatively, showing that almost all cyclic quadrilaterals are not even
affinely subtransitive; the paper generalizes their result and argument to the
first cardinality beyond a simplex in every dimension. Its sole displayed
**Theorem** states that almost every set of $d+2$ points on
$S^{d-1}\subset\mathbb R^d$ is not even *affinely* subtransitive: there is no
nonconstant affine map embedding it into a transitive set. “Almost every” is
implemented by choosing $x_0,\ldots,x_{d+1}$ uniformly at random from the
sphere.

The proof fixes a finite orthogonal group $G\le O(n)$ and elements
$g_1,\ldots,g_{d+1}\in G$, using countability of finite groups and of their
orthogonal representations up to conjugacy. A putative affine embedding must
satisfy equation (1),

$$
f(x_k)=g_kf(x_0)\qquad(1\le k\le d+1).
$$

After sending the almost surely affinely independent tuple $(x_0,\ldots,x_d)$ to
$(0,e_1,\ldots,e_d)$, the last point acquires affine coordinates
$\alpha\in\mathbb R^d$. The resulting rational map $\phi$ is dominant; footnote
1 gives the explicit inclusion
$\operatorname{image}(\phi)\supset(0,1)^{d-1}\times(1,\infty)$.

Writing $f(x)=Ax+b$, the first $d$ orbit equations determine

$$
A=(g_1b-b,\ldots,g_db-b),
$$

and the last becomes the linear condition (2)

$$
\sum_{k=1}^d\alpha_k(g_kb-b)+b=g_{d+1}b.
$$

After quotienting by the common fixed-point space of the $g_k$, existence of a
nonconstant solution is equivalent to rank deficiency of a matrix whose maximal
minors are polynomials in $\alpha$. These minors are not all identically zero:
for $\alpha=(1/(2d),\ldots,1/(2d))$, the point $\alpha$ lies inside the simplex
generated by $0,e_1,\ldots,e_d$, so the resulting configuration cannot be mapped
onto a sphere by a nonconstant affine map. Consequently the exceptional
$\alpha$'s, and hence the exceptional spherical tuples, lie in proper algebraic
subvarieties and have measure zero.

The scope is sharp in cardinality in the stated generic sense: the introduction
records, as cited background from references [1] and [4] rather than as a new
theorem, that every affinely independent set of $d+1$ points is subtransitive.
The paper does not classify the exceptional $d+2$-point configurations,
characterize subtransitive sets, or prove any Euclidean Ramsey theorem.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/discrete_geometry/E0174/_index|#174]]: the
problem asks for a characterization of the Ramsey sets. The paper notes
(p. 1) that Leader, Russell and Walters answered negatively, in connection
with some conjectures in Euclidean Ramsey theory, whether every set on a
sphere is subtransitive, and its
[[discrete_geometry/eberhard_2012_almost_all_sets_d_2_points_d_1_sphere_are_not_subtransitive/theorem_p1|Theorem]]
shows that almost every $(d+2)$-point subset of $S^{d-1}\subset\mathbb R^d$
lies on a sphere but is not subtransitive, indeed not affinely subtransitive.
The paper proves nothing about which sets are Ramsey.
