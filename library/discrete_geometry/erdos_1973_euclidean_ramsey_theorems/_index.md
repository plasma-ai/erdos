## Source and scope

The copy read for this card is the published scan from the [Rényi
archive](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1973-08.pdf), with 23 physical pages
matching printed pp. 341–363. The paper was received on 5 November 1972; this is
distinct from publication in May 1973. The scan's later scanning metadata is not
a mathematical revision date. The [source record](source_record.json) records
this edition. The scan prints "Copyright © 1973 by Academic Press, Inc. All
rights of reproduction in any form reserved" on its first page, every other
right reserved.

The proof pages reconstruct the source's geometric, affine, field and
product arguments, with their prerequisites and compressed steps expanded.
The introductory Ramsey, van der Waerden and Gallai theorems, foundational
compactness, and Theorem 5's negative seven-color construction are identified
at their exact external scopes. The separate surface-embedding thesis
comment is not treated as a complete proof. The
[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/external_inputs|external-input inventory]]
and [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/historical_questions|historical questions]]
make these boundaries explicit.

## Small configurations and explicit colorings

[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_5|Theorem 5]]
distinguishes the positive three-color unit-pair statement from the external
negative seven-color statement. The exact seven-point spindle has one
canonical proof in the Paper II folder. The two-circle construction of
[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_6|Theorem 6]]
forces an equilateral triangle in three dimensions with two colors.
[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_7|Theorem 7]]
uses a monochromatic four-cycle to force a square, and
[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_8|Theorem 8]]
transfers a monochromatic equilateral triangle to any prescribed three-point
configuration, including collinear triples.

[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_9|Theorem 9]]
and [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/corollary_10|Corollary 10]]
give the planar three-scale and 30–60–90 triangle deductions, and the
paper then conjectures, in the
[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/conjecture_p347|unnumbered conjecture on p. 347]],
that every non-equilateral triangle is forced in two-colorings of the plane.
[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_11|Theorem 11]]
uses circles and a spherical band for the four-point L configuration.
[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_12|Theorem 12]]
records the distinct four-, three- and two-color shell obstructions for
three, four and six equally spaced collinear points.

## Spherical necessity and field colorings

The central [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_13|Theorem 13]]
proves that a Ramsey set must be spherical, with a color count independent
of ambient dimension. Its exact prerequisites are the
[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/lemma_14|affine-relation criterion]],
the [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/lemma_15|real scalar obstruction]],
and the complete [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_16|field-coloring theorem]].
The last proof includes the rational, transcendental, finite-algebraic and
arbitrary-field cases, rather than referring out for its essential algebra.

[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_17|Theorem 17]]
shows why the linear difference obstruction does not extend to arbitrary
higher-degree forms. [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/corollary_18|Corollary 18]]
gives an explicit $(2k)^k$ color bound under algebraic independence of the
coefficient ratios; [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/corollary_19|Corollary 19]]
handles irrational collinear triples with sixteen colors.

## Products, bricks and few-color copies

The [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/compactness|finite-witness reduction]]
and [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_20|product theorem]]
prove Ramsey closure under orthogonal products. The
[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/brick_corollaries|brick corollaries]]
give all brick subsets, including the regular-simplex centroid examples.
[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_23|Theorem 23]]
constructs four-point brick realizations from squared triangle inequalities;
the [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/nonobtuse_five_point_obstruction|five-point obstruction]]
shows why the same condition is insufficient for larger configurations.
[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_24|Theorem 24]]
proves sphere-Ramsey bricks for every sufficiently large radius and dimension.

In the source's distinct $\ell$-Ramsey convention, a forced copy may use
at most $\ell$ colors. The
[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/lemma_26|common-bisector criterion]]
and [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/lemma_27|paired-color separation]]
prove [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_25|Theorem 25]]:
not fitting in $\ell-1$ concentric spheres precludes $m$-Ramsey for
$m<\ell$. The [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/finite_sphere_obstruction|finite polynomial witness]]
supplies the compressed extension to infinite sets in a fixed Euclidean
space. [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_28|Theorem 28]]
multiplies the allowed color counts under finite orthogonal products.

## Source precision and later connections

Theorem 5 says the seven-color pair-forcing statement is false, and
Theorem 25 uses the strict endpoint $m<\ell$. Several source compressions
also need care. Theorem 13
uses individual squared norms as colored scalar variables; Corollary 19's
fractional errors are signed; the product theorem's pattern exponent is
the finite witness size rather than its ambient dimension. The five-point
nonobtuse example is affinely dependent, despite its informal “simplex”
name. Every correction or expansion is explained on its result page and
is distinguished from an author-issued erratum.

The original Ramsey product and spherical obstruction connect to the
[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1975_euclidean_ramsey_theorems_ii/_index|second paper]],
the [[discrete_geometry/frankl_1990_partition_property_simplices_euclidean_space/_index|Frankl–Rödl exponential simplex method]],
and the distinct recent proofs of
[[discrete_geometry/moore_2026_pyramid_ramsey_base/_index|Moore]],
[[discrete_geometry/mirabi_2026_one_point_extensions_euclidean_ramsey_sets/_index|Mirabi]]
and [[discrete_geometry/ivan_2026_generalised_prisms_euclidean_ramsey_theory/_index|Ivan–Leader–Walters]].
Historical questions in this paper are not silently promoted to current
open-problem claims. No formal verification or Lean build is claimed.

**Bears on.**

- [[../wiki/problems/discrete_geometry/E0174/_index|#174]]:
  [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_13|Theorem 13]]
  (p. 349) proves that every Ramsey set is spherical, a necessary condition;
  [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_20|Theorem 20]]
  (p. 357) and the
  [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/brick_corollaries|brick corollaries]]
  (pp. 357–358) prove that products of Ramsey sets are Ramsey and that every
  subset of the vertices of a brick is Ramsey, so being such a subset is a
  sufficient condition (p. 360). The paper leaves open whether
  either condition is both necessary and sufficient (p. 360); it does not
  characterize the Ramsey sets.
- [[../wiki/problems/discrete_geometry/E0173/_index|#173]]:
  [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/corollary_10|Corollary 10]]
  (p. 346) shows that the $30^\circ$–$60^\circ$ right triangle has a
  monochromatic copy in every two-coloring of the plane, and
  [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_9|Theorem 9]]
  (p. 346) that no such coloring misses the equilateral triangles of all
  three sides $d$, $\sqrt3d$, $2d$. The
  [[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/conjecture_p347|conjecture on p. 347]]
  is posed there, unproved; its two clauses together imply the problem's
  statement. None of these decides the problem.

The paper's results concern copies in one color, or in few colors, with every
color treated alike; none addresses the planar one-sided questions of Problems
188 and 214, in which one color class must avoid unit distance.
