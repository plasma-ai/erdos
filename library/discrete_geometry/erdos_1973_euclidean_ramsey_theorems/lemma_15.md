**Source.** Published pp. 349–350, Lemma 15; proved through Theorem 16 on pp. 351–354 (published scan).

**Statement.** Given real $c_1,\ldots,c_k$ and $b\ne0$, some finite coloring
of $\mathbb R$ has no monochromatic solution of
$$
\sum_{i=1}^k c_i(x_i-x_0)=b.
$$

**Complete proof.** Apply
[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1973_euclidean_ramsey_theorems/theorem_16]]
to $F=\mathbb R$ and these coefficients. A monochromatic solution of the
displayed equation would be a forbidden paired solution on setting
$x_i'=x_0$ for every $i$. This proves the lemma. $\square$
