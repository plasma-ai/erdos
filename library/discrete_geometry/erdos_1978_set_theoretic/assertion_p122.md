**Source.** The passage on p. 122 of P. Erdős, *Set-theoretic,
measure-theoretic, combinatorial, and number-theoretic problems concerning
point sets in Euclidean space*, Real Anal. Exchange 4 (1978/79), no. 2,
113--138, doi:10.2307/44151159, as identified on the
[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1978_set_theoretic/_index|source card]]. Pages are
those of the journal print; the statements are unnumbered.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the passage was read clause by clause on
p. 122.

## Statement

Let $S$ be a plane set of infinite planar measure. The paper asserts, as not
difficult to see, the following (p. 122).

- For every real $a>0$, $S$ contains three points $x_1,x_2,x_3$ such that the
  triangle $(x_1,x_2,x_3)$ has area $a$.
- The hypothesis may be weakened, by a theorem of a reader of the
  *Matematikai Lapok*, where Erdős posed the statement as a problem: it
  suffices that some line meets $S$ in a set of positive linear measure and
  that $S$ has points arbitrarily far from that line.
- The triangle of area $a$ may be taken isosceles, or right-angled. More
  generally, in the paper's words "slightly vaguely", one condition besides
  the area may be imposed on the triangle.
- On the other hand, some plane set of infinite planar measure contains no
  equilateral triangle of unit area, which the paper calls very easy to see.

## Proof pointer

None is printed. For the first statement the paper says the proof is an easy
consequence of the Lebesgue density theorem and leaves it to the reader; for
the isosceles and right-angled forms and for the equilateral example it gives
no argument.
