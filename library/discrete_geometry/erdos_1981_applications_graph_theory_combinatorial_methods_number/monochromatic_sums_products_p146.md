## Statement

**Hindman's theorem** (Section 2, p. 146). Graham and Rothschild
conjectured that if the integers are split into two classes, there is
always an infinite sequence $a_1<a_2<\cdots$ all of whose sums
$\sum_k\epsilon_ka_k$, $\epsilon_k=0$ or $1$ (display (3)), are in the
same class; Hindman proved it.

**Question (4)** (p. 146). Is there, for every split of the integers into
two classes, an infinite sequence $a_1<a_2<\cdots$ such that all the sums
$\sum_k\epsilon_ka_k$ and all the products $\prod_ka_k^{\epsilon_k}$,
$\epsilon_k=0$ or $1$, are in the same class?

**Question (5)** (pp. 146-147). If (4) is false, does it remain true that
for each $n$ there is a sequence $1<a_1<\cdots<a_n$ such that all the $2^n$
sums and products
$\sum_{k=0}^n\epsilon_ka_k$, $\prod_{k=0}^na_k^{\epsilon_k}$,
$\epsilon_k=0$ or $1$, are in the same class? (The index range $k=0$ to
$n$ is as printed, for a sequence starting at $a_1$.)

**Report** (p. 147). Hindman disproved (4), in a paper to appear in the
J. of Combinatorial Theory; (5) remains open for $n\ge3$.

**Source.** P. Erdős, *Some applications of graph theory and combinatorial
methods to number theory and geometry*, Algebraic methods in graph theory,
Vol. I, II (Szeged, 1978), Colloq. Math. Soc. János Bolyai 25, North-Holland,
Amsterdam-New York, 1981, 137--148 (MR 83g:05001); Section 2, displays
(3)--(5), pp. 146--147.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: displays (3)--(5) and the report were
read clause by clause on the page images of pp. 146-147. Nothing here is
proved in the paper.
