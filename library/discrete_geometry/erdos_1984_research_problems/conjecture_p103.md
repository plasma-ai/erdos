**Source.** Problem 36, p. 103, of P. Erdős, *Research problems*, Period.
Math. Hungar. 15 (1984), no. 1, 101--103, doi:10.1007/BF02109375. The
edition read is named on the

## Statement

**Context.** The constant $c$ is the absolute constant in
[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1984_research_problems/theorem_p102_beck|Beck's theorem as reported]]:
$n$ points with property $P_{n-k}$, $2\le k\le n$, determine at least $ckn$
distinct lines.

**The remark** (p. 103). Erdős writes that the value of $c$ given by Beck
seems too small, and, quoted: "It would be tempting to conjecture that
$c = 1/6$." He states that $c\le1/6$ follows from Sylvester's result,
citing Burr, Grünbaum and Sloane, and adds that perhaps the conjecture
$c=1/6$ is too optimistic and that one should first look for a
counterexample.

## Proof pointer

None in the paper; the bound $c\le1/6$ is attributed to Sylvester's result
without argument, and Sylvester's estimate is display (3) on
[[discrete_geometry/erdos_1984_research_problems/conjecture_p101|conjecture_p101]].
