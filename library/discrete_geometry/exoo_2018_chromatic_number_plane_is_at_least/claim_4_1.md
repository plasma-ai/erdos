## Statement

**Claim 4.1** (p. 6, quoted). "Let $A$, $B$ and $C$ be the vertices of an
equilateral triangle of side length $1/\sqrt{3}$. There exists a unit distance
graph of order 627 containing $A$, $B$ and $C$ among its vertices which cannot
be properly 4-colored if points $A$, $B$ and $C$ are identically colored."

This is assertion (c) of the paper's three-step argument (p. 2).

## Proof pointer

Pages 7--10. The paper starts from a unit distance graph $G_{51}$ of order
$51$ whose first three vertices form an equilateral triangle of side
$1/\sqrt3$ (p. 7); it reports $13357$ proper 4-colorings of $G_{51}$ in which
these three vertices share a color (p. 8). For each such coloring, vertices
whose colors are forced (circumcenters at distance $1$ from three differently
colored vertices) are added until two points at distance $1$ share a color.
A common set of $576$ added vertices eliminates all $13357$ colorings, giving
the graph $G_{627}$ with $2982$ unit edges and an automorphism group of order
$6$ (p. 8). Non-4-colorability with $A$, $B$, $C$ identically colored is
checked by a depth-first coloring search (Algorithm 1, p. 10). The Appendix
(pp. 11--12) lists $109$ vertices from which all $627$ are generated by
rotations through multiples of $2\pi/3$ and reflections in the lines $y=0$ and
$\sqrt3x\pm y=0$; the full coordinates are posted at the paper's reference
[9].
