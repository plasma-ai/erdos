## Statement

Setting (p. 7). Section 4 works in $\mathbb R^d$ with a norm
$\lVert\cdot\rVert$, congruence being as on the page of
[[discrete_geometry/fiscus_2024_new_class_geometrically_defined_hypergraphs_arising/theorem_4_3|Theorem 4.3]].
Let $Q$ be a collection of finite subsets of $\mathbb R^d$, each with at least
two elements, and let $E_0$ be the sets congruent to some $f\in Q$. For each
positive integer $t$ let $B_t$ be the sets $f\cup T$ with $f\in Q$,
$T\subseteq\mathbb R^d$, $f\cap T=\emptyset$ and $\lvert T\rvert=t$, and let
$E_t$ be the sets congruent to some member of $B_t$. $Q$ need not be finite.

**Theorem 4.4** (p. 7). With $t$ a positive integer and $Q$, $B_t$, $E_0$,
$E_t$ as above, let $\mathcal H_0=(\mathbb R^d,E_0)$ and
$\mathcal H_t=(\mathbb R^d,E_t)$. If $\chi(\mathcal H_0)<\infty$, then
$\chi(\mathcal H_t)=\chi(\mathcal H_0)$.

The authors present the section as giving $m$-uniform hypergraphs with the
chromatic number of the unit-distance graph of $(\mathbb R^d,\lVert\cdot\rVert)$
by giving up edge sets generated by finitely many $m$-gons, and say their
method does not provably give hypergraphs equivalent to that graph (p. 7).

## Proof pointer

pp. 7--8. Every edge of $\mathcal H_t$ contains an edge of $\mathcal H_{t-1}$,
so $\chi(\mathcal H_t)\le\chi(\mathcal H_{t-1})\le\chi(\mathcal H_0)$, and it
suffices to show $\chi(\mathcal H_t)=\chi(\mathcal H_{t-1})$. If a proper
$k$-coloring of $\mathcal H_t$ had $k<\chi(\mathcal H_{t-1})$, some edge $B$ of
$\mathcal H_{t-1}$ would be monochromatic, so no point outside $B$ could take
its color. A translate of any finite vertex set missing $B$ is then colored
properly with $k-1$ colors, and Theorem 2.1 with translation invariance gives
$\chi(\mathcal H_t)\le k-1$, a contradiction.
