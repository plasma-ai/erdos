## Statement

**Conjecture 11.1.3** (p. 282). For every triangle $T$,
$\mathbb{E}^2\not\xrightarrow{3}T$: there is a partition
$\mathbb{E}^2=C_1\cup C_2\cup C_3$ in which no class contains a triangle
congruent to $T$.

Here $\xrightarrow{r}$ is the relation of the
[[discrete_geometry/graham_2017_euclidean_ramsey_theory/conjecture_11_1_1|Conjecture 11.1.1 page]]
(p. 281), and the printed statement is the negated arrow with $3$ over it.
Unlike Conjectures 11.1.1 and 11.1.2 it is a negative statement, and it
covers every triangle, equilateral or not.

## Scope

This is a conjecture, not a result proved in the chapter, and the chapter does
not say whether degenerate triples count as triangles here. In three
dimensions,
[[discrete_geometry/graham_2017_euclidean_ramsey_theory/theorem_11_1_4|Theorem 11.1.4(c)]]
reports the opposite for three colors and every nondegenerate right triangle.

**Source.** R. L. Graham, Euclidean Ramsey theory, Chapter 11 of
J. E. Goodman, J. O'Rourke and C. D. Tóth (eds.), Handbook of Discrete and
Computational Geometry, 3rd edition, CRC Press, Boca Raton, FL, 2017; the
notation on p. 281 and the conjecture on p. 282. Pages are those printed on
the edition named on the
[[discrete_geometry/graham_2017_euclidean_ramsey_theory/_index|source card]].
