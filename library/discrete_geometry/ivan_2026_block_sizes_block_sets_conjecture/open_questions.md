***

These are the statements posed in the selected 2026 publication, with the
same labels in arXiv v1. They are recorded as source questions; this page is
not an independent current-status search or a proof of them.

**Conjecture 1, published p. 2.** For all positive integers $m,k$ and every
template $T$ over $[m]$, there exist positive integers $n,d$
such that every $k$-coloring of $[m]^n$ contains a monochromatic
degree-$d$ block set with template $T$. Here degree is the common block
size, and both $n,d$ are chosen before the coloring. The paper attributes
this conjecture to
[[discrete_geometry/leader_2012_transitive_sets_euclidean_ramsey_theory/conjectures|Leader–Russell–Walters]].

**Conjecture 4, published p. 7.** For every positive integer $m$ and every
template $T$ over $[m]$, there is a $d$ such that, for every
$k$, some $n$ gives the same conclusion. Thus $d$ may depend on $T$ but
not on $k$. The print does not say here that $d$ is positive; the degree
is a block size, which Conjecture 1 takes positive, and degree $0$ would
make the statement trivial. The
[[discrete_geometry/ivan_2026_block_sizes_block_sets_conjecture/fixed_pattern|fixed-pattern deduction]]
proves that one could also fix the order pattern before $k$.
[[discrete_geometry/ivan_2026_block_sizes_block_sets_conjecture/theorem_2|Theorem 2]]
rules out a bound independent of $T$; it does not refute Conjecture 4.

**Question 5, published p. 8.** Given $k$, do there exist $d$
and $n$ such that every $k$-coloring of $\mathbb Z^n$ has a
monochromatic triple $x-v,x,x+v$ with $\|v\|_1=d$?
The linear progression condition is essential: a triple merely having
distances $d,d,2d$ does not suffice in this norm.

**Question 6, published p. 9.** Given $r$, $t$ and $k$, do there
exist $d$ and $n$ such that every $k$-coloring of
$\mathbb Z^n$ has pairwise disjointly supported vectors
$u_1,\ldots,u_t$, each of one-norm $d$, and a monochromatic translate
of

$$
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^t\lambda_i u_i:
\lambda_i\in\mathbb Z,\ \sum_i|\lambda_i|\le r\right\}?
$$

This is the coefficient ball of radius $r$ generated by the $u_i$.
Disjoint support gives
$\|\sum_i\lambda_i u_i\|_1=d\sum_i|\lambda_i|$; its ambient
one-norm radius is therefore $rd$. The case $r=t=1$ is precisely
Question 5. Neither question as printed says that $d$ is positive; both
are read with $d\ge1$, since $d=0$ allows $v=0$, or zero vectors $u_i$,
and makes them trivial. The parameters $k$ and $r,t$ are read as
positive integers.

The paper identifies $112233$ and $12345$ as unresolved templates in its
discussion. It cites earlier work for $1\,2^p3^q$ and for $1234$;
those are external results, not additional same-paper proofs here.
Its suggestions that progress on Questions 5–6 might aid $112233$ are
explicitly exploratory, not established implications.

The final paragraph, published p. 9 and arXiv v1, p. 10, recalls the cyclic
kite $\{(\pm1,0),(a,\pm\sqrt{1-a^2})\}$ for transcendental
$a\in(-1,1)$. Its failure to embed in a finite transitive set is the
external [[discrete_geometry/leader_2011_transitive_sets_cyclic_quadrilaterals/corollary_2|LRW Corollary 2]].
The paper asks whether this kite is Ramsey as a test distinguishing the
two rival conjectures. The explicit interval makes the real, nondegenerate
domain visible. This is a dated source question and an imported
nonembedding result, with no additional proof or current-status claim here.
