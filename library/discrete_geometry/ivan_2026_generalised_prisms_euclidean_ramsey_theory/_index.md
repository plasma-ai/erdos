**Main results.** The paper's main theorem is
[[discrete_geometry/ivan_2026_generalised_prisms_euclidean_ramsey_theory/theorem_1|Theorem 1]] (p. 2): if one finite group $G$ of isometries of
$\mathbb R^d$ acts transitively on each of two finite sets $X$ and $Y$, then
for every $\lambda\ne0$ the generalised prism
$(X\times\{0\})\cup(Y\times\{\lambda\})$ is contained, up to congruence,
in a finite transitive set, and in a soluble one when $G$ is soluble, so that
it is then Ramsey by Kříž's theorem. Its proof runs through
[[discrete_geometry/ivan_2026_generalised_prisms_euclidean_ramsey_theory/lemma_2|Lemma 2]] (p. 3), which raises the height, and
[[discrete_geometry/ivan_2026_generalised_prisms_euclidean_ramsey_theory/lemma_3|Lemma 3]] (p. 4), which gives arbitrarily small heights.
[[discrete_geometry/ivan_2026_generalised_prisms_euclidean_ramsey_theory/corollary_4|Corollary 4]] (p. 6) makes a pyramid over a finite
transitive base subtransitive, and subsoluble, hence Ramsey, when the base is
subsoluble; [[discrete_geometry/ivan_2026_generalised_prisms_euclidean_ramsey_theory/corollary_5|Corollary 5]] (p. 6) makes two concentric
regular polygons in parallel planes Ramsey. Section 3 (pp. 7–9) states the
block sets conjecture of Leader, Russell and Walters as
[[discrete_geometry/ivan_2026_generalised_prisms_euclidean_ramsey_theory/conjecture_6|Conjecture 6]] (p. 7) and proves
[[discrete_geometry/ivan_2026_generalised_prisms_euclidean_ramsey_theory/theorem_7|Theorem 7]] (p. 8): the template $1223333$ has no transitive
soluble symmetry group but embeds in $12233333$, which has one. Section 4
(pp. 9–10) poses [[discrete_geometry/ivan_2026_generalised_prisms_euclidean_ramsey_theory/conjecture_8|Conjecture 8]] (p. 9), that one point
added off the hyperplane of any Ramsey set gives a Ramsey set, and asks
about two prism-type configurations that Theorem 1 does not cover.

**Points read differently from the print.** Each is recorded on the page
named. Lemma 2's statement defines $Z'$ with $\lambda$ where $\lambda''$ is
meant, and its soluble case needs a chosen soluble transitive group rather
than the full symmetry group ([[discrete_geometry/ivan_2026_generalised_prisms_euclidean_ramsey_theory/lemma_2|Lemma 2]]). Lemma 3's proof
asserts that $X$ and $Y$ have the same centre, which is neither true in
general nor needed ([[discrete_geometry/ivan_2026_generalised_prisms_euclidean_ramsey_theory/lemma_3|Lemma 3]]). Corollary 4's printed proof
covers the subsoluble clause only when a soluble group acts on the base
itself; the corpus supplies the missing step
([[discrete_geometry/ivan_2026_generalised_prisms_euclidean_ramsey_theory/corollary_4|Corollary 4]],
[[discrete_geometry/ivan_2026_generalised_prisms_euclidean_ramsey_theory/isometric_extension|isometric extension]]). The p. 7 remark that, for
algebraically independent values, the geometric set of a template has only
coordinate-permutation symmetries fails for $1122$
([[discrete_geometry/ivan_2026_generalised_prisms_euclidean_ramsey_theory/template_symmetry_qualification|counterexample]]); Theorem 7 uses
only the coordinate action. Theorem 7's diagram repeats $\alpha^5$ where
$\alpha^6$ is meant, and its group list names three groups as "neither".
The p. 9 two-colour remark calls the second sphere blue where the argument
forces it red ([[discrete_geometry/ivan_2026_generalised_prisms_euclidean_ramsey_theory/two_color_observation|two-colour observation]]).

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/discrete_geometry/E0174/_index|#174]]
(characterise the Ramsey sets): Theorem 1 and Corollaries 4 and 5 give
families of subsoluble, hence Ramsey, sets (generalised prisms on two orbits
of one soluble finite isometry group, pyramids over finite transitive
subsoluble bases, prisms on concentric regular polygons), a sufficient
condition and not a characterisation; Conjecture 8 conjectures a closure property of the Ramsey
sets, which later papers address; Conjecture 6 and Theorem 7 concern the block
sets conjecture, which implies that every subtransitive set is Ramsey. The
paper does not decide the problem.
