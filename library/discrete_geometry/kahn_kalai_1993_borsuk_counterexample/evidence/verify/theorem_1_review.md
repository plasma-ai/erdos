## Mathematical review

For `m=4k`, unordered balanced cuts number
`binom(m,m/2)/2`. Their crossing-edge incidence vectors have constant weight
`m^2/4`, so they lie in an affine hyperplane of dimension
`d_m=binom(m,2)-1`. Full affine-span equality is neither asserted nor needed.
If `r=|A intersect C|`, the common support has size
`r^2+(2k-r)^2=2(r-k)^2+2k^2`, and the squared Euclidean distance is
`m^2/2-2|S_P intersect S_Q|`. The diameter is exactly `m/2`, attained exactly
when `r=k`. The candidate correctly identifies the source's printed “minimal
distance” as a one-word defect: minimum support intersection gives maximum
Euclidean distance.

For a smaller-diameter subfamily, choosing either one side of each unordered
cut gives distinct `m/2`-subsets with intersection `m/4` forbidden. Kahn--Kalai
Theorem 2 therefore bounds the subfamily by
`2 binom(m-1,m/4-1)`. Together with the cut count and
`binom(m-1,m/4-1)=binom(m,m/4)/4`, this yields exactly

`f(d_m) >= Q_m = binom(m,m/2)/binom(m,m/4)`.

Stirling's formula gives
`log Q_m = m(3 log(3)/4-log(2))+O(log m)`, while
`sqrt(d_m)=m/sqrt(2)+O(1)`. I independently replayed every rational series
endpoint in the author's numerical certificate. The certified lower bound
`0.184996155349592644198816229694763579324` exceeds the certified upper
bound `0.18481843699254182520` for `log(1.203)`. Hence the limiting base is
strictly above 1.203, as claimed.

For an arbitrary large integer `D`, the inverse
`x_D=(1+sqrt(8D+9))/8` satisfies `d(x_D)=D`. If `p_D` is the largest prime at
most `x_D`, the prime number theorem gives `p_D/x_D -> 1`; monotonicity of
`d(x)=8x^2-2x-1` gives `e_D=d(p_D)<=D`, and `e_D/D -> 1`. Euclidean embedding
then transfers the eligible-dimension lower bound. The strict base slack
`log(1.2)/log(1.203)<1` gives
`(1.203)^sqrt(e_D) >= (1.2)^sqrt(D)` eventually. No quantitative prime-gap
result is being used.

Finally, `(1.2)^sqrt(d)>d+1` eventually. A hypothetical cover of the finite
configuration by `d+1` smaller-diameter sets can be intersected with the
configuration and disjointized by assigning each point to one containing
set. This produces the forbidden partition, and a common rescaling gives
diameter one. The transfer matches the exact union wording of E505.
