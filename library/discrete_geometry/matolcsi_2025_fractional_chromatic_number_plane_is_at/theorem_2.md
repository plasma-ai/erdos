## Statement

Setting (pp. 4--5). Graphs are finite unit-distance graphs $G\subseteq\mathbb R^2$.
The independence ratio of $G$ is $\alpha_1(G)=\alpha(G)/|G|$, with $\alpha(G)$
the independence number (p. 4). The paper defines

$$
\chi_{f,0}(\mathbb R^2)=\sup\{\chi_f(G)\},\qquad
\alpha_1(\mathbb R^2)=\inf\{\alpha_1(G)\},\qquad
\rho(\mathbb R^2)=\frac1{\alpha_1(\mathbb R^2)},
$$

the supremum and infimum over finite $G\subseteq\mathbb R^2$, and calls
$\rho(\mathbb R^2)$ the finitary Hall ratio of the plane (pp. 4--5); $\chi_f(G)$
is the fractional chromatic number (p. 3).

**Theorem 2** (p. 7, quoted). "$\chi_{f,0}(\mathbb R^2)=\rho(\mathbb R^2)$."

Equivalently, $\sup_G\chi_f(G)=\sup_G|G|/\alpha(G)$ over finite planar
unit-distance graphs $G$ (p. 2).

**Consequence stated in the paper** (p. 7). Combined with Corollary 1, Theorem 2
shows that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a finite graph $G\subseteq\mathbb R^2$
whose largest independent subset has size at most $(\frac14+\varepsilon)|G|$,
and the proof shows $G$ can be taken to be a large discrete cube in the lattice
generated by a finite graph $H$ with $\chi_f(H)\approx4$. The deduction uses the
finitary bound $\chi_{f,0}(\mathbb R^2)\ge4$ that the proof of
[[discrete_geometry/matolcsi_2025_fractional_chromatic_number_plane_is_at/corollary_1|Corollary 1]]
establishes. With it the paper records the chain (p. 5)

$$
0.22936\ldots=\delta_{\mathrm{Croft}}\le m_1(\mathbb R^2)\le\alpha_1(\mathbb R^2)\le\frac14,
$$

where $m_1(\mathbb R^2)$ is the measurable independence ratio of the plane.

## Proof pointer

P. 7. The inequality $\chi_{f,0}\ge\rho$ follows from
$\chi_f(G)\,\alpha_1(G)\ge1$ for every finite $G$. For the reverse, fix a finite
$G$, choose a basis $v_1,\dots,v_d$ of the rational span of its vertices, scaled
so that the vertices have integer coordinates bounded by some $k$, and take the
cube $A$ of lattice points with coordinates in $[-N,N]$. $A$ contains an
independent set $B$ with $|B|\ge\alpha_1(\mathbb R^2)|A|$. The traces
$G\cap(B+t)$ over lattice translates $t$, each weighted $1/|B|$, form a
fractional colouring of $G$ of weight at most
$(2N+2k+1)^d/(\alpha_1(\mathbb R^2)(2N+1)^d)$, which tends to
$1/\alpha_1(\mathbb R^2)$ as $N\to\infty$.

## Dependencies

The inequality $\chi_f(G)\,\alpha_1(G)\ge1$ for finite graphs, cited in the
paper.

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/discrete_geometry/E1070/_index|Problem 1070]]: the problem
  asks for the order of $f(n)$, the least independence number of an $n$-point
  planar unit-distance graph, and whether $f(n)\ge n/4$. The consequence above
  gives, for every $\varepsilon>0$, a finite graph with independence ratio at
  most $\frac14+\varepsilon$; disjoint far-apart copies of such graphs then give
  $f(n)\le(\frac14+o(1))n$, a step the paper does not write out. It does not
  decide whether $f(n)\ge n/4$.
