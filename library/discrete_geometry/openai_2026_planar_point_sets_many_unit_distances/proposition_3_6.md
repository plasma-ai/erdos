## Statement

With

$$
G=\operatorname{Gal}(F^{\mathrm{ur},3}/F),
$$

let $E\subseteq F^{\mathrm{ur},3}$ be the fixed field of $\Phi(G)$; it is the
finite extension of $F$ with $\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)=G/\Phi(G)$. Given a
positive integer $t$ and any finite set of rational primes to avoid, one can
pick $t$ distinct rational primes $q_1,\ldots,q_t$ outside that set, each of
which splits completely in the Galois closure of $E(i)$ over $\mathbb Q$.

Any rational prime $q_b$ with that splitting property satisfies:

1. $q_b\equiv1\pmod4$;
2. $q_b$ splits completely in $F$;
3. at each prime $v$ of $F$ dividing $q_b$, Frobenius is the identity in
   $G/\Phi(G)$, so any Frobenius element of $G$ at $v$ lies in $\Phi(G)$.

## Deductions and application

The Frattini quotient is finite because $G$ is finitely generated, so the
normal closure of $E(i)$ is a finite Galois extension of $\mathbb Q$.
Chebotarev supplies infinitely many rational primes with identity Frobenius
in this extension; deleting a finite set leaves enough to choose $t$.

Complete splitting in the normal closure implies complete splitting in its
subfields $F$, $E$, and $\mathbb Q(i)$. An unramified rational prime splits
in $\mathbb Q(i)$ exactly when it is $1$ modulo $4$, giving the first two
properties. For a prime $v\mid q_b$ of $F$, complete splitting in $E/F$
makes its Frobenius trivial in
$\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)=G/\Phi(G)$, giving the third property.

In Proposition 3.8 the excluded set contains every prime dividing
$3D$, where $D=\prod_i r_i$. Because $F/\mathbb Q$ is cubic and each
$q_b$ splits completely, there are exactly three primes $v$ of $F$ above
each $q_b$. The proof kills one Frobenius representative for each of the
resulting $3t$ primes. In an unramified pro-extension the decomposition group
is topologically generated by Frobenius; killing that element forces complete
splitting in every finite quotient of the new tower.
