## Statement

Fix the Fourier transform
$\widehat f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R^2}f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}\,dx$, which is
real and radial for real radial integrable $f$. A Schwartz function is
smooth, with every derivative decaying faster than any inverse power of
$|x|$. **Theorem 1.1 (Sharp Fourier certificate).** The manuscript
claims (p. 3): "There is a real radial Schwartz function
$f:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R$ such that"

$$
\widehat f(0)=1,\qquad f(0)=\frac{2}{\sqrt3},\qquad
\widehat f(\xi)\ge0\ \ (\xi\in\mathbb R^2),\qquad
f(x)\le0\ \ (|x|\ge1).
$$

The manuscript says that the theorem "gives an affirmative answer to the
two-dimensional case of Cohn and Elkies's Conjecture 7.3" (p. 3), a
conjecture asking for auxiliary functions that make their linear-programming
bound sharp: their Theorem 3.1 applies to a Schwartz function and to arbitrary
packings, and for disks of radius $1/2$ gives the covered-area density bound
$\operatorname{vol}B(0,1/2)\,f(0)/\widehat f(0)$, which equals
$(\pi/4)(2/\sqrt3)=\pi/(2\sqrt3)$, the density of the lattice generated by
$(1,0)$ and $(1/2,\sqrt3/2)$. The
equal-origin form of their Theorem 3.2 is
$g(x)=(\sqrt3/2)\,f((\sqrt3/2)^{1/2}x)$, with $g(0)=\widehat g(0)=1$,
$\widehat g\ge0$ and $g(x)\le0$ for $|x|\ge(2/\sqrt3)^{1/2}$. The proof also
identifies the zeros within the sign domains: writing $f(r)$ and
$\widehat f(\rho)$ for the radial profiles,

$$
\{r^2:\ r\ge1,\ f(r)=0\}=\mathcal N,\qquad
\{\rho^2:\ \rho\ge0,\ \widehat f(\rho)=0\}=\tfrac43\mathcal N,
$$

where $\mathcal N=(12\mathbb Z+\{0,1,3,4,7,9\})\cap(0,\infty)$, the zero at
$r=1$ is simple with $f'(1)=-2/(3\sqrt3)$, and every other listed zero is
exactly double (display (5.5)). Two qualifications are the manuscript's own:
the density bound so recovered is "the classical packing-density theorem
recovered from the new Fourier certificate, not a new density claim"
(p. 24), and $g$ has zeros off the shells of the covolume-one triangular
lattice, so it "does not meet the additional zero-set requirement" of Cohn
and Elkies's Conjecture 8.1 (p. 3).

## Proof pointer

The proof occupies Sections 2--5 with the finite computations in Appendix A.
Section 2 builds the family: with $b=\sqrt3/2$, $h=2/5$ and $s=b|x|^2$, a
cardinal function $p=PR$ is the trigonometric vanishing factor
$P(s)=\prod_{a\in A}(2\sin(\pi(s-a)/12))^2$, $A=\{0,1,3,4,7,9\}$, times a
rational function $R$ with a double and a simple pole at each node of
$\{0\}\cup\mathcal N$; Lemma 2.1 writes $p$ as the Fourier--Stieltjes
transform of a finite measure on $[-1,1]$, so that $p(n)=Q_nc_n$ and
$p'(n)=Q_n(d_n+D_nc_n)$ with explicit periodic $Q_n>7/4$, $|D_n|<53/25$, and
Proposition 2.2 shows that $e^{-\pi hs}p(s)$ is radial Schwartz with
transform $e^{-\pi hs}K_p(s)$, where the kernel $K_p$ carries the extra
Gaussian decay $\operatorname{Im}z(t)\ge26/435$. The node set covers all
positive values of $j^2+jk+k^2$, the shell coordinates of the covolume-one
triangular lattice $\Lambda$, which is a rotation of its dual. Two such
functions with fixed node-zero data are coupled as $H_i=p_i+K_{p_{3-i}}$, so
that $F=e^{-\pi hs}H_1$ has transform $e^{-\pi hs}H_2$, and the conditions
$H_1(n)=H_2(n)=H_2'(n)=0$, $H_1'(n)=-Q_n\mathbf 1_{\{n=1\}}$ become coupled
coefficient equations (Lemma 2.4). Section 3 solves them on
$\ell^1(\mathcal N;\mathbb R^2)$: envelope bounds make the operator's rows
beyond node $100$ tiny, Lemma 3.2 (the Appendix A interval computation)
certifies the $6\times6$ block on $\{1,3,4\}$, the coupling to nodes
$7$--$100$, and the residuals of the tabulated $51$-node lists, and Lemma 3.3
assembles a bounded inverse by two Schur complements; Proposition 3.4
concludes that unique exact lists exist within $.00003$ of the table. Section
4 proves the signs for the exact functions: on $84$ half-gaps between nodes
and gap midpoints up to $41.5$, the Taylor quotient of the exact function at
the node's zero order is compared with the deleted-jet Taylor polynomial of
the tabulated function, whose degree-$28$ Bernstein coefficients are
positive (Lemma 4.1, Appendix A), with the coefficient and truncation errors
bounded by the envelopes; beyond $41.5$, a lower bound for the rational part,
a second-derivative bound for the remainder and Lemma 4.2's quadratic lower
bound $P(s)\ge.68(s-m)^2$ give a positive margin. Proposition 4.3 collects
the strict signs and the exact zero orders. Section 5 fixes the
normalization: Poisson summation on $\Lambda$ for the dilations of $F$ gives
an identity in the dilation parameter whose value and derivative at $1$,
using only the exact jets at the nodes, force
$F(0)=\widehat F(0)=6Q_1e^{-\pi h}>0$ (Lemma 5.1); the final
$f(x)=F(x/\sqrt b)/(bF(0))$ then has
$\widehat f(0)=1$, $f(0)=1/b=2/\sqrt3$, and the signs of Proposition 4.3
transported by $s=|x|^2$ and $s=b^2|\xi|^2$, which is the theorem. The
hypotheses of Cohn and Elkies's Theorem 3.1 are checked in Section 5.3 only
for the density consequence, not for the theorem itself.

## Dependencies

The two-dimensional Gaussian Fourier transform $e^{-\pi a|x|^2}\mapsto
a^{-1}e^{-\pi|\xi|^2/a}$ for $\operatorname{Re}a>0$ and Poisson summation on
a planar lattice with termwise differentiation (derived in the text); the
Bernstein range enclosure (Titi and Garloff 2019, Section 3, with the text's
own elementary proof); the first Fejér quadrature rule (Waldvogel 2006, with
exactness and positivity proved in the text); Arb ball arithmetic (Johansson
2017) through `python-flint` at $192$ bits for the computation behind Lemma
3.2 and Lemma 4.1; Cohn and Elkies 2003, Theorem 3.1, for the density
consequence stated after the theorem; and Sardari 2021, Theorem 1.7, for the
remark that uniqueness holds only within the coefficient family. External
premises are taken at statement level; none was checked here, and the
interval computation was not run here.
