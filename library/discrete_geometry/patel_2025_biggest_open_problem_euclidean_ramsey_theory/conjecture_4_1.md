**Source.** Conjecture 4.1, p. 10, Section 4, of Nikhil Patel, *The Biggest
Open Problem in Euclidean Ramsey Theory*, University of Chicago
Mathematics REU 2025 paper (dated August 21, 2025), as named on the
[[discrete_geometry/patel_2025_biggest_open_problem_euclidean_ramsey_theory/_index|source card]]; labels and pages are the paper's own.

## Statement

Setting (Definition 1.2, p. 2). For $r\in\mathbb N$, a finite set
$X\subset\mathbb R^d$ is $r$-Ramsey if every $r$-coloring of $\mathbb R^n$
contains a monochromatic congruent copy of $X$ for sufficiently large $n$,
and Ramsey if it is $r$-Ramsey for all $r\in\mathbb N$. Copies are
congruent copies, images of $X$ under an isometry; scaled copies are not
counted (p. 2).

**Conjecture 4.1** (p. 10). "All 4-point subsets of the circle are Ramsey."

The paper poses it as the natural next case after
[[discrete_geometry/patel_2025_biggest_open_problem_euclidean_ramsey_theory/theorem_2_12|Theorem 2.12]], all three-point subsets of a circle
being Ramsey, and attributes no source to it. It is a special case of
[[discrete_geometry/patel_2025_biggest_open_problem_euclidean_ramsey_theory/conjecture_3_1|Conjecture 3.1]]. The paper explains (p. 10) why it
is hard: by Leader, Russell and Walters, almost all 4-point subsets of a circle
are not subtransitive, and all known results rest either on transitive
solvable symmetry groups or on non-sphericity, neither of which applies to
such sets.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the statement and its setting were read
clause by clause on the printed pages. Nothing here is independently
reviewed.
