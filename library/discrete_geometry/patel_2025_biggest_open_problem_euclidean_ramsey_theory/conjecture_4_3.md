**Source.** Conjecture 4.3, p. 10, Section 4, of Nikhil Patel, *The Biggest
Open Problem in Euclidean Ramsey Theory*, University of Chicago
Mathematics REU 2025 paper (dated August 21, 2025), as named on the
[[discrete_geometry/patel_2025_biggest_open_problem_euclidean_ramsey_theory/_index|source card]]; labels and pages are the paper's own.

## Statement

Setting (Definition 1.2, p. 2). For $r\in\mathbb N$, a finite set
$X\subset\mathbb R^d$ is $r$-Ramsey if every $r$-coloring of $\mathbb R^n$
contains a monochromatic congruent copy of $X$ for sufficiently large $n$,
and Ramsey if it is $r$-Ramsey for all $r\in\mathbb N$. Copies are
congruent copies, images of $X$ under an isometry; scaled copies are not
counted (p. 2).

Transcendental kites (p. 9). For transcendental $a\in(-1,1)$, the cyclic kite
$\{(-1,0),(a,\sqrt{1-a^2}),(1,0),(a,-\sqrt{1-a^2})\}\subset\mathbb R^2$.
The paper reports (p. 9) that Leader, Russell and Walters (Transitive sets and
cyclic quadrilaterals, J. Combinatorics 2 (2011)) gave these as an explicit
class of spherical sets that are not subtransitive.

**Conjecture 4.3** (p. 10). "All transcendental kites are not Ramsey."

That is, no transcendental kite is Ramsey. The paper attributes the conjecture
to Leader, Russell and Walters (2011) and notes (pp. 10--11) that a Ramsey
transcendental kite would disprove [[discrete_geometry/patel_2025_biggest_open_problem_euclidean_ramsey_theory/conjecture_3_3|Conjecture 3.3]],
and a non-Ramsey one would disprove [[discrete_geometry/patel_2025_biggest_open_problem_euclidean_ramsey_theory/conjecture_3_1|Conjecture 3.1]].
