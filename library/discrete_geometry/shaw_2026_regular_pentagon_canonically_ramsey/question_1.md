**Question 1** (p. 8): "Is the regular hexagon canonically Ramsey?"

This records the question in the 19 August 2026 preprint. It is not a
separate current-status certification. No proof of either answer is
claimed here.

The [[discrete_geometry/shaw_2026_regular_pentagon_canonically_ramsey/composite_obstruction|complete product-host obstruction]]
shows that, for every positive integer $n$ and every positive scale
$a$, the host $aC_6^n$ admits a coloring with no monochromatic
or rainbow congruent hexagon. A general finite Euclidean host need
not be of this form. Its possible existence is therefore untouched.

The [[discrete_geometry/shaw_2026_regular_pentagon_canonically_ramsey/divisor_normal_form|divisor normal form]]
explains why the prime argument does not force the desired dichotomy
for composite order: intermediate proper divisors give color
relations that are neither universal nor equality. This does not
extend the obstruction from a particular family of hosts to every
host.

On pp. 2–3, the source also discusses possible implications between
ordinary and canonical Ramsey properties and closure under products.
Its p. 3 prose moves between a product being Ramsey and being
canonically Ramsey. Those are distinct assertions under the
[[discrete_geometry/shaw_2026_regular_pentagon_canonically_ramsey/definitions|definitions]].
No general product theorem or implication between the two properties
is used or deduced in this compilation. The precise positive
product result supplied here is
[[discrete_geometry/shaw_2026_regular_pentagon_canonically_ramsey/theorem_2|Theorem 2]]
for powers of a single prime polygon.

The further p. 3 aside about hypothetical avoiding colorings for
$\{0,1,2\}$, including its sphere and coordinate-permutation
comments, is not reconstructed here. It is not an input to either
numbered theorem or to the concluding polygon deductions above.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/discrete_geometry/E0174/_index|Problem 174]],
as context only. The problem asks for a characterisation of the Ramsey
sets, where the number of colours is fixed before the host is chosen. This page concerns
the distinct canonical Ramsey property, one finite host for colourings
with any number of colours, and shows no set Ramsey or non-Ramsey; the
regular polygons are already Ramsey by Kříž's theorem, as the paper
recalls (p. 2).
