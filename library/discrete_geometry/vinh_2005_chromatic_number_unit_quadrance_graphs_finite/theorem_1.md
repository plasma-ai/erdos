## Statement

Setting (p. 1). For an odd prime power $q$, the quadrance of two points
$A_1=(x_1,y_1)$ and $A_2=(x_2,y_2)$ of $\mathbb F_q^2$ is
$Q(A_1,A_2)=(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2$ (Definition 1). The unit-quadrance graph
$D_q$ has vertex set $\mathbb F_q^2$, two points adjacent when their quadrance
is $1$, and $\chi(\mathbb F_q^2)$ denotes its chromatic number. The paper
recalls from Medrano, Myers, Stark and Terras that $D_q$ is regular of degree
$q-(-1)^{(q-1)/2}$.

**Theorem 1** (p. 2). If $q=p^n>3$ with $p$ an odd prime, then

$$
q^{1/2}\bigl(\tfrac12+o(1)\bigr)\le\chi(\mathbb F_q^2)\le\frac{p^n+p^{n-1}}{2}
=q\bigl(\tfrac12+o(1)\bigr).
$$

The $o(1)$ terms are as $q\to\infty$. The upper bound
$(p^n+p^{n-1})/2$ holds for every such $q$, not only asymptotically: it counts
the colors of an explicit coloring.

**The lower bound as printed.** The proof (p. 3) combines Hoffman's bound
(Lemma 5, p. 3: $\chi(G)\ge1-\lambda_1/\lambda_*$ for the largest and least
eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\lambda_*$) with Lemma 4 (p. 3, attributed to
Medrano et al.), which states that every eigenvalue of $D_q$ other than the
degree has absolute value at most $q^{1/2}$, and writes
$\chi(D_q)\ge1+(q\pm1)/q^{1/2}=q^{1/2}(1+o(1))$. Lemma 4 is false as
stated: $D_5$ has the eigenvalue $-(1+\sqrt5)\approx-3.236$, whose absolute
value exceeds $\sqrt5$ (computed here from the $25\times25$ adjacency
matrix, and recorded in Table 1 of Medrano et al.). What Medrano et al.
prove is $|\lambda|\le2q^{1/2}$ (see the
[[discrete_geometry/medrano_1996_finite_analogues_euclidean_space/_index|Medrano et al. card]]);
with that bound Hoffman's inequality gives
$\chi(D_q)\ge1+(q\pm1)/(2q^{1/2})=q^{1/2}(\tfrac12+o(1))$, which is the lower
bound Theorem 1 states. The theorem's constant $\tfrac12$ is therefore
supported; the proof's intermediate $q^{1/2}(1+o(1))$ is not.

**Source.** Le Anh Vinh, On chromatic number of unit-quadrance graphs (finite
Euclidean graphs), arXiv:math/0510092v1 (2005): the setting on p. 1,
Theorem 1 on p. 2, Lemmas 1 to 5 on pp. 2-3, the proof on pp. 3-4. The
edition read is identified on the
[[discrete_geometry/vinh_2005_chromatic_number_unit_quadrance_graphs_finite/_index|source card]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the setting, the statement and the proof were
read clause by clause on the printed pages; the eigenvalue counterexample to
Lemma 4 was recomputed here. Nothing here is independently reviewed.

## Proof pointer

Pages 3-4. The lower bound is the spectral argument described above. The
upper bound is a coloring by parallel lines. Pick $a\in\mathbb F_q^*$ with
$a^2+1$ a non-square; by
[[discrete_geometry/vinh_2005_chromatic_number_unit_quadrance_graphs_finite/lemma_1|Lemma 1]]
every line $y=ax+c$ contains no two points at unit quadrance. Lemma 3
(p. 3, for prime powers $q\ge5$) supplies $t\in\mathbb F_q^*$ such that no
point of $y=ax+c$ is at unit quadrance from a point of $y=ax+c+t$, for every
$c$: writing the quadrance out, a unit pair would force $a^2+1-t^2$ to be a
square, and Lemma 2 (p. 2, a quadratic-character count) gives a square
$t^2$ for which $a^2+1-t^2$ is not. The paper splits $\mathbb F_q$ into
$p^{n-1}$ cosets $\{\alpha_i,\alpha_i+t,\ldots,\alpha_i+(p-1)t\}$ of the
additive subgroup generated by $t$, gives the two lines with intercepts
$\alpha_i+2kt$ and $\alpha_i+(2k+1)t$ one color for $0\le k\le(p-3)/2$, and
gives the last line of each coset a color of its own, for
$p^{n-1}(p+1)/2$ colors in all. The intercept of that last line is printed
as $\alpha_i+p-1$ (p. 4), where $\alpha_i+(p-1)t$ is meant.

## Dependencies

Lemmas 1 to 3 of the same paper; Lemma 4, cited from A. Medrano, P. Myers,
H. M. Stark and A. Terras, Finite analogues of Euclidean space, J. Comput.
Appl. Math. 68 (1996), 221-238, in the corrected form $|\lambda|\le2q^{1/2}$;
Lemma 5, Hoffman's bound, cited from A. J. Hoffman, On eigenvalues and
colorings of graphs, in Graph Theory and Its Applications (B. Harris, ed.),
Academic Press, 1970, 79-91.

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/discrete_geometry/E0188/_index|Problem 188]]: the paper
  does not treat Problem 188, which concerns red-blue colorings of the real
  plane. Its graph $D_q$ is the finite-field analogue of the unit-distance
  graph of the plane, and the line coloring of the upper bound uses lines
  free of unit pairs, which exist over $\mathbb F_q$ but not over
  $\mathbb R$. The theorem gives no bound on the problem's $K_*$.
