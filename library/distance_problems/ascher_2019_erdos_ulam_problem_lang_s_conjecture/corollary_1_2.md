## Statement

Rational distance sets and general position are as in
[[distance_problems/ascher_2019_erdos_ulam_problem_lang_s_conjecture/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]] and the
[[distance_problems/ascher_2019_erdos_ulam_problem_lang_s_conjecture/definition_p1|definition on p. 1]].

**Corollary 1.2** (p. 1, quoted). "If there exist rational distance sets in
general position of cardinality larger than any fixed constant, then Lang's
Conjecture does not hold."

The paper states it to stress the parallel between Lang's Conjecture and
Erdős's question on seven points in general position with rational distances
(p. 1).

## Proof pointer

The contrapositive of [[distance_problems/ascher_2019_erdos_ulam_problem_lang_s_conjecture/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]]; the paper gives no
separate proof.

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/distance_problems/E0213/_index|Problem 213]]: an
  affirmative answer for every $n\ge4$ would give, for each $n\ge7$, an
  integer distance set in general position in the paper's sense with $n$
  points, and so by this corollary would refute Lang's Conjecture. This is a
  conditional relation between two open questions and settles neither.
