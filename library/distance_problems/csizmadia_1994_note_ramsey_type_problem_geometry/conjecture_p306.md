## Statement

**Conjecture** (p. 306, unnumbered, quoted). The authors state "our
conjecture that for any colouring and for any five-point configuration $K$,
one can find either two red points at distance 1 from each other or an
isometric copy of $K$ all of whose points are blue."

Here a colouring is a colouring of the plane in the two colours red and blue,
as in Theorem 1 of the paper.

**Source.** György Csizmadia and Géza Tóth, *Note on a Ramsey-Type Problem in
Geometry*, J. Combin. Theory Ser. A **65** (1994), 302–306: the last paragraph
of the proof of Proposition 2, p. 306. The edition read is identified on the
[[distance_problems/csizmadia_1994_note_ramsey_type_problem_geometry/_index|source
card]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the statement was read clause by clause on
the printed page. It is a conjecture; the paper gives no proof.

## Evidence in the paper

The paper presents
[[distance_problems/csizmadia_1994_note_ramsey_type_problem_geometry/proposition_2|Proposition
2]] as support: in the
[[distance_problems/csizmadia_1994_note_ramsey_type_problem_geometry/standard_coloring|standard
coloring]], which has no red unit-distance pair, every five-point
configuration has an all-blue translate. That covers one colouring only. In
the same colouring,
[[distance_problems/csizmadia_1994_note_ramsey_type_problem_geometry/theorem_1|Theorem
1]] gives an eight-point configuration with no all-blue congruent copy, so the
conjecture cannot be extended to eight points.

This page records the conjecture as the paper poses it. It does not determine
its current status.

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/distance_problems/E0214/_index|Problem 214]]: the
  conjecture would extend Juhász's four-point forcing theorem, which answers
  Problem 214, from four-point to five-point configurations. It does not
  concern the unit square itself.
