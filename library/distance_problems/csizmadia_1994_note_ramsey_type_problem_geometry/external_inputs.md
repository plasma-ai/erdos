***

**Source.** Published paper, p. 306 and reference [6].

Proposition 2 imports the following bound: a covering of the plane by congruent
disks has covering density at least

$$
\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{27}}=\frac{2\pi}{3\sqrt3}.
$$

Only the periodic, locally finite case is needed here. Covering density counts
the sum of the disk areas per fundamental cell, with overlap and repeated
centers counted with multiplicity. It is not the area fraction of the union,
which equals one for a covering. The source cites L. Fejes Tóth, *Regular
Figures*, International Series of Monographs on Pure and Applied Mathematics, Vol.
48, MacMillan Company, New York (1964), p. 172. Its complete proof is external to this
compilation; the quoted interface was checked in Csizmadia–Tóth's paper, not
against a newly acquired copy of the book.

The source calls the density of the union of five translated red sets
$5\pi/(8\sqrt3)$. For the intended contradiction this must be read as the total
covering density of the disk family, counting multiplicity. The actual union
density need not be the sum when disks overlap. The
[[distance_problems/csizmadia_1994_note_ramsey_type_problem_geometry/proposition_2|rewritten
proof]] makes that distinction explicit.

The introduction also cites Juhász's four-point forcing theorem and twelve-point
counterexample. They provide historical context, and are not hypotheses in
either proof compiled here. The
[[distance_problems/juhasz_1979_ramsey_type_theorems_plane/_index|Juhász
source]] requires its own complete proof compilation.
