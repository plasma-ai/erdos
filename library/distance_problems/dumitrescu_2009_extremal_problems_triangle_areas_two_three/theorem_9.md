## Statement

**Theorem 9** (p. 18, quoted). "For any integer $n$, there exists an $n$-element
point set in $\mathbb R^3$ that spans $\Omega(n^{4/3})$ triangles of maximum
area, all incident to a common point."

In the plane the number of maximum-area triangles is at most $n$, by Braß, Rote
and Swanepoel as recalled on p. 1.

## Proof pointer

Page 18. Take the origin together with Ábrego and Fernández-Merchant's points on
the unit sphere, which have $\Omega(n^{4/3})$ orthogonal pairs, with the two
halves near $(1,0,0)$ and $(0,1,0)$. Each orthogonal pair forms with the origin
a right isosceles triangle of area $1/2$, and every other triangle is smaller.

## Dependencies

The construction of Ábrego and Fernández-Merchant (p. 18), not proved here.

## Bears on

None in the corpus. The result concerns maximum-area triangles in three-space,
which no Erdős problem in the corpus asks about.
