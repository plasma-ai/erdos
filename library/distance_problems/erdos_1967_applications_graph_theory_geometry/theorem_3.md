## Statement

Notation (p. 969). $(x_i,x_j)$ is the distance between $x_i$ and $x_j$.

**Theorem 3** (p. 970, stated without proof). Let $x_1,\dots,x_n$ be $n$
points in four-dimensional space. There are an absolute constant $c$ and
an $n_0=n_0(\varepsilon,c)$ such that for $n>n_0(\varepsilon,c)$ any
$\frac14n^2(1+\varepsilon)$ of the distances $(x_i,x_j)$ include more than
$n^c$ distinct numbers.

The print does not quantify $\varepsilon$; the dependence of $n_0$ on it
reads it as an arbitrary positive number. Unlike
[[distance_problems/erdos_1967_applications_graph_theory_geometry/theorem_2|Theorem 2]],
the statement does not say the points are distinct.

## Proof pointer

None in the paper. Erdős says (p. 971) that the proof is similar to that
of Theorem 2, and that both are no doubt special cases of a more general
theorem estimating the number of distinct values among
$\frac14n^2+nf(n)$ of the distances.
