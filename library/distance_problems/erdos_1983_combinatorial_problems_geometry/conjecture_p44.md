## Statement

**Conjecture** (p. 44). If $a_1<a_2<\cdots$ is an infinite sequence of
integers with $\sum 1/a_i=\infty$, then the sequence contains arbitrarily
long arithmetic progressions; in the lecture's second wording, for every $k$
there are terms $a_{i_1},\dots,a_{i_k}$ forming an arithmetic progression
of $k$ terms.

Erdős says he conjectured this more than forty years before, offers a prize
for it, and notes that by Euler's divergence of the sum of the
reciprocals of the primes it would imply arbitrarily long arithmetic
progressions among the primes. He recalls as context Szemerédi's theorem
(1972), that a sequence of positive density contains arbitrarily long
arithmetic progressions, and Furstenberg's ergodic proof.
