(1983), 35--54, the transcript of an invited address at the 17th New Zealand
Mathematics Colloquium (Dunedin, 17--19 May 1982), as named on the

pages are the journal's own.

## Statement

**Problem and conjecture** (p. 53). How many distinct distances do $n$
points in the plane determine? Erdős thinks the answer is
$n/\sqrt{\log n}$, which the lattice points attain: by a theorem of Landau
the number of distinct values $\sqrt{u^2+v^2}$ in the relevant range is of
order $n/\sqrt{\log n}$ (the print writes "the number of distinct
integers of the form $\sqrt{u^2+v^2}$ is $n/\sqrt{\log n}$"). He offers a
prize for the problem.

**Reported lower bounds** (p. 53). Erdős originally proved that $n$ points
determine $\sqrt n$ distinct distances; Leo Moser improved this to
$n^{2/3}$, and Fan Chung very recently to $n^{5/7}$, with a proof Erdős
calls very tricky. The print states the bounds without constants.
