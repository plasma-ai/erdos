## Statement

**Definition** (p. 51). $n(k)$, if it exists, is the smallest integer such
that any $n(k)$ points in the plane, no three on a line, contain a convex
$k$-gon containing none of the points in its interior.

**Reported values** (pp. 51--52).

1. $n(4)=5$, which the lecture calls trivial and sketches.
2. $n(5)=10$, proved by Harborth.
3. Nobody had proved that $n(6)$ exists. Its non-existence would mean that
   for every $t$ there are $t$ points in the plane, no three on a line, with
   every convex hexagon containing one of the points in its interior.
   Harborth suggested that $n(6)$ may exist while $n(7)$ does not; Erdős
   says he does not know.

Erdős says he noticed the variant in 1976.
