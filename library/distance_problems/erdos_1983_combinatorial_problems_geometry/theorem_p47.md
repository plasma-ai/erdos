## Statement

**Conjecture** (p. 47, from the Euclidean Ramsey papers). If $S$ is a set
in the plane no two of whose points are at distance $1$, then the
complement of $S$ contains four points forming the vertices of a unit
square.

**Theorem** (p. 47, R. Juhász, as reported). If $S$ is a set in the plane
containing no two points at distance $1$, then for every set of four points
$x_1,\dots,x_4$ the complement of $S$ contains four points congruent to
them. In particular the conjecture holds.

**Reported limit** (p. 47). Juhász also observed that this cannot be
extended to every $k$: for large $k$ there are $S$ with no two points at
distance $1$ and points $x_1,\dots,x_k$ such that the complement of $S$
contains no $y_1,\dots,y_k$ congruent to them. Erdős says it may still hold
for five points.
