## Statement

Setting (p. 2). Let $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ be $n$ points in the plane, not all on a
line, and let $L_1,\ldots,L_m$ be the lines they determine. The paper notes
that if $m>1$ then $m\ge n$, which it says follows from Gallai's theorem and
from a theorem of de Bruijn and Erdős, and records Grünbaum's question of
which values $m$ can take.

**Theorem** (pp. 2-3, unnumbered). Erdős reports (p. 2, continuing on p. 3,
quoted): "I showed that for $m>cn^{3/2}$ all possible values of $m$ can occur
except $\binom n2-1$ and $\binom n2-3$." The constant $c$ is not specified.

The survey gives no proof. Its additional references for Section II (p. 4)
include its reference [8], P. Erdős, On a problem of Grünbaum, Canad. Math.
Bull. **15** (1972), 23-25, but the sentence itself cites no reference. The
paper adds (p. 3) that the analogous problems in higher dimensions, with
lines replaced by hyperplanes, can probably be settled without great
difficulty.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the setting and the statement were read
clause by clause on pp. 2-3 of the print. The paper prints no proof, so none
was checked.

**Source.** P. Erdős, Problems and results in combinatorial geometry, in
Discrete geometry and convexity (New York, 1982), Ann. New York Acad. Sci.
**440** (1985), 1-11, Section II, pp. 2-3. The edition read is identified on
the
[[distance_problems/erdos_1985_problems_results_combinatorial_geometry/_index|source card]].
