## Contents

The paper is a numbered list of open problems, with prizes, in three
sections: 1. Number theory (1.1--1.6, pp. 261--264), 2. Combinatorics
(2.1--2.8, pp. 264--267) and 3. Combinatorial geometry (3.1--3.5, pp.
267--269). The items the catalog pages consume:

- 1.3 (pp. 262--263): for an infinite sequence $A$ let $f(n)$ count the
  representations $n=a_i+a_j$ and $g_A(x)$ the number of $n<x$ with
  $f(n)>0$. After the Erdős--Turán conjecture for asymptotic bases of order
  2, p. 263 asks whether each $\epsilon>0$ admits a
  sequence $A$ with $f(n)<c(\epsilon)$ for all $n$ and, at the same time,
  $g_A(x)>(1-\epsilon)x$ for all $x$. Erdős would be satisfied with the
  weaker version in which the upper density of $\{n:f(n)>0\}$ exceeds
  $1-\epsilon$, and records that he and Sárközy believe that $f(n)<c$
  forces that upper density below $1-\epsilon(c)$. This is the question of
  [[../wiki/problems/additive_combinatorics/E0749/_index|#749]].
- 2.5 (p. 266): "Let $f(n)$ be the smallest integer for which every graph
  of $n$ vertices every vertex of which has degree $\ge f(n)$ contains a
  $C_4$ (i.e. a cycle of length 4). Is it true that for $n>n_0$ (1)
  $f(n+1)\ge f(n)$? If this is too optimistic is it at least true that
  there is an absolute constant $c$ for which for every $m>n$ (2)
  $f(m)>f(n)-c$? The proof of (2) is perhaps easy, but so far the problem
  is open." This is the question of [[../wiki/problems/ramsey_theory/E0085/_index|#85]],
  statement and weaker version both.
- 3.1 (p. 267): $f_k(n)$ is the largest $m$ for which some $n$ points
  $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ in $\mathbb R^k$ have, for every $i$, at least $m$ of
  the other points at one common distance from $x_i$. Erdős conjectures
  that $f_2(n)<n^{\epsilon}$ for every $\epsilon>0$ once $n>n_0(\epsilon)$,
  and suggests the sharper $f_2(n)<n^{c/\log\log n}$; the lattice points
  show $f_2(n)>n^{c_1/\log\log n}$. He dates to 1946 his conjecture that
  "among any $n$ points in the plane the same distance can occur at most
  $n^{1+c/\log\log n}$ times" (p. 267), the question of
  [[../wiki/problems/distance_problems/E0090/_index|#90]], which the new
  conjectures would strengthen considerably, and offers a prize for a
  proof of $f_2(n)=o(n^{\epsilon})$ and a prize for a
  counterexample. He also records $f_2(n)<cn^{2/5}$ (Pach and Sharir),
  Fishburn's values $f_2(6)=3$ and $f_2(8)=4$, and, on p. 268, the bounds
  $c_1n^{1/3}<f_3(n)<c_2n^{3/4}$ and $n/2+2\le f_4(n)<(n/2)(1+o(1))$ of
  Erdős, Makai and Pach.
- 3.2 (p. 268): for $n$ points in the plane with mutual distances at least
  1 and minimal diameter (Thue: asymptotically the triangular lattice),
  Erdős guesses that the number $h(n)$ of incongruent extremal sets tends
  to infinity, records the belief that extremal sets have a large
  intersection with the triangular lattice, and conjectures that for
  $n>n_0$ an extremal set contains an equilateral triangle of side 1,
  noting that Sendov and Simonovits doubted it; his prizes are
  for a counterexample that works for infinitely many $n$ and
  for a proof. This is the question of
  [[../wiki/problems/distance_problems/E0099/_index|#99]].
- 3.4 (p. 269): for $n$ points in general position (no three collinear, no
  four concyclic), $h(n)$ is the largest integer such that any such $n$
  points determine at least $h(n)$ distinct distances; Erdős conjectured
  $h(n)/n\to\infty$ but could not prove $h(n)>n-1$, nor exclude
  $h(n)<cn$ for some $c<1$. This is the question of catalog problem #98;
  that page does not yet cite this paper, so it is not linked here.

The other items (1.1 sets with no product of $k$ terms a square, 1.2 the
Cameron--Erdős count of sequences in which no term is a sum of distinct
other terms, 1.4 bases of order three with $f(n)=1$, 1.5 the Burr--Erdős
decomposition question, 1.6 Sidon sequences with
$g(n)=n^{1/2}+o(n^{\epsilon})$, 2.1--2.4 and 2.6--2.8 on
independent sets, cycle lengths, $C_4$-free subgraphs of the hypercube and
related graph questions, 3.3 squares packed in a unit square, 3.5
equilateral triangles in six-dimensional space) were not matched to catalog
pages here.

## Compiled scope

Items 1.3, 2.5, 3.1, 3.2 and 3.4 were read on the page images and are
recorded by statement; the remaining items were skimmed for the list above.
The paper contains no proofs. Nothing here is independently reviewed.
