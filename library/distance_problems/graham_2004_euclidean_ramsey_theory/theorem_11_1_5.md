## Statement

**Theorem 11.1.5** (p. 4, quoted, cited to [O'D00a] and [O'D00b], P.
O'Donnell). "For any $g>0$, there is [sic] 4-chromatic unit distance graph in
$\mathbb E^2$ with girth greater than $g$."

The print omits the article before "4-chromatic". The chapter introduces the
theorem as some evidence, in the author's opinion, that
$\chi(\mathbb E^2)\ge5$, and notes that the Moser graph has girth $3$. As
printed, the statement does not say whether the graph is finite, and the
chapter does not define "unit distance graph", in particular whether two
vertices at distance $1$ must be adjacent.

**Source.** R. L. Graham, Euclidean Ramsey theory, Chapter 11 of the
*Handbook of Discrete and Computational Geometry*, 2nd edition, CRC Press
(2004), read in the preprint of the chapter identified on the
[[distance_problems/graham_2004_euclidean_ramsey_theory/_index|source card]],
whose own page number is cited: the theorem on p. 4.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the statement was read on the page image of
the preprint. The chapter gives no proof, and O'Donnell's papers were not
read here. Nothing here is independently reviewed.

## Proof pointer

No proof is printed; the chapter cites O'Donnell's two papers [O'D00a] and
[O'D00b].

## Dependencies

None in the chapter; the chromatic number is defined on

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/discrete_geometry/E0705/_index|Problem 705]]: the
  problem asks whether some girth bound forces every finite unit distance
  graph in the plane to be 3-colourable. Theorem 11.1.5 gives 4-chromatic
  unit distance graphs in the plane of girth above every bound; as printed
  it states neither finiteness nor the adjacency convention, so the chapter
  alone does not fix which form of the problem it answers.
