**Source.** Theorem 1 on p. 2 of
Ron Graham and Eric Tressler, *Open problems in Euclidean Ramsey
theory*, in A. Soifer (ed.), *Ramsey Theory: Yesterday, Today, and
Tomorrow*, Progress in Mathematics, Birkhäuser (2011), 115--120,
doi:10.1007/978-0-8176-8092-3_7. Page numbers here are those of the
authors' preprint, the edition read, as identified on the
[[distance_problems/graham_2010_open_problems_euclidean_ramsey_theory/_index|source card]].
The theorem is Kříž's (the paper's reference [14]: I. Kříž, Permutation
groups in Euclidean Ramsey theory, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 112 (1991),
899--907) and is not proved in the survey.

## Statement

Setting (p. 1). A finite set $X$ is Ramsey when for every integer $r$ there
is a least integer $N(X,r)$ such that for $N\ge N(X,r)$ every $r$-coloring
of $\mathbb{E}^N$ contains a monochromatic set congruent to $X$.

**Theorem 1** (p. 2, quoted). "Suppose $X \subseteq \mathbb{E}^N$ has a
transitive group of isometries with a solvable subgroup with at most two
orbits. Then $X$ is Ramsey."

The survey states it without proof and without spelling out the group action;
the precise hypotheses are in Kříž's paper.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the statement was read on p. 2 of the
preprint. Kříž's paper was not read for this page.
