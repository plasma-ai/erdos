**Source.** Theorem 2 on p. 2 of
Ron Graham and Eric Tressler, *Open problems in Euclidean Ramsey
theory*, in A. Soifer (ed.), *Ramsey Theory: Yesterday, Today, and
Tomorrow*, Progress in Mathematics, Birkhäuser (2011), 115--120,
doi:10.1007/978-0-8176-8092-3_7. Page numbers here are those of the
authors' preprint, the edition read, as identified on the
[[distance_problems/graham_2010_open_problems_euclidean_ramsey_theory/_index|source card]].
The theorem is Kříž's (the paper's reference [15]: I. Kříž, All trapezoids
are Ramsey, Discrete Math. 108 (1992), 59--62) and is not proved in the
survey.

## Statement

**Theorem 2** (p. 2, quoted). "If $X$ is the set of vertices of a trapezoid,
$X$ is Ramsey."

Ramsey is meant in the sense of p. 1, restated on the
[[distance_problems/graham_2010_open_problems_euclidean_ramsey_theory/theorem_1|Theorem 1]] page.

Context the paper reports beside it (p. 2): Frankl and Rödl proved that every
non-degenerate simplex is Ramsey (reference [10]), and every Ramsey set is
spherical, that is, lies on the surface of a sphere in some dimension
(reference [8]).

The survey does not qualify "trapezoid". Four coplanar points on a sphere
lie on a circle, so by the reported result of [8] the theorem can only hold
for trapezoids inscribed in a circle, the isosceles ones; a trapezoid that is
not isosceles is not spherical and so not Ramsey.

preprint. Kříž's paper was not read for this page.
