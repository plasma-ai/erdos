## Definitions

These are the paper's conventions (pp. 152–153), restated.

**Colorings** (p. 152). A coloring always means a $2$-coloring of the plane
$E^2$ in red and blue. Nothing more is assumed: the coloring need not be
measurable or have any other regularity. The hypothesis of no blue points at
distance $t$ means that no two blue points are exactly $t$ apart.

**Circles.** $\gamma_P(r)$ denotes the circle (not the disc) with center $P$
and radius $r$.

**$t$-alternating circle** (p. 152). For a given coloring, a circle is
$t$-alternating if any two of its points at distance $t$ have different
colors. The paper excludes the degenerate case in which $t$ exceeds the
diameter, so the radius satisfies $r\ge t/2$.

**Complementary pair** (p. 152). Points $X\in\gamma_P(r)$ and
$X'\in\gamma_Q(r)$ correspond when the segment $XX'$ is a translate of
$PQ$, that is, $X'=X+(Q-P)$. The circles $\gamma_P(r)$ and $\gamma_Q(r)$,
of the same radius, form a complementary pair if, for every red point on
either circle, the corresponding point on the other circle is blue. Two
corresponding points may both be blue.

**Regular $t$-rhombus** (p. 153). A four-point configuration forming a
rhombus with side $t$ and angle $60^\circ$; it consists of two equilateral
triangles of side $t$ sharing a side, and its diagonals have lengths $t$ and
$\sqrt3\,t$. A regular triangle is an equilateral triangle.

**Congruent configurations.** Theorem 1 and Lemma 4 ask for a red
configuration congruent to a given one, with no restriction on position or
orientation; Lemma 4's hypothesis, by contrast, concerns a translate.

**Source.** R. Juhász, Ramsey type theorems in the plane, J. Combin. Theory
Ser. A 27 (1979), 152–160; pp. 152–153. The copy read is identified on the
