## Statement

**Theorem 2** (p. 159). "There exist a coloring of the plane without blue
points at distance 1 and a configuration $\mathscr{K}$ consisting of 12
points such that any configuration which is congruent to $\mathscr{K}$
contains necessarily a blue point."

So the conclusion of
[[distance_problems/juhasz_1979_ramsey_type_theorems_plane/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
fails for some twelve-point configuration.

**The construction** (p. 159, Figure 6). Take the point lattice whose base
parallelogram is a regular $2$-rhombus. Around each lattice point color blue
the open disc of radius $1/2$, together with the boundary points below the
horizontal diameter and the left endpoint of that diameter; color every other
point red. The configuration $\mathscr{K}$ is the set of points of a lattice
$\mathscr{L}$ generated by a regular $\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$-rhombus that lie
inside the circle $\gamma_C(2\sqrt3/3+\tfrac12)$, where $C$ is the center of
a base triangle of $\mathscr{L}$; the paper states there are exactly $12$
such points.

**Source.** R. Juhász, Ramsey type theorems in the plane, J. Combin. Theory
Ser. A 27 (1979), 152–160; Theorem 2, its proof and Figure 6 on p. 159. The
copy read is identified on the
[[distance_problems/juhasz_1979_ramsey_type_theorems_plane/_index|source card]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the statement and the construction were read
clause by clause on the page images. The count of $12$ lattice points was
rechecked here by direct computation; no lattice point lies on the circle,
and the farthest of the twelve is at distance $\sqrt7/2$ from $C$.

## Proof pointer

Page 159. The boundary rule puts exactly one point of each antipodal
boundary pair in blue. Distinct discs are centered at least $2$ apart, so
two points of different discs are at distance at least $1$, with equality
only for facing boundary points, of which the same rule makes one red.
Hence no two blue points are at distance $1$ (the paper calls this obvious).
Every closed disc of radius $2\sqrt3/3+\tfrac12$ contains one of the blue
discs with its blue boundary points, and every lattice generated by a regular
$\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$-rhombus, in any position, has a point in each blue disc
with its blue boundary points. Any congruent copy of $\mathscr{K}$ is the
part of such a lattice inside such a disc, so it contains a blue point. The
paper says this "can be seen easily from the preceding observations".
