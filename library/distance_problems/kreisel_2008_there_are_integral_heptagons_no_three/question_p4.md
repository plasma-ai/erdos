## Statement

**Question** (p. 4, quoted, because it is a question as posed). "Are there
eight points in the plane, no three on a line, no four on a circle with
pairwise integral distances?"

The paper poses it as a substitute for Erdős's question, which it describes
as having been asked first for five points, then six, then seven. In the
same section (p. 4) it also asks for further examples or an infinite family
of seven-point sets in general position with integral distances, and, since
both of its examples are in non-convex position, for a convex one. It
records that no $7_2$-cluster is known, and that the smallest
$6_2$-cluster has diameter $1886$; an $n_2$-cluster is a plane integral
point set in general position whose points also have integral coordinates,
as Bell and Noll required, and the paper equates these with the plane
integral point sets in general position of characteristic $1$.

**Source.** Tobias Kreisel and Sascha Kurz, There are integral heptagons, no
three points on a line, no four on a circle, arXiv:0804.1303v1 (2008);
published in Discrete Comput. Geom. 39 (2008), no. 4, 786--790. Labels and
pages here are those of arXiv v1: Section 3, p. 4. The edition read is
identified on the
[[distance_problems/kreisel_2008_there_are_integral_heptagons_no_three/_index|source card]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the section was read clause by clause on the

## Proof pointer

None: the paper poses the question and does not answer it.
