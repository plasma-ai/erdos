## Statement

Let $c_0=14/\log2$, and for practical $n$ write $h(n)$ for the smallest $h$
such that each of $1,2,\dots,n$ is a sum of at most $h$ distinct divisors of
$n$. Then the bound

$$
h(n)\le c_0(\log\log n)^2
$$

holds for infinitely many practical $n$.

## Proof pointer

Immediate from
[[divisors/doorn_2026_practical_numbers_egyptian_fractions/proposition_4_1|Proposition 4.1]]:
for every real $x\ge x_0$ there is a practical $n$ with $x\le n<x^2$ and
$h(n)\le c_0(\log\log x)^2-1<c_0(\log\log n)^2$, and $x\to\infty$ gives
infinitely many such $n$ (Section 4 opens by calling the proposition the
stronger version of this theorem).

## Lean statement and fidelity

`∀ N : ℕ, ∃ n : ℕ, N < n ∧ ∀ m : ℕ, 1 ≤ m → m ≤ n → ∃ F ⊆ n.divisors,
(F.card : ℝ) ≤ c0 * (Real.log (Real.log n)) ^ 2 ∧ ∑ d ∈ F, d = m`, with
`c0 = 14 / Real.log 2`. This is the theorem with "practical" left implicit:
every $1\le m\le n$ is represented, and $0$ is the empty sum, which is what
the catalog's `Nat.IsPractical` asks (defined in formal-conjectures'
`FormalConjecturesForMathlib`, not in Mathlib). The right side of the catalog's
`Erdos18.erdos_18a` (some $C>0$ with $h(m)<(\log\log m)^C$ for infinitely
many practical $m$) follows with $C=3$ once $\log\log n>c_0$, i.e.
$n>\exp\exp c_0$, which the "for every $N$ there is $n>N$" form supplies;
neither step is formalized. The file was not built here.
