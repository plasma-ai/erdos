## Statement

Fix a field $K$, an integer $d\ge0$, and $m\le d+1$ distinct points
$z_1,\ldots,z_m\in K^s$. Evaluation at these points is a surjective linear
map from the space of polynomials of total degree at most $d$ onto $K^m$.
Consequently, if $K$ has $q$ elements, independently uniform choices of
$a$ such polynomials vanish at all $m$ points with probability $q^{-am}$.

There is also a coefficient-counting version. Let $K_0\subseteq L$ be
fields, let $P_1,\ldots,P_a$ be degree-at-most-$d$ polynomials over $L$,
and let $M$ be the number of monomials of degree at most $d$ in $2b$
variables. Suppose all $aM$ coefficients, together with $h$ further
scalars, are algebraically independent over $K_0$.

Form a bipartite graph with two copies of $L^b$, joining $x$ on the left to
$y$ on the right when $P_j(x,y)=0$ for every $j$. Let $U$ be a finite set
of its vertices, and $E$ a set of its edges supported on $U$, with
$|E|\le d+1$. If each of the $h$ further scalars is a coordinate of a
vertex in $U$, then

$$
h+a|E|\le b|U|.
$$

## Proof

For each $i\ne j$, choose a coordinate where $z_i$ and $z_j$ differ. A
linear polynomial in that coordinate can be normalized to take value one
at $z_i$ and zero at $z_j$. Multiplying these $m-1$ factors gives a
polynomial $L_i$ of degree at most $m-1$ with
$L_i(z_j)=\mathbf 1_{i=j}$. Thus $\sum_i v_iL_i$ interpolates any prescribed
values $v_i$. The case $m=0$ is the zero-dimensional target and is immediate.

Over a finite field, every fiber of the surjective evaluation map is a
translate of its kernel and has the same size. A uniform polynomial
therefore has a uniform evaluation vector. Taking $a$ independent
polynomials gives the stated probability.

For the coefficient bound, orient every edge from left to right. Distinct
edges have distinct $2b$-coordinate evaluation points, even if some vertices
on opposite sides have the same coordinate vector. Let $B$ be the subfield
of $L$ generated over $K_0$ by all coordinates of vertices in $U$. It is
generated by at most $b|U|$ scalars. Interpolation over $B$ shows that, for
each $P_j$, its vanishing on $E$ supplies $|E|$ independent linear
relations on its $M$ coefficients. The relations for different polynomials
occupy separate coefficient blocks. Gaussian elimination therefore
expresses all coefficients as $B$-linear combinations of at most
$aM-a|E|$ of them. Equivalently, their span over $B$ has dimension at most
that number; either formulation also covers redundant generators.

The field generated by $B$ and these remaining coefficient generators
contains all $aM+h$ algebraically independent scalars. A field generated by
$r$ elements over $K_0$ has transcendence degree at most $r$. Hence

$$
aM+h\le b|U|+aM-a|E|,
$$

and cancellation proves the claim.
