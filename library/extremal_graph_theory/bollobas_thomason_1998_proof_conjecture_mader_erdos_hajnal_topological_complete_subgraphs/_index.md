# extremal_graph_theory/bollobas_thomason_1998_proof_conjecture_mader_erdos_hajnal_topological_complete_subgraphs

[[extremal_graph_theory/bollobas_thomason_1998_proof_conjecture_mader_erdos_hajnal_topological_complete_subgraphs/theorem_1|theorem_1]]: Bollobás and Thomason's linkage theorem: a graph G of connectivity at least
k with a minor H satisfying 2 delta(H) >= |H| + 3k/2 is (k, ceil(k/2))-linked,
so every k of its vertices contain ceil(k/2) that can be joined in any
prescribed pairing by vertex-disjoint paths.

[[extremal_graph_theory/bollobas_thomason_1998_proof_conjecture_mader_erdos_hajnal_topological_complete_subgraphs/theorem_4|theorem_4]]: Bollobás and Thomason's main theorem, that every graph G of size
256 p^2 |G| contains a topological complete subgraph of order p; the proof
of the conjecture of Mader and of Erdős and Hajnal, with the constant 256,
in the authors' own text.

## Contents

- Abstract and § 1, Introduction (p. 883, page image). The abstract's first
  sentence states the result: "We show that every graph $G$ of size at
  least $256p^2|G|$ contains a topological complete subgraph of order $p$."
  Its second sentence places the result as a small sharpening of Komlós and
  Szemerédi's recent bound and as the proof of the conjecture of Mader and
  of Erdős and Hajnal. Here $|G|$ is the order of $G$ and its size
  $e(G)$ is the number of edges (the introduction writes the hypothesis as
  $e(G)\ge f(p)|G|$). The definition, quoted: "A topological complete graph
  of order $p$ comprises $p$ vertices $\{v_1,\ldots,v_p\}$ and $\binom p2$
  pairwise vertex disjoint paths $P_{i,j}$, $1\le i<j\le p$, such that
  $P_{i,j}$ joins $v_i$ to $v_j$." The conjecture, quoted: "It was
  conjectured by Mader [11], and also by Erdös and Hajnal [6], that there is
  a positive constant $c$ such that any graph $G$ of size at least
  $cp^2|G|$ contains a topological complete subgraph of order $p$." The
  survey of what was known: Jung [7] showed by complete bipartite graphs
  that any such constant satisfies $c>1/16$; Ajtai, Komlós and Szemerédi
  [1] noticed that almost every graph is an example giving $c>1/8$ (also
  Erdős and Fajtlowicz [5], Bollobás and Catlin [4]); Mader [11] first found
  a function $f(p)$ such that $e(G)\ge f(p)|G|$ forces a topological
  complete subgraph of order $p$, and later [13] showed that
  $f(p)=3\cdot2^{p-3}-p$ suffices (printed as $3.2^{p-3}-p$); Komlós and
  Szemerédi [8] proved that for each $\eta>14$ there is a constant $c_\eta$
  for which $f(p)=c_\eta p^2(\log p)^\eta$ suffices; and Alon and Seymour
  (see [8]) observed that a theorem of Robertson and Seymour [14] with
  results of Mader [12], Kostochka [10] and Thomason [15] gives
  $f(p)=cp^2(\log p)^{1/2}$. The introduction then states the paper's aim:
  to prove the conjecture of Mader [11] and of Erdős and Hajnal [6] by
  showing that $f(p)=256p^2$ suffices, by a proof the authors describe as
  similar in spirit to Alon and Seymour's. It adds that Komlós and Szemerédi
  [9] refined their own method into a second proof of the conjecture "very
  shortly after we had written this paper".
- § 2, Linking and dense minors (pp. 883--885, page images). Quoted
  (p. 883): "We call a subset $\{v_1,\ldots,v_l\}$ of the vertices of a
  graph linkable if, for any permutation $\pi$ of $\{1,\ldots,l\}$ the graph
  contains vertex disjoint $v_{\pi(2i-1)}$--$v_{\pi(2i)}$ paths,
  $1\le i\le\lfloor l/2\rfloor$. The graph itself is said to be
  $(k,l)$-linked if every set of $k$ vertices contains a subset of size $l$
  which is linkable." A graph $H$ with $V(H)=\{v_1,\ldots,v_h\}$ is a minor
  of $G$, written $G\succ H$, if $G$ contains disjoint vertex sets
  $V_1,\ldots,V_h$ with each $G[V_i]$ connected and a $V_i$--$V_j$ edge
  whenever $v_iv_j\in E(H)$ (p. 884). Theorem 1 (p. 884, quoted): "Let $G$
  be a graph with $\kappa(G)\ge k$ such that $G\succ H$, where
  $2\delta(H)\ge|H|+3k/2$. Then $G$ is $(k,\lceil k/2\rceil)$-linked."
  Paged on [[extremal_graph_theory/bollobas_thomason_1998_proof_conjecture_mader_erdos_hajnal_topological_complete_subgraphs/theorem_1|theorem_1]], with a proof sketch;
  its proof (pp. 884--885) runs through Lemma 2 (p. 884), a statement,
  proved by rerouting, about families of disjoint paths into the minor's
  vertex sets that enter those sets as few times as possible. The paper notes that
  Theorem 1 resembles the theorem of Robertson and Seymour [14] that Alon
  and Seymour used.
- § 3, Topological complete subgraphs (pp. 885--886, page images). The
  section opens by recalling that average degree of order $k\sqrt{\log k}$
  is needed and (Kostochka [10], Thomason [15]) sufficient for a complete
  minor of order $k$, and that Lemma 3 shows a linear average degree already
  forces "quite a dense minor"; its proof "is almost verbatim that of Lemma
  1 of [15]" and rests on ideas of Mader [11]. Lemma 3 (p. 885, quoted):
  "Let $0<\beta<1$ be the root of the equation $1=\beta(1+\log(2/\beta))$
  and let $k\ge3$ be an integer. Let $G$ be a graph of size $e(G)\ge k|G|$.
  Then $G\succ H$, where $H$ is some graph satisfying $|H|\le k+2$ and
  $2\delta(H)\ge|H|+\lfloor\beta k\rfloor-1$." The paper says the
  numerical value of $\beta$ "slightly exceeds 0.37". The proof
  (pp. 885--886) passes to minimal minors and neighborhoods of
  minimum-degree vertices, and in fact shows the slightly stronger
  $G\succ K_2+H$ with $|H|\le k$ and
  $2\delta(H)\ge|H|+\lfloor\beta k\rfloor-3$ (p. 885).
  Then Mader's theorem is cited (p. 886): "a graph of order $n$ and size
  greater than $(2k-3)(n-k-1)$ contains a $k$-connected subgraph" ([12]).
  Theorem 4 (p. 886, quoted): "Let $p$ be a positive integer and let $G$ be
  a graph of size $256p^2|G|$. Then $G$ contains a topological subgraph of
  order $p$." Its proof is on
  [[extremal_graph_theory/bollobas_thomason_1998_proof_conjecture_mader_erdos_hajnal_topological_complete_subgraphs/theorem_4|theorem_4]].
  A filing observation, not a review verdict: the theorem is printed with
  "of size $256p^2|G|$" where the abstract has "of size at least
  $256p^2|G|$", and with "topological subgraph" for the "topological
  complete subgraph" of p. 883; the proof's first step, Mader's theorem,
  uses only $e(G)>(2k-3)(|G|-k-1)$ with $k=128p^2$, which any
  $e(G)\ge256p^2|G|$ satisfies, so the abstract's form is what is proved.
  The closing paragraph (p. 886) says that the aim was a short proof of the
  conjecture of Mader [11] and of Erdős and Hajnal [6], and that the method
  as given clearly yields a constant smaller than $256$. It announces a
  stronger result, obtained by modifying the method along the lines of
  Robertson and Seymour's proof [14] of their theorem: a graph $G$ of size
  $22k|G|$ contains a $k$-linked subgraph, where $k$-linked means that any
  $2k$ prescribed distinct vertices can be joined in $k$ prescribed pairs by
  $k$ vertex-disjoint paths (the paper defines: "a graph is said to be
  $k$-linked if, for any sequence $v_1,\ldots,v_{2k}$ of $2k$ distinct
  vertices, there are $k$ vertex disjoint paths $P_1,\ldots,P_k$ with $P_i$
  joining $v_{2i-1}$ to $v_{2i}$, $1\le i\le k$", p. 886). The authors say that this
  result, of independent interest, implies the conjecture with a constant
  below $256$, that its proof is more involved than the one given and will
  appear elsewhere [3], and that the method of Komlós and Szemerédi [9] may
  be able to give a best possible constant, which theirs cannot.
- References (p. 887, page image), fifteen items, listed in part above; the

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0718/_index|#718]]: Theorem 4
(printed p. 886, PDF p. 4), "Let $p$ be a positive integer and let $G$ be a
graph of size $256p^2|G|$. Then $G$ contains a topological subgraph of order
$p$", with $p=r$ and in the abstract's form "of size at least $256p^2|G|$"
(p. 883), states the problem's assertion with $C=256$: every graph on $n$
vertices with at least $256r^2n$ edges contains a subdivision of $K_r$. This paper is Bollobás and Thomason's own proof
paper, but not one of the two papers the site names: the site credits their
proof to its key BoTh96, their *Highly linked graphs*. The abstract (p. 883)
states the theorem with "at least" and names it the proof of "a conjecture
made by Mader and by Erdös and Hajnal", the conjecture the problem records
as Erdős, Hajnal and Mader's; the introduction (p. 883) records the lower
bounds $c>1/16$ (Jung) and $c>1/8$ (Ajtai, Komlós and Szemerédi) on any
such constant, Mader's earlier $f(p)=3\cdot2^{p-3}-p$ and the Komlós and
Szemerédi bound $c_\eta p^2(\log p)^\eta$, and says Komlós and Szemerédi's
alternative proof [9] was completed "very shortly after we had written this
paper"; the closing paragraph (p. 886) names the authors' 1996 paper *Highly
linked graphs* ([3], the site's key BoTh96) as the home of the stronger
$22k|G|$ linkage result that implies the conjecture with a smaller constant.
This is the theorem that
[[extremal_graph_theory/fox_2013_chromatic_number_clique_subdivisions_conjectures_hajos/theorem_3_1|Fox, Lee and Sudakov quote as Theorem 3.1]]
with "at least $256t^2n$ edges", the abstract's form. This card reads the
theorem on the page image and its proof at filing depth; nothing is
independently reviewed.
