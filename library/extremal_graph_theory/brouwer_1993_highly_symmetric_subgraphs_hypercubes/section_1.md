## Statement

Printed pp. 25--26, Section 1 ("The 7-cube minus a Hamming code"), read on the
page images of the version of record named on the
[[extremal_graph_theory/brouwer_1993_highly_symmetric_subgraphs_hypercubes/_index|source card]].
The section has no theorem label; its results are the numbered properties
(1) to (6) on p. 26, and this page is named for the section.

**Setting (p. 25).** The vertices of the hypercube on a set $I$ are the
subsets of $I$, a vector space over $\mathbb F_2$ under symmetric difference
$x+y$, with $x\sim y$ when $|x+y|=1$. Take $I=\mathbb Z_7$ and let $H$
consist of $\emptyset$, the seven sets $\{1+i,2+i,4+i\}$ ($i\in I$), and the
complements of these eight sets. The paper states that $H$ is a subspace and
a perfect $1$-error-correcting code, hence a Hamming code: its vertices are
pairwise nonadjacent and every vertex outside $H$ has exactly one neighbor in
$H$. The subgraph $\Gamma$ of the $7$-cube induced by $X=2^I\setminus H$ has
$112$ vertices, is regular of valency $6$, and is vertex-transitive.

**The coloring (pp. 25--26).** For an edge $xy$ of $\Gamma$ with $x$ of odd
weight, define $i,j\in I$ by $x+\{i\}\in H$ and $y=x+\{j\}$; the edge is red
when $j-i\in\{1,2,4\}$ and white otherwise. $\Gamma_R$ and $\Gamma_W$ are the
red and white subgraphs, both on the vertex set $X$.

**Properties (p. 26).** The paper states, with short indications for (1) to
(3) and (6):

1. $\Gamma_R\cong\Gamma_W$; for odd-weight $u\in H$, $x\mapsto x+u$ is an
   isomorphism.
2. "$\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma_R)=\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma_W)$ is solvable of order 168,
   acts (sharply) transitively on the edges of both $\Gamma_R$ and
   $\Gamma_W$, and has two orbits on their vertex set $X$." The group is
   generated by the translations by members of the even-weight subcode $H_0$
   of $H$, the cyclic shifts of $I$, and the permutation $i\mapsto2i$ of $I$.
3. "$\Gamma_R$ has diameter 8; for any vertex $x$ of odd weight there is a
   unique antipode $x+\{i,i+3,i+5,i+6\}$ at distance 8, where $i$ is
   determined by $x+\{i\}\in H$; no two vertices of even weight have
   distance 8."
4. Every quadrangle of $\Gamma$ has three edges of one color and one of the
   other. If $xy$ is a white edge, then $x$ and $y$ are at distance $3$ in
   $\Gamma_R$, joined there by a unique path $x\sim u\sim v\sim y$.
5. "$\Gamma_R$ has girth 10."
6. "$\Gamma_R$ is an 8-cover of the Heawood graph, the point-line incidence
   graph of the Fano plane." Identifying vertices of $\Gamma_R$ that differ
   by an element of $H_0$ gives a graph isomorphic to the Heawood graph on
   $I\cup I'$, with $i\sim j'$ if and only if $i-j\in\{1,2,4\}$.

So $\Gamma_R$ and $\Gamma_W$ are isomorphic cubic graphs of girth $10$ on
$112$ vertices that are edge-transitive but not vertex-transitive, as the
abstract (p. 25) puts it.

**The three-coloring (p. 25).** The paper notes that once both color classes
have girth $10$, "it follows that the edges of $2^I$ can be colored with three
colors such that there are no monochromatic $g$-gons for $g<10$." Section 3
(p. 28) recalls this as a three-coloring of the edges of the $n$-cube without
monochromatic quadrangle or hexagon for $n\le7$.

**Attribution and identification (pp. 26 and 28).** The paper says the graph
was constructed in Dejter and Guan (its reference [7]) and may be the graph
R. M. Foster constructed according to Bouwer (reference [2]). A remark added
in proof (p. 28) states that it differs from the unique trivalent graph on
$112$ vertices with girth $10$ in Foster's census, since it is not
vertex-transitive.

**Source.** A. E. Brouwer, I. J. Dejter and C. Thomassen, *Highly symmetric
subgraphs of hypercubes*, J. Algebraic Combin. 2 (1993), 25--29,
doi:10.1023/A:1022472513494; Section 1 on printed pp. 25--26, the remark
added in proof on p. 28.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the setting, the coloring and properties (1)
to (6) were read clause by clause on the page images of printed pp. 25--26.
The paper's indications of proof were read but not checked; properties (4)
to (6) are stated without proof apart from the identification in (6).

## Proof pointer

Pp. 25--26. Property (1) is witnessed by translation by an odd-weight
codeword; for (2) the paper exhibits the group of order 168 and shows it is
sharply edge-transitive, preserving the parity of the weight; (3) is
verified by growing the distance classes from $x=\{1\}$ and $x=\{0,1\}$, which
the paper says also shows that the full automorphism group is no larger than
the group of (2); (6) is the quotient by $H_0$. Not checked here.

## Dependencies

Self-contained; the facts that $H$ is a perfect code and a subspace are
stated in the paper as standard.

## Bears on

context for
