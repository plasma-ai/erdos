one half.

## Contents

The survey (introduction, pp. 1--2) collects Erdős's graph-theory problems
with their sources, the earliest and latest known references, and the
prizes Erdős offered, which the author, with Ron Graham, undertakes to
honor. Its four sections are Ramsey theory (classical, graph, multicolored
and size Ramsey numbers, and induced Ramsey theory), extremal graph theory
(Turán numbers, subgraph enumeration, triangle-free graphs, graph coloring
problems, covering and packing, and general extremal problems), random
graphs, and hypergraphs (Ramsey theory for hypergraphs, Turán problems and
$\Delta$-systems, with infinite graphs set as its last subsection), followed
by the acknowledgment and references (pp. 30--45).

- Problem 75, "A decomposition problem about odd cycles", proposed by Erdős
  and Graham (the survey's [102]), preprint p. 19: $K_{2^n}$ is the union
  of $n$ bipartite graphs while $K_{2^n+1}$ is not, and $f(n)$ is the least
  $m$ such that every partition of the edges of $K_{2^n+1}$ into $n$
  classes has a class containing an odd cycle of length at most $m$; the
  problem asks for $f(n)$ and whether it is bounded. This is the question
  of #609, which the site words with $n$-colorings of the edges of
  $K_{2^n+1}$ in place of decompositions and asks to estimate $f(n)$.
- Problem 73, preprint p. 19 (a prize problem): does some sequence
  $A$ of density $0$ admit a constant $c(A)$ and a threshold $n_0(A)$ such
  that, for $n>n_0(A)$, each $n$-vertex graph with $c(A)n$ edges has a
  cycle of length in $A$? The survey quotes Erdős's guesses about the
  powers of two and the squares, and Alon's observation that $cn\log n$
  edges force a cycle whose length is a power of two. This is the question
  of #72, whose page cites the later solution,
  [[extremal_graph_theory/verstraete_2005_unavoidable_cycle_lengths_graphs/_index|Verstraëte 2005]],
  rather than this survey.
- Problem (35), "A Turán problem for even cycles", proposed by Erdős (the
  survey's [70], the 1938 Tomsk paper), preprint p. 9: prove that
  $t(n,C_{2k})\ge cn^{1+1/k}$; the lower bounds then known (probabilistic
  $n^{1+1/(2k-1)}$; Ramanujan graphs $n^{1+2/3k}$; Lazebnik, Ustimenko and
  Woldar $n^{1+2/(3k-3)}$, the survey's [178] = Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 32
  (1995), 73--79); "This conjecture is open except for the case of $C_4$,
  $C_6$ and $C_{10}$ (see Benson [21] and also Wenger [215] for a different
  construction)." This is the question of #572
  ([[extremal_graph_theory/chung_1997_open_problems_paul_erdos_graph_theory/problem_35|problem_35]]).
- Problem (36), "A problem on Turán numbers for an $n$-cube", proposed by
  Erdős and Simonovits (the survey's [130], the 1970 Balatonfüred paper),
  preprint p. 9: determine $t(n,Q_k)$, in particular $t(n,Q_3)$;
  $t(n,Q_3)\le cn^{8/5}$ and "No better lower bound than $cn^{3/2}$ is
  known." This is the question of #576
  ([[extremal_graph_theory/chung_1997_open_problems_paul_erdos_graph_theory/problem_36|problem_36]]).
- Problem (80), "A conjecture on a spanning cube in a random graph", proposed
  by Erdős and Bollobás (the survey's [88], Erdős's Bielefeld technical report
  of November 1990), preprint p. 21: the conjecture that, for $n=2^d$, the
  random graph on $n$ vertices with edge density $1/2$ has the
  $d$-dimensional cube as a spanning subgraph; Alon and Füredi [7] proved
  it for edge density above $1/2$. This is the statement of #578, before
  its solution
  ([[extremal_graph_theory/chung_1997_open_problems_paul_erdos_graph_theory/problem_80|problem_80]]).
