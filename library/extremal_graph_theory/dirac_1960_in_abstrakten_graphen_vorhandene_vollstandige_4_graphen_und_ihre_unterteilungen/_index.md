## Contents

- Introduction (p. 61, page image). The paper continues the author's
  companion paper on 4-chromatic graphs and complete 4-graphs but drops the
  chromatic number: it gives general conditions for a graph to contain a
  complete 4-graph or a subdivision of one, and studies how densely
  3-connected graphs are woven through with them. Most theorems are followed
  by counterexamples showing them best possible in a natural sense. The
  definitions (graph, complete $k$-graph, subdivision) are those of the
  companion paper's introduction; the terminology is König's, and Menger's
  theorem and its extension (the author's [2], Théorème I) are assumed. In
  the paper's notation $\{4\}$ is a complete 4-graph, $\{4U\}$ a subdivision
  of one ("Unterteilung"), and $\{5\}$ a complete 5-graph; a graph
  ("Graph") has vertices ("Knotenpunkte") and edges ("Kanten") and no
  multiple edges (not restated in this paper; the case $N=4$ of the proof
  of Satz 6 uses it).
- § 1, Allgemeine Resultate (pp. 61--63). Satz 1 (p. 61): a graph with no
  $\{4\}$ and no $\{4U\}$ is planar, from Kuratowski's theorem. Satz 2
  (p. 62): if joining a new vertex to every vertex of a graph gives a planar
  graph, the graph has no $\{4\}$ and no $\{4U\}$. Hilfssatz 1 (p. 62): a
  path joining two vertices of a $\{4\}$ or $\{4U\}$ and otherwise disjoint
  from it lies in some $\{4\}$ or $\{4U\}$ of the union. The definition of
  an $n$-connected graph ($n\ge2$; connected, at least $n+1$ vertices, not
  disconnected by deleting $n-1$ vertices or $n-1$ edges) follows. Satz 3
  (p. 63): in a 2-connected graph containing a $\{4\}$ or $\{4U\}$, every
  edge belongs to one.
- § 2, Schwache hinreichende Bedingungen für die Existenz von $\{4\}$-s
  oder $\{4U\}$-s in abstrakten Graphen (pp. 63--70). Seven cycles and four
  vertices of degree at least 3 are necessary but not sufficient, even in
  2-connected graphs (Figures 1--5); the author knows no useful necessary
  and sufficient condition and gives sufficient ones. Satz 4 (p. 63): in a
  finite 2-connected graph in which all vertices but at most one have degree
  at least 3, any two vertices lie in a common $\{4\}$ or $\{4U\}$ and so
  does any edge; proved (pp. 64--65) through a longest cycle through the
  two vertices and its chords. Remarks (pp. 65--67): the theorem fails for
  infinite graphs (a two-way infinite path plus a vertex joined to all of
  it; the two-way infinite ladder), and there are infinite 2-connected
  graphs without $\{4\}$ or $\{4U\}$ in which every vertex has finite degree
  above any given bound, or infinite degree, built by attaching pairs of
  quadrilaterals (Figure 1); a finite 2-connected graph in which all
  vertices but two have degree at least 3 need not contain one (Figures
  2--5, p. 67). Satz 5 (p. 67): a finite graph in which all vertices but at
  most one have degree at least 3 contains a $\{4\}$ or $\{4U\}$; proved by
  passing to a block with one attachment vertex and applying Satz 4. Satz 6
  (p. 68), paged at
  [[extremal_graph_theory/dirac_1960_in_abstrakten_graphen_vorhandene_vollstandige_4_graphen_und_ihre_unterteilungen/satz_6|satz_6]]:
  a finite graph with $N\ge4$ vertices and $E\ge2N-2$ edges contains a
  $\{4\}$ or $\{4U\}$; proved by showing $E\le2N-3$ for the graphs without
  one, by induction on $N$ with Satz 5, and best possible by the graphs of
  Figures 3--5. Satz 7 (p. 68): in a finite 2-connected graph with all
  degrees at least 3, any edge and any vertex lie in a common $\{4\}$ or
  $\{4U\}$; remarks (pp. 69--70) show that the degree condition cannot be
  dropped and that three vertices, or two edges, need not lie in one.
- § 3, $\{4\}$-s und $\{4U\}$-s in dreifach zusammenhängenden Graphen
  (pp. 70--77), valid for finite and infinite 3-connected graphs. Satz 8
  (p. 70): three internally disjoint paths between two vertices of a
  3-connected graph lie in a $\{4\}$ or $\{4U\}$ with those vertices as
  branch vertices, and in two if each path has at least two edges. Satz 9
  (p. 71): two edges and $n-2$ vertices of an $n$-connected graph
  ($n\ge2$) lie on a common cycle; remarks: not three edges, and not $n+1$
  vertices in general, though an $n$-connected graph with fewer than $2n+1$
  vertices is Hamiltonian (the author's [4]). Sätze 10 and 11 (pp. 73--74):
  a cycle together with an edge at one of its vertices, or with two edges at
  a vertex off it, lies in a $\{4\}$ or $\{4U\}$ with prescribed branch
  vertices. Satz 12 (p. 74): a cycle with $K$ vertices of a 3-connected
  graph on $N$ vertices lies in at least $\max\{N-K,K/4\}$ different
  $\{4\}$-s and $\{4U\}$-s; the Remark (p. 75) says, without
  proof, that for $K\ge5$ the bound is easily improved to
  $\max\{N-K,[K/4]\}+2$, and it states the paper's one conjecture,
  that a given cycle always lies in at least $N-K/2-1$ of them, a value the
  graphs of Figure 11 attain. Satz 13 (p. 76) combines Sätze 9--11; its
  remarks show that four vertices need not lie on a cycle and five need not
  lie in a $\{4U\}$.
- § 4, $\{4\}$-s und $\{4U\}$-s in dreifach zusammenhängenden kritischen
  4-chromen Graphen (pp. 77--79). A 4-chromatic graph is critical when
  deleting any vertex or any edge makes it 3-colorable. The remarks to
  Sätze 8, 9, 10, 11 and 13 (not Satz 12) all remain true for 3-connected
  critical 4-chromatic graphs, shown by the graph of Figure 12 (p. 77) and
  the graph of Figure 9; the author does not know whether for every $N$
  some such graph has $N$ vertices no five of which lie in a $\{4U\}$
  (p. 79).
- § 5, $\{4\}$-s und $\{4U\}$-s in $n$-fach zusammenhängenden Graphen,
  $n\ge4$ (pp. 79--85). Hilfssätze 2 and 3 (p. 79) count the $\{4\}$-s and
  $\{4U\}$-s through two or three internally disjoint paths in an
  $n$-connected graph. Satz 14 (p. 80; $n\ge3$): lower bounds,
  quartic in $n$, on the number of $\{4\}$-s and $\{4U\}$-s with two given
  vertices as branch vertices, separately for adjacent and nonadjacent
  pairs. Satz 15 (p. 81):
  a cycle and a vertex on it lie in at least $(n-2)^2$ of them with that
  vertex as branch vertex. Satz 16 (p. 81) with its Korollar (p. 82): for
  $n\ge4$, a cycle and three edges at a vertex off it lie in a common
  $\{4\}$ or $\{4U\}$, so the vertex is a branch vertex of at least
  $\binom n3$ of them containing the cycle; false for $n=3$ (Figure 13).
  Satz 17 (p. 82): a cycle with $K$ vertices lies in at least
  $\max\{(N-K)\binom n3,K(n-2)^2/4\}$ of them. Sätze 18--20 (pp. 83--84):
  for $n\ge4$ ($n\ge3$ in Satz 19), prescribed vertices and edges lie in a
  common $\{4\}$ or $\{4U\}$, and any $n+2$ vertices of an $n$-connected
  graph of order at least $n+2$ lie in a common $\{4U\}$, while $n+3$ need
  not (p. 84). The
  closing remarks (p. 85) say which of Sätze 14, 15 and 17 are best
  possible, and the author does not know whether critical 4-chromatic graphs
  of connectivity greater than 3 exist.
- What the paper does not contain (a filing observation, not a review
  verdict). The text layer of all 25 pages was searched for a statement
  about complete 5-graphs or $3N-5$ edges and the passages found were read:
  the paper states no conjecture that $3N-5$ edges force a subdivision of
  $K_5$. Its only conjecture is the Remark to Satz 12 (p. 75) on the number
  of $\{4\}$-s and $\{4U\}$-s through a given cycle, and $\{5\}$ appears
  only in Kuratowski's theorem (p. 61), in the proof of Satz 2 (p. 62) and
  as the example for Satz 15 (p. 85). Where the $K_5$ conjecture that
  Erdős's 1981 paper and the site attribute to this paper was stated was not
  checked here. The paper also does not characterize the graphs with $2N-3$
  edges and no $\{4U\}$; Thomassen's 1974 paper cites the author's 1964
  Math. Ann. paper (its [2], p. 71; not held) for that characterization.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0718/_index|#718]]: Satz 6
(printed p. 68, PDF p. 8) is the theorem the site's commentary credits to
the paper: a finite graph with $N\ge4$ vertices and at least $2N-2$ edges
contains a complete graph on four vertices or a subdivision of one, so in
the site's words every graph on $n$ vertices with at least $2n-2$ edges
contains a subdivision of $K_4$; the paper adds the hypotheses finite and
$N\ge4$, and its next sentence says the graphs of Figures 3--5 (p. 67),
which have $2N-3$ edges, show the theorem best possible, the observation
Erdős's 1981 paper records. The conjecture that $3n-5$ edges force a
subdivision of $K_5$, which the site and that paper also attribute to
[Di60], is not stated in this paper (above). The problem's status rests on
the 1996 and 1998 proofs and is unchanged.
[[../wiki/problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0916/_index|#916]]: Satz 6 is "the result of
Dirac [Di60]" the site's commentary says the problem strengthens, and the
theorem Erdős's 1967 survey states with $n\ge4$
([[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1967_extremal_problems_graph_theory/question_p57|Er67b, p. 56]]);
the printed hypothesis is $N\ge4$, so the survey's range is the paper's
own. The best-possible sentence (p. 68) supplies, in the paper's own text,
the graphs with $2n-3$ edges and no subdivision of $K_4$ that the problem
page uses to make the value $2n-2$ exact, and
[[extremal_graph_theory/thomassen_1974_minimal_condition_implying_special_k4_subdivision_graph/theorem|Thomassen 1974]]
reproves the theorem for $n\ge3$ as its Corollary. The problem's status
rests on Thomassen's theorem and the failure of the site's wording at $n=1$,
and is unchanged.
