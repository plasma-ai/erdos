## Statement

"**5. Open questions.** These results suggest a number of related problems.
For example, suppose that the graph $G^{(n)}$ has $[n^2/4]+k$ edges, where
$k$ is a fixed positive integer. Then it is clear that $G^{(n)}$ can be
covered by fewer than $[n^2/4]$ complete graphs. What then is the new
minimum as a function of $k$? Here it may be advantageous to use complete
graphs of order greater than 3 if $k$ is large."

The heading and the first four sentences are on p. 109 and the last
sentence on p. 110. The paper does not number the questions of Section 5;
"question (i)" is this corpus's name for the first of them. "Covered" is
the paper's word from Section 2 (pp. 106--107): complete graphs cover $G$
when $G$ is their sum, and they may share edges. Theorem 4 states
edge-disjointness as an extra condition; the question does not. The paper
does not spell out why fewer than $[n^2/4]$ pieces suffice above the Turán
number.

**Source.** P. Erdős, A. W. Goodman and L. Pósa, *The representation of a
graph by set intersections*, Canad. J. Math. 18 (1966), 106--112; Section 5
on printed pp. 109--110 = PDF pp. 4--5 of the Rényi archive's scan
(`1966-21.pdf`), read on the page images. The edition read is identified in the
[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1966_representation_graph_set_intersections/_index|source digest]].
The rest of Section 5, the circuit-decomposition question, is paged at
[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1966_representation_graph_set_intersections/section_5|section_5]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the passage was read clause by clause on
the page images of pp. 109--110. It is a question; nothing is proved.

## Proof pointer

None. Erdős's 1971 list restates the question and records Lovász's covering
bound and the lack of a sharpening for edge-disjoint pieces
