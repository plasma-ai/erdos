# extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1971_unsolved_problems_graph_theory_combinatorial_analysis

[[extremal_graph_theory/_index|..]]

[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1971_unsolved_problems_graph_theory_combinatorial_analysis/item_1|item_1]]: Erdős's 1971 statement of the Bollobás-Erdős question whether every graph
with n edges has a rectangle-free subgraph with cn^{3/4} edges, Folkman's
counterexample K_{m,m^2}, the revised conjecture with cn^{2/3}, and the
footnote attributing a proof of cn^{2/3} to Szemerédi.

[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1971_unsolved_problems_graph_theory_combinatorial_analysis/item_10|item_10]]: Erdős's 1971 statement of Moon and Moser's bounds on the largest number
f(n) of different clique sizes in a graph on n vertices, his own lower
bound with the iterated-logarithm count L(n), Bondy's unpublished bounds
on the least excess h(n) of a pancyclic graph, the two unproved
divergences, and the footnote that Spencer proved f(n) > n - log n/log 2 - c.

[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1971_unsolved_problems_graph_theory_combinatorial_analysis/item_11|item_11]]: Erdős's 1971 statements of Gallai's path question (every connected graph
on n vertices the union of [(n+1)/2] edge-disjoint paths) and of the
Erdős-Gallai cycle question (h(n) < c n log n, probably h(n) < c_1 n, with
h(n) > (1 + c_2) n from K_2(3, n-3)), with the covering variant.

[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1971_unsolved_problems_graph_theory_combinatorial_analysis/item_12|item_12]]: Erdős's 1971 question whether every graph on n vertices with [n^{1+ε}]
edges contains a non-planar subgraph with at most c_ε vertices, with the
remark that c_ε tends to infinity as ε tends to 0.

[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1971_unsolved_problems_graph_theory_combinatorial_analysis/item_13|item_13]]: Erdős's 1971 statement that a graph with [n^2/4]+[(n-1)/2] edges can avoid
every saturated planar subgraph on more than three vertices, the
conjecture that [n^2/4]+[(n+1)/2] edges force one, and the sentence
"Simonovits has just proved this conjecture".

[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1971_unsolved_problems_graph_theory_combinatorial_analysis/item_15|item_15]]: Erdős's 1971 record of the Kővári-Sós-Turán bound, the wished-for matching
lower bound for K_2(r,r), the asymptotic formula for K_2(2,2), the
conjectured order of f(n;C_{2r}), and his restatement of Turán's
hypergraph problems g(n;k,l) with the conjectured values for g(3n;3,4) and
g(2n;3,5) as printed.

[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1971_unsolved_problems_graph_theory_combinatorial_analysis/item_24|item_24]]: Preserves the printed non-strict range, diagnoses its edge-domain
inconsistency, and records the bounds and note added in proof.

[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1971_unsolved_problems_graph_theory_combinatorial_analysis/item_3|item_3]]: Erdős's 1971 theorem that k < cn excess edges over the Turán number force k
edge-disjoint triangles, Sauer's example on 2n+4 vertices, the question
whether k - f(c_1) triangles always exist for k < c_1 n, and the question
for the least u_r forcing a triangle in graphs of chromatic number at least
r, with the footnote that Simonovits determined u_r.

[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1971_unsolved_problems_graph_theory_combinatorial_analysis/item_4|item_4]]: Erdős's 1971 statement that every graph with C(n-1,2)+2 edges is
Hamiltonian, that for n > n_0(k) every graph with C(n-k,2)+C(k+1,2)+1 edges
contains a cycle on n-k vertices, and the request to determine or estimate
n_0(k).

[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1971_unsolved_problems_graph_theory_combinatorial_analysis/item_5|item_5]]: Erdős's 1971 statement of the Erdős-Rényi question for the smallest f(n)
such that all but o(C(C(n,2),f(n))) labeled graphs with n vertices and
f(n) edges are Hamiltonian, with the bound f(n) < cn^{3/2} of Moon, Moser
and Pálásti and the belief that f(n) < n^{1+ε}.

[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1971_unsolved_problems_graph_theory_combinatorial_analysis/item_7|item_7]]: Erdős's 1971 statement of Ore's orientation problem, the remark that a
triangle-free graph need not admit such an orientation (Ore; Gallai's
counterexample, the Grötzsch graph of Figure 1), and Erdős's remark that
he could find no counterexample of girth greater than four.

***

P. Erdős: Some unsolved problems in graph theory and combinatorial analysis,
Combinatorial Mathematics and its Applications (Proc. Conf., Oxford, 1969) , pp.
97--109, Academic Press, London, 1971 MR 43 #3125; Zentralblatt 221.05051.

This is a numbered list of twenty-four problems with the state of knowledge for
each, not a theorem paper; the items relevant here are the following. Item 1
asks whether every graph with n edges has a rectangle-free (C_4-free) subgraph
with at least c n^{3/4} edges, reports Folkman's counterexample built from a
complete bipartite-like graph on x_i and y_j, suggests c n^{2/3} instead, and
adds in a footnote that Szemeredi proved c n^{2/3} (Problem 1008). Item 7 is
Ore's problem of orienting a graph so that no directed circuit exists and none
appears after reversing any single edge; Erdos notes Gallai showed the
triangle-free Grotzsch graph is a counterexample and that he could find no
counterexample of girth greater than four (Problem 1006). Item 10 records
Bondy's unpublished bounds log n/log 2 < h(n) < log n/log 2 + L(n) for the
least h(n) with a graph on n vertices and n+h(n) edges containing a C_k for
every 3<=k<=n, and the unproved h(n) - log n/log 2 tending to infinity (Problem
1016). Item 11 states the Erdos-Goodman-Posa theorem that every graph on n
vertices is a union of at most [n^2/4] edge-disjoint complete graphs, with
Lovasz's bound for graphs with k > n^2/4 edges by complete graphs that need not
be edge-disjoint (Lovasz observes that it fails for edge-disjoint ones), and
item 11 also gives h(n) < c n log n for decomposition into edge-disjoint edges
and circuits with the conjecture h(n) < c_1 n and the lower bound (1+c_2)n from
K_2(3,n-3) (Problems 1017 and 184). Item 12 asks for a bounded-size non-planar
subgraph in every graph with n^{1+eps} edges and notes c_eps tends to infinity
as eps tends to 0 (Problem 1018); item 13 states that every graph on n vertices
with [n^2/4]+[(n+1)/2] edges contains a saturated planar subgraph on more than
three vertices, just proved by Simonovits (Problem 1019). Item 15 collects
extremal numbers and defines G_k (a vertex x joined to y_1,...,y_k with each
pair of y's joined to its own z), conjecturing f(n;G_k) < c_k n^{3/2}, proved
only for k=3, and f(n;G_k - x) < n^{3/2 - eps_k} with the unpublished lower
bound (7): for every eps > 0 there is a K_eps with f(n;G_{K_eps} - x) >
n^{3/2-eps} (Problems 926 and 1021). Item 17 conjectures the Lovasz-type
property-B statement with c_t tending to infinity (Problem 1022), item 18
records the unpublished Erdos-Kleitman bounds c_1 2^n/n^{3/2} < l_n < c_2
2^n/n^{3/2} for the largest union-free family with the conjecture max l_n =
(1+o(1)) c 2^n/n^{3/2}, both as printed (the exponent 3/2 is a misprint for
1/2: the floor(n/2)-element subsets alone form such a family, of
C(n,floor(n/2)) ~ (2/(pi n))^{1/2} 2^n sets, so the printed upper bound and
conjecture are false) (Problem 1023), item 22 asks for max of the sum of a
monotonic subsequence of n distinct numbers, calling it unsettled (Problem
1026), and item 24 defines H(n) for plus-minus one edge labelings of K_n with
n/4 <= H(n) < c n^{3/2} and a footnote that Spencer and Erdos proved H(n) > c
n^{3/2} (Problem 1028).

Source: <https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1971-25.pdf>.

Read status for item 6 (printed pp. 98--99, PDF pp. 2--3, read clause by
clause on the page images on 2026-09-18): the paper defines $g(3,n)$ as the
least number of vertices of a triangle-free graph with chromatic number
$n$, prints (1) $c_1n^2\log n/\log\log n<g(3,n)<c_2n^2(\log n)^2$, asks for
an improvement of (1) and for an asymptotic formula for $g(3,n)$, and asks
whether $\lim_{n=\infty}g(3,n+1)/g(3,n)=1$; it then defines $f(l,n)$ as the
least number of vertices forcing a graph to contain either a $K_l$ or $n$
independent points, prints (2)
$c_3n^2\log n/\log\log n<f(3,n)<c_4n^2(\log n)^2$, asks for an improvement
of (2) and for an asymptotic formula for $f(l,n)$, and says of the ratio
question, "I cannot even prove $\lim_{n=\infty}f(l,n+1)/f(l,n)=1$." The
same page prints Yackel's (3) $f(n,n)<c_2\binom{2n}n/\sqrt n$ and Erdős's
(4) $f(n,n)>2^{n/2}$, and asks for a proof that
$\lim_{n=\infty}f(n,n)^{1/n}$ exists and for a nontrivial lower bound on
$f(n,n)$ obtained without probabilistic methods. Display (1) is correct as
printed: its lower bound follows from the Graver--Yackel bound
$f(3,n)\ll n^2\log\log n/\log n$, and its upper bound from Erdős's 1961
triangle-free graphs on $N$ vertices with independence number
$O(\sqrt N\log N)$. Display (2) repeats the bounds of (1) for $f(3,n)$,
whereas the bounds known in 1971 were
$n^2/(\log n)^2\ll f(3,n)\ll n^2\log\log n/\log n$ (Erdős 1961; Graver and
Yackel 1968), so (2) is a misprint and its lower bound is false; both
displays are recorded here as printed. The read depth for item 6 is claims
checked.

Read status for items 11 and 15: claims checked (printed pp. 101--103, PDF
pp. 5--7, read clause by clause on the page images). Item 11
(pp. 101--102) asks, after the Erdős--Goodman--Pósa theorem, whether every
connected graph on $n$ vertices decomposes into $[\tfrac12(n+1)]$
edge-disjoint paths, noting that Lovász proved this when every vertex of
$G$ has odd valency; it then defines $h(n)$ as the least $h(n)$ such that
every graph on $n$ vertices is the union of $h(n)$ edge-disjoint edges and
circuits, reports the bound $h(n)<cn\log n$ that Erdős writes was shown
(the 1966 paper with Goodman and Pósa, per item_11) with the guess
$h(n)<c_1n$ and the lower bound $h(n)>(1+c_2)n$ from $K_2(3,n-3)$, and asks
in a covering variant whether $n-1$ edges and circuits suffice when they
need not be edge-disjoint [29]; the passage is quoted at
[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1971_unsolved_problems_graph_theory_combinatorial_analysis/item_11|item_11]].
Item 15 (pp. 103--104) prints, with $f(n;G')$ the least edge count forcing
$G'$: (1) $f(n;K_2(r,r))<c_rn^{2-1/r}$ (Kővári, the Turáns and Erdős); the
wished-for lower bound (2) $f(n;K_2(r,r))>c_r'n^{2-1/r}$, proved for $r=2$
and $r=3$ only, with nothing close to it known for $r\ge4$; (3)
$f(n;K_2(2,2))=(1+o(1))n^{3/2}/2$ (Brown, Mrs Turán, Rényi and Erdős); the
guess that a graph on $n$ vertices with no triangle and no rectangle has
at most $(1+o(1))n^{3/2}/2\sqrt2$ edges; (4) the guess
$c_r^{(1)}n^{1+1/r}<f(n;C_{2r})<c_r^{(2)}n^{1+1/r}$, whose upper bound Erdős
calls not hard and whose lower bound was open for $r>2$; then the graphs
$G_k$ and displays (5)--(7). The paper proves nothing; it records the state
of knowledge in 1971. The list has no item on the cube $Q_3$ (all thirteen
pages were read for it). Item 15's closing paragraph (printed p. 104 = PDF
p. 8, read clause by clause on the page image, display (8) on
a 300 dpi crop) restates Turán's problems: $g(n;k,l)$ is the least $s$ such
that whenever $|S|=n$ and $A_1,\dots,A_s$ are subsets of $S$ with $|A_i|=k$
for $1\leqslant i\leqslant s$, some $B\subset S$ with $|B|=l$ has all its
$k$-element subsets among the $A_i$; Turán settled $k=2$ for every $l$ and
the problem is open for $k>2$; the limit $\lim_{n=\infty}g(n;k,l)/\binom nk$
exists for all $k$ and $l$, and for no $k>2$ was its value known; and
display (8) as printed, "$g(3n;3,4)=3n\binom n2+1$,
$g(2n;3,5)=2n\binom n2+1$", whose proof Erdős calls elusive, with the
references [2], [10], [14], [25], [33], [34]. The printed first value is
$n^3$ short of Turán's construction on $3n$ elements (the $n^3$ triples
meeting all three classes and the $3n\binom n2$ triples with two elements
in one class and one in the next), so display (8) as printed does not
describe the construction Turán's conjecture rests on; it is recorded as
printed and not corrected here. Displays (1)--(4) and the closing paragraph
are paged at
[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1971_unsolved_problems_graph_theory_combinatorial_analysis/item_15|item_15]].

vertex-disjoint $K_l$'s with all edges of one color there are when the
different $K_l$'s must all have that same color, and says the answer for

(Ramsey's theorem gives $f(l)<4^l$); the fraction is
$\tfrac13$ on the crop. [32] is Moon, *Disjoint triangles in chromatic
graphs*, Math. Mag. 39 (1966), 259--261, and [31] is Lovász, *On chromatic
number of finite set-systems*, Acta Math. Acad. Sci. Hung. 19 (1968),
59--67, as printed on p. 109.

## Item 24 and Problem 1028

Item 24, printed p.107 / PDF p.11, directly concerns
[[../wiki/problems/discrepancy/E1028/_index|Problem 1028]]. It prints
$1\le i\le j\le n$, not $i<j$. The same paragraph's edge language and
count of $2^{\binom n2}$ sign functions diagnose the non-strict
inequality as a source typo; the
[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1971_unsolved_problems_graph_theory_combinatorial_analysis/item_24|item record]]
preserves the printed formula and explains the intended indexing.
The historical bounds are for unordered-edge $H(n)$, not independently
signed ordered pairs.

pages, printed p. $n$ = PDF p. $n-96$); its original acquisition date is
unknown. This selected-item clarification does not review the other twenty-three
items or their proof status. No notice is printed in the scan; the hosting
archive's site footer speaks for the site, not the paper
(https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/, prints "(C) 2005-2007 All rights reserved.
All material on this site is for scientifics purposes only."); the conference
volume has no online edition, so the publisher's page was not consulted and no
