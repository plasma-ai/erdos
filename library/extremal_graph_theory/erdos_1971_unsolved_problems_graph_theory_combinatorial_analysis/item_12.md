## Statement

Item 12 (printed p. 102) reads in full, with $G(n;k)$ a graph of $n$ vertices
and $k$ edges:

"Is it true that every $G(n;[n^{1+\varepsilon}])$ contains a subgraph which
is non-planar and has at most $c_\varepsilon$ vertices? It is not difficult
to see that $c_\varepsilon\to\infty$ as $\varepsilon\to0$."

**Source.** P. Erdős, *Some unsolved problems in graph theory and
combinatorial analysis*, Combinatorial Mathematics and its Applications
(Proc. Conf., Oxford, 1969), Academic Press (1971), 97--109; item 12 on
printed p. 102 = PDF p. 6 of the Rényi archive scan (`1971-25.pdf`; printed
p. $n$ is PDF p. $n-96$), read on the page image. The artifact
is identified in the
[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1971_unsolved_problems_graph_theory_combinatorial_analysis/_index|source digest]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the two sentences were read clause by clause
on the page image. The paper proves nothing here; the remark on
$c_\varepsilon$ is asserted without argument.

## Proof pointer

None in the paper. The catalog's Problem 1018 records the affirmative answer of
Kostochka and Pyber (Combinatorica 8 (1988), 83--86; filed as
[[extremal_graph_theory/kostochka_pyber_1988_small_topological_complete_subgraphs_dense_graphs/_index|kostochka_pyber_1988_small_topological_complete_subgraphs_dense_graphs]]),
who find a subdivided $K_5$ of bounded order.
