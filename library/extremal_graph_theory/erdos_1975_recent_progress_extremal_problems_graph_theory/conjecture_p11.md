## Statement

Section 3, printed p. 11 (PDF p. 9 of the scan; the scan carries no
printed page numbers and the article occupies pp. 3--14, so PDF p. $n$ is
printed p. $n+2$), page image, as printed: "An older conjecture of Sauer and
Berge states that every regular graph of valency four contains a regular
subgraph of valency three. Chvatal just stated the following more general
conjecture: Let $g$ be a graph every vertex of which has valency $\ge4$. Then
$g$ contains a regular subgraph of valency three."

The typescript prints "Chvatal" without the accent, the graph as a lower-case
$g$ and the sign as $\geqslant$. The paragraph stands between the remark
"I expect $A(n)<n^{1+\varepsilon}$ for every $\varepsilon>0$ and
$n>n_0(\varepsilon)$, but perhaps $A(n)/n\to\infty$" that closes the $A(n)$
discussion and the paragraph "Szemerédi recently posed the following problem"
on $F(n,k)$, both of which are recorded on
[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1975_recent_progress_extremal_problems_graph_theory/problem_p10|problem_p10]].
The paper gives no argument and no reference for either conjecture. Two
statements are printed: the Berge--Sauer conjecture for $4$-regular graphs,
and Chvátal's form for every graph of minimum degree at least $4$, which
contains the first as the regular case.

**Source.** P. Erdős, *Some recent progress on extremal problems in graph
theory*, Congr. Numer. XIV (1975), 3--14; Section 3, printed p. 11 = PDF p. 9
of the scan, read on the rendered page image (the OCR text layer
serves only to locate the passage). The artifact is identified in the
[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1975_recent_progress_extremal_problems_graph_theory/_index|source digest]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the two sentences were read clause by clause
on the page image on 2026-09-18. Conjectures; nothing to prove in the source.
