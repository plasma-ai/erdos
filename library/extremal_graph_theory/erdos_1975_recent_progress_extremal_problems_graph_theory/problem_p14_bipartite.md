## Statement

Chapter 4, printed p. 14 (PDF p. 12 of the scan; PDF p. $n$ is
printed p. $n+2$), page image, as printed: "Let $G$ be a bipartite graph of
$n$ vertices with $[n^{2/3}]$ black and $n-[n^{2/3}]$ white vertices. Is it
true that if the number of edges is greater than $cn$ then our graph contains
a $C_6$? It is easy to see that it contains a $C_8$."

The last cycle prints as $C_8$; a 300 dpi render of the line confirms the
subscript $8$ (the OCR text layer reads it as $C_3$, which is wrong). $[x]$
is the integer part; $c$ is a constant not specified on the page. The two
classes have $[n^{2/3}]$ and $n-[n^{2/3}]$ vertices, $n$ vertices in all;
Erdős's 1979 typescript states the same question with $[n^{2/3}]$ white and
$n$ black vertices, $n+[n^{2/3}]$ in all (recorded on the problem page). The
question is stated without an argument; the $C_8$ remark is called easy and
not proved.

The paragraph that follows on the same page is the Erdős--Hajnal conjecture
on the sum $\sum1/r_i$ over the cycle lengths $r_i$ of a $G(n;[kn])$, a
different problem not paged here.

**Source.** P. Erdős, *Some recent progress on extremal problems in graph
theory*, Congr. Numer. XIV (1975), 3--14; Chapter 4, printed p. 14 = PDF
p. 12 of the scan, read on the rendered page image and on a 300 dpi
render of the three sentences. The artifact is identified in the
[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1975_recent_progress_extremal_problems_graph_theory/_index|source digest]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the three sentences were read clause by
clause on the page images on 2026-09-18. A question and an unproved remark;
nothing to check in the source.

## Proof pointer

not held) with a negative answer; see the problem page.
