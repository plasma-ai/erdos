## Statement

Chapter 1, PDF p. 5 (printed p. 7 by the article's pagination), page image:
"Reimann [sic] constructs a bipartite graph of $n$ vertices and
$(1+o(1))n^{3/2}/(2\sqrt2)$ edges which contains no $C_4$. He shows that if
$n=2(q^2+q+1)$ then his graph is in fact extremal.

Assume that $G(n)$ contains no $C_4$ and no $C_3$. Then perhaps ($e(G)$ is
the number of edges of $G$).

$$
\max e(G(n))=\Bigl(\frac1{2\sqrt2}+o(1)\Bigr)n^{3/2}.
$$

In a recent paper Bondy and Simonovits make a penetrating study of the $G(n)$
which contain no $C_{2k}$, but many unsolved problems remain."

Since $(n/2)^{3/2}=n^{3/2}/(2\sqrt2)$, the displayed conjecture is the
statement of Problem 573. The typescript spells Reiman's name "Reimann",
in the text and in the chapter's reference list (same page), which cites
I. Reiman, Über ein Problem von K. Zarankiewicz, Acta Math. Acad. Sci. Hung.
9 (1958), 269--278, and A. Bondy and M. Simonovits (printed
"I. Simonovits"), Cycles of even length in graphs, J. Combinatorial Theory
16 (1974), 97--105. The same chapter (PDF p. 2 = printed p. 4) records the
$C_4$ bounds (1) $f(n;C_4)<c_1n^{3/2}$ ("In 1936 I needed ... for the
following number theoretic problem") and (2) $f(n;C_4)>c_2n^{3/2}$, "Miss E.
Klein proved (2)".

**Source.** P. Erdős, *Some recent progress on extremal problems in graph
theory*, Congr. Numer. XIV (1975), 3--14; Chapter 1, PDF p. 5 of the
scan (the scan carries no printed page numbers; the article occupies pp. 3--14,
so PDF p. $n$ is printed p. $n+2$), read on the rendered page image (the OCR
text layer garbles the exponents). The artifact is identified in the
[[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1975_recent_progress_extremal_problems_graph_theory/_index|source digest]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the passage was read clause by clause on the
page image; displays (1)--(2) on PDF p. 2 likewise. A conjecture; nothing to
prove in the source.
