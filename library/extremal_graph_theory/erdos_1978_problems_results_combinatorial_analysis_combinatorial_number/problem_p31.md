## Statement

Section 2 of the paper writes $G(n;k)$ for a graph of $n$ vertices and $k$
edges (printed p. 30) and closes with: "Finally I mention a problem of Sauer
and myself. Denote by $f_3(n)$ the smallest integer for which every
$G(n;f_3(n))$ contains a regular subgraph of valency three. Almost nothing
is known about $f_3(n)$; almost certainly $f_3(n)<n^{1+\varepsilon}$. We do
not even know if $f_3(n)<Cn$ is true or false. I offer 100 dollars for an
answer."

So $f_3(n)$ is one more than the largest number of edges of a graph on $n$
vertices with no $3$-regular subgraph. The page states no bound of its own.

**Source.** P. Erdős, Problems and results in combinatorial analysis and
combinatorial number theory, Proc. Ninth Southeastern Conf. on
Combinatorics, Graph Theory, and Computing (1978), 29--40; printed p. 31
(PDF p. 3 of the scan), with the notation $G(n;k)$ from printed
p. 30 (PDF p. 2). The scan's text layer is unreliable; the statement was
read on the rendered page image.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the definition of $f_3(n)$, the two
conjectural sentences and the prize sentence were read clause by clause on
the page image. There is no proof to check.
