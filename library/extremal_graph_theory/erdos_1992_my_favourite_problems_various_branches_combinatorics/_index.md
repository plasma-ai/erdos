combinatorics. Matematiche (Catania) 47 (1992), fasc. II, 231-240.

This is a problem paper, not a theorem paper: Section 1 reviews those of the
twenty problems Erdős posed at Catania in 1989 where new results had appeared
(Kahn's near-solution of the Erdős–Faber–Lovász conjecture, Fleischner–Stiebitz
on 3-coloring the triangle-plus-Hamiltonian-cycle graphs, Fan Chung's disproof
of the hypercube c_6 conjecture, Kahn's disproof of the Erdős–Lovász
blocking-set conjecture), Section 2 poses new graph problems 6-16, and Section 3
poses combinatorial number theory and geometry problems 17-24, with a
prize attached to clarifying e(n;k) in problem 14 and prizes elsewhere.
Item 4 (p. 232), reviewing Problem 6 of the 1989 list, is the source of Erdős
Problems 23 and 24: for graphs on (2k+1)n vertices whose smallest odd cycle has
length at least 2k+1, he asks whether deleting at most n^2 edges always leaves a
bipartite graph, noting this is still open for every k ≥ 1, and whether such a
graph has at most n^{2k+1} cycles of length 2k+1, recording a slightly weaker
result of Győri and Győri's proof of this conjecture for k=2 and n > n_0(k) (the
k=2 case is the triangle-free graph on 5n vertices). Problem 22 (p. 239) is
Erdős Problem 56: for a_1<...<a_t ≤ n with no k+1 pairwise relatively prime, he
conjectured the maximum is attained by the multiples of the first k primes,
records that Levon Khachatrian found a counterexample, and says the conjecture
probably remains true for n > n_0(k), perhaps already for n > (1+c)p_k^2; it is
of a related conjecture (all prime factors of the b's exceed p_k, no two b's
coprime, maximum attained by the multiples of p_{k+1}) that he says he is no
longer sure after Khachatrian's counterexample. Problem 21 (pp. 238-239) carries
Erdős Problems 123, 845 and 1110: Jansen and others proved by a simple induction
that every n is a sum of distinct integers of the form 2^α 3^β none dividing any
other, Erdős then asks whether all n can be written as b_1+...+b_ℓ with
b_1<...<b_ℓ<2b_1 and all b's composed of 2, 3 and 5, and states that if the b's
are restricted to the form 2^α 3^β then surely almost all integers cannot be so
written even when b_ℓ/b_1 is allowed to be a large constant. Problems 23 and 24
(pp. 239-240), on sequences with a_i a_j never squarefree and on writing every
large n as a sum of distinct squares from one class of an arbitrary 2-coloring
of the squares, are also posed here without proof.

**Results to transcribe.**

- Item 4 (p. 232, reviewing Problem 6 of the 1989 list): Asks whether every
  graph on (2k+1)n vertices whose smallest odd cycle has size ≥ 2k+1 becomes
  bipartite after deleting at most n^2 edges, still open for every k ≥ 1, and
  whether it has at most n^{2k+1} cycles of length 2k+1, which Győri proved for
  k = 2 and n > n_0(k) after a slightly weaker result.
- Problem 21 (pp. 238-239): Jansen and others proved every n is a sum of
  distinct 2^α 3^β none dividing another; Erdős asks whether every n equals
  b_1+...+b_ℓ with b_1<...<b_ℓ<2b_1 and all b's composed of 2, 3, 5, and
  asserts that for b's of the form 2^α 3^β almost all integers fail even with
  b_ℓ/b_1 a large constant.
- Problem 22 (p. 239): Erdős's conjecture that among a_1<...<a_t ≤ n with no k+1
  pairwise relatively prime the maximum t is given by the multiples of the first
  k primes was refuted by Khachatrian; Erdős says it probably remains true for
  n > n_0(k), perhaps for n > (1+c)p_k^2, and that after Khachatrian's
  counterexample he is no longer sure of a related conjecture (prime factors of
  the b's all > p_k, no two b's coprime, maximum by the multiples of p_{k+1}).
- Problem 23 (p. 239): With Sárközy: for a_1<...<a_k ≤ n such that a_i a_j is
  never squarefree, is k maximal for the even numbers together with the odd
  non-squarefree numbers? Also asks whether a_i ≡ 7 (mod 25) is maximal for a_i
  a_j + 1 never squarefree.
- Problem 24 (pp. 239-240): With Burr: divide the squares into two classes
  arbitrarily; is there an absolute constant c such that every n > n_0 is a sum
  of distinct squares from the same class? Erdős expects yes, and notes squares
  may be replaced by kth powers.
- Problem 12 (p. 234, page image): "Here is an old problem of Brandon Mc Kay
  and myself, which I completely forgot. Let G(n; cn^2) be a graph, the
  largest trivial subgraph of which has size less than α_c log n (following a
  notation of Bollobás we call a complete or empty graph trivial). Is it true
  that there is an ε so that for every t < εn^2 our graph has an induced
  subgraph which contains exactly t edges? We only proved this for t < ε(log
  n)^2. Perhaps our conjecture was too optimistic." The site's Problem 88
  drops the edge-count hypothesis cn^2, which follows from the trivial-subgraph
  hypothesis by Erdős and Szemerédi.
- Problem 13 (p. 234, page image): "Noga Alon and Bollobás asked: Let G(n)
  be a graph the largest trivial subgraph of which has size < c log n. Then
  such a graph has at least cn^2 induced subgraphs, no two of which have the
  same number of edges and vertices. Perhaps n^{5/2} is the correct order of
  magnitude but V. T. Sós and I could only prove n^{3/2}. V. T. Sós, Faudree
  and I thought taht [sic] such a G(n) has an induced subgraph of size c_1 n
  and at least [c_2 sqrt(n)] vertices of different degree." The last sentence
  is the conjecture of the site's Problem 637; the n^{5/2} question is the
  site's Problem 636 (linked above).
- Problem 14 (pp. 234--236, page images): With Rothschild: $g(n;c)$, the
  largest book size forced in every graph on $n$ vertices with $cn^2$ edges
  each in a triangle; Szemerédi's $g(n;c)\to\infty$, Alon and Trotter's
  $g(n;c)<k(c)n^{1/2}$ for $c<1/4$, $g(n;c)>\alpha_cn$ for $c>1/4$; the
  edge-count form $g(n;k)$ with (1) $g(n;k)>f(c)n$ for $k>n^2/4-cn$; the
  question (2) $g(n;c)>\log n$ for every $c$ and large $n$, with the remark
  that Rothschild and Erdős expected much more; then e(n;k), the least edge
  count forcing an edge in k triangles when every edge lies in a triangle;
  Szemerédi's regularity lemma gives e(n;k)=o(n^2) and Ruzsa observed e(n;2) > c
  n r_3(n). Erdős offers a prize for clearing these questions up (p. 236, after
  the e(n;k) passage).
