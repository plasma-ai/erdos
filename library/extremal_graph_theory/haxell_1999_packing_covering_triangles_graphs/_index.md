# extremal_graph_theory/haxell_1999_packing_covering_triangles_graphs

[[extremal_graph_theory/_index|..]]

[[extremal_graph_theory/haxell_1999_packing_covering_triangles_graphs/lemma_1|lemma_1]]: The first of the four transversal bounds that combine into Haxell's
Theorem 5: τ(G) ≤ (3 - γ)ν(G), where γν(G) is the largest number of
edge-disjoint triangles each meeting a fixed maximum packing in exactly
one edge.

[[extremal_graph_theory/haxell_1999_packing_covering_triangles_graphs/lemma_2|lemma_2]]: The second of the four transversal bounds that combine into Haxell's
Theorem 5: τ(G) ≤ (3/2 + 5γ/2 + 2β)ν(G), with γ and β the relative sizes
of largest independent families of type-(B,1) and type-(B,2) triangles.

[[extremal_graph_theory/haxell_1999_packing_covering_triangles_graphs/lemma_3|lemma_3]]: The third of the four transversal bounds that combine into Haxell's
Theorem 5: τ(G) ≤ (3 - δ)ν(G), with δν(G) the size of a largest
independent family of triangles that share exactly one edge with the
auxiliary packing B', that edge lying outside E[B].

[[extremal_graph_theory/haxell_1999_packing_covering_triangles_graphs/lemma_4|lemma_4]]: The fourth of the four transversal bounds that combine into Haxell's
Theorem 5: τ(G) ≤ (3 + 3δ - β)ν(G); the lemma whose 3δ term the closing
remark and the 2026 preprint of Yi each propose to sharpen.

[[extremal_graph_theory/haxell_1999_packing_covering_triangles_graphs/theorem_5|theorem_5]]: Haxell's theorem that every graph G satisfies τ(G) ≤ (3 - ε)ν(G) with
ε ≥ 3/23, that is τ(G) ≤ (66/23)ν(G), the refereed general bound toward
Tuza's conjecture that Problem 167 records, with the closing remark on the
improvement to ε = (23 - sqrt(481))/8.

## Contents

- Abstract and § 0, Introduction (pp. 251--252, page images). The abstract
  states the result in this form: when $\nu(G)$ is the largest size of a
  set of pairwise edge-disjoint triangles in $G$, some set $C$ of at most
  $(3-\varepsilon)\nu(G)$ edges meets every triangle of $G$
  ($E(T)\cap C\ne\emptyset$ for every triangle $T$), with
  $\varepsilon>\frac3{23}$; its last sentence (quoted): "This is the first
  nontrivial bound known for a long-standing conjecture of Tuza." The
  introduction calls a family $\mathscr F$ of triangles *independent* when
  no two of its triangles share an edge, and a set $C\subset E(G)$ a
  *transversal* for $G$ when each triangle of $G$ has an edge in $C$;
  $\nu(G)$ is the largest size of an independent family and $\tau(G)$ the
  smallest size of a transversal. It then records the trivial bounds
  $\nu(G)\le\tau(G)\le3\nu(G)$, the examples $K^4$ and $K^5$ with
  $\tau(G)=2\nu(G)$, and Tuza's conjecture of 1981 [4] that
  $\tau(G)\le2\nu(G)$ for every graph $G$. The partial results recalled
  (pp. 251--252): Tuza [5] for planar graphs, for $K_5$-free chordal
  graphs and for graphs on $n$ vertices with $7n^2/16$ or more edges;
  Krivelevich [3] for graphs without homeomorphic copies of $K_{3,3}$, a
  paper that also considers two fractional versions of the conjecture;
  Tuza [5], $\tau(G)\le7\nu(G)/3$ for tripartite $G$, improved in [2] to
  $(2-\varepsilon)\nu(G)$ for a small positive $\varepsilon$. Then the
  statement of the result (p. 252, quoted): "In this note we show that for
  any graph $G$, we have $\tau(G)\le(3-\varepsilon)\nu(G)$, where
  $\varepsilon>3/23$."
- § 1, Proof of the main result (pp. 252--254, page images). Fix $G$ and a
  maximum independent family $\mathscr B$ of triangles, $|\mathscr B|=\nu$.
  A triangle is of type $(\mathscr B,i)$ if it has exactly $i$ edges in
  $E[\mathscr B]$, the edge set of the triangles of $\mathscr B$; every
  triangle has a type $i\in\{1,2,3\}$ by maximality. $\mathscr B_1$ is a
  maximum independent family of type-$(\mathscr B,1)$ triangles,
  $|\mathscr B_1|=\gamma\nu$. Lemma 1 (p. 252, quoted): "We have
  $\tau(G)\le(3-\gamma)\nu(G)$." Proof: each $T_1\in\mathscr B_1$ meets one
  $T_1'\in\mathscr B$; the set $\mathscr F$ of these has $|\mathscr F|=
  |\mathscr B_1|$ by maximality of $\mathscr B$; $E(T_1)\cup E(T_1')$ is a
  $K_4$ minus an edge; with $e(T_1)$ the shared edge and $e'(T_1)$ the
  missing edge when it lies in $G$, $C=E[\mathscr B\setminus\mathscr F]\cup
  \{e(T)\}\cup\{e'(T)\}$ has at most $(3-\gamma)\nu$ edges and is a
  transversal, since a triangle edge-disjoint from
  $\mathscr B\setminus\mathscr F$ must share an edge with both $T_1$ and
  $T_1'$ for some $T_1$, hence contain $e(T_1)$ or $e'(T_1)$. $G'$ is $G$
  minus the edges of the triangles of $\mathscr B_1$, with
  $\nu(G')=\nu(1-\gamma)$ and only types $(\mathscr B,2)$ and
  $(\mathscr B,3)$ left; $\mathscr B_2$ is a maximum independent family of
  type-$(\mathscr B,2)$ triangles in $G'$, $|\mathscr B_2|=\beta\nu$. Lemma 2
  (p. 252, quoted): "We have $\tau(G)\le(3/2+5\gamma/2+2\beta)\nu$." Proof:
  $C=E[\mathscr B_1]\cup E[\mathscr B_2]\cup C'$ with $C'$ a minimum set of
  edges whose removal makes the graph $H$ on
  $E[\mathscr B]\setminus(E[\mathscr B_1]\cup E[\mathscr B_2])$ bipartite,
  so $|C'|\le|E(H)|/2$; every remaining type-$(\mathscr B,3)$ triangle lies
  in $H$. $\mathscr B'$ is a maximum independent family in $G'$ subject to
  $|E[\mathscr B']\setminus E[\mathscr B]|\ge\beta\nu$ (it exists because of
  $\mathscr B_2$), $|\mathscr B'|\le\nu(1-\gamma)$; $\mathscr S$ is the set
  of triangles with exactly one edge in $E[\mathscr B']$, that edge in
  $E[\mathscr B']\setminus E[\mathscr B]$; $\mathscr B_1'$ is a maximum
  independent subset of $\mathscr S$, $|\mathscr B_1'|=\delta\nu$. Lemma 3
  (p. 253, quoted): "We have $\tau(G)\le(3-\delta)\nu$." Proof: the Lemma 1
  construction inside $G'$ with $\mathscr B'$ and $\mathscr B_1'$ gives a
  transversal $C'$ of $G'$ with $|C'|\le3|\mathscr B'|-\delta\nu$, using that
  the only edge of $T_1'$ in $E[\mathscr B']\setminus E[\mathscr B]$ is
  $e(T_1)$ (a second one would make $T_1'$ a type-$(\mathscr B,1)$
  triangle, of which $G'$ has none); $C'\cup E[\mathscr B_1]$ is a
  transversal of $G$ of size at most $(3-\delta)\nu$. Lemma 4 (p. 253,
  quoted): "We have $\tau(G)\le(3+3\delta-\beta)\nu$." Proof (pp. 253--254):
  $C=E[\mathscr B_1]\cup E[\mathscr B_1']\cup(E[\mathscr B]\cap
  E[\mathscr B'])$; $\mathscr B'$ is maximal in $G'$, so $E[\mathscr B']$ is
  a transversal of $G'$, and a triangle of $G'$ disjoint from
  $E[\mathscr B']\cap E[\mathscr B]$ cannot contain two or three edges of
  $E[\mathscr B']\setminus E[\mathscr B]$, so it lies in $\mathscr S$ and
  contains an edge of $E[\mathscr B_1']$. Theorem 5 (p. 254, quoted): "We
  have $\tau(G)\le(3-\varepsilon)\nu$, where $\varepsilon\ge3/23$." Proof,
  the displayed combination of the four lemmas with weights $1$,
  $\frac25$, $\frac{12}5$ and $\frac45$: $\frac{23}5\tau(G)\le[(3-\gamma)+
  (\frac35+\gamma+\frac45\beta)+(\frac{36}5-\frac{12}5\delta)+(\frac{12}5+
  \frac{12}5\delta-\frac45\beta)]\nu(G)=\frac{66}5\nu(G)$, so
  $\tau(G)\le\frac{66}{23}\nu(G)$; recomputed here, the weights sum to
  $\frac{23}5$, the parameters cancel and the constants sum to
  $3+\frac{51}5=\frac{66}5$. Closing remark (p. 254, quoted): "The bound for
  $\varepsilon$ can be improved slightly to $(23-\sqrt{481})/8>3/23$ by
  using induction in Lemma 4 to replace the $3\delta$ bound by
  $(3-\varepsilon)\delta$." No proof of the remark is printed;
  $3-(23-\sqrt{481})/8=(1+\sqrt{481})/8=2.866\ldots$, against
  $\frac{66}{23}=2.869\ldots$. A filing observation, not a review verdict:
  the abstract and p. 252 state the result with the strict
  "$\varepsilon>3/23$", while Theorem 5 states "$\varepsilon\ge3/23$" and
  its printed proof gives exactly $\frac{66}{23}$; the strict form rests on
  the closing remark. The bound carries no error term: the theorem is
  $\tau(G)\le\frac{66}{23}\nu(G)$ for every graph $G$.
- References (p. 254, page image): five items, listed above.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0167/_index|#167]]: Theorem 5
(printed p. 254, PDF p. 4), "We have $\tau(G)\le(3-\varepsilon)\nu$, where
$\varepsilon\ge3/23$", that is $\tau(G)\le\frac{66}{23}\nu(G)$ for every
graph $G$, is the refereed general bound toward Tuza's conjecture
$\tau(G)\le2\nu(G)$; the site's commentary writes it as
"$\le(3-\frac3{23}+o(1))k$", and the paper's bound carries no $o(1)$ term.

tightness of $2$ for $K^4$ and $K^5$, and Tuza's 1981 conjecture with its
Bolyai citation [4], the same entry as the page's [Tu81]. The closing remark
(p. 254) is the improvement to $(1+\sqrt{481})/8$ that the 2026 preprint of
Yi attributes to the paper "but the proof was omitted"; the paper prints the
remark as one sentence that names the method, and no proof. The paper does not
prove or disprove the conjecture and settles nothing the problem page leaves
open. Lemmas 1--4 ([[extremal_graph_theory/haxell_1999_packing_covering_triangles_graphs/lemma_1|lemma_1]] to
