For a graph $G$, let $\operatorname{hom}(C_{2q},G)$ be the number of ordered
$2q$-tuples $(x_1,\ldots,x_{2q})$ such that every consecutive pair, including
$x_{2q}x_1$, is an edge. Vertices may repeat. The following are the external
inputs stated in Section 2.1 of Janzer's manuscript. Their proofs are
not reproduced in that manuscript or in this source unit.

## Conflicting edges and vertices

**Lemma 2.1.** Let $q\geq2$, let $G=(V,E)$ have $n$ vertices, and let
$\mathrel\sim$ be a symmetric relation on $E$. Suppose that for every
$uv\in E$ and $w\in V$, the vertex $w$ has at most $s$ neighbors $z$ for
which $uv\sim wz$. The number of homomorphic $2q$-cycles having

$$
x_ix_{i+1}\sim x_jx_{j+1}
$$

for some $i\ne j$ is at most

$$
32q^{3/2}s^{1/2}\Delta(G)^{1/2}n^{1/(2q)}
 \operatorname{hom}(C_{2q},G)^{1-1/(2q)}. \tag{1}
$$

The manuscript identifies this as Lemma 2.1 of its reference [16].

**Lemma 2.2.** Let $q\geq2$, let $G$ have $n$ vertices, let
$X_1,X_2\subseteq V(G)$, and let $\mathrel\sim$ be a symmetric relation on
$V(G)$. Suppose that for every $u\in V(G)$ and $v\in X_i$, the vertex $v$ has
at most $\Delta_i$ neighbors in $X_{3-i}$, among which at most $s_i$ are
related to $u$. Put

$$
M=\max\{\Delta_1s_2,\Delta_2s_1\}.
$$

Among homomorphic $2q$-cycles whose vertices alternate between $X_1$ and
$X_2$, in either orientation, the number having $x_i\sim x_j$ for some
$i\ne j$ is at most

$$
32q^{3/2}M^{1/2}n^{1/(2q)}
 \operatorname{hom}(C_{2q},G)^{1-1/(2q)}. \tag{2}
$$

This is the $\ell=0$ special case of [16, Lemma 2.4].

## Bipartite core and cycle lower bounds

**Lemma 2.3.** If an $n$-vertex graph $G$ has positive average degree $d$,
then there are $D_1,D_2\geq d/4$ and a nonempty bipartite subgraph $G'$ with
parts $X_1,X_2$ such that

$$
\frac{D_i}{256(\log n)^2}\leq d_{G'}(x)\leq d_G(x)\leq D_i
\qquad(x\in X_i). \tag{3}
$$

The upper inequality is recorded in the manuscript as $d_G(x)\leq D_i$;
the displayed $d_{G'}(x)\leq d_G(x)$ is automatic because $G'\subseteq G$.
The manuscript omits the proof, saying that it follows the proof of
[16, Lemma 5.4] with only minor changes.

**Lemma 2.4.** If $G$ is bipartite with parts $X,Y$, every vertex of $X$ has
degree at least $s$, and every vertex of $Y$ has degree at least $t$, then

$$
\operatorname{hom}(C_{2q},G)\geq s^qt^q
\qquad(q\geq1). \tag{4}
$$

This is cited as [16, Lemma 4.4].

**Lemma 2.6 (Sidorenko).** Every $n$-vertex graph satisfies

$$
\operatorname{hom}(C_{2q},G)
 \geq\left(\frac{2e(G)}n\right)^{2q}
\qquad(q\geq2). \tag{5}
$$

**Lemma 2.7 (Morris--Saxton).** For each $q\geq2$ there are constants
$C(q),c(q)>0$ such that every $n$-vertex graph with
$e\geq C(q)n^{1+1/q}$ edges contains at least

$$
c(q)\frac{e^{2q}}{n^{2q}} \tag{6}
$$

unlabeled copies of $C_{2q}$.

## Almost-regular subgraph

Call a graph $K$-almost regular if $\Delta(G)\leq K\delta(G)$.

**Lemma 2.8 (Erdős--Simonovits).** Fix $0<\alpha<1$, and take $n$
sufficiently large in terms of $\alpha$. Every $n$-vertex graph with at least
$n^{1+\alpha}$ edges contains a $K$-almost-regular subgraph on

$$
m\geq n^{\alpha(1-\alpha)/(1+\alpha)} \tag{7}
$$

vertices and at least $\frac25m^{1+\alpha}$ edges, where

$$
K=10\cdot2^{1/\alpha^2+1}. \tag{8}
$$
