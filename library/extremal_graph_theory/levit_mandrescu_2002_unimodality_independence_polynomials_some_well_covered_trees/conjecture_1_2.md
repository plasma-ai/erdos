## Statement

**Conjecture 1.2** (p. 4, quoted), credited to Alavi, Malde, Schwenk and
Erdős, "The vertex independence sequence of a graph is not constrained",
Congr. Numer. 58 (1987) (the paper's reference [1]). "Independence
polynomials of trees are unimodal."

The abstract (p. 1) reports that Alavi, Malde, Schwenk and Erdős (1987)
asked whether the independence polynomial is unimodal for trees "(or
perhaps forests)". Alongside it the paper records Conjecture 1.1 (p. 3),
which it credits to Brown, Dilcher and Nowakowski (2000): the independence
polynomial of every well-covered graph is unimodal. Before stating
Conjecture 1.2 the paper gives two well-covered trees, one with only real
roots and one without, to show that Newton's real-roots criterion does not
settle the conjecture even for well-covered trees (pp. 3–4).

## Proof pointer

The paper proves neither conjecture. It proves Conjecture 1.2 for the
well-covered spiders ([[extremal_graph_theory/levit_mandrescu_2002_unimodality_independence_polynomials_some_well_covered_trees/theorem_3_1|Theorem 3.1]]) and the centipedes
([[extremal_graph_theory/levit_mandrescu_2002_unimodality_independence_polynomials_some_well_covered_trees/theorem_4_2|Theorem 4.2]]), states it for the trees $G_{m,n}$,
$m,n\geq2$ ([[extremal_graph_theory/levit_mandrescu_2002_unimodality_independence_polynomials_some_well_covered_trees/theorem_4_5|Theorem 4.5]]), whose printed proof lists
only some of the cases, and leaves open whether its transformations give a
claw-free graph with the same independence polynomial for a general
well-covered tree (p. 14).
