# extremal_graph_theory/mader_1973_ein_extremalproblem_des_zusammenhangs_von_graphen

[[extremal_graph_theory/_index|..]]

[[extremal_graph_theory/mader_1973_ein_extremalproblem_des_zusammenhangs_von_graphen/examples_p228|examples_p228]]: Mader's 1973 examples showing that no constant c_n makes (n/2)e(G) + c_n
edges force two vertices joined by n internally disjoint paths, for odd
n ≥ 5 and even n ≥ 6: from an (n − 2)-regular graph with m cut cliques and a
universal vertex, graphs with (n/2)(e − 1) + m(n/2 − 2), or m(n − 5), edges
and no two vertices joined by n such paths; the disproof of the
vertex-disjoint form of the Bollobás–Erdős conjecture the site records as
k_m(n) > (m/2)n + C.

[[extremal_graph_theory/mader_1973_ein_extremalproblem_des_zusammenhangs_von_graphen/korollar|korollar]]: Mader's exact edge-disjoint threshold: every graph with more than
(n/2)(e(G) − 1) edges has two vertices joined by n edge-disjoint paths, and
for every m ≥ n ≥ 2 there is a graph on m vertices with [(n/2)(m − 1)]
edges and no such pair; in the site's notation ℓ_m(n) = [(m/2)(n − 1)] + 1,
which is 1 + n·C(m,2) at the problem's parameters.

[[extremal_graph_theory/mader_1973_ein_extremalproblem_des_zusammenhangs_von_graphen/satz_1|satz_1]]: Mader's theorem that every finite graph G on at least n vertices with more
than (n/2)(e(G) − 1) − (1/2)σ_n(G) edges, σ_n(G) the sum over vertices of
degree below n − 1 of their degree deficits, contains two vertices joined by
n edge-disjoint paths; the edge-disjoint form of the Bollobás–Erdős
conjecture for every n.

[[extremal_graph_theory/mader_1973_ein_extremalproblem_des_zusammenhangs_von_graphen/satz_2|satz_2]]: Mader's girth-restricted vertex-disjoint bound: for n ≥ 4, every finite graph
G of girth at least n + 2 with at least n + 1 vertices and more than
(n/2)(e(G) − (n − 1)) edges contains two vertices joined by n paths that
pairwise share only their ends.

## Contents

- Introduction and notation (p. 223, page image). The paper opens with its
  question: how many edges a graph with a given number of vertices can have
  without containing two vertices joined by $n$ edge-disjoint paths
  ("kantendisjunkte Wege"). Conventions, quoted: "Sei $e(G)$ die Anzahl
  der Ecken, $\kappa(G)$ die Anzahl der Kanten des endlichen,
  ungerichteten, schlingenlosen Graphen $G$ ohne mehrfache Kanten" ($e(G)$
  the number of vertices, Ecken, and $\kappa(G)$ the number of edges,
  Kanten, of a finite undirected loopless graph without multiple edges).
  The announcement, quoted: "Es wird bewiesen, daß in jedem Graphen $G$ mit
  $\kappa(G)>\frac n2(e(G)-1)$ zwei Ecken existieren, die durch $n$
  kantendisjunkte Wege verbunden sind, und diese Abschätzung ist
  bestmöglich" (it is proved that in every graph $G$ with
  $\kappa(G)>\frac n2(e(G)-1)$ two vertices exist that are joined by $n$
  edge-disjoint paths, and this estimate is best possible). The next
  sentence attributes the case $n=4$ to [1] and the case $n=5$ to [4], and
  footnote 1 adds that, by an oral communication of Bollobás, Leonard has
  proved the inequality for $n=6$ as well. The paper also derives properties
  of the extremal graphs and closes with the analogous question for
  vertex-disjoint paths ("eckendisjunkte Wege"). Notation: $E(G)$ and $K(G)$
  the vertex and edge sets; $[x,y]=[y,x]$ the edge between $x$ and $y$;
  $\lambda(x,y;G)$ the maximum number of edge-disjoint paths between $x\ne y$
  in $G$; $\gamma(x,G)$ the degree of $x$; and, displayed,
  $\bar\lambda(G):=\max_{x\ne y}\lambda(x,y;G)$,
  $E_m(G):=\{x\in E(G)\mid\gamma(x,G)\le m\}$, $e_m(G):=|E_m(G)|$ and
  $\sigma_n(G):=e_0(G)+e_1(G)+\cdots+e_{n-2}(G)
  =\sum_{x\in E_{n-1}(G)}(n-1-\gamma(x,G))$, $n$ a natural number. A filing
  note: a vertex of degree $d\le n-1$ is counted by $e_d,\ldots,e_{n-2}$,
  that is $n-1-d$ times, so the two forms of $\sigma_n$ agree; $e_m(G)$
  counts vertices of degree at most $m$, as the site's thread reads it, not
  of degree exactly $m$.
- Satz 1 and its proof (pp. 223--226; the statement on the page image of
  PDF p. 1, the proof on the page images of PDF pp. 1--4 for structure),
  paged at
  [[extremal_graph_theory/mader_1973_ein_extremalproblem_des_zusammenhangs_von_graphen/satz_1|satz_1]].
  Quoted: "Satz 1. Jeder endliche Graph $G$ mit
  $\kappa(G)>\frac n2(e(G)-1)-\frac12\sigma_n(G)$ und $e(G)\ge n$ enthält
  zwei Ecken $x$ und $y$ mit $\lambda(x,y;G)\ge n$." The proof first counts
  edges by degrees: if $E_{n-1}(G)=E(G)$ then (1)
  $\kappa(G)=\frac{n-1}2e(G)-\frac12\sigma_n(G)$, and if exactly one vertex
  has degree at least $n$ then (2)
  $\kappa(G)\le\frac n2(e(G)-1)-\frac12\sigma_n(G)$ (p. 224). Then induction
  on the number of vertices, supposing $\bar\lambda(G)<n$: since
  $\frac{n-1}2e(G)\le\frac n2(e(G)-1)$ for $e(G)\ge n$, (1) and (2) give
  two vertices $a_1$, $a_2$ of degree at least $n$; Menger's theorem (cited
  to Wagner [7], Satz 9.3) gives a set $T$ of edges separating them with
  $|T|=\lambda(a_1,a_2;G)<n$, $G-T$ the disjoint union of $G_1\ni a_1$ and
  $G_2\ni a_2$, and $C_\nu$ the set of endpoints of $T$ in $G_\nu$. The
  induction hypothesis applied to $\bar G_\nu:=(E(G)-R_\nu,K(G)-K(G_\nu))$
  gives (3) $|C_1|+|C_2|>n$ (pp. 224--225). For the graphs $G'_\nu$
  obtained from $G$ by identifying the vertices outside $G_\nu$ to one
  vertex $A_\nu$, the assertion (A) $\lambda(x,y;G'_\nu)\le\lambda(x,y;G)$
  for $x,y\in E(G_\nu)$ is proved by rerouting a path through $A_\nu$ along
  two of the $a_1$--$a_2$ paths, so $\bar\lambda(G'_\nu)<n$ (p. 225); and
  the induction hypothesis, or (1) for a $G'_\nu$ with $e(G'_\nu)\le n$,
  applied to $G'_1$, $G'_2$ in three cases (I, $e(G'_1)\ge n$ and
  $e(G'_2)\ge n$; II, $e(G'_1)\le n$ and $e(G'_2)\ge n$; III,
  $e(G'_1)\le n$ and $e(G'_2)\le n$) gives (4) $|C_1|+|C_2|<n$ through the
  displayed inequalities ($\beta$), ($\gamma$), ($\delta$), the identity
  ($\beta'$) from (1) and the sharpenings ($\gamma'$), ($\gamma''$) of
  ($\gamma$), against (3) (pp. 225--226). Footnote 2 (p. 225) defines
  $W[a,b]$ as the subpath of a path $W$ between $a$ and $b$.
- Korollar and its proof (pp. 226--227, page images), paged at
  [[extremal_graph_theory/mader_1973_ein_extremalproblem_des_zusammenhangs_von_graphen/korollar|korollar]].
  Quoted: "Korollar. Für jeden Graphen $G$ mit $\kappa(G)>\frac n2(e(G)-1)$
  gilt $\bar\lambda(G)\ge n$. Zu jeder ganzen Zahl $m\ge n\ge2$ existiert
  ein Graph $G$ mit $e(G)=m$, $\kappa(G)=\bigl[\frac n2(e(G)-1)\bigr]$ und
  $\bar\lambda(G)<n$." Proof: $e(G)>n$ follows from the edge count, so
  the first claim is Satz 1; for the second, when $m$ is odd or $n$ even
  there is (by the Erdős--Gallai criterion, Harary [3], Satz 6.2, or by
  factorizations of complete graphs) a graph on $m$ vertices with one vertex
  of degree $m-1$ and all others of degree $n-1$, and when $m$ is even and
  $n$ odd one with one vertex of degree $m-1$, one of degree $n-2$ and all
  others of degree $n-1$; it has $\bigl[\frac n2(e(G)-1)\bigr]$ edges "und
  es ist offensichtlich $\bar\lambda(G)<n$" (and obviously
  $\bar\lambda(G)<n$).
- The extremal graphs (pp. 227--228; the definition on the page image, the
  rest for structure). A graph with one vertex of degree $e(G)-1$ and all
  others of degree less than $n$ is "vom Typ $n$" (of type $n$). The paper
  contrasts the cases: by [1], for $n=4$ every 2-connected graph $G$ with
  $\kappa(G)=\frac n2(e(G)-1)$ and $\bar\lambda(G)<n$ is of type $n$,
  whereas for every $n\ge5$ there are, the paper says, easily given
  2-connected extremal graphs with arbitrarily many vertices that are not
  of type $n$ (no construction is printed). For $G$ with $e(G)>n\ge2$,
  $\kappa(G)=\frac n2(e(G)-1)-\frac12\sigma_n(G)$ and $\bar\lambda(G)<n$
  and two vertices of degree at least $n$: $|C_1|+|C_2|=n$, $|T|=n-1$ and
  equality in ($\delta$), ($\beta$) and ($\gamma$) (or ($\gamma'$),
  ($\gamma''$)); (Z) at most one vertex of $C_1$, and of $C_2$, is
  incident with two or more edges of $T$; the graph $H:=(C_1\cup C_2,T)$
  is a tree with at least $e(H)-2$ leaves; and (Y) there are
  $y_1\in C_1$, $y_2\in C_2$ with $[y_1,y_2]\in T$ such that
  every edge of $T$ is incident with $y_1$ or $y_2$, so $G$ splits "längs
  $[y_1,y_2]$" (along that edge) into two extremal graphs $G'_1$, $G'_2$.
  For 2-connected $G$, by Satz 1 of [6] the set $T$ can be chosen so that
  one component has exactly one vertex of degree at least $n$, which is
  $y_1$, and $G'_1$ is of type $n$. Remarks (p. 228): with the hypothesis
  weakened to degree at least $n-1$ the same holds for $T$, so $G$ is
  $(n-1)$-edge-connected when $\sigma_n(G)=0$; and in a graph $G$ with
  $\kappa(G)=\frac n2(e(G)-1)-\frac12$, $\sigma_n(G)=0$ and
  $\bar\lambda(G)<n$ a smallest edge set separating two vertices of degree
  at least $n$ need not have the form (Y), and $|T|=n-2$ can occur.
- The vertex-disjoint problem (pp. 228--229, page images), paged at
  [[extremal_graph_theory/mader_1973_ein_extremalproblem_des_zusammenhangs_von_graphen/examples_p228|examples_p228]].
  Notation: $\mu(a,b;G)$ the maximum number of paths between $a\ne b$ that
  pairwise share only $a$ and $b$, $\bar\mu(G)=\max_{a\ne b}\mu(a,b;G)$, and
  $\tau(G)$ the girth ("Taille"). The question: how many edges a graph with
  $e(G)=m$ and $\bar\mu(G)<n$ can have. For $n\le4$ the extremal graphs
  are the same as for edge-disjoint paths, by [1]; for $n\ge5$ the paper
  says the problem seems "keine einfache Lösung zuzulassen" (to admit no
  simple solution); the related problem of the maximal number of edges
  without two adjacent vertices $a$, $b$ with $\mu(a,b;G)\ge n$ was solved
  in [5]. Then, quoted: "Die folgenden Beispiele zeigen, daß keine
  Konstante $c_n$ mit der Eigenschaft existiert, daß für jeden endlichen
  (2-fach zusammenhängenden) Graphen $G$ mit $\kappa(G)\ge\frac n2e(G)+c_n$
  gilt $\bar\mu(G)\ge n$" (the following examples show that no constant
  $c_n$ exists such that every finite, 2-connected, graph $G$ with
  $\kappa(G)\ge\frac n2e(G)+c_n$ has $\bar\mu(G)\ge n$). The construction,
  for odd $n\ge5$ (in parentheses, even $n\ge6$): a connected graph $G'$,
  regular of degree $n-2$, containing $m$ disjoint complete graphs
  $H_1,\ldots,H_m$ with $e(H_\mu)=n-2$ (resp. $n-3$) such that
  $G'-K(H_\mu)$ falls into $n-2$ (resp. $n-3$) components for every $\mu$;
  "Solche Graphen lassen sich ohne Schwierigkeit angeben" (such graphs can
  be given without difficulty), with footnote 3 that for even $n$ no graphs
  of the first kind exist and for odd $n\ge7$ the second construction also
  works. $G$ is $G'$ with a new vertex $A$ joined to every vertex of $G'$;
  then $\kappa(G)=\frac n2(e(G)-1)$ and $\mu(a,b;G)=n-2$ (resp. $n-3$) for
  $a\ne b$ in $E(H_\nu)$. $\bar G$ adds, for odd $n$, a new vertex $x_\mu$
  joined to every vertex of $H_\mu$ for each $\mu$, and for even $n$ two
  new vertices $x^1_\mu$, $x^2_\mu$ each joined to every vertex of $H_\mu$
  and to each other. Quoted: "Dann ist auch noch $\bar\mu(\bar G)<n$ und es
  gilt $\kappa(\bar G)=\frac n2(e(\bar G)-1)+m(\frac n2-2)$ (bzw.
  $\kappa(\bar G)=\frac n2(e(\bar G)-1)+m(n-5)$)." Edge counts recomputed
  here: $\kappa(G)=\frac{n-2}2e(G')+e(G')=\frac n2(e(G)-1)$; for odd $n$,
  $e(\bar G)=e(G)+m$ and $\kappa(\bar G)=\kappa(G)+m(n-2)
  =\frac n2(e(\bar G)-1)-\frac{mn}2+m(n-2)=\frac n2(e(\bar G)-1)+m(\frac n2-2)$;
  for even $n$, $e(\bar G)=e(G)+2m$ and
  $\kappa(\bar G)=\kappa(G)+2m(n-3)+m=\frac n2(e(\bar G)-1)+m(n-5)$, as
  printed. The absence of $n$ internally disjoint paths in $\bar G$ is
  asserted, not argued. The passage closes with the remark that if
  "kleine" (small) cycles are forbidden, in particular the complete graphs
  $V_m$ with $m\ge3$, then $\kappa(G)>\frac n2(e(G)-1)$ does force
  $\bar\mu(G)\ge n$.
- Satz 2 (pp. 229--231; the statement, (X) and the footnotes on the page
  image of PDF p. 7, the proofs and the closing remarks on the page images
  of PDF pp. 8--9), paged at
  [[extremal_graph_theory/mader_1973_ein_extremalproblem_des_zusammenhangs_von_graphen/satz_2|satz_2]].
  Quoted: "Satz 2. Jeder endliche Graph $G$ der Taille
  $\tau(G)\ge n+2$ mit $\kappa(G)>\frac n2(e(G)-(n-1))$ und $e(G)\ge n+1$
  enthält zwei Ecken $a$ und $b$ mit $\mu(a,b;G)\ge n$, falls $n\ge4$ ist"
  (every finite graph of girth at least $n+2$ with more than
  $\frac n2(e(G)-(n-1))$ edges and at least $n+1$ vertices contains two
  vertices joined by $n$ internally disjoint paths, for $n\ge4$), with
  footnote 4 that by a theorem of Erdős and Sachs [2] graphs satisfying
  these hypotheses exist. (X): every graph with $e(G)\ge n+1$,
  $\tau(G)\ge n+2$ and $\kappa(G)>\frac n2(e(G)-(n-1))$ contains, for
  $n\ge4$, at least two vertices of degree at least $n$; proved (p. 230) by
  counting edges around a shortest cycle $C$ and the set $N$ of vertices
  adjacent to it, each adjacent to exactly one vertex of $C$ since
  $\tau(G)>4$. Proof of Satz 2 (pp. 230--231): induction on $e(G)$; a vertex
  set $T$ with $|T|=n-1$ whose removal leaves parts $G'_1$, $G'_2$ of at
  least two vertices each gives $\bar\mu(G)\ge n$ by the induction
  hypothesis and an edge count; by (X) there are vertices $x_1\ne x_2$ of
  degree at least $n$, and if $\mu(x_1,x_2;G)<n$ Menger's theorem supplies
  such a $T$, directly when $[x_1,x_2]\notin K(G)$ and, when
  $[x_1,x_2]\in K(G)$, from a separating set $T'$ of $G-[x_1,x_2]$ with
  $|T'|=n-2>0$ enlarged by one of $x_1$, $x_2$, using $\tau(G)>3$.
  Closing remarks (p. 231): Satz 2 also holds for $n\le3$, the pentagon
  being the only exception, for $n=3$; sharpenings hold, for instance
  $\bar\mu(G)\ge4$ for every graph with $\kappa(G)>2(e(G)-3)$, $e(G)\ge6$
  and $\tau(G)\ge5$ up to one exception, and $\bar\mu(G)\ge8$ for every
  graph with $\kappa(G)>4(e(G)-7)$, $e(G)\ge10$ and $\tau(G)\ge6$; Satz 2
  "legt die Vermutung nahe" (suggests the conjecture) that every graph with
  $\kappa(G)>\frac n2(e(G)-1)$ and $\bar\mu(G)<n$ must contain triangles,
  though such a graph need not contain a $V_4$ (a complete graph on four
  vertices), by identifying two suitable graphs $H_1$, $H_2$ along an edge,
  for $n\ge5$. No argument is printed for the remarks.

$\kappa(G)=\bigl[\frac n2(e(G)-1)\bigr]$ und $\bar\lambda(G)<n$", is the
site's $\ell_m(n)=\lfloor\frac m2(n-1)+1\rfloor$ for every $m\ge2$; at the
problem's parameters, a graph on $1+n(m-1)$ vertices with $1+n\binom m2$
edges has more than $\frac m2\cdot n(m-1)=n\binom m2$ edges and at least $m$
vertices, so the answer is yes for every $m\ge2$ under that reading, and the
extremal graphs of the Korollar's proof at $e(G)=1+n(m-1)$, a universal
vertex over an $(m-2)$-regular graph, have exactly $n\binom m2$ edges, one
short (an arithmetic check made here). Under the vertex-disjoint reading,
the examples of pp. 228--229 (PDF pp. 6--7) show "daß keine Konstante $c_n$
mit der Eigenschaft existiert, daß für jeden endlichen (2-fach
zusammenhängenden) Graphen $G$ mit $\kappa(G)\ge\frac n2e(G)+c_n$ gilt
$\bar\mu(G)\ge n$", for odd $n\ge5$ and even $n\ge6$: the site's "for all
$m\ge6$ and any $C>0$, there exists an $n$ such that $k_m(n)>\frac m2n+C$",
which the site and Sørensen--Thomassen report as disproving the conjectured
$k_m(1+(m-1)n)=1+\binom m2n$ for every such $m$; the paper prints no
statement about the conjecture itself under this reading and does not name
Bollobás and Erdős.
The paper settles nothing the page leaves open: the page's status is the
site's label attached to the vertex-disjoint disproof, which is recorded through
[[extremal_graph_theory/sorensen_thomassen_1974_k_rails_graphs/theorem_4|Sørensen and Thomassen's Theorem 4]]
and
[[extremal_graph_theory/leonard_1973_conjecture_bollobas_erdos/counterexample_p281|Leonard's counterexample]],

**Results.**

- [[extremal_graph_theory/mader_1973_ein_extremalproblem_des_zusammenhangs_von_graphen/satz_1|Satz 1]]
  (p. 223): every finite graph $G$ with $e(G)\ge n$ and
  $\kappa(G)>\frac n2(e(G)-1)-\frac12\sigma_n(G)$ contains two vertices
  joined by $n$ edge-disjoint paths; by induction on the number of vertices
  through Menger's theorem (pp. 223--226).
- [[extremal_graph_theory/mader_1973_ein_extremalproblem_des_zusammenhangs_von_graphen/korollar|Korollar]]
  (p. 226): more than $\frac n2(e(G)-1)$ edges force $\bar\lambda(G)\ge n$,
  and for every $m\ge n\ge2$ some graph on $m$ vertices with
  $\bigl[\frac n2(m-1)\bigr]$ edges has $\bar\lambda(G)<n$; the exact
  edge-disjoint threshold.
- [[extremal_graph_theory/mader_1973_ein_extremalproblem_des_zusammenhangs_von_graphen/examples_p228|Examples (pp. 228--229)]]:
  for odd $n\ge5$ and even $n\ge6$, graphs $\bar G$ with
  $\kappa(\bar G)=\frac n2(e(\bar G)-1)+m(\frac n2-2)$, or $+m(n-5)$, and
  $\bar\mu(\bar G)<n$, so no constant $c_n$ makes $\frac n2e(G)+c_n$ edges
  force two vertices joined by $n$ internally disjoint paths.
- [[extremal_graph_theory/mader_1973_ein_extremalproblem_des_zusammenhangs_von_graphen/satz_2|Satz 2]]
  (p. 229): for $n\ge4$, every finite graph $G$ with $\tau(G)\ge n+2$,
  $e(G)\ge n+1$ and $\kappa(G)>\frac n2(e(G)-(n-1))$ has $\bar\mu(G)\ge n$;
  through the auxiliary statement (X) and induction with Menger's theorem
  (pp. 229--231).
