## Statement

"If $A,B$ are subsets of $V(G)$, they are anticomplete if $A\cap B=\emptyset$
and there are no edges of $G$ between $A$ and $B$. There is a well-known
problem of El-Zahar and Erdős [1, 2]:

**1.1 Problem:** Is the following true? For all integers $t,c\ge1$, there
exists $d\ge1$, such that if $\chi(G)\ge d$ and $\omega(G)<t$, then there
are anticomplete subsets $A,B\subseteq V(G)$ with $\chi(A),\chi(B)\ge c$.

This remains open. El-Zahar and Erdős proved that under the same
hypotheses, there are anticomplete subsets $A,B\subseteq V(G)$ with
$\chi(A)\ge3$ and $\chi(B)\ge c$, but there has been little further
progress."

The paragraph then refers to [4] for an analogue for infinite graphs of
infinite chromatic number, and notes that the bound on $\omega(G)$ cannot be
dropped: a large complete graph is a counterexample.

The references [1] and [2] are the 1985 Combinatorica paper of El-Zahar and
Erdős and Erdős's 1985 Kalamazoo paper; [4] is Milner, Discrete Math. 136
(1994).

**Source.** T. Nguyen, A. Scott and P. Seymour, *On a problem of El-Zahar
and Erdős*, arXiv:2303.13449v1 (23 March 2023), printed p. 1 = PDF p. 3,
read on the page image; published as J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 165 (2024),
211--222 (the journal text was not compared, so the label is the
preprint's). The artifact is identified in the
[[extremal_graph_theory/nguyen_2024_problem_el_zahar_erdos/_index|source digest]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the problem, the sentence "This remains
open" and the attribution paragraph were read clause by clause on the page
image. A note recorded on the consuming page: the asymmetric statement
"$\chi(A)\ge3$ and $\chi(B)\ge c$" attributed here to [1, 2] was not located
in the copies of either paper read for their cards.
