# extremal_graph_theory/pyber_1996_covering_edges_connected_graph_paths

[[extremal_graph_theory/_index|..]]

[[extremal_graph_theory/pyber_1996_covering_edges_connected_graph_paths/theorem_0|theorem_0]]: Pyber's forest case of Gallai's conjecture: a graph on n vertices in which
every cycle contains a vertex of odd degree, that is, whose even-degree
vertices induce a forest, is covered by floor(n/2) edge-disjoint paths,
and K_{2m+1} minus m-1 independent edges, an odd semi-clique, shows the
theorem is best possible.

[[extremal_graph_theory/pyber_1996_covering_edges_connected_graph_paths/theorem_i|theorem_i]]: Pyber's covering theorem: every connected graph on n vertices is covered by
n/2 + O(n^(3/4)) paths that may share edges, the asymptotic form of
Gallai's conjecture for coverings, with Theorem II's n/2 + 4e/n paths for a
graph with e edges.

[[extremal_graph_theory/pyber_1996_covering_edges_connected_graph_paths/theorem_ii|theorem_ii]]: Pyber's edge-count covering bound: every connected graph on n vertices with
e edges is covered by n/2 + 4(e/n) paths that may share edges, stronger
than Theorem I when the graph has few edges.

## Contents

- Abstract and Introduction (pp. 152--153). The abstract announces the
  covering bound of Theorem I, at most $n/2+O(n^{3/4})$ paths for every
  connected graph on $n$ vertices, and reads it as the asymptotic truth of
  a weak form of Gallai's conjecture. Lovász's theorem (p. 152), quoted:
  "Every graph $G$ on $n$ vertices can be covered by $\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor$
  edge-disjoint paths and cycles." The paper recalls that the Erdős--Gallai
  conjecture, $O(n)$ edge-disjoint cycles and edges cover the edges of $G$,
  is still open, with [10] cited for details, while without edge-disjointness
  "by a result of the author [9], $n-1$ cycles and edges suffice" (p. 152;
  the statement Problem 184 records from second-hand sources). Corollary [8]
  (p. 152): "Let $G$ be a graph on $n$ vertices. (i) Then $G$ can be covered by
  $n-1$ edge-disjoint paths. (ii) If each vertex of $G$ has odd degree then
  $G$ can be covered by $n/2$ edge-disjoint paths." Donald [2] strengthened
  (i) to $\lfloor(3/4)n\rfloor$ edge-disjoint paths. Theorem 0 (p. 152,
  quoted): "Suppose that each cycle of $G$ contains a vertex of odd degree.
  Then $G$ can be covered by $\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ edge-disjoint paths."
  Example (p. 153): deleting $m-1$ independent edges from the complete graph
  $K_{2m+1}$ leaves a graph $G$ on $n=2m+1$ vertices with exactly
  $(n-1)\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1$ edges, so that $\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1$ paths
  are needed to cover it, while its only cycle with all degrees even is a
  triangle; the paper concludes that Theorem 0 is best possible. Then the
  Conjecture (p. 153, quoted): "Every connected graph $G$ on $n$ vertices
  can be covered by $\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor$ edge-disjoint paths." The
  paper calls this best possible, notes that strong partial results exist
  but a full proof looks very hard, and records that Chung [1] suggested
  the case of covering paths that need not be edge-disjoint. Theorem I
  (p. 153, quoted): "Every connected graph $G$ on $n$ vertices can be
  covered by $n/2+O(n^{3/4})$ paths." Theorem II (p. 153, quoted): "Every
  connected graph on $n$ vertices with $e$ edges can be covered by
  $n/2+4(e/n)$ paths." Example (p. 153), introduced with the remark
  (quoted) "It is curious to note that one cannot prove an asymptotic
  version of Gallai's conjecture without proving the conjecture itself":
  if $H$ is an $m$-vertex counterexample to the conjecture, so that every
  path partition of $H$ has at least $m/2+1$ paths, and the $n$-vertex
  graph $G$ is a vertex $v$ joined by one edge to each of $k$
  vertex-disjoint copies of $H$, then partitioning $G$ takes at least
  $k(m/2+1)-\lfloor k/2\rfloor$ paths, which for $k\ge2m+3$ is at least
  $n/2+n/(2m+1)$, so no bound of the form $n/2+o(n)$ holds as
  $n\to\infty$.
- § 1, Edge-disjoint coverings (pp. 153--156). Lemma 1.1 (p. 154, quoted):
  "Let $\Sigma$ be a path and cycle partition of a graph $G$ with the number
  of cycles minimal among path and cycle partitions having at most $|\Sigma|$
  elements. Let $C$ be a cycle of $\Sigma$ and $x$ an arbitrary vertex of
  $C$. There exist two vertices $y,z\in V(C)$ such that $(x,y),(x,z)\in E(G)$
  and both $y$ and $z$ have even degree in $G$." Its proof "uses the method
  of Lovász [8]": from each $C$-neighbor of $x$ a sequence of
  $G$-neighbors of $x$ is traced through the paths of $\Sigma$ ending at
  odd vertices, and an exchange of $C$ and those paths for paths alone
  would reduce the number of cycles. Corollary 1.2 (p. 155, quoted): "Let
  $\Sigma$ be a cycle and path partition as in Lemma 1.1. Then for each cycle
  $C\in\Sigma$ there is a cycle $K$ of $G$ such that $V(K)\subseteq V(C)$ and
  the vertices of $K$ have even degree in $G$." Proof: the subgraph of $G$
  induced by the even-degree vertices of $C$ has minimum degree at least two
  by Lemma 1.1. Then (p. 155) Theorem 0 follows at once from Corollary 1.2
  and Lovász's theorem. Corollary 1.3 (p. 155, quoted):
  "Let $G$ be a $k$-connected graph on $n$ vertices. Then $G$ can be covered
  by $\lfloor n/2\rfloor+\lceil n/2k\rceil$ paths." Its proof removes a
  maximal matching $I$ between even-degree vertices, applies Theorem 0 to
  $G\setminus I$, and covers $I$ by $\lceil n/2k\rceil$ paths using
  Häggkvist and Thomassen's theorem that $k-1$ independent edges of a
  $k$-connected graph lie on a cycle. Lemma 1.4 (p. 155, quoted): "Let the
  graph $H$ be an edge-disjoint but not vertex-disjoint union of two cycles
  $C_1$ and $C_2$. Suppose $H$ cannot be covered by two paths. Then $C_1$
  has an edge $e$ with both endvertices in $V(C_2)$." The proof uses
  Thomason's theorem that a Hamiltonian decomposition of a 4-regular
  multigraph is never unique. The paper adds (p. 156) that a much stronger
  form of this lemma would be the key to improving its results, and asks
  (quoted): "Perhaps it is possible to characterize pairs of edge-disjoint
  cycles $C_1$, $C_2$ that cannot be covered by two paths."
  Lemma 1.5 (p. 156): the same conclusion for a cycle $C$ and a path $P$,
  $C$ has an edge with both endvertices in $P$.
- § 2, The proofs of Theorems I and II (pp. 156--158). Lemma 2.1 (p. 156,
  quoted): "Let $G$ be a connected graph on $n$ vertices and let $x\le n$ be
  an even number. There exists a subgraph $R\subset G$ such that (i) any
  path $P$ of the graph $G\setminus R$ contains at most $x$ vertices that
  have even degree in $G\setminus R$ (ii) there is at most one vertex $w_0$
  of even degree greater than $x^2$ in $G\setminus R$, and (iii) the edges
  of $R$ can be covered by $2(n/x)$ paths of $G$." Its proof strips paths
  through $x$ even vertices at a time and uses the Erdős--Gallai theorem
  [3] that $x\cdot n$ edges force a path of length $2x$. The proof of
  Theorem I (p. 157) is summarized on the result page. The proof of Theorem
  II (pp. 157--158) starts from a partition into exactly $\lceil n/2\rceil$
  paths and cycles with the fewest cycles, calls an element long if it has
  at least $4e/n$ edges, exchanges pairs of a long cycle and a short
  element, then pairs of short cycles, whose union two paths cover, and
  finishes with Lemmas 1.4 and 1.5 on a short element $S$ with
  $|V(S)|\le4(e/n)+1$.
- § 3, Infinite graphs (pp. 158--159). Lovász's extension of Corollary (ii)
  to locally finite graphs with all degrees odd; Rado's theorem on
  independent transversals in a matroid; and the Theorem (p. 159, quoted):
  "Let $G$ be a finite graph. Then $G$ has a covering $\mathcal P$ by
  edge-disjoint paths such that $\mathcal P$ has a cycle-free transversal",
  proved from a minimal covering and Rado's theorem, with the question of
  its extension to infinite graphs.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0583/_index|#583]]: Theorem 0
(printed p. 152, PDF p. 1), quoted above, $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$
edge-disjoint paths when every cycle of $G$ has a vertex of odd degree, is
the forest case that page's table attributes to the
paper: a cycle of $G$ all of whose vertices have even degree is exactly a
cycle of the subgraph induced by the even-degree vertices, so the hypothesis
says that subgraph is a forest, and the quotation as Theorem 1.1 of
[[extremal_graph_theory/bonamy_2019_gallai_s_path_decomposition_conjecture_graphs/theorem_1_3|Bonamy and Perrett]]
agrees with the printed statement. The theorem is stated for every graph,
connected or not, and gives $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ paths, one fewer than the
conjecture's $\lceil n/2\rceil$ when $n$ is odd; the Example of p. 153, the
complete graph on $2m+1$ vertices minus $m-1$ independent edges, needs
$\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1$ paths and has a triangle of even-degree vertices, so
the floor cannot survive one even cycle; these graphs are odd semi-cliques
in the sense of Bonamy and Perrett's
[[extremal_graph_theory/bonamy_2019_gallai_s_path_decomposition_conjecture_graphs/question_1_1|Question 1.1]].
Theorem I (printed p. 153, PDF p. 2), quoted above, $n/2+O(n^{3/4})$
covering paths for every connected graph on $n$ vertices, is the covering bound
the site records for the paper; the paths may share edges, so it does not
bound the path number of the problem, and the paper's second Example
(p. 153) shows why no such asymptotic statement can be proved for
edge-disjoint paths short of the conjecture itself: one $m$-vertex
counterexample yields connected graphs needing $n/2+n/(2m+1)$ paths. The
paper states the conjecture as $\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor$ edge-disjoint paths
(p. 153), the form of Erdős's 1971 question, and calls it Gallai's.
[[extremal_graph_theory/pyber_1996_covering_edges_connected_graph_paths/theorem_ii|Theorem II]]
(printed p. 153), $n/2+4(e/n)$ covering paths for a connected graph with $e$
edges, is the second covering bound that page records; its paths may also
share edges, so it does not bound the path number either. The paper does
not settle the problem.
