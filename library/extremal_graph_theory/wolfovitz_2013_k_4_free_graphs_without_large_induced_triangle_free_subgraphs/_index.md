# extremal_graph_theory/wolfovitz_2013_k_4_free_graphs_without_large_induced_triangle_free_subgraphs

[[extremal_graph_theory/wolfovitz_2013_k_4_free_graphs_without_large_induced_triangle_free_subgraphs/theorem_1_1|theorem_1_1]]: Wolfovitz's upper bound f_{3,4}(n) ≤ n^{1/2} (ln n)^{120} for all large n on
the largest order of a triangle-free induced subgraph forced in every
K_4-free graph on n vertices, derived from Theorem 1.2, the bound with
exponent 110 at n = q^2 + q + 1 for large prime powers q, with the
consequence ln f_{3,4}(n) = 0.5 ln n + O(ln ln n).

[[extremal_graph_theory/wolfovitz_2013_k_4_free_graphs_without_large_induced_triangle_free_subgraphs/theorem_1_2|theorem_1_2]]: Wolfovitz's bound f_{3,4}(n) ≤ n^{1/2} (ln n)^{110} for n = q^2 + q + 1 and
every sufficiently large prime power q, proved by a random union of
tripartite graphs on the lines of a projective plane of order q followed
by a variant of the K_4-free process; Theorem 1.1 is derived from it.

## Contents

- Abstract and § 1, Introduction (pp. 623--624, page images). The
  abstract's definition, quoted (p. 623): "Let $f_{3,4}(n)$, for a natural
  number $n$, be the largest integer $m$ such that every $K_4$-free graph
  of order $n$ contains an induced triangle-free subgraph of order $m$."
  The abstract then states the bound of Theorem 1.1 and calls it, by known
  results, "tight up to a polylogarithmic factor". The introduction's
  general function, quoted (p. 623): "Let $f_{r,s}(n)$, for natural numbers
  $2\le r<s$ and $n$, be the largest integer $m$ such that every $K_s$-free
  graph of order $n$ contains an induced $K_r$-free subgraph of order $m$."
  Theorem 1.1 (p. 623, quoted): "For every sufficiently large $n$,
  $f_{3,4}(n)\le n^{1/2}(\ln n)^{120}$." The review of earlier results
  (pp. 623--624), in the corpus's words: Erdős and Rogers [6] gave the
  first upper bound, $f_{3,4}(n)\le c_1n^{1-c_2}$ for positive constants
  $c_1$ and $c_2<10^{-4}$; Bollobás and Hind [2] added a lower bound and
  improved the upper one, with positive constants $c_3,c_4$ such that
  $c_3n^{1/2}\le f_{3,4}(n)\le c_4n^{7/10+\varepsilon}$ for every
  $\varepsilon>0$ and all large $n$; Krivelevich [10, 9] sharpened both
  sides to $c_5(n\ln\ln n)^{1/2}\le f_{3,4}(n)\le c_6n^{3/5}(\ln n)^{1/2}$
  for positive constants $c_5,c_6$; and Warnke [15] and, independently, the
  author [16] obtained a result implying an upper bound below Krivelevich's by a
  factor $(\ln n)^{3/10}$. A filing observation, not a review verdict: the
  upper bound $c_6n^{3/5}(\ln n)^{1/2}$ is attributed jointly to the 1994
  paper [10] and the 1995 paper [9]; the 1994 paper's Corollary 1 gives
  $cn^{2/3}(\log n)^{1/3}$, so the $n^{3/5}$ bound is read here as the 1995
  paper's, which is not held. The preprint's $(\ln n)^{3/10}$ improvement of
  $n^{3/5}(\ln n)^{1/2}$ is $n^{3/5}(\ln n)^{1/5}$, the bound its arXiv
  abstract states. The paper then observes (p. 624) that the earlier
  results did not give sharp estimates for $\ln f_{3,4}(n)$ and that Theorem 1.1 together
  with the Bollobás--Hind and Krivelevich lower bounds fixes it: for
  $n\to\infty$, $\ln f_{3,4}(n)=0.5\ln n+O(\ln\ln n)$, which the paper
  records as one case of the Dudek--Rödl conjecture [3]. A
  closing remark (p. 624) says that the exponent $120$ can be lowered by
  optimizing constants in the proof, but that the author does not expect
  this alone to give the optimal upper bound on $f_{3,4}(n)$.
- § 1.1, Outline of the proof of Theorem 1.1 (pp. 624--625; p. 624 on the
  page image, p. 625 in the text layer). Theorem 1.2 (p. 624, quoted): "For
  every sufficiently large prime power $q$, for $n=q^2+q+1$,
  $f_{3,4}(n)\le n^{1/2}(\ln n)^{110}$."
  The derivation of Theorem 1.1: for large $n$, Bertrand's postulate gives
  a prime power $q$ with $n\le q^2\le5n$; with $n'=q^2+q+1$, Theorem 1.2
  supplies a $K_4$-free graph on $n'$ vertices whose every vertex set
  larger than $n'^{1/2}(\ln n')^{110}$ contains a triangle; because $n'$ is
  at most a constant multiple of $n$, every set of more than
  $n^{1/2}(\ln n)^{120}$ vertices spans a triangle, and since $n\le n'$ a
  $K_4$-free graph of order $n$ with the same property exists. For the rest
  of the paper $q$ is
  a sufficiently large prime power, $n=q^2+q+1$ and
  $s=s(n)=\lfloor n^{1/2}(\ln n)^{110}\rfloor$, and the task is reduced to
  exhibiting a $K_4$-free graph of order $n$ with no induced triangle-free
  subgraph of order $s$. The plan: a random graph $G$
  of order $n$ in which, with positive probability, every edge lies in few
  copies of $K_4$ and every $s$-set $S$ spans a large family $\mathcal{T}_S$ of
  triangles, each sharing an edge with few others in $\mathcal{T}_S$; then a
  random deletion of edges of $G$ leaving a $K_4$-free graph in which, with
  positive probability, every $S$ keeps a triangle of $\mathcal{T}_S$.
- § 2, The random graph $G$ (pp. 625--629, text layer). A projective plane
  of order $q$ on points $P$ and lines $L$, with (P1) every two points on
  exactly one line, (P2) every two lines meeting in exactly one point, (P3)
  every point on $q+1$ lines and every line through $q+1$ points;
  $|P|=|L|=q^2+q+1=n$. Each line is put into $L'\subseteq L$ independently
  with probability $(\ln n)^2/(q+1)$; independently for each $l\in L'$, each
  point $p$ of $l$ gets a uniformly random color $\gamma_{l,p}\in\{1,2,3\}$
  and $G_l$ is the complete tripartite graph on the points of $l$ with the
  color classes as parts; $G$ is the union of the $G_l$ over $l\in L'$ on
  the vertex set $P$ (the paper credits a similar construction of Dudek
  and Rödl [3] as partial inspiration). Lemma 2.1 (p. 625, quoted): "With
  probability at least $2/3$, every edge in $G$ belongs to at most
  $32(\ln n)^8$ copies of $K_4$ in $G$." Its proof: Claim 2.2, that with
  probability at least $2/3$ every point lies on at most $2(\ln n)^2$ lines
  of $L'$ and every two points have at most $4(\ln n)^4$ points lying on the
  intersection of two lines of $L'$, one through each of them (Chernoff and
  the union bound); then a copy of $K_4$ through an edge $\{p_1,p_2\}$
  corresponds to one of three incidence types (Figure 1, p. 626), four
  collinear points spanning no $K_4$ by the definition of $G$ and (P1), and
  the first two types together, like the third, allow at most $16(\ln n)^8$
  choices of the vertex set, $32(\ln n)^8$ in all. McDiarmid's inequality
  is recalled (p. 627). The definition (p. 627), quoted: "Say
  that $S\subseteq P$ with $|S|=s$ is nice, if $S$ spans in $G$ a family
  $\mathcal{T}_S$ of at least $s(\ln n)^{200}$ triangles, such that every
  triangle in $\mathcal{T}_S$ shares an edge with at most $3(\ln n)^{100}$
  other triangles in $\mathcal{T}_S$." Lemma 2.3 (p. 627, quoted):
  "With probability at least $2/3$, every set $S\subseteq P$ with $|S|=s$ is
  nice." Its proof (pp. 627--628) conditions on $L'$ with every point on at
  least $0.5(\ln n)^2$ lines of $L'$ and $|L'|\le2n^{1/2}(\ln n)^2$, cuts
  the sets $S\cap l$ over the lines $l\in L'$ with more than $(\ln n)^{100}$
  points of $S$ into blocks $F_i$ of size $\lfloor(\ln n)^{100}\rfloor$
  (properties (Q1)--(Q4)), counts the triangles inside the blocks (each
  triple of a block spans a triangle with probability at least $10^{-1}$),
  and applies McDiarmid's inequality to get at least $s(\ln n)^{200}$
  triangles with probability at least $1-n^{-2s}$; triangles sharing an
  edge lie in one block by (P1), (Q3) and (Q4). Lemma 2.4 (p. 629): with
  positive probability both properties hold.
- § 3, Proof of Theorem 1.2 (pp. 629--630, text layer). Fix a $G$ with both
  properties. Each edge $e$ of $G$ gets a birthtime $\beta_e$ drawn
  uniformly from $[0,1]$; the edges are traversed in birthtime order
  and an edge is marked unless it lies in a copy of $K_4$ whose other five
  edges are all already marked; $G'$ is the graph of the marked edges,
  which the paper describes as the $K_4$-free process (its [15, 16]) run on
  $G$ in place of $K_n$, and $G'$ is $K_4$-free with probability 1. Lemma
  3.1 (p. 629, quoted): "With positive probability,
  for every set $S\subseteq P$ with $|S|=s$, there exists a triangle
  $T\in\mathcal{T}_S$ such that $T\subseteq G'$." Its proof (pp. 629--630):
  for $T\in\mathcal{T}_S$ let $A_T$ be the set of edges outside $T$ of the
  copies of $K_4$ sharing an edge with $T$, so $|A_T|\le(\ln n)^9$, and
  $E_T$ the event that every edge of $T$ is born before every edge of $A_T$,
  which implies $T\subseteq G'$ and has probability
  $3!|A_T|!/(|A_T|+3)!\ge(\ln n)^{-30}$; the number $X$ of
  $T\in\mathcal{T}_S$ with $E_T$ has $E(X)\ge|\mathcal{T}_S|(\ln n)^{-30}$,
  changing one birthtime changes $X$ by at most $a_e\le(\ln n)^{110}$, and
  $\sum_ea_e^2\le|\mathcal{T}_S|(\ln n)^{120}$, so McDiarmid's inequality
  with $|\mathcal{T}_S|\ge s(\ln n)^{200}$ gives $\Pr(X=0)\le n^{-2s}$, and
  the union bound over the sets $S$ finishes.
- References (p. 631), sixteen items, listed above.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0620/_index|#620]]: Theorem 1.1
(printed p. 623, PDF p. 1), "For every sufficiently large $n$,
$f_{3,4}(n)\le n^{1/2}(\ln n)^{120}$", is the bound the site's commentary
attributes to the paper, $f(n)\ll n^{1/2}(\log n)^{120}$; $f_{3,4}(n)$,
defined in the abstract through induced subgraphs, is the site's $f(n)$, the
largest order of a triangle-free induced subgraph forced in every $K_4$-free
graph on $n$ vertices, and the paper's logarithm is the natural one, which
the site's and the later literature's $(\log n)^{120}$ absorb into the
constant. With the Bollobás--Hind and Krivelevich lower bounds the
paper records $\ln f_{3,4}(n)=0.5\ln n+O(\ln\ln n)$ (p. 624), the site's
$f(n)=n^{1/2+o(1)}$. The paper leaves the polylogarithmic factor open and
says so (p. 623: "tight up to a polylogarithmic factor"; the remark on
p. 624); it settles nothing the problem page leaves open. Theorem 1.2 (p. 624), the
bound $f_{3,4}(n)\le n^{1/2}(\ln n)^{110}$ at $n=q^2+q+1$ for every
sufficiently large prime power $q$, is the step from which the paper derives
Theorem 1.1. Its account of the
author's preprint (p. 624: a $(\ln n)^{3/10}$ factor improvement of
Krivelevich's $n^{3/5}(\ln n)^{1/2}$) confirms the page's reading that the
preprint's $O(n^{3/5}(\ln n)^{1/5})$ is a different, weaker result.
