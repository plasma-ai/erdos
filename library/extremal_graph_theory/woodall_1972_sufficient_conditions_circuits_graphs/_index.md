# extremal_graph_theory/woodall_1972_sufficient_conditions_circuits_graphs

[[extremal_graph_theory/_index|..]]

[[extremal_graph_theory/woodall_1972_sufficient_conditions_circuits_graphs/corollary_11_1|corollary_11_1]]: Woodall's answer to Erdős's 1969 question: a graph on n ≥ 2r + 3 vertices
with at least C(n − r − 1, 2) + C(r + 2, 2) + 1 edges contains a circuit of
every length from 3 to n − r, and a graph on r + 3 ≤ n < 2r + 3 vertices with
at least [n²/4] + 1 edges does too; the first bound is sharp by a complete
(n − r − 1)-gon and a complete (r + 2)-gon sharing one vertex. Problem 1012's
f(k) ≤ 2k + 3, with the small range covered as well.

[[extremal_graph_theory/woodall_1972_sufficient_conditions_circuits_graphs/theorem_11|theorem_11]]: Woodall's main undirected theorem: a graph on n ≥ r + 3 vertices, each of
valency at least k, contains a circuit of every length from 3 to n − r once
its number of edges reaches the bound of its case, the cases split by
whether n ≥ 2r + 3 and by the size of k, each bound least possible.

[[extremal_graph_theory/woodall_1972_sufficient_conditions_circuits_graphs/theorem_2d|theorem_2d]]: Woodall's directed form of Ore's theorem: a directed graph on n ≥ 2
vertices in which the out-valency of a plus the in-valency of b is at least
n for every two distinct vertices a, b with no edge from a to b has a
directed Hamiltonian circuit.

## Contents

- Summary and § 1, Definitions (pp. 739--740, page images). The summary
  says that lower bounds on vertex valencies, on the number of edges, or on
  both, can force a circuit of at least, or of exactly, a
  specified length; that the paper lists many such theorems, old and new;
  that its culminating undirected result gives the number of edges a graph
  on $n$ vertices with minimum valency at least $k$ needs to force a circuit
  of length exactly $n-r$, Erdős having posed the case $k=0$ as an unsolved
  problem in 1969 (the paper's [5]); and that its main directed result is a
  directed form of Ore's theorem. Graphs are finite without loops or
  multiple edges; $\rho(a)$ is the valency of $a$, and in a directed graph
  $\rho_{\mathrm{out}}(a)$ and $\rho_{\mathrm{in}}(a)$ count the edges
  leaving and entering $a$; a path has distinct vertices, a circuit is a
  path closed by one more edge, and the length is the number of edges, so a
  circuit in an undirected graph has length at least $3$. An italic letter
  that stands for a number is a non-negative integer, and $[\alpha]$ is the
  integer part.
- § 2, Undirected graphs: examples (pp. 740--741, page images). The paper
  says every result stated is best possible in a sense, and collects the
  witnesses here. $G_1(n,k)$, for $0<k<\frac12n$: $k$ independent vertices,
  each joined to the same $k$ vertices of a complete graph on $n-k$ further
  vertices; $\binom{n-k}2+k^2$ edges, minimum valency $k$, not Hamiltonian.
  $G_2(n,d)$, for $n=k(d-1)+1$ with $k>0$ and $d>1$: $k$ complete $d$-gons
  sharing exactly one common vertex; $\frac12d(n-1)$ edges, minimum valency
  $d-1$, no circuit of length $d+1$ or more. $G_3(n)$: the complete
  bipartite graph with parts of $[\frac12n]$ and $n-[\frac12n]$ vertices;
  $[\frac14n^2]$ edges, no odd circuit, every even length from $4$ to
  $2[\frac12n]$. $G_4(n,r)$, for $r\ge0$ and $n\ge2r+3$: complete graphs on
  $n-r-1$ and on $r+2$ vertices that share exactly one vertex;
  $\binom{n-r-1}2+\binom{r+2}2$ edges, minimum valency $r+1$, no circuit of
  length $n-r$ or more. Here the paper records the question it goes on to
  answer: "In 1969 Erdös asked whether a graph on $n$ vertices, with more
  edges than $G_4(n,r)$, must contain a circuit of length $n-r$ (see [5],
  Problem 4)" (p. 741), and adds that Corollary 11.1 shows that it must.
  $G_5(n,k,r)$, for $n\ge2k+r+1$ and $k>0$: $k+r$ independent vertices,
  each joined to the same $k$ vertices of a complete graph on $n-k-r$
  further vertices; $\binom{n-k-r}2+k(k+r)$ edges, minimum valency $k$, no
  circuit of length $n-r$ or more; $G_5(n,k,0)=G_1(n,k)$.
- § 3, Results about Hamiltonian circuits (pp. 741--742, page images), for
  $G$ on $n\ge3$ vertices. Theorem 1 (Dirac): minimum valency $\frac12n$.
  Theorem 2 (Ore): $\rho(a)+\rho(b)\ge n$ for every nonadjacent pair.
  Theorem 3 (Pósa, as simplified by Nash-Williams): at most $j-1$ vertices
  of valency at most $j$ when $j<\frac12(n-1)$, and at most $j$ when
  $j=\frac12(n-1)$. Theorem 4 (Erdős [4]): minimum valency $k$ and either
  $k\ge\frac12n$ or at least
  $f(n,k)=\max\{\binom{n-k}2+k^2+1,\binom{[\frac12(n+2)]}2+[\frac12(n-1)]^2+1\}$
  edges, the Theorem of
  [[extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1962_remarks_paper_posa/theorem_p227|Erdős 1962]];
  the paper proves it on p. 742 by checking the hypotheses of Theorem 3.
  Theorems 1, 3 and 4 are sharp by $G_1$, and Theorem 2 by $G_1$ for odd
  $n$ and in general by any $G_4(n,r)$.
- § 4, Circuits of at least a specified length (pp. 742--743, page
  images). Theorem 5 (Dirac): minimum valency
  $d>1$ gives a circuit of length at least $d+1$. Theorem 6 (new):
  $\rho(a)+\rho(b)\ge2d-1$ for every nonadjacent pair, on $n\ge d+1\ge3$
  vertices, gives a circuit of length at least $d+1$; a corollary of the
  directed Theorem 6D. Theorem 7 (Erdős and Gallai [6], Theorem (2.7)): for
  $d>1$, at least $\frac12d(n-1)+1$ edges give a circuit of length at least
  $d+1$; the paper gives a new proof by induction on $n$.
- § 5, Circuits of exactly a specified length (pp. 744--749, page images). A
  graph on $n$ vertices is pancyclic when it has a circuit of every length
  from $3$ to $n$. Theorem 8 (Bondy [1]): a Hamiltonian graph with at least
  $\frac14n^2$ edges is pancyclic or is $G_3(n)$ with $n$ even. Corollary
  8.1: under Theorem 1 or Theorem 2 the same conclusion, and in either case
  every even length from $4$ to $n$. Corollary 8.2: pancyclic under any of
  (1) minimum valency at least $\frac12(n+1)$, (2) $\rho(a)+\rho(b)\ge n+1$
  ($n$ even) or $\ge n$ ($n$ odd) for nonadjacent pairs, (3) minimum valency
  $k$ and the edge bound of Theorem 4. Theorem 9 (Bondy [2], Corollary 3.1):
  at least $\frac14(k(2n-k)+1)$ edges and a longest circuit of length $k$
  give every length from $3$ to $k$. Corollary 9.1: $[\frac14n^2]+1$ edges
  and a circuit of length $k$ give every length from $3$ to $k$. Corollary
  9.2: $[\frac14n^2]+1$ edges on $n\ge3$ vertices give every length from $3$
  to $[\frac12(n+3)]$. Theorem 10 (Dirac): a connected graph on at least
  $2k$ vertices with no cut-vertex and minimum valency $k>1$ has a circuit
  of length at least $2k$. Lemma 11.1: $\binom{n-k}2+\binom{k+1}2+1$ edges
  with minimum valency $k$ give a connected graph without a cut-vertex.
  Sublemma 11.2.1: $\binom{n-k}2+\binom{k+1}2$ edges, minimum valency $k$
  and a path on at least $h+1\ge4$ vertices give a circuit of every length
  from $3$ to $h$; the paper credits the completion of the proof to the
  referee (p. 746). Lemma 11.2: on $n\ge r+3$ vertices, with the edge bound
  of Lemma 11.1, minimum valency $k$ and at most $j+r-1$ vertices of valency
  at most $j$ when $j<\frac12(n-r-1)$ (at most $j+r$ when
  $j=\frac12(n-r-1)$), a circuit of every length from $3$ to $n-r$; proved
  by induction on $r$ through a graph with one added vertex joined to all
  others and Sublemma 11.2.1. A Conjecture (p. 747) would extend Pósa's
  theorem: a connected graph without a cut-vertex whose low-valency counts
  obey the Lemma 11.2 condition has a circuit of length at least $n-r$. The
  main theorem, Theorem 11 (pp. 747--748), with
  $f(n,k)=\binom{n-k}2+\binom{k+1}2$ and $f(n,k,r)=\binom{n-k-r}2+k(k+r)$:
  a graph on $n\ge r+3$ vertices with minimum valency $k$ has a circuit of
  every length from $3$ to $n-r$ once it has the number of edges of its
  case. Case A, $n\ge2r+3$: A1, $k\le r+1$, $f(n,r+1)+1$; A2,
  $r+1\le k<\frac12(n-r)$,
  $\max\{f(n,k),f(n,k,r),f(n,[\frac12(n-r-1)],r)\}+1$; A3,
  $\max\{r+1,\frac12(n-r)\}\le k<\frac12n$, $f(n,k)+1$; A4, $k=\frac12n$,
  $[\frac14n^2]+1$; A5, $k>\frac12n$, no bound. Case B, $n<2r+3$: B1,
  $k\le\frac12n$, $[\frac14n^2]+1$; B2, $k>\frac12n$, no bound. The paper
  states that each bound is least possible, by $G_4(n,r)$ for A1, by
  $G_4(n,k-1)$, $G_5(n,k,r)$ and $G_5(n,[\frac12(n-r-1)],r)$ for A2, by
  $G_4(n,k-1)$ for A3 and by $G_3(n)$ for A4 and B1; that the bounds stay
  best possible for a circuit of length exactly $n-r$, except that A4 and B1
  might improve when $n-r$ is even; and that for a circuit of length at
  least $n-r$ the A4 and B1 bounds drop (no bound at $k=\frac12n$ by Theorem
  1, none for $k\ge n-r-1$ by Theorem 5, and $\frac12(n-r-1)(n-1)+1$ by
  Theorem 7). Proof (pp. 748--749): A5 and B2 from Corollary 8.2 (1); A4
  from Theorem 1 and Corollary 9.1; B1 from Corollary 9.2; A3 from Lemma
  11.1, Theorem 10 and Corollary 9.1; A2 by checking the hypotheses of Lemma
  11.2 as in the proof of Theorem 4; A1 by induction on $r$, with $r=0$ from
  Corollary 8.2 (3) and the step removing a vertex of valency $k\le r+1$.
  Corollary 11.1 (p. 749) is the case $k=0$, introduced as the answer to
  Erdős's question, with the note that Bondy ([2], Theorem 2) settled the
  case $r=1$ and that the case $r=0$ is the case $k=1$ of Theorem 4, noted
  earlier by Ore [14]; paged at
  [[extremal_graph_theory/woodall_1972_sufficient_conditions_circuits_graphs/corollary_11_1|corollary_11_1]].
- § 6, Directed graphs (pp. 749--754, page images). The Rédei and
  Harary--Moser theorem on directed graphs in which every two vertices are
  joined in at least one direction; Theorem 1D (Ghouila-Houri) and
  Corollary 1D.1; Conjecture 3D (Nash-Williams), a directed Pósa condition;
  Theorem 5D, minimum out-valency $d>0$ gives a directed circuit of length
  at least $d+1$; Theorem 6D,
  $\rho_{\mathrm{out}}(a)+\rho_{\mathrm{in}}(b)\ge2d-1$ for every ordered
  pair of distinct vertices with $a$ not joined to $b$, on $n\ge d+1\ge2$
  vertices, gives a directed circuit of length at least $d+1$; and the
  section's main result, Theorem 2D, the directed form of Ore's theorem:
  $\rho_{\mathrm{out}}(a)+\rho_{\mathrm{in}}(b)\ge n$ for every such pair,
  on $n\ge2$ vertices, gives a directed Hamiltonian circuit, proved through
  Lemmas 2D.1--2D.3 by extending a longest circuit.
- Acknowledgment (p. 754) to the referee, and References 1--17
  (pp. 754--755), listed above.

**Results.**

- [[extremal_graph_theory/woodall_1972_sufficient_conditions_circuits_graphs/corollary_11_1|Corollary 11.1]]
  (p. 749): on $n\ge r+3$ vertices, $\binom{n-r-1}2+\binom{r+2}2+1$ edges
  when $n\ge2r+3$, or $[\frac14n^2]+1$ edges when $n<2r+3$, force a circuit
  of every length from $3$ to $n-r$; the first bound is least possible by
  $G_4(n,r)$; the case $k=0$ of Theorem 11.
- [[extremal_graph_theory/woodall_1972_sufficient_conditions_circuits_graphs/theorem_11|Theorem 11]]
  (pp. 747--748): the seven-case edge bound that,
  with minimum valency $k$ on $n\ge r+3$ vertices, forces a circuit of
  every length from $3$ to $n-r$, each bound least possible; proved on
  pp. 748--749 from Corollaries 8.2, 9.1 and 9.2, Theorems 1 and 10 and
  Lemmas 11.1 and 11.2.
- [[extremal_graph_theory/woodall_1972_sufficient_conditions_circuits_graphs/theorem_2d|Theorem 2D]]
  (p. 751): the directed form of Ore's theorem, the main directed result,
  proved on pp. 751--754 through Lemmas 2D.1--2D.3 and Theorem 6D.
- Theorem 6 (p. 742, not paged): the paper's own undirected result on
  valency sums of nonadjacent pairs, a corollary of the directed Theorem 6D
  (p. 750).
