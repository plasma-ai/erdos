# Corvaja–Zannier: Integral points, divisibility between values of polynomials and entire curves on surfaces

[[factorials_binomials/_index|..]]

[[factorials_binomials/corvaja_zannier_2010_integral_points_divisibility_between_values_polynomials_entire_curves_surfaces/lemma_1|lemma_1]]: Corvaja and Zannier's translation of divisibility into integrality: at a
place of good reduction, the valuation of one local equation is at most that
of another exactly when the lifted point on the blow-up of the two curves'
transverse intersections is integral for the strict transform of the first.

[[factorials_binomials/corvaja_zannier_2010_integral_points_divisibility_between_values_polynomials_entire_curves_surfaces/theorem_1|theorem_1]]: Corvaja and Zannier's degeneracy theorem for a smooth cubic surface in P_3
with two hyperplane sections removed whose union is six lines, with its
entire-curve analogue (Theorem 1 bis), the generalized Albanese G_m
(Theorem 10) and the sharpness statements of Theorems 8 and 8 bis.

[[factorials_binomials/corvaja_zannier_2010_integral_points_divisibility_between_values_polynomials_entire_curves_surfaces/theorem_2|theorem_2]]: Corvaja and Zannier's two-variable divisibility theorem: for three pairs of
nonzero polynomials over the S-integers in general position with
deg f_i >= max{1, deg g_i}, the S-integral points (x,y) with f_i(x,y)
dividing g_i(x,y) for i = 1, 2, 3 are not Zariski-dense; with Corollary 1.

[[factorials_binomials/corvaja_zannier_2010_integral_points_divisibility_between_values_polynomials_entire_curves_surfaces/theorem_3|theorem_3]]: Corvaja and Zannier's Corollary 2, degeneracy of integral points on the
plane blown up at three points with the strict transform of four lines
removed, and Theorem 3, that this surface is simply connected with trivial
generalized Albanese variety.

[[factorials_binomials/corvaja_zannier_2010_integral_points_divisibility_between_values_polynomials_entire_curves_surfaces/theorem_4|theorem_4]]: Corvaja and Zannier's homogeneous divisibility theorem: for r >= 5
absolutely irreducible ternary forms F_i of one degree, at least deg G,
meeting G transversely with no three F_i sharing a zero, the S-integral
points with F_i(x,y,z) dividing G(x,y,z) for all i are not Zariski-dense;
with Corollary 3 and Theorem 5 on Del Pezzo surfaces of degree four.

[[factorials_binomials/corvaja_zannier_2010_integral_points_divisibility_between_values_polynomials_entire_curves_surfaces/theorem_6|theorem_6]]: Corvaja and Zannier's cyclic divisibility theorem: for n >= 6 linear forms
in three variables indexed by Z/nZ, any three independent, the points where
each F_i(x,y,z)(x,y,z) equals the product of the ideals (F_{i-1},F_i) and
(F_i,F_{i+1}) are not Zariski-dense; with its geometric form Proposition 1
and the entire-curve analogue Theorem 6 bis.

[[factorials_binomials/corvaja_zannier_2010_integral_points_divisibility_between_values_polynomials_entire_curves_surfaces/theorem_7|theorem_7]]: Corvaja and Zannier's parametric S-unit equation theorem: for polynomials
f, g, h of one degree with S-integral coefficients and no common zero, a
finite set of S-units contains one of u, v, u/v for every solution, so the
solutions are not Zariski-dense; with the classification of Theorem 11.

[[factorials_binomials/corvaja_zannier_2010_integral_points_divisibility_between_values_polynomials_entire_curves_surfaces/theorem_9|theorem_9]]: Corvaja and Zannier's sharpness result for Theorem 6: for at most five
linear forms in general position, a suitable ring of S-integers has a
Zariski-dense set of solutions of the cyclic ideal identities; with
Theorem 12, its n = 5 surface case, and Corollary 4 on five forms dividing
a quadratic form.

## Overview

The paper studies when polynomial divisibility conditions force algebraic
degeneracy of integral points on rational surfaces, together with parallel
statements for entire curves. Its basic translation is **Lemma 1 (§2)**: if two
curves on a smooth surface have local equations $\varphi=0$ and $\psi=0$ and
meet transversely, then after blowing up their intersection points, at each
non-archimedean place $\nu$ where the transversality, the local equations and
the blow-up survive reduction, the valuation inequality
$\nu(\varphi(P))\leq\nu(\psi(P))$ for a point $P$ off both curves is
equivalent to $\nu$-integrality of the lifted point relative to the strict
transform of the first curve. Thus divisibility of polynomial values is
converted into integral-point conditions on blow-ups.

The principal affine divisibility result is **Theorem 2 (§1; proof in §2)**. For
three pairs of nonzero polynomials $(f_i,g_i)\in\mathcal O_S[X,Y]^2$ satisfying
the four stated general-position conditions and
$\deg f_i\geq\max\{1,\deg g_i\}$, the points
$(x,y)\in\mathcal O_S^2$ satisfying $f_i(x,y)\mid g_i(x,y)$ for all $i=1,2,3$
are not Zariski-dense. In the proof, the authors blow up $\deg(f_i)^2$ suitably
chosen points on each curve $f_i=0$; the strict transforms have
self-intersection zero and pairwise intersections $d_i d_j$, while their
intersections with the line at infinity are $d_i$. **Proposition 2 (§2)**
supplies the resulting integral-point degeneracy criterion; its intersection
calculations are recorded in equations **(5)–(12)**. **Corollary 1 (§1)** gives
the concrete conclusion that, for a polynomial $g$ of degree at most one,
nonzero at $(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)$, the solutions of $xy(1-x-y)\mid g(x,y)$ are not
Zariski-dense. **Corollary 2 (§1)** interprets the linear case as a blow-up of
$\mathbf P^2$ at three points, and **Theorem 3 (§1; proof in §3 via Lemmas
2–4)** proves that the resulting open surface is simply connected and hence has
trivial generalized Albanese variety.

For homogeneous divisibility, **Theorem 4 (§1; proof in §2)** treats absolutely
irreducible forms $F_1,\ldots,F_r$ of one degree and an absolutely irreducible
form $G$, under transverse intersection and no-triple-intersection hypotheses.
If $r\geq5$ and $\deg F_i\geq\deg G$, then the projective classes of integral
triples satisfying

$$
F_i(x,y,z)\mid G(x,y,z)\qquad(1\leq i\leq r) \tag{1}
$$

are not Zariski-dense in $\mathbf P^2$. The proof again blows up $F_i=G=0$,
adding auxiliary centers when $\deg F_i>\deg G$, and invokes the earlier
integral-point theorem of Corvaja–Zannier [5]. **Corollary 3 (§1; proof in §2)**
applies this to a degree-four Del Pezzo surface with five conics removed, one
from each of five reducible hyperplane sections no three of which meet. **Theorem 5 (§1; proof in §2)** gives a related degeneracy
result after removing three hyperplane sections and one component of a reducible
fourth section.

A second central construction concerns cyclically arranged linear forms.
**Theorem 6 (§1)** states that for $n\geq6$ and linear forms $F_i$ in three
variables with every three independent, the projective points satisfying the
ideal identities

$$
F_i(x,y,z)(x,y,z)=(F_{i-1},F_i)(F_i,F_{i+1}) \tag{2}
$$

for every cyclic index $i$ are not Zariski-dense. After enlarging $S$ so that
$\mathcal O_S$ is a unique factorization domain and any three of the forms
stay independent modulo every place outside $S$, for coprime $S$-integer
coordinates, writing $\beta_i=\gcd(F_i,F_{i-1})$ gives
$F_i=\beta_i\beta_{i+1}\alpha_i$, and condition (2) says that every residual
factor $\alpha_i$ is an $S$-unit. The
geometric equivalent is **Proposition 1 (§2)**, concerning the blow-up of the
consecutive intersections of $n$ lines. Its proof computes $D_i^2=-1$,
$D\cdot D_i=n-4$, and $D^2=n(n-4)$; the auxiliary root satisfies equation
**(4)**, and the required numerical inequality reduces to
$7n^3-70n^2+207n-192>0$, exactly for integral $n\geq6$. Theorem 1, on the
non-density of integral points on a smooth cubic surface after removing six
lines lying on two hyperplanes, is deduced from the $n=6$ case. **Theorem 10
(§3)** identifies the generalized Albanese of this open cubic surface as
$\mathbf G_m$.

For the family of unit equations

$$
f(t)u+g(t)v=h(t), \tag{3}
$$

**Theorem 7 (§1; proof in §2)** assumes that $f,g,h$ have the same degree and no
common zero. It produces a finite set $\Phi\subset\mathcal O_S^*$ such that
every solution has at least one of $u,v,u/v$ in $\Phi$, hence the solutions are
not Zariski-dense. The compactification is a Hirzebruch surface. **Theorem 11
(§3)** classifies the relevant algebraic curves when $f,g,h$ have the same
degree and are pairwise coprime: every morphism $\mathbf A^1\to X$ is
constant, while every morphism $\mathbf G_m\to X$ has one of $u,v,u/v$
constant; fibers over zeros of $fgh$ supply the expected
$\mathbf G_m$-families.

The results are essentially ineffective: as explained in §1 and used throughout
§2, the main arithmetic input is the Main Theorem of [5], ultimately based on
the Schmidt–Schlickewei Subspace Theorem. Siegel’s theorem and the $S$-unit
theorem are cited background, not reproved. The analytic analogues (**Theorems 1
bis and 6 bis**, and the analogues stated after Theorem 2, Theorem 3 and
Theorem 5) are said in §2 to follow the same way with Cartan’s Second Main
Theorem in place of the Subspace Theorem, as explained by Levin, and §2
explicitly omits their proofs.

Sharpness is addressed in §4. **Theorems 8 and 8 bis (§1; proof of Theorem 8
in §4)** exhibit Zariski-dense integral points, after a finite extension of
$k$ and a suitable choice of $S$, and Zariski-dense entire curves when only
five lines lying on two hyperplane sections are removed from a smooth cubic
surface, and also for a particular configuration of nine lines. **Theorem
9 (§1)** says that the conclusion of Theorem 6 fails for $1\leq n\leq5$; the
geometric $n=5$ case is proved as **Theorem 12 (§4)** using a pencil of conics
and **Lemma 5**. **Corollary 4 (§4)** consequently shows that for any five
linear forms $F_i$ in general position there are a non-degenerate quadratic form
$G$ and a finite set $S$ for which all five divisibilities
$F_i(x,y,z)\mid G(x,y,z)$ hold on a Zariski-dense set. These are density
constructions, not exceptions to Theorems 2 or 4, because the relevant degree or
geometric hypotheses differ.

## Relation to E699

Write $B_k(N)=\binom Nk$. For fixed $i<j$, let $S_i$ consist of the archimedean
place and the primes $p<i$. An E699 counterexample $(n,i,j)$ is exactly the
condition

$$
\min\{v_p(B_i(n)),v_p(B_j(n))\}=0\quad\text{for every }p\notin S_i,
$$

or equivalently that $\gcd(B_i(n),B_j(n))$ is an $S_i$-unit. This resembles the
paper’s theme of controlling common local divisors, but it is not one of its
divisibility systems: Theorems 2 and 4 impose inequalities
$v_p(f(x))\leq v_p(g(x))$ for several fixed polynomial pairs, whereas E699
forbids simultaneous positivity of two valuations and lets $i,j$ vary.

For fixed $i,j$, primes $p>j$ give a useful elementary reduction. Since $p$
divides neither $i!$ nor $j!$ and the numerator factors of $B_i(n)$ occur among
those of $B_j(n)$,

$$
p>j,\,p\mid B_i(n)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\mid B_j(n).
$$

Hence an E699 counterexample forces $B_i(n)$ to have all prime factors at most
$j$. After enlarging $S$ to the primes $\leq j$, this says that the
integer-valued polynomial $B_i(n)$ is an $S$-unit. The one-variable consequence
of Siegel’s theorem quoted in §1—apply it to $f(X)=B_i(X)$ and $g(X)=1$ after
placing the denominators in $S$—therefore gives finiteness for each fixed pair
$(i,j)$ when $i\geq2$. This is cited background rather than a new or effective
result of the paper, and it gives neither a uniform bound as $j$ varies nor the
finite exclusions needed for E699.

The closest reusable device is **Lemma 1 (§2)**: if a reformulation of a
restricted E699 case introduces cofactors and turns valuation requirements into
polynomial divisibilities, the lemma identifies the corresponding blow-up and
boundary divisor. **Proposition 2** or **Theorem 2** could then prove
non-density provided three divisibility conditions, the degree inequalities, and
all four general-position hypotheses were verified. In the direct binomial model
these hypotheses fail: the factors $N,N-1,\ldots$ depend on one parameter, their
homogenized linear forms are highly non-generic, and the binomial polynomials
have denominators involving precisely some primes in the critical interval
$i\leq p\leq j$. Enlarging $S$ enough to make all coefficients integral discards
those primes and so cannot by itself prove E699.

**Theorem 6** is only structurally suggestive. Its decomposition
$F_r=\beta_r\beta_{r+1}\alpha_r$ separates neighboring gcd factors from $S$-unit
residual factors, but it requires a cyclic configuration of linear forms in
three variables with every three independent and the exact ideal identities (2).
Consecutive factors of binomial coefficients satisfy neither condition;
moreover, the symbol $n\geq6$ there counts forms and is unrelated to E699’s
upper binomial argument.

Thus the paper supplies a geometric dictionary for valuation inequalities and
qualitative, ineffective degeneracy theorems that might support an auxiliary
two-parameter reformulation. It does not establish a common prime $p\geq i$,
provide effective row bounds, or control the varying finite prime interval
$[i,j]$, and it gives no case of Problem 699.
