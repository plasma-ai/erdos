## Statement

Setting (p. 2). $k$ is a number field, $S$ a finite set of places of $k$
containing the archimedean ones, and $S$-integrality of a point of
$X=\tilde X\setminus D$ means that its reduction modulo no place outside $S$
lies in the reduction of $D$.

**Theorem 1** (§1, p. 2, quoted). "Let $\tilde X\subset\mathbb P_3$ be a
smooth cubic surface defined over $k$; let $H_1,H_2$ be hyperplane sections
such that the curve $H_1\cup H_2$ consists of six lines. Then the $S$-integral
points on the affine surface $X:=\tilde X\setminus(H_1\cup H_2)$ are not
Zariski-dense."

**Theorem 1 bis** (p. 3). If $\tilde X\subset\mathbb P_3$ is a smooth complex
cubic surface and $X$ is obtained from it by removing six lines lying on two
planes, then for every non-constant holomorphic map $f:\mathbb C\to X$ the
image $f(\mathbb C)$ is an algebraic curve. Its proof is among those §2
(p. 9) omits, as following by Cartan's Second Main Theorem in place of the
Subspace Theorem.

**Theorem 10** (§3, p. 17). If $\tilde X\subset\mathbb P_3$ is a smooth cubic
hypersurface and $X\subset\tilde X$ is the open subset obtained by removing six
lines on $\tilde X$ lying on two planes, then the generalized Albanese variety
of $X$ is a one-dimensional torus. The proof (pp. 17--18) shows that every
morphism $X\to\mathbb G_m$ is $\lambda\pi^N$, where $\pi$ is the quotient of
the two linear forms defining $H_1,H_2$.

**Remarks in the paper** (p. 2). A finiteness statement cannot hold: of the
twenty-seven lines of $\tilde X$, twenty-one remain on $X$, each with two
points at infinity, so after enlarging the ring of $S$-integers there are
twenty-one infinite families of integral points. The authors describe the
theorem as Vojta's conjecture for cubic surfaces with two hyperplane sections
removed, under the condition that both sections are completely reducible.

**Sharpness** (Theorem 8 and Theorem 8 bis, p. 8; proof of Theorem 8 on
pp. 22--23). Let $\tilde X$ be a smooth cubic surface over a number field $k$
and $L_1,\ldots,L_5$ five lines lying on two hyperplane sections of
$\tilde X$, with $X=\tilde X\setminus(L_1\cup\cdots\cup L_5)$. There are a
finite extension $k'$ of $k$ and a finite set $S$ of places of $k'$ such that
$X(\mathcal O_S)$ is Zariski-dense. There is also a configuration of nine
lines $L_1',\ldots,L_9'$ with a Zariski-dense set of $S$-integral points on
$\tilde X\setminus(L_1'\cup\cdots\cup L_9')$. Theorem 8 bis is the
corresponding statement for holomorphic maps $\mathbb C\to\tilde X$ with
Zariski-dense image omitting the five, respectively nine, lines.

## Proof pointer

Page 12. After enlarging $k$, $\tilde X$ is the blow-up of $\mathbb P_2$ at six
points $P_1,\ldots,P_6$ in general position, and the six removed lines are the
strict transforms of the lines through $P_i,P_{i+1}$, $i\in\mathbb Z/6\mathbb
Z$. The theorem is then the case $n=6$ of Theorem 6, through its geometric
form Proposition 1.
