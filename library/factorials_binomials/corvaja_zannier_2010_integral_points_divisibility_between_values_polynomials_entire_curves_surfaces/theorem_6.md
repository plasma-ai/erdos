## Statement

Setting (pp. 2, 6, 9). $k$ is a number field, $S$ a finite set of places of
$k$ containing the archimedean ones, $\mathcal O_S$ the ring of $S$-integers,
and $(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_h)$ the ideal of $\mathcal O_S$ generated by
$\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_h$.

**Theorem 6** (§1, p. 6). Let $n\geq6$ be an integer and, for
$i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, let $F_i$ be a linear form in three variables,
such that any three of the $F_i$ are linearly independent. Then the set of
points $(x:y:z)\in\mathbb P_2(k)$ satisfying, for every
$i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, the equality of ideals

$$
F_i(x,y,z)\cdot(x,y,z)=\bigl(F_{i-1}(x,y,z),F_i(x,y,z)\bigr)\cdot\bigl(F_i(x,y,z),F_{i+1}(x,y,z)\bigr)
\tag{2}
$$

is not Zariski-dense in $\mathbb P_2$. By homogeneity one may take $x,y,z$ to
be $S$-integers; otherwise the ideals are read as fractional ideals of $k$
(p. 6).

**Form without ideals** (pp. 6--7). After enlarging $S$, $\mathcal O_S$ is a
unique factorization domain and any three of the forms stay independent
modulo every place outside $S$, so for coprime $S$-integers $x,y,z$ any three
of the values $F_i(x,y,z)$ are coprime. Put
$\beta_i=\gcd(F_i(x,y,z),F_{i-1}(x,y,z))$; then $\beta_i,\beta_j$ are
coprime for $i\neq j$, and $F_i(x,y,z)=\beta_i\beta_{i+1}\alpha_i$ for an
$S$-integer $\alpha_i$. Theorem 6 says the points $(x:y:z)$ with every
$\alpha_i$ an $S$-unit are degenerate.

**Proposition 1** (§2, p. 10), stated by the paper as equivalent to Theorem
6. Let $n\geq6$ and, for $i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, let
$H_i\subset\mathbb P_2$ be a line defined over $k$, no three of them meeting.
Let $P_i=H_i\cap H_{i+1}$, let $\tilde X\to\mathbb P_2$ be the blow-up at
$P_1,\ldots,P_n$, let $D_i$ be the strict transform of $H_i$ and
$D=D_1+\cdots+D_n$. Then no set of $S$-integral points of
$\tilde X\setminus D$ is Zariski-dense.

**Theorem 6 bis** (§1, p. 7). Let $n\geq6$ and, for
$i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, let $\varphi_i$ be a linear form in three
variables with complex coefficients, no three of them linearly dependent. Let
$f,g,h:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C$ be entire functions without common zeros.
Suppose that for every $i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ and every zero
$p\in\mathbb C$ of $\varphi_i(f,g,h)$, $p$ is also a zero of
$\varphi_{i-1}(f,g,h)$ or of $\varphi_{i+1}(f,g,h)$, and (the print reads
"either $\varphi_{i-1}(f,g,h)$ or $\varphi_{i-1}(f,g,h)$" [sic]; the order
condition that follows involves $\varphi_{i-1}$ and $\varphi_{i+1}$)

$$
\operatorname{ord}_p\varphi_i(f,g,h)=\max\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_p\varphi_{i-1}(f,g,h),\operatorname{ord}_p\varphi_{i+1}(f,g,h)\bigr).
$$

Then $f/h$ and $g/h$ are algebraically dependent. Its proof is among those §2
(p. 9) omits.

The paper also says, without proof, that Theorem 6 extends to forms of
arbitrary degree under some general position conditions (p. 7). Theorem 9
(p. 8) shows the bound $n\geq6$ cannot be lowered; see the
[[factorials_binomials/corvaja_zannier_2010_integral_points_divisibility_between_values_polynomials_entire_curves_surfaces/theorem_9|Theorem 9 page]].

## Proof pointer

Pages 10--12. Proposition 1 comes from the Main Theorem of Corvaja--Zannier
(2004) in the form of Theorem 2.1 of Corvaja--Zannier (2006) with all weights
$1$. Each $D_i$ is a line through two blown-up points, so $D_i^2=-1$,
$D_i\cdot D_{i\pm1}=0$ and $D_i\cdot D_j=1$ otherwise; hence
$D\cdot D_i=n-4$ and $D^2=n(n-4)$. With $\xi$ the positive root of
$\xi^2+2(n-4)\xi-n(n-4)=0$ (4), the required inequality becomes
$\xi>(n^2-n)/(2(2n-4))$, which reduces to $7n^3-70n^2+207n-192>0$, true for
positive integers $n$ exactly when $n\geq6$ (p. 11). Theorem 6 follows
(pp. 11--12): after enlarging $S$, Lemma 1 with
$\varphi=F_i/F_{i-1}$ and $\psi=F_{i+1}/F_{i-1}$ shows that a solution of (2)
with all $F_i(x,y,z)\neq0$ lifts to an $S$-integral point for $D$.

  $\binom nj$ for all $1\leq i<j\leq n/2$. The theorem's factorization
  $F_i=\beta_i\beta_{i+1}\alpha_i$ separates the common factors of
  neighbouring values from an $S$-unit remainder, but it concerns a cyclic
  family of linear forms in three variables, any three independent; the paper
  does not apply it to binomial coefficients, and it gives no case of the
  problem. Its $n$ counts forms and is not the problem's $n$.
