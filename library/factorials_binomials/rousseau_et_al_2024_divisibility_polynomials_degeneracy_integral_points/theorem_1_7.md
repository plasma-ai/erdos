## Statement

**Theorem 1.7** (p. 3). Let $n\ge2$ and $q\ge3n$ be integers, and for each
index $i\in\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z$ let $F_i$ be a linear form in
$k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]$, the forms being in general position. Then there is a
Zariski closed subset $Z$ of $\mathbf P^n$ containing every point
$[x_0:\cdots:x_n]\in\mathbf P^n(k)$ that satisfies, for each
$i\in\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z$, the equality of ideals
$$
F_i(x_0,\ldots,x_n)\cdot(x_0,\ldots,x_n)
=\prod_{j=i-n+1}^{i}\bigl(F_j(x_0,\ldots,x_n),\ldots,F_{j+n-1}(x_0,\ldots,x_n)\bigr).
$$

**Notes on the print.** The statement does not say in which ring the ideals
are taken, and it does not say that $Z$ is proper. The proof (pp. 20--21)
works in $\mathcal O_S$, enlarging $S$ so that the coordinates are
$S$-integers and $\mathcal O_S$ is a unique factorization domain; it reads the
identity, its display (6.17), as the local-height equalities (6.18), deduces
that the lifted point is integral with respect to every $\widetilde H_i$
(6.20), and concludes that the solutions other than $P_1,\ldots,P_q$ lie in
the Zariski closure of $\pi(W)$, where $W$ is the closed set given by
[[factorials_binomials/rousseau_et_al_2024_divisibility_polynomials_degeneracy_integral_points/theorem_1_6|Theorem 1.6]].
Definition 2.3 (p. 6) makes that set proper but asserts finiteness, not
emptiness, of each integral set outside it; the proof states containment.
This page records the statement as printed and does not resolve the
difference.

**Source.** Erwan Rousseau, Amos Turchet, Julie Tzu-Yueh Wang, Divisibility
of polynomials and degeneracy of integral points, Math. Ann. 388 (2024),
no. 2, 1969--1999; labels and pages are those of the preprint
arXiv:2106.11337v1, the edition identified on the
[[factorials_binomials/rousseau_et_al_2024_divisibility_polynomials_degeneracy_integral_points/_index|source card]].
Statement on p. 3, proof on pp. 20--21.

## Proof pointer

Pp. 20--21, as summarized in the notes above: the ideal identity becomes the
integrality condition (6.20) on the blow-up of
[[factorials_binomials/rousseau_et_al_2024_divisibility_polynomials_degeneracy_integral_points/theorem_1_6|Theorem 1.6]],
through Definition–Theorem 2.1 (p. 5) and the pullback formula (6.1).
