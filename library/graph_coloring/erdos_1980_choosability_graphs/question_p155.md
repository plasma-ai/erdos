## Statement

**Definition** (p. 155). Each node carries $a$ distinct letters, and from
each node a $b$-subset is to be chosen, with the chosen subsets disjoint on
adjacent nodes; $G$ is *$(a:b)$-choosable* if such a choice can be made
whatever the letters are. Thus $(a:1)$-choosability is $a$-choosability.

**Open question** (p. 155, quoted). "If $G$ is $(a:b)$-choosable, does it
follow that $G$ is $(am:bm)$-choosable?"

**Open question** (p. 155, quoted). "If $G$ is $(a:b)$-choosable, and
$\frac cd>\frac ab$, does it follow that $G$ is $(c:d)$-choosable?"

The paper does not say what range of $m$ the first question intends; the
site's formulation takes every integer $m\ge1$.

## Proof pointer

The paper proves nothing about either question.
