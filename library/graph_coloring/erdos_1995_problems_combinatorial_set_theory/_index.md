# graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory

[[graph_coloring/_index|..]]

[[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_1|section_1]]: Erdős recalls the Erdős–Rado conjecture ω^2 → (ω^2,3)^2 proved by Specker,
Chang's ω^ω → (ω^ω,3)^2 and Milner's ω^ω → (ω^ω,k)^2, and reports that
Darby disproved his conjecture that α → (α,3)^2 implies α → (α,k)^2 for
every finite k.

[[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_2|section_2]]: Erdős and Hajnal ask for the least n, if it exists, such that every
triangle-free graph on 1, ..., n has three independent vertices a, b, a+b,
and Hajnal suggests an independent Hindman set.

[[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_3|section_3]]: Erdős restates problems of his paper with Hajnal and Szemerédi: the
slowly growing edge-deletion question, small subgraphs of chromatic number n
in uncountably chromatic graphs, the functions f_G^(1) and f_G^(2) with the
bound f_G^(2)(n) > (1-ε)n, and the open question f_G^(1)(n) > cn for a
graph of chromatic number and power aleph-one, with a prize.

[[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_4|section_4]]: Erdős states the Erdős–Hajnal conjecture that chromatic number at least
f_r(m) forces a subgraph of chromatic number m whose shortest odd cycle
exceeds 2r+1, open even for r = 1, and its finite version with girth
greater than r, which Rödl proved for r = 3.

[[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_5|section_5]]: Erdős recalls Sierpiński's c ↛ (aleph-one, aleph-one)^2 and his own 1954
question whether the pairs of reals split into three classes with every
uncountable set containing a pair from each class, which holds if
c = aleph-one and fails consistently by Shelah.

[[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_6|section_6]]: Erdős corrects an Erdős–Hajnal claim on triple systems of chromatic number
aleph-one and offers a prize for a characterization of the finite triple
systems contained in every triple system of chromatic number greater than
aleph-zero.

[[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_7|section_7]]: Erdős asks whether two graphs of uncountable chromatic number always have a
common subgraph of chromatic number 4, and perhaps one of infinite chromatic
number.

[[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_8|section_8]]: Erdős reports Hajnal's theorem that a graph of chromatic number greater than
aleph-zero has two vertex-disjoint such subgraphs, and Komjáth's answers to
Hajnal's question about aleph-one such pieces: yes at chromatic number
aleph-one, and consistently no relative to a measurable cardinal.

[[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_9|section_9]]: Erdős states the Erdős–Hajnal conjecture that the distinct odd cycle
lengths of a graph of infinite chromatic number have divergent reciprocal
sum, with guesses about their density, and asks for a density-0 sequence
infinitely many of whose terms are cycle lengths in every such graph.

***

Erdős, Paul, On some problems in combinatorial set theory. Publ. Inst. Math.
(Beograd) (N.S.) 57(71) (1995), 61-65. No notice is printed in the file, the
publisher's typeset PDF; the publisher's issue page
(http://elib.mi.sanu.ac.rs/pages/browse_issue.php?db=publ&rbr=77, read
2026-10-02) lists the article and states no copyright, license or terms, and
neither the issue's impressum nor the site's About page states any; the term is
unstated.

This memorial note for Đ. Kurepa states old and new problems in nine numbered
sections with their history, and proves nothing: every theorem in it is
reported with attribution. Section 1 (pp. 61–62) reviews the Erdős–Rado
conjecture $\omega^2\to(\omega^2,3)^2$ (proved by Specker), Chang's
$\omega^\omega\to(\omega^\omega,3)^2$ and Milner's
$\omega^\omega\to(\omega^\omega,k)^2$ for every $k<\omega$, and reports,
from a letter of Laver, that Darby disproved Erdős's conjecture that
$\alpha\to(\alpha,3)^2$ implies $\alpha\to(\alpha,k)^2$ for every finite
$k$. Section 2 (p. 62) asks, with Hajnal, for the least $n$ such that every
triangle-free graph on $1,\ldots,n$ has independent vertices $a$, $b$, $a+b$.
Section 3 (pp. 62–63) restates problems of the 1982 Erdős–Hajnal–Szemerédi
paper: whether a graph of infinite chromatic number can have every $n$-vertex
subgraph made bipartite by deleting $f(n)$ edges for an arbitrarily slow
$f(n)\to\infty$; whether the Erdős–Hajnal graphs whose $n$-chromatic
subgraphs are all large exist at uncountable chromatic number, which Erdős
expects not; and, for the functions $f_G^{(1)}$ and $f_G^{(2)}$ (printed as
"the smallest integer" for which every $n$-vertex induced subgraph contains an
independent, resp. induced bipartite, set of that size, read as the largest
such size), the theorem that for every $\varepsilon>0$ and every cardinal
$\kappa\ge\aleph_0$ some graph of chromatic number $\kappa$ has
$f_G^{(2)}(n)>(1-\varepsilon)n$ for all $n<\omega$, and the open question
whether some graph of chromatic number and power $\aleph_1$ has
$f_G^{(1)}(n)>cn$ for a constant $c$ (no condition on $c$ is printed), with
a prize offered for the section's problems. Section 4 (pp. 63–64) gives
the Erdős–Hajnal conjecture that chromatic number at least $f_r(m)$ (printed
$f_k(m)$) forces a subgraph of chromatic number $m$ whose smallest odd cycle
exceeds $2r+1$, open even for $r=1$, and its finite version with girth $>r$,
proved by Rödl for $r=3$. Sections 5 to 8 (p. 64 to p. 65) concern three
classes of pairs of reals, triple systems of uncountable chromatic number,
common subgraphs of two uncountably chromatic graphs, and Komjáth's results
on splitting uncountably chromatic graphs. Section 9 (p. 65) states the
Erdős–Hajnal conjecture that the distinct odd cycle lengths
$n_1<n_2<\cdots$ of a graph of infinite chromatic number satisfy
$\sum1/n_i=\infty$, perhaps with upper density $\frac12$, and asks for a
density-0 sequence infinitely many of whose terms are cycle lengths of every
such graph.

**Results.**

- [[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_1|Section 1]] (pp. 61–62): the ordinal relations for $\omega^2$ and
  $\omega^\omega$, and Darby's reported counterexample to Erdős's
  conjecture from $(\alpha,3)$ to $(\alpha,k)$.
- [[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_2|Section 2]] (p. 62): the Erdős–Hajnal problem on independent $a$, $b$, $a+b$ in
  triangle-free graphs on the integers, and Hajnal's Hindman-set guess.
- [[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_3|Section 3]] (pp. 62–63): the Erdős–Hajnal–Szemerédi problems, bound (1) for
  $f_G^{(2)}$ and the open linear question for $f_G^{(1)}$.
- [[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_4|Section 4]] (pp. 63–64): the Erdős–Hajnal conjecture on subgraphs of chromatic
  number $m$ without short odd cycles, and its finite version.
- [[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_5|Section 5]] (p. 64): the three-class question for pairs of reals.
- [[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_6|Section 6]] (p. 64): finite triple systems contained in every triple system of
  chromatic number $>\aleph_0$.
- [[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_7|Section 7]] (p. 64): a common subgraph of chromatic number $4$ in two graphs of
  uncountable chromatic number.
- [[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_8|Section 8]] (pp. 64–65): Hajnal's and Komjáth's results on splitting graphs of
  uncountable chromatic number.
- [[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/section_9|Section 9]] (p. 65): the Erdős–Hajnal reciprocal-sum conjecture for odd cycle
  lengths, and cycle lengths along a density-0 sequence.
