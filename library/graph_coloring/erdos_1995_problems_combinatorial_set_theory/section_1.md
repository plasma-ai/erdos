## Statement

Section 1 runs from printed p. 61 to p. 62 and uses Rado's arrow notation.

**The ω^2 and ω^ω relations (p. 61).** Erdős and Rado conjectured

$$
\omega^2\to(\omega^2,3)^2, \tag{1}
$$

that is, every graph on a sequence of order type $\omega^2$ contains a
triangle or an independent set of type $\omega^2$. Erdős reports that Specker
proved (1) in 1954, together with $\omega^n\not\to(\omega^n,3)^2$ for every
$n>2$ and $\omega^2\to(\omega^2,k)^2$ for every $k<\omega$. The relation

$$
\omega^\omega\to(\omega^\omega,3)^2 \tag{2}
$$

was proved by Chang; Milner simplified the proof and also proved

$$
\omega^\omega\to(\omega^\omega,k)^2\quad\text{for every }k<\omega, \tag{3}
$$

and Jean Larson found a much simpler proof of (3). Larson's first unsolved
problem, as Erdős reports it, asks whether
$\omega^{\omega^2}\to(\omega^{\omega^2},3)^2$.

**Erdős's conjecture and Darby's counterexample (p. 62).** Erdős had
conjectured that if $\alpha\to(\alpha,3)^2$ holds for an ordinal $\alpha$,
then $\alpha\to(\alpha,k)^2$ holds for every finite $k$. He reports that
R. Laver informed him by letter that Darby disproved this: there is a
countable ordinal $\alpha$ with $\alpha\to(\alpha,3)^2$ and
$\alpha\not\to(\alpha,6)^2$. Erdős adds that Darby obtained further results, which he hoped
would soon be published.

**Source.** P. Erdős, *On some problems in combinatorial set theory*, Publ.
Inst. Math. (Beograd) (N.S.) 57(71) (1995), 61–65; Section 1, printed
pp. 61–62. The edition is identified on the
[[graph_coloring/erdos_1995_problems_combinatorial_set_theory/_index|source card]].
