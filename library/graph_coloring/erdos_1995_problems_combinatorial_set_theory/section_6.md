## Statement

Section 6 is on printed p. 64.

Erdős and Hajnal had claimed that a triple system of chromatic number
$\aleph_1$ must have two triples with a common pair. In a paper with
Rothschild (printed "Rotschild") they found the error: the claim holds only
when the system has only $\aleph_1$ triples. They proved that there are
"edge disjoint triple systems of arbitrarily large chromatic number".

The paper of Erdős, Galvin and Hajnal (On set systems having large chromatic
number and not containing prescribed subsystems, Infinite and finite sets,
Coll. Math. Soc. János Bolyai 10 (1975), 425–513) studies these questions
systematically. Erdős states one unsolved problem: characterize the
finite triple systems that occur in every triple system of chromatic number
$>\aleph_0$. He offers a prize for a solution. For graphs the problem is
solved: a graph of chromatic number $\ge\aleph_1$ contains every finite
bipartite graph and need not contain any fixed odd cycle.

**Source.** P. Erdős, *On some problems in combinatorial set theory*, Publ.
Inst. Math. (Beograd) (N.S.) 57(71) (1995), 61–65; Section 6, printed p. 64.
