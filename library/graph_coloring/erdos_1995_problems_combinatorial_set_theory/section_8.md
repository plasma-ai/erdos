## Statement

Section 8 runs from printed p. 64 to p. 65.

Erdős states as easy that if $G$ has power $m$ and chromatic number
$\ge\aleph_0$, then $G$ contains $m$ vertex-disjoint subgraphs of infinite
chromatic number. Galvin asked whether a graph of chromatic number
$>\aleph_0$ always has two vertex-disjoint subgraphs of chromatic number
$>\aleph_0$; Hajnal proved that it does. Hajnal then asked whether the vertex
set of a graph of chromatic number $>\aleph_0$ can be split into $\aleph_1$
sets each inducing a graph of chromatic number $>\aleph_0$.

Erdős reports Komjáth's answers: affirmative when the chromatic number of $G$
is $\aleph_1$; and, if the existence of a measurable cardinal is consistent,
it is consistent that some graph of uncountable chromatic number is not the
vertex-disjoint union of $\aleph_1$ graphs of uncountable chromatic number.
Erdős adds that the chromatic number of such a graph is no doubt
enormous, and cites Komjáth's paper (P. Komjáth, A Galvin–Hajnal
conjecture on uncountably chromatic graphs, in A tribute to Paul Erdős,
Cambridge University Press, eds. A. Baker, B. Bollobás and A. Hajnal,
pp. 313–316).

**Source.** P. Erdős, *On some problems in combinatorial set theory*, Publ.
Inst. Math. (Beograd) (N.S.) 57(71) (1995), 61–65; Section 8, printed
pp. 64–65. The edition is identified on the
