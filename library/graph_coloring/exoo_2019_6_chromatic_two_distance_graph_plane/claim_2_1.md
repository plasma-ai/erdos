## Statement

Setting (p. 3). With $[a,b,c,d]$ the point
$\bigl(a\sqrt3/12+b\sqrt{11}/12,\ c/12+d\sqrt{33}/12\bigr)$, Step 1 lists a set
$S$ of 23 such points. Adding the reflections of the points of $S$ across
the $x$-axis and across the $y$-axis gives a set $T$ of 57 points; $U_k$ is
the image of $T$ under rotation by $k\pi/3$ about the origin
($k=0,\ldots,5$), and $V=U_0\cup\cdots\cup U_5$. The rotation by $\pi/3$
sends $[a,b,c,d]$ to $[(a-c)/2,(b-3d)/2,(3a+c)/2,(b+d)/2]$. $G$ is the
$\{1,2\}$-graph on $V$.

**Claim 2.1** (p. 3, quoted). "The graph $G$ has 205 vertices, 966 edges of
length 1, 423 edges of length 2, and exactly 18 5-colorings."

## Proof pointer

Section 3 (pp. 5--6). The paper says the edge counts follow by direct
computation from the authors' published vertex and adjacency data, and that
the count of 18 colorings comes from an exhaustive recursive search: the
vertices are grouped into orbits of the dihedral group generated by the
construction's reflections and rotation, ordered by degree and by adjacency
to earlier vertices, and each color assignment propagates its forced
consequences, the work being split across processors at a fixed depth
(Algorithm 1). The paper reports the run time for $G$ (3780 seconds elapsed
on 48 threads). It does not say whether colorings differing by a
permutation of the colors are counted as distinct.
