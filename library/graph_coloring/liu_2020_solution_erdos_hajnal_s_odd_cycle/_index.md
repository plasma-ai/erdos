## Main results and proof organization

[[graph_coloring/liu_2020_solution_erdos_hajnal_s_odd_cycle/theorem_1_4|Theorem 1.4]]
shows that for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, sufficiently large chromatic
number $k$ forces all odd cycle lengths in some interval
$[L,Lk^{1-\varepsilon}]$. Consequently the sum of reciprocals of
**distinct** odd cycle lengths is at least
$(1/2-o_k(1))\log k$. The coefficient is asymptotic, not an exact
$\frac12\log k$ lower bound. The
[[graph_coloring/liu_2020_solution_erdos_hajnal_s_odd_cycle/odd_cycle_harmonic_sum|harmonic-sum consequence]]
includes the compactness passage to infinite chromatic number and the
matching example $K_k$.

[[graph_coloring/liu_2020_solution_erdos_hajnal_s_odd_cycle/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]]
gives, from average degree $d\geq d_0$, every even cycle length in
$[\log^8L,L]$ for some $L\geq d/(10\log^{12}d)$.
[[graph_coloring/liu_2020_solution_erdos_hajnal_s_odd_cycle/corollary_1_3|Corollary 1.3]]
then makes increasing sequences of even integers with
$\sigma_{i+1}\leq\exp(\sigma_i^{1/10})$ unavoidable at high average
degree. This includes every exponentially bounded increasing sequence
of even integers after discarding finitely many initial terms. The
[[graph_coloring/liu_2020_solution_erdos_hajnal_s_odd_cycle/powers_of_two_in_infinite_chromatic_graphs|infinite-chromatic power-of-two consequence]]
is the implication credited to Zach Hunter on the problem site.

Both main theorems rely on
[[graph_coloring/liu_2020_solution_erdos_hajnal_s_odd_cycle/theorem_2_7|Theorem 2.7]],
which prescribes path lengths between fixed endpoints of a bipartite
sublinear expander without a large $1$-subdivided clique.
[[graph_coloring/liu_2020_solution_erdos_hajnal_s_odd_cycle/corollary_2_5|Expander extraction]]
uses the external Komlós–Szemerédi theorem. The internal proof builds
vertex expansions, robustly finds small adjusters, joins them, then
uses a supply of two-edge adjustments to make a long path exact. The
odd-cycle proof adds the distinct construction of a minimal odd cycle
of bipartite subgraphs, proving nonconsecutive members disjoint and
varying paths in a weighted independent subcollection. These methods
are shared dependencies of #57 and #63 rather than separate proofs of
the same theorem.

## Further results and coverage limits

- **Corollary 1.2, p. 3:** average degree $d$ implies
  $\sum_{t\in C(G)}1/t\geq(1/2-o_d(1))\log d$. Its proof sums the
  reciprocals of the even integers in Theorem 1.1's interval, using
  $\log L-8\log\log L\geq(1-o(1))\log d$. The complete balanced
  bipartite graph gives the matching constant. This is relevant to #65.
- **Corollary 1.5, p. 4:** for fixed positive integers $a,b$, chromatic
  number $k$ implies
  $\sum_{t\in C(G),\ t\equiv a\pmod b}1/t
  \geq(1/b-o_k(1))\log k$. For even $b$, the residue has fixed parity,
  so one uses the corresponding interval theorem; for odd $b$, the even
  and odd portions each have step $2b$, and their bounds add. For the
  even part one first takes a subgraph of minimum degree at least $k-1$.
  This paragraph is a proof sketch, not a separately reviewed full proof.
- **Corollary 1.6, p. 4:** for each $C\in\mathbb N$ there is $k_0$ such
  that every increasing odd sequence with $\sigma_{i+1}\leq C\sigma_i$
  is met by a cycle in every graph of chromatic number at least
  $\max\{k_0,\sigma_1^2\}$. Theorem 1.4, with a fixed exponent loss,
  gives an interval whose multiplicative width exceeds $C$ and whose
  upper end exceeds $\sigma_1$. Taking the first sequence member above
  its lower end proves the claim. This is a sketch.
- **Theorem 1.7, pp. 5 and 31–32:** for every integer $k$ there exists
  $d$ such that average degree at least $d$ forces a balanced subdivision
  of $K_k$, with all replacement paths having the same length. Section
  4.4 applies the robust path lemma repeatedly, reserving vertex
  expansions at different scales to maintain disjointness. Its full
  separate proof has not been extracted in this #57/#63 compilation.
