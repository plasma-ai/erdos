## Restatement of the entire conditional finite claim

Fix any source-feasible finite canonical high skeleton. Its row slots and column
slots are finite sets, with their two vertex roles distinguished. Its exposed
cells form a matching $M$, and its exposed nonnegative integer multiplicities
$j_{ab}$ and exposed mass $J$ give residual integer degrees

$$
d_a=s_a-\sum_{b:(a,b)\in M}j_{ab},\qquad
d'_b=t_b-\sum_{a:(a,b)\in M}j_{ab}.
$$

The required finite interface is

$$
0\leq d_a,d'_b\leq U,\qquad U\geq2,\qquad
\sum_a d_a=\sum_b d'_b=m_0=n-J\geq0,
$$

with $R=\lfloor U/2\rfloor$ and
$R<j_{ab}\leq\min(s_a,t_b)$ on $M$. Thus exposed cells have multiplicity at
least two. The source's full profile construction is not an additional
conclusion of the finite claim. Only the displayed residual properties and the
matching property of $M$ are used here.

The random object is an unconditioned uniform bijection between the two sets of
$m_0$ unused stubs, with those residual degrees. Its cell counts are $r'_{ab}$.
Let $H_{\rm res}$ be the simple bipartite graph with an edge in precisely the
cells with $r'_{ab}\geq2$. Let $\beta$ mean binary cycle-space dimension for
the corresponding simple graph. Define

$$
\mathcal E=
\{r'_{ab}=0\text{ on }M,\quad r'_{ab}\leq R\text{ off }M\}.
$$

The local reward is defined for nonnegative integers by

$$
g(0)=g(1)=g(2)=1,\qquad
g(x)=2^{\binom{x}{2}-1}\quad(x\geq3).
$$

The attachment being bounded is exactly

$$
\mathcal A(M,j)=
\mathbb E_{\rm res}\left[
 \left(\prod_{a,b}g(r'_{ab})\right)
 2^{\beta(M\cup H_{\rm res})}\mathbf1_{\mathcal E}\right].
$$

In particular, the expectation is not taken under the law conditioned on
$\mathcal E$. The event may have probability zero; no positive-probability
assumption is needed.

If $m_0=0$, the assertion is $\mathcal A(M,j)=1$. All subsequent definitions
and inequalities concern $m_0>0$ only. In that branch set, on every cell,

$$
\theta_{ab}=\frac{\exp(1)d_a d'_b}{m_0}.
$$

For every integer $x\geq3$, let $\Delta_x=g(x)-g(x-1)$. On
$E_0=(I_{\rm row}\times I_{\rm col})\setminus M$, define

$$
\lambda_e=\sum_{x=3}^{R}\Delta_x\frac{\theta_e^x}{x!},\qquad
q_e=\frac{\theta_e^2}{2}+\lambda_e.
$$

On $M$, set $\lambda_e=q_e=0$ without changing $\theta_e$. An empty
higher-threshold range contributes zero, and an empty product contributes one.
The quantities $\Delta_x,\theta_e,\lambda_e,q_e$ are nonnegative.

Assume the exact source joint estimate: for every nonnegative integer demand
array $\mathbf x$ supported in $E_0$, including an empty or infeasible array,

$$
\mathbb P_{\rm res}(r'_e\geq x_e\text{ for every }e)
\leq \prod_{e:x_e>0}\frac{\theta_e^{x_e}}{x_e!}.
$$

The empty demand has both sides equal to one. A demand exceeding the available
total, a row degree, or a column degree has probability zero. No assertion of
independence of cells is among the hypotheses.

Let $\mathfrak E(M)$ contain all edge subsets of the complete bipartite cell
set $M\cup E_0$ whose vertex degrees are even. For a nonnegative integer $r$,
write

$$
T_R(r)=1+\sum_{x=3}^{R}\Delta_x\mathbf1_{\{r\geq x\}},\qquad
S_R(r)=\mathbf1_{\{r\geq2\}}+
       \sum_{x=3}^{R}\Delta_x\mathbf1_{\{r\geq x\}}.
$$

For each $F\in\mathfrak E(M)$, the frozen expression is equivalently

$$
\Phi_F(r')=
\prod_{e\in F\setminus M}S_R(r'_e)
\prod_{e\in E_0\setminus F}T_R(r'_e).
$$

The complete claim assessed is the conjunction of the following statements:

1. On $\mathcal E$, the reward times the cycle-space cardinality is exactly
   $\sum_{F\in\mathfrak E(M)}\Phi_F(r')$.
2. For every $F\in\mathfrak E(M)$,

$$
\mathbb E_{\rm res}[\Phi_F(r')\mathbf1_{\mathcal E}]
\leq
\prod_{e\in F\setminus M}q_e
\prod_{e\in E_0\setminus F}(1+\lambda_e).
$$

3. Summing these inequalities gives the source's finite bound (9.8):

$$
\mathcal A(M,j)\leq
\left(\prod_{e\in E_0}(1+\lambda_e)\right)
\left(\sum_{F\in\mathfrak E(M)}
             \prod_{e\in F\setminus M}q_e\right).
$$

These statements cover every finite input just described, without a
large-$n$ condition or an error term. They do not give (9.9), (9.10), a later
residual activity estimate, a second-moment bound, amplification, an asymptotic
result, or a resolution of E625. They also do not reconstruct the upstream
canonical decomposition or the full proof of the prescribed-cell estimate.

## The three weakest steps, independently rederived

### 1. Counting even subsets after truncation and restriction

For every nonnegative integer $r$, including $r>R$,

$$
S_R(r)=T_R(r)\mathbf1_{\{r\geq2\}}.
$$

For $r<2$, all higher-threshold indicators vanish and the assertion is zero on
both sides. For $r\geq2$, the initial term of $S_R$ is one, which is the
initial term of $T_R$. This argument also handles $R=1$ or $R=2$.

Consequently, without yet imposing the event, every $F$ contributes

$$
\Phi_F(r')=
\left(\prod_{e\in E_0}T_R(r'_e)\right)
\mathbf1_{\{F\setminus M\subseteq H_{\rm res}\}}.
$$

An even set in the complete cell graph satisfies the latter containment if and
only if it is an even subset of $M\cup H_{\rm res}$. Edges of $M$ need no
residual threshold. This counts actual edge subsets, not their restrictions
outside $M$; no injectivity theorem about restrictions is required here.

For any finite graph, identify an edge subset with its incidence vector over
$\mathbb F_2$. The condition of even degree at every vertex is exactly that
the vector lies in the kernel of the vertex-edge incidence map. This is the
binary cycle space; a vector space of dimension $\beta$ over $\mathbb F_2$
has $2^\beta$ elements. Thus the independent counting calculation gives the
following global identity for the truncated reward:

$$
\sum_{F\in\mathfrak E(M)}\Phi_F(r')=
\left(\prod_{e\in E_0}T_R(r'_e)\right)
2^{\beta(M\cup H_{\rm res})}.
$$

On $\mathcal E$, telescoping yields $T_R(r'_e)=g(r'_e)$ on every off-$M$
cell. When $r'_e\leq2$, the sum is empty and both values are one. When
$3\leq r'_e\leq R$, its value is
$1+g(r'_e)-g(2)=g(r'_e)$. No return gives $g(r'_e)=g(0)=1$ on $M$.
This proves the claimed event-restricted identity. It also identifies exactly
why the corresponding identity with the original reward need not hold outside
the event.

### 2. A joint threshold bound followed by numerical factorization

Fix $F$. At a selected off-$M$ cell the allowed choices are a demand of two
with coefficient one, or one demand $x\in\{3,\ldots,R\}$ with coefficient
$\Delta_x$. At an unselected off-$M$ cell the choices are a demand of zero
with coefficient one, or one of those higher demands. These are sums inside a
single cell factor, so the expansion never imposes two separate demands on the
same cell and never introduces a spurious factor $\theta_e^{2+x}$.

For a chosen demand $k$ at a cell, set its numerical bound weight to

$$
B_e(0)=1,\qquad B_e(k)=\theta_e^k/k!\quad(k>0).
$$

For each complete choice, let $c$ be the product of its coefficients and let
$\mathbf x$ be the resulting array. The exact indicator product is the
indicator of the joint event, so

$$
\mathbb E[c\mathbf1_{\{r'\geq\mathbf x\}}
                 \mathbf1_{\mathcal E}]
\leq c\mathbb P(r'\geq\mathbf x)
\leq c\prod_{e\in E_0}B_e(x_e).
$$

The first inequality uses $c\geq0$ and the unconditioned probability law.
The second uses a single application of the joint estimate to the complete
array. For its source specialization, the row and column index sets are the
finite slot sets, the degrees are the residual lists, and the prescribed set
is $D=\{e:x_e>0\}\subseteq E_0$. This supplies every hypothesis of
Lemma 6.2. Shared rows and columns are allowed. If row, column, or total
feasibility fails, the probability is zero, as required by the same statement.
The empty demand is handled separately by one on each side. No conditional
probability or product of marginal probabilities is substituted.

All choices form a finite Cartesian product. Summing their numerical weights
therefore factors by distributivity. The sum at a selected cell is
$\theta_e^2/2+\sum_{x=3}^R\Delta_x\theta_e^x/x!=q_e$; the sum at an
unselected cell is $1+\lambda_e$. This proves the fixed-$F$ bound. The only
factorization is in a deterministic finite sum after the probability estimate.

Here $\Delta_3=4-1=3$, and for $x\geq4$ the exponent difference in the
two rewards is $x-1$, so $g(x)/g(x-1)=2^{x-1}>1$. This independently supplies
the coefficient nonnegativity essential to removing the event indicator.

### 3. Composition with the attachment and insertion of missing factors

The sample space and cell set are finite; all rewards and truncated expressions
are finite. Multiply the identity from step 1 by $\mathbf1_{\mathcal E}$
and average. Finite linearity gives

$$
\mathcal A(M,j)=
\sum_F\mathbb E[\Phi_F\mathbf1_{\mathcal E}].
$$

Applying step 2 to each summand gives a product of selected $q_e$ factors and
unselected $1+\lambda_e$ factors. Since $\lambda_e\geq0$, multiplying this
nonnegative product by
$\prod_{e\in F\setminus M}(1+\lambda_e)$ can only enlarge it. The enlarged
product contains $1+\lambda_e$ for every cell in $E_0$, independently of
$F$, and hence gives exactly (9.8) after summation.

This derivation does not divide by $q_e$ or $\lambda_e$, so it includes zero
activities. It also does not bound the even-set sum by a product. That latter
step belongs to the excluded later result. The only sum-expectation exchange
here is finite.

Separately, if $m_0=0$, every cell count is zero, the event holds, and every
reward is one. The residual support has no edges. A matching $M$ has no
nonempty even edge subset, so its binary cycle space has dimension zero. The
attachment is therefore one, without dividing by $m_0$.

## Strongest attempted refutation and boundary attacks

The strongest attempted refutation was to force the purported reward identity
to fail where dropping the event would either hide a large on-$M$ reward or
replace an uncapped off-$M$ reward by its smaller truncation. Both mechanisms
really can fail in finite residual systems satisfying the displayed setup.
They do not refute the frozen proof, because it keeps the event at precisely
the point where these replacements are made.

For a no-return test, take one row and one column of size seven, $U=7$, $R=3$,
$M$ equal to the sole cell, and expose $j=4$ pairs there. Then $J=4$, the two
residual degrees are three, and $m_0=3$. Every residual matching has $r'=3$ in
the exposed cell. The off-$M$ cap is vacuous, and even a cap of three on that
cell would be respected. But the original residual reward is $g(3)=4$, while
$E_0$ is empty and the only even subset of the one-edge graph is empty. Thus
$\sum_F\Phi_F=1$, not four. The actual event excludes every such residual
matching because of no return. The actual attachment is zero and the actual
bound is $0\leq1$. This is a concrete witness against removing no return
from the reward identity, not against the identity as stated.

For an off-$M$ cap test, take one row and one column of size six, $U=6$, $R=3$,
and $M=\varnothing$. Then $m_0=6$ and the sole residual cell has count six.
Its reward is $g(6)=2^{14}$, whereas its truncation is $1+\Delta_3=4$.
The support graph is one edge, so the cycle factor is one. Again the uncapped
identity fails, but the actual event is false and both event-restricted sides
are zero. The proof only removes the event after replacing the integrand by the
nonnegative truncated expansion on that event. It never makes the false
replacement exposed by this test.

I also attacked two distinct possible bookkeeping failures:

- **Cycles completed by exposed edges.** Let the two row sizes and two column
  sizes all be five, $U=5$, $R=2$, and let $M$ be the two diagonal cells with
  exposed multiplicity three each. The residual degrees are all two and
  $m_0=4$. On the no-return event the two off-diagonal counts are two. The
  residual support alone is a matching, but its union with $M$ is a four-cycle.
  The even subsets are the empty set and the entire four-cycle, so the
  integrand and $\sum_F\Phi_F$ both equal two on this event. There are
  $2!2!=4$ permitted stub bijections among $4!=24$, giving
  $\mathcal A=1/3$. Here $\lambda=0$, $\theta=\exp(1)$ on each cell,
  and the right side of (9.8) is $1+\exp(4)/4$. Counting only even subsets
  of the residual graph would miss the cycle, but the frozen family uses
  $M\cup E_0$ and counts it correctly.
- **Correlated cell counts.** In the same uniform residual degree system,
  each event $r'_{12}=2$ and $r'_{21}=2$ has probability $1/6$, and their
  intersection also has probability $1/6$, not $1/36$. A product of marginal
  probabilities would therefore give a false upper bound. The frozen proof
  instead applies the joint source estimate once. Its bound for these two
  demands is $\exp(4)/4$, which is valid; no probabilistic factorization is
  used. Demands sharing a row or column likewise remain within that joint
  theorem's explicit interface.

Further boundary checks found no exception. For $R=1$, a selected off-$M$
edge cannot meet its threshold on the cap event; unselected factors are one.
For $R=2$, all higher increments disappear but threshold-two support and even
cycles remain. A zero row or column degree forces all incident cell counts to
zero; a selected such cell gives both a zero left side and a zero $q_e$ factor.
Empty demand arrays and empty cell products are handled without $0^0$.
Nonempty demands exceeding any row, column, or total budget have empty events.
None of these checks needs event probability to be positive.

The explicit examples above test failure mechanisms. They are not numerical
sampling offered as evidence for a universal theorem; the universal deductions
are the finite identities and inequalities in the preceding section.
