## Statement

Setting as on
[[graph_coloring/scott_2018_survey_chi_boundedness/conjecture_3_1|3.1]]:
graphs are finite and simple, and an ideal is $\chi$-bounded if
$\chi(G)\le f(\omega(G))$ for some function $f$ and every $G$ in it.

**3.3** (p. 4, quoted). "For every tree $T$, the ideal of all graphs
containing no subdivision of $T$ as an induced subgraph is $\chi$-bounded."

The survey introduces it as a known weakening of the Gyárfás–Sumner
conjecture and cites it to Scott's paper "Induced trees in graphs of large
chromatic number", J. Graph Theory 24 (1997) (its reference [105]); the
survey gives no proof.

**Source.** Alex Scott, Paul Seymour, A survey of χ-boundedness, J. Graph
Theory 95 (2020), no. 3, 473–504, doi:10.1002/jgt.22601; arXiv:1812.07500.
The edition read and its page numbering are named on the
[[graph_coloring/scott_2018_survey_chi_boundedness/_index|source card]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the statement was read on the page image.
The proof is in the cited paper and was not read.

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/graph_coloring/E0738/_index|Problem 738]]: a weaker
  form of the problem's assertion. By the de Bruijn–Erdős argument recorded
  on [[graph_coloring/scott_2018_survey_chi_boundedness/conjecture_3_1|3.1]],
  the result gives that every triangle-free graph of infinite chromatic number
  contains, for every tree $T$, some subdivision of $T$ as an induced
  subgraph; this is a check made here, not a statement of the survey. It
  does not give $T$ itself.
