## Statement

Setting (pp. 1--3). Fix an integer $m>0$ with prime factorization
$m=p_1^{a_1}\cdots p_r^{a_r}$, $a_i>0$. A *vanishing sum of $m$-th roots of
unity of weight $n$* is an equation $\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_n=0$ with every
$\alpha_i$ an $m$-th root of unity in $\mathbb C$, repetitions allowed.
$W(m)$ is the set of weights $n$ of such sums; the empty sum counts, so
$0\in W(m)$. Throughout, $\mathbb N$ denotes the nonnegative integers,
$0$ included (p. 2).

In the group ring language of the paper, $G=\langle z\rangle$ is cyclic of
order $m$, $\varphi:\mathbb ZG\to\mathbb Z[\zeta_m]$ is the ring map with
$\varphi(z)=\zeta_m$, and vanishing sums correspond to the elements of
$\mathbb NG\cap\ker\varphi$, the weight being the augmentation $\varepsilon$
(the sum of the coefficients).

**Main Theorem** (p. 2). For every such $m$,

$$
W(m)=\mathbb Np_1+\cdots+\mathbb Np_r .
$$

**Theorem 5.2** (p. 12) restates it in the group ring: under the notation of
Theorem 4.8, every $x\in\mathbb NG\cap\ker\varphi$ has
$\varepsilon(x)\in\sum_{i=1}^r\mathbb Np_i$.

Consequences the paper notes (p. 2): $W(m)$ depends only on the set of prime
divisors of $m$, not on their multiplicities, and every nonempty vanishing sum
of $m$-th roots of unity has weight at least the smallest prime divisor of $m$.
**Corollary 5.6** (p. 13) spells out even $m$: $W(m)=2\mathbb N$ when $m$ is a
power of $2$, and otherwise $W(m)$ is every nonnegative integer except the odd
numbers below $p$, where $p$ is the smallest odd prime dividing $m$.

## Proof pointer

The inclusion $\supseteq$ is immediate (p. 1): for each $i$ the $p_i$-th roots
of unity sum to zero, a vanishing sum of weight $p_i$, and sums of vanishing
sums vanish. For $\subseteq$ (pp. 12--13), write $x$ as a sum of minimal
elements of $\mathbb NG\cap\ker\varphi$. By the
[[group_theory/lam_leung_2000_vanishing_sums_roots_unity/theorem_4_8|Lower Bound Theorem 4.8]]
a minimal element is either a rotation of some $\sigma(P_i)$, of weight $p_i$,
or has weight greater than $p_1(p_2-1)>(p_1-1)(p_2-1)$; in the second case
Lemma 5.1 (p. 12: for coprime positive integers $p,q$, every integer
$n\ge(p-1)(q-1)$ lies in $\mathbb Np+\mathbb Nq$; cited to LeVeque, proof left
to the reader) puts the weight in $\mathbb Np_1+\mathbb Np_2$.

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/integer_sequences/E0774/_index|Problem 774]]: the theorem
  restricts the total weight of any vanishing sum of $n$-th roots of unity with
  nonnegative integer coefficients to the semigroup generated by the primes
  dividing $n$. It applies to a roots-of-unity relation only once that
  relation is a genuine vanishing sum with nonnegative coefficients; the paper
  says nothing about dissociated sets or the problem.
