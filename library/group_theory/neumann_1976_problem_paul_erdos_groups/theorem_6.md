## Statement

For a group $G$, the paper's graph $\Gamma(G)$ (p. 467) has the elements of
$G$ as vertices, with $g$ and $h$ joined exactly when $[g,h]\ne1$, where
$[g,h]=g^{-1}h^{-1}gh$; a complete subgraph is a set of elements of $G$ no two
of which commute. A **PE-group** is a group whose graph contains no infinite
complete subgraph, and a **FIZ-group** is a group whose center has finite
index (p. 467).

**Theorem 6** (p. 470). "The group $G$ is a PE-group if, and only if, it is a
FIZ-group."

That is, $\Gamma(G)$ has no infinite complete subgraph if and only if
$|G:Z(G)|$ is finite.

**Bound from the proof** (p. 470). If $|G:Z(G)|=n$, then among any $n+1$
elements of $G$ two lie in the same coset of $Z(G)$ and so commute; hence
$\Gamma(G)$ has no complete subgraph with more than $n$ vertices.

**Remarks of p. 471.** The paper states, without proof, that the bound $n$
"can be immediately improved to $n-1$ in general", and a little further when
the arithmetic of $n$ is taken into account. The bound $n-1$ fails for
abelian $G$, where $n=1$ and a single element is a complete subgraph, so
"in general" is read here as covering non-abelian $G$; that reading, and the
following argument for it, are this page's and not the paper's. For
non-abelian $G$, a complete subgraph with at least two vertices contains no
central element and meets each non-central coset of $Z(G)$ at most once, so
it has at most $n-1$ vertices, and a single vertex is within the bound since
$n\ge4$ when $G/Z(G)$ is not cyclic. The paper further records (p. 471):

- the value $n-1=3$ is attained by the quaternion group and by the dihedral
  group of order $8$; when $n=2^d$, the value $n-1$ is attained by the free
  group of rank $d$ of the variety generated by the quaternion group (a remark
  credited to M. F. Newman); for the symmetric group of degree $3$, $n=6$
  while the largest complete subgraph has order $4$;
- if $\Gamma(G)$ has no complete subgraph of order greater than $m$, the proof
  of Theorem 6 yields $\log n=O(m^2)$, a bound the paper calls very crude; the
  author's guess, "based on no evidence", is $n=O(m^2)$;
- among the finite values of the exact maximum order $m$ of a complete
  subgraph, $2$ is the only one that does not occur: $2$ fails by Erdős's
  remark that for non-commuting $a,b$ the element $ab$ commutes with neither;
  the paper's example is the dihedral group of order $4(m-1)$, whose graph has
  a complete subgraph of order $m$ and none larger (the paper omits the
  verification; the example covers $m\ge3$, and $m=1$ is the abelian case).

## Proof pointer

Pages 468--470. The forward direction combines Lemma 1 (p. 468), every
PE-group is an FC-group (every conjugacy class finite), proved by Ramsey's
theorem, with Corollary 5 (p. 470), no group that is FC but not FIZ is a
PE-group. Corollary 5 comes from Lemma 4 (pp. 469--470), which extends a pair
of sequences $a_1,\dots,a_n$ and $b_1,\dots,b_n$ with the $a_i$ pairwise
non-commuting, $[a_i,b_j]=1$ for $i\ne j$, $[a_i,b_i]\ne1$ and the $b_i$
pairwise commuting by one more term each, using Lemma 2 and Corollary 3
(p. 469): an FC-group with an abelian subgroup of finite index is FIZ, so the
centralizer of the finitely many $a_i,b_i$, a subgroup of finite index, is
not abelian. Iterating gives an infinite pairwise non-commuting sequence. The
converse is the coset count above.

## Dependencies

Ramsey's theorem (infinite form), and Lemmas 1, 2 and 4 and Corollaries 3
and 5 of the same paper.
