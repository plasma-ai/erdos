## Statement

Setting (pp. 1--2). A set $A\subset\mathbb N$ is primitive when no two distinct
elements divide one another. Leaving aside the degenerate set $A=\{1\}$, the
paper measures a primitive set by

$$
f(A)=\sum_{a\in A}\nu_0(a),\qquad \nu_0(a)=\frac{1}{a\log a},
$$

with $\nu_0$ defined on $\mathbb N_{\ge1}=\{2,3,4,\ldots\}$ (display (1.1)).

**Theorem 1.1** (Erdős–Sárközy–Szemerédi, #1196; p. 2, quoted). "If $A$ is a
primitive set contained in $[x,\infty)$ for some $x\ge2$, then
$f(A)\le1+O\left(\frac{1}{\log x}\right)$." By the paper's notation
(p. 6, Section 1.2), the implied constant is absolute.

The paper says (pp. 2--3) that this proves the conjecture
$f(A)\le1+o(1)$ as $x\to\infty$ with a more quantitative error term, and
that by the known asymptotic (1.2) for $f(\mathbb N_k)$, quoted from its
reference [24], the bound is sharp up to the lower-order term. The previous
best bound it records is $e^\gamma\pi/4+o(1)\approx1.399$, due to Lichtman
(its reference [29]).

## Proof pointer

Section 4, pp. 18--20. After reducing to $A\subset[x,X]$ by a limiting
argument, the proof runs the von Mangoldt downward chain on $\mathbb N$, in
which $n$ passes to $n/q$ with probability $\Lambda(q)/\log n$, with an
initial mass on $[x,X]$ chosen so that the hitting mass at each $n$ in that
range is $\nu_0(n)$. Since a primitive set meets a divisibility chain at most
once, $f(A)$ is at most the total initial mass; after relabeling this is the
sum (4.2) of $\Lambda(q)/(r\log^2r)$ over $q\mid r$ with $r/q<x$, which
Lemma 3.3(i) bounds by $1+O(1/\log x)$ (4.3). Remark 4.2 (p. 19) gives a
second proof through the upward von Mangoldt chain and the weight
$\nu_\Lambda$, with error $2\gamma/\log x+O(1/\log^2x)$; Section 10.1
(pp. 29--30) recasts the argument as a flow network, and Remark 6.2
(pp. 24--25) derives the qualitative form from Theorem 1.3.

Remark 4.1 (p. 19) records that Nat Sothanaphan, using GPT, analyzed a
variant of the argument to obtain
$f(A)\le1+\gamma/\log x+O(1/\log^2x)$; the paper does not prove that bound.
Remark 4.3 (p. 20) states extensions to $A\cap D$ for divisor-closed $D$ and
to sets coprime to a modulus or made of multiples of $d$.
