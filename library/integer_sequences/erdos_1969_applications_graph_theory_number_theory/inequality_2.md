## Statement

Immediately after the proof outline of (1), Erdős writes (p. 78): "It
would be interesting to sharpen (1) and prove that for a certain absolute
constant $c$

$$
\max k=\Pi(x)+cx^{2/3}/(\log x)^2+o\Bigl(\frac{x^{2/3}}{(\log x)^2}\Bigr) \tag{2}
$$

I have not been able to prove (2)." Here $\max k$ is the largest size of
a sequence $a_1<\dots<a_k\le x$ in which no term divides the product of two
others, as in (1).

**Source.** P. Erdős, *Some applications of graph theory to number theory*,
The Many Facets of Graph Theory (Kalamazoo 1968), Springer (1969), 77--82;
display (2) on printed p. 78 (PDF p. 2), read on the page image. The same
question appears as display (2) of Erdős's 1970 Chapel Hill lecture
(printed p. 136), recorded on
[[integer_sequences/erdos_1970_applications_graph_theory_number_theory/display_2|its own page]].
