## Statement

"Assume now that no $a_i$ divides the product of two others, then I proved
that [2] ($\pi(x)$ denotes the number of primes not exceeding $x$)

$$
\pi(x)+\frac{c_1x^{2/3}}{(\log x)^2}<\max k<\pi(x)+\frac{c_2x^{2/3}}{(\log x)^2}. \tag{1}
$$

The proof of both the upper and the lower bound used combinatorial
methods." Here $a_1<\dots<a_k\le n$ is a sequence of integers (the
paragraph's opening line writes the range as $\le n$ and the display as
$x$); [2] is the 1938 Tomsk paper.

**Source.** P. Erdős, *Some applications of graph theory to number theory*,
Proc. Second Chapel Hill Conf. on Combinatorial Mathematics and its
Applications (1970), 136--145; display (1) on printed p. 136 (PDF p. 1 of
the ten-page scan), read on the page image.
