## Statement

"Probably

$$
\max k=\pi(x)+\frac{cx^{2/3}}{(\log x)^2}+o\Bigl(\frac{x^{2/3}}{(\log x)^2}\Bigr) \tag{2}
$$

for a certain $c$; but I could not prove (2)." Here $\max k$ is the
largest size of a sequence up to $x$ in which no term divides the product
of two others, as in (1).

**Source.** P. Erdős, *Some applications of graph theory to number theory*,
Proc. Second Chapel Hill Conf. on Combinatorial Mathematics and its
Applications (1970), 136--145; display (2) on printed p. 136 (PDF p. 1),
read on the page image.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the display and its sentence were read
clause by clause on the page image. A conjecture; no proof.
