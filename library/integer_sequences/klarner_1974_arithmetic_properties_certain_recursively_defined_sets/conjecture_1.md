## Statement

Setting (pp. 447--449). $P=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}$; $\langle\rho:1\rangle$ is the
least subset of $P$ containing $1$ and closed under the operation $\rho$; a
*per-set* is a finite union of infinite arithmetic progressions in $P$.

**Conjecture 1** (p. 457, quoted). "Suppose $r,m_1,\cdots,m_r\in P$, and
$(m_1,\cdots,m_r)=1$. Then $\langle m_1x_1+\cdots+m_rx_r:1\rangle$ is a
per-set."

## Status in the paper

The paper says all the results of its Section 3 were motivated by attempts to
prove this conjecture (p. 457). It notes that, for $r\ge3$, Theorem 10
(p. 459) passes the per-set property from
$\langle m_1x_1+\cdots+m_{r-1}x_{r-1}:1\rangle$, when
$(m_1,\ldots,m_{r-1})=1$, to $\langle m_1x_1+\cdots+m_rx_r:1\rangle$, and that
most of its effort went into the case $\langle mx+ny:1\rangle$ with
$(m,n)=1$, where it proves
[[integer_sequences/klarner_1974_arithmetic_properties_certain_recursively_defined_sets/theorem_11|Theorem 11]]
for $m=2$ and odd $n$. It adds, in parentheses on p. 457, that Conjecture 1
has been proved, citing the authors' paper "Sets generated by iteration of a
linear operation", then to appear in Pacific J. Math. (its reference [2]).
That proof is not in this paper.
