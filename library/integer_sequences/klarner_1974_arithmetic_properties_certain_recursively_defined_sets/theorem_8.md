## Statement

Setting (pp. 447, 451--452). $P=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}$, $N=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$,
$[a,b]$ is the set of integers $x$ with $a\le x\le b$, and
$\langle m_ix+n_i\,(i\in I):A\rangle$ is the least subset of $P$ containing
$A$ and closed under each map $x\mapsto m_ix+n_i$. In Section 2 the set of
unary operations is finite except possibly in Theorem 8, so the index set $I$
may be infinite here. A set is a *per-set* when it is a finite union of
infinite arithmetic progressions, and a *near per-set* when it differs from a
per-set by a finite set (pp. 448--450).

**Theorem 8** (p. 456). Let $A\subseteq P$ be nonempty and $I\subseteq P$, and
let $m_i\in P$ and $n_i\in N$ for $i\in I$. Let $\sigma$ be a positive real
number with $\sum_{i\in I}m_i^{-\sigma}<1$, and put
$S=\langle m_ix+n_i\,(i\in I):A\rangle$. Then for every $t\in N$

$$
\bigl|[1,t]\cap S\bigr|\le\Bigl(1-\sum_{i\in I}m_i^{-\sigma}\Bigr)^{-1}
\sum_{a\in[1,t]\cap A}\Bigl(\frac ta\Bigr)^{\sigma}.
$$

**Corollary of Theorem 8** (p. 456). If moreover $\sigma<1$, and either $A$
is finite or $A$ is infinite with $\sum_{a\in A}a^{-\sigma}$ convergent, then
$S$ has density zero and is neither a per-set nor a near per-set. The paper
says this applies to $\langle 2x+1,3x+1:A\rangle$ whenever
$\sum_{a\in A}a^{-\tau}<\infty$ for some $\tau<1$.

The paper introduces the result (pp. 455--456) as one whose essentials
P. Erdős communicated to the authors, and names $\langle 2x+1,3x+1:1\rangle$
as an instance. No separate proof of the corollary is printed.

## Proof pointer

Pp. 456--457. With $\sum m_i^{-\sigma}=1-\delta$, let $L(t)$ be the set of
finite words $\lambda$ over $I$ whose multiplier product
$m_{\lambda(1)}\cdots m_{\lambda(r)}$ is at most $t$; induction on $t$, by the
first letter, gives $|L(t)|\le t^\sigma/\delta$. Each element $y\le t$ of the
set generated from a single $a\in A$ is reached by a word whose multiplier
product times $a$ is at most $y$, which gives an injection into $L([t/a])$;
summing over $a\in A$ gives the bound.
