## 3. Independent restatement of the mathematics and its use

Write

$$
Z(q)=\sum_{n\ge1}q^n\left(\frac{q-1}{q^n-1}\right)^2,
\qquad \lvert q\rvert>1,
$$

with complex $q$ in the definition. The unnumbered theorem says that for
every integer $q$ with $\lvert q\rvert\ge2$, $Z(q)\notin\mathbb Q$. Its assertion
includes negative bases and has no irrationality assertion for arbitrary
noninteger complex bases. The same source gives, on this definition domain,

$$
Z(q)=(q-1)^2 S(q),\qquad
S(q)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{n}{q^n-1}
    =\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\sigma(n)}{q^n},
\qquad \sigma(n)=\sum_{d\mid n,\ d>0}d.
$$

For the theorem's integer bases the multiplier is nonzero rational; thus the
irrationality of either $Z(q)$ or $S(q)$ is equivalent to that of the other.
At $q=2$, the multiplier is exactly 1. The frozen E0250 question is precisely
whether $S(2)$ is irrational. It is therefore an exact specialization of this
published theorem, assuming that theorem as the source result being cited.

Define $f(x)=\prod_{n\ge1}(1-x^n)$ on the complex disk $\lvert x\rvert<1$, and put

$$
F_q=f(1/q),\qquad G_q=q^{-1}f'(1/q).
$$

The unnumbered lemma asserts that $1,F_q,G_q$ are linearly independent over
$\mathbb Q$ for every integer $q$ with $\lvert q\rvert\ge2$. Equivalently, every
pair of fixed integers $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$ gives
$\alpha_q=aF_q+bG_q\notin\mathbb Q$. This equivalence includes cases with
one coefficient zero. It does not assert independence over $\mathbb R$ or
$\mathbb C$, or an irrationality statement for arbitrary complex $q$.

The sparse-series part is internal to the lemma's proof. For $k\ge1$, set

$$
P_k^-=\frac{k(3k-1)}2,\qquad P_k^+=\frac{k(3k+1)}2.
$$

The printed pentagonal expansion and its derivative give the coefficients of
$\alpha_q=\sum_{n\ge0}a(n)q^{-n}$ as

$$
a(0)=a,\qquad a(P_k^\pm)=(-1)^k(a+bP_k^\pm),
$$

with all other positive-index coefficients zero. The support may be smaller
because some displayed coefficients can vanish. With $n_k=P_k^+$, there
are $k$ stated zero coefficients immediately following $n_k$ and $k-1$
immediately preceding it. For each fixed $a,b$, the bound
$\lvert a(n)\rvert\le n^2$ holds eventually.

Under the contradictory rationality assumption
$\alpha_q=\eta/\delta$, where $\eta,\delta\in\mathbb Z$ and
$\delta\ne0$, the note invokes theorem 2 of its reference [3] to assert
eventually in $k$

$$
\eta q^{n_k}-\delta\sum_{n=0}^{n_k}a(n)q^{n_k-n}=0. \tag{12}
$$

The subsequent outline deduces $q^k\mid\delta a(n_k)$, incompatible with
the eventual nonzero polynomial expression
$\delta(-1)^k(a+bP_k^+)$. Equation (12) is the specific printed use of an
unread external theorem, not a general sparse-series theorem extracted from
the 1995 note.

Finally, the displayed logarithmic derivative gives
$G_q/F_q=-S(q)$. A rational value of $Z(q)$ at an allowed integer base
would make $S(q)$ rational and yield the forbidden relation
$S(q)F_q+G_q=0$. This is the lemma-to-theorem-to-E0250 chain actually used
by the frozen pages.

## 5. Consequential re-derivations and strongest attacks

### A. Domain, divisor convention, scalar, and base attacks

For $\lvert q\rvert>1$, let $r=\lvert q\rvert^{-1}<1$. None of $q^n-1$ is zero. The elementary
identity $z/(1-z)^2=\sum_{j\ge1}jz^j$, applied to $z=q^{-n}$, gives

$$
\frac{Z(q)}{(q-1)^2}
=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{q^{-n}}{(1-q^{-n})^2}
=\sum_{n,j\ge1}j q^{-nj}.
$$

Rearrangement is justified for each such $q$ because

$$
\sum_{n,j\ge1}j r^{nj}
=\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{j r^j}{1-r^j}
\le\frac1{1-r}\sum_{j\ge1}j r^j<\infty.
$$

Summing over $n$ yields $\sum_{j\ge1}j/(q^j-1)$. Collecting the
coefficient of $q^{-m}$ instead gives $\sum_{j\mid m,j>0}j=\sigma(m)$.
This establishes precisely the consumed identities, including the positive
divisor convention, without reading Hardy–Wright. It also rules out an
accidental switch from base $q$ to reciprocal base in the extraction.

At a negative integer base the same absolutely convergent identities hold;
the powers of $q$ retain their signs. No positivity assumption was used.
For example, the factor at $q=-2$ is $(-2-1)^2=9$, not a factor obtained
by replacing $q$ with $\lvert q\rvert$. The frozen formulas keep $q$ throughout.
Since $(q-1)^2\in\mathbb Q\setminus\{0\}$ at every permitted integer
base, multiplying by it preserves rationality and irrationality. This argument
does not extend the theorem to arbitrary complex bases.

The boundary values are outside the definition used: $q=0$ fails
$\lvert q\rvert>1$ and the inverse-base operations, $q=1$ gives zero denominators in the
written formula, and $q=-1$ gives zero denominators at even $n$. No limiting
value is substituted for them. The source's (2) was read as context; no assertion
about all complex paths $q\to1$ was audited or adopted.

At $q=2$, no arithmetic factor remains. The notation distinction is also
substantive: the first term of $Z(2)$ is 2 and the remaining terms are positive,
so $Z(2)>2$. Meanwhile $\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-2}<2$, since for $n\ge2$,
$n^{-2}<[n(n-1)]^{-1}$, whose sum is 1. Thus the extraction's $Z(2)$,
written there as $\zeta(2;2)$, cannot be confused with the classical
$\zeta(2)$. No numerical approximation or outside theorem is needed.

**Attack outcome:** refutation-failed for domains, conventions, signs, the
scalar reformulation and the exact E0250 specialization.

### B. Linear-independence equivalence and final composition

For the forward equivalence, if $aF_q+bG_q=c\in\mathbb Q$ with integers
$(a,b)\ne(0,0)$, then $-c+aF_q+bG_q=0$ is a nontrivial rational relation
on $1,F_q,G_q$. Conversely, any nontrivial rational relation has
$(r_1,r_2)\ne(0,0)$, since $r_1=r_2=0$ would force $r_0=0$.
Clearing the denominators of $r_1,r_2$ gives integers $a,b$, not both zero,
such that $aF_q+bG_q=-Dr_0\in\mathbb Q$. This proves the stated
equivalence, including zero values of one coefficient and rational relations
whose constant coefficient vanishes.

The definitions are meaningful on the indicated disk: the tail of
$\prod(1-x^n)$ has absolutely summable logarithms on compact subdisks,
and none of the individual factors vanishes. It defines a nonvanishing analytic
function there. Logarithmic differentiation is legitimate locally: for
$\lvert x\rvert\le\rho$ with $0<\rho<1$, the derivative series is dominated by a constant times
$\sum n\rho^{n-1}$. Thus

$$
\frac{x f'(x)}{f(x)}=-\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{n x^n}{1-x^n},
\qquad
\frac{G_q}{F_q}=-\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{n}{q^n-1}=-S(q).
$$

The lemma itself also implies $F_q\ne0$, so its use in the denominator
cannot evade the linear-independence hypothesis. If $Z(q)$ were rational,
then $S(q)$ would be rational, and
$0\cdot1+S(q)F_q+1\cdot G_q=0$ would violate that lemma. The coefficient
of $G_q$ is 1 even when $S(q)=0$, so the alleged contradiction is
nontrivial. This verifies the consumed implication from the lemma and identities;
it does not independently establish the lemma's unread external premise.

**Attack outcome:** refutation-failed for the claimed equivalence and the
logarithmic-derivative application, including their zero and sign cases.

### C. Sparse ranges, cancellations, parameter dependence and divisibility

This was the strongest attack on the extracted outline. An off-by-one error
in the preceding gap would falsely erase an actual coefficient, and overlooking
coefficient cancellation could turn an eventual statement into a false uniform
one. Both possibilities were checked directly.

The exact gaps are

$$
P_k^+-P_k^-=k,\qquad P_{k+1}^- -P_k^+=2k+1.
$$

Hence $P_k^-<P_k^+<P_{k+1}^-$, the branches never collide, and the immediately preceding gap
consists exactly of the non-pentagonal indices $n_k-j$ for $1\le j\le k-1$.
The following gap actually contains $2k$ zero positions; the source's stated
first $k$ zeros are therefore correct. At $k=1$, $n_1=2$ and the
preceding range is empty. Choosing $a=1,b=0$ gives $a(P_k^-)=(-1)^k\ne0$:
this would refute a preceding range extended to $j=k$. The frozen extraction
stops at $k-1$, exactly as the source does.

For any fixed $a,b$ and positive $n$, the extracted coefficient formula
gives $\lvert a(n)\rvert\le \lvert a\rvert+\lvert b\rvert n$. In particular
$\lvert a(n)\rvert\le n^2$ whenever $n\ge\max(1,\lvert a\rvert+\lvert b\rvert)$. This explicit sufficient
threshold depends on the coefficients. An all-$n$ bound would fail already
for $a=100,b=0,n=1$, where $\lvert a(1)\rvert=100$; no such bound is claimed. The same
linear estimate proves absolute convergence of $\sum a(n)q^{-n}$ for
every fixed $a,b$ and $\lvert q\rvert>1$.

Coefficient cancellation is real. Given any $K\ge1$, take
$a=-P_K^+, b=1$; then $a(n_K)=0$. Thus no parameter-independent assertion
that all sufficiently indexed $a(n_k)$ are nonzero would follow. The frozen
wording correctly makes the parameters fixed and allows vanishing coefficients.
For a fixed pair, if $b=0$ then $a\ne0$ and $a+bP_k^+=a\ne0$.
If $b\ne0$, the strictly increasing $P_k^+$ permits at most one zero of
$a+bP_k^+$. Consequently the expression is eventually nonzero for that pair.

Now assume exactly the printed eventual equality (12), leaving its external
justification unverified. Since $n_k\ge k$, the term $\eta q^{n_k}$ is
divisible by $q^k$. Every summand with $n\le n_k-k$ has exponent at least
$k$, and the $k-1$ remaining indices before $n_k$ have zero coefficients.
Reduction of (12) modulo $q^k$ therefore gives
$q^k\mid\delta a(n_k)$. This works for negative integer $q$ as well:
a nonzero multiple has absolute value at least $\lvert q\rvert^k$. But

$$
0<\lvert \delta a(n_k)\rvert
\le \lvert \delta\rvert\left(\lvert a\rvert+\lvert b\rvert\frac{3k^2+k}{2}\right)<\lvert q\rvert^k
$$

for all sufficiently large $k$, a contradiction. All parameters here are
fixed before taking $k$ sufficiently large; no uniform threshold in $a,b$,
$q$, or the rational denominator is asserted. The explicit nonzero denominator
is essential and is present in the extraction.

The signs and derivative coefficients can also be checked without a new
external input: differentiating each printed term $x^{P_k^\pm}$ and
multiplying by $x$ produces $P_k^\pm x^{P_k^\pm}$, giving exactly
$(-1)^k(a+bP_k^\pm)$ in (9). The sparse expansions converge locally inside
the disk, as do their derivative series. No constant derivative term is added.

**Attack outcome:** refutation-failed for the sparse transcription, range
boundaries, fixed-parameter qualifications, and deductions conditional on (12).
The examples above are witnesses against tempting stronger formulations,
not defects in the frozen formulations. The full hypotheses and applicability
of the external theorem producing (12) remain unchecked.
