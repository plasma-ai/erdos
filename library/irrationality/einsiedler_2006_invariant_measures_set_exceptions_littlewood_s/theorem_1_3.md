## Statement

Setting (pp. 514, 519--520). $A$ is the group of positive diagonal
$k\times k$ matrices, acting by left multiplication on
$X=\operatorname{SL}(k,\mathbb R)/\operatorname{SL}(k,\mathbb Z)$, the space
of unimodular lattices in $\mathbb R^k$.

**Conjecture 1.1** (Margulis, p. 514), which defines *algebraic*. Quoted from
p. 514: "Let $\mu$ be an $A$-invariant and ergodic probability measure on
$X=\operatorname{SL}(k,\mathbb R)/\operatorname{SL}(k,\mathbb Z)$ for
$k\ge3$. Then $\mu$ is algebraic; i.e. there is a closed, connected group
$L>A$ so that $\mu$ is the $L$-invariant measure on a single, closed
$L$-orbit." The paper calls it a special case of more general conjectures
of Margulis and of Katok and Spatzier (p. 514).

**Theorem 1.3** (p. 515). Quoted: "Let $\mu$ be an $A$-invariant and
ergodic measure on
$X=\operatorname{SL}(k,\mathbb R)/\operatorname{SL}(k,\mathbb Z)$ for
$k\ge3$. Assume that there is some one-parameter subgroup of $A$ which acts
on $X$ with positive entropy. Then $\mu$ is algebraic."

The paper calls this a partial result towards Conjecture 1.1 (p. 515). The
statement says "measure"; Section 6 (p. 542) carries out the proof for an
$A$-ergodic and invariant probability measure. The paper explains
that its method needs the positive-entropy hypothesis: without it the
conditional measures on the unipotent foliations are trivial and carry no
information (pp. 518, 524).

**Read depth.** Claims checked: Conjecture 1.1 and the statement were read
clause by clause on pp. 514--515. The derivation in Section 6 (pp. 542--545)
was read through, not checked step by step; Sections 3--5 were not checked.

## Proof pointer

The theorem is the case $\Gamma=\operatorname{SL}(k,\mathbb Z)$ of
[[irrationality/einsiedler_2006_invariant_measures_set_exceptions_littlewood_s/theorem_2_1|Theorem 2.1]], which for each pair of indices $a,b$ allows
trivial conditional measures on $U_{ab}$ and $U_{ba}$, invariance under the
copy $H_{ab}$ of $\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb R)$, or "exceptional returns".
Section 5 (Theorem 5.1 and Proposition 5.2, p. 540) rules out exceptional
returns for $\operatorname{SL}(k,\mathbb Z)$. In Section 6 (pp. 542--545) the
indices $a,b$ with $\mu$ invariant under $U_{ab}$ form an equivalence relation,
giving a product $H$ of groups $\operatorname{SL}(k_i,\mathbb R)$ that preserves
$\mu$. The Margulis--Tomanov measure classification (Theorem 6.1, p. 543),
applied with the group generated by $A$ and $H$, and Lemma 6.2 (p. 544) with
Poincaré recurrence, which forces $\sum k_i=k$, give that $\mu$ is the invariant
measure on a single closed orbit of the group generated by $A$ and $H$.
