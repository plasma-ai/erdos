**Source.** P. Erdős, *Sur l'irrationalité d'une certaine série*, C. R. Acad.
Sci. Paris Sér. I Math. 292 (1981), no. 17, 765--768. The unnumbered
paragraph following the Théorème on p. 765. Bibliographic details are on the
[[irrationality/erdos_1981_sur_l_irrationalite_d_une_certaine/_index|source card]].

## Statement

For an increasing sequence of integers $a_1<a_2<\cdots$, Erdős writes that it
seems very likely that

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n}{n}=+\infty
$$

is a sufficient condition for

$$
\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{a_n}{2^{a_n}}
$$

to be irrational (p. 765).

In the same paragraph he adds that he could not find a sequence with
$\limsup(a_{n+1}-a_n)=+\infty$ and a rational sum, but he thinks such an
example exists and could be easy to find (p. 765).

After the proof (p. 767) he adds that the hypothesis
$a_{n+1}-a_n\to\infty$ of the
[[irrationality/erdos_1981_sur_l_irrationalite_d_une_certaine/theorem_p765|Théorème]]
can be weakened a little, but that for the moment he cannot replace it by a
substantially better condition.

No proof is offered; this is a conjecture.
