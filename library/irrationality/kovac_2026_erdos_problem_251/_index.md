**Claim.** The note's abstract (p. 1) says: "We disprove the assertion that, if
$p_n$ is the $n$-th prime and $(g_n)_{n\ge1}$ is a sequence of integers
with $g_n\ge2$ and $g_n=o(p_n)$, then $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_n/(g_1\cdots g_n)$
must be irrational. In fact, we construct such a sequence for which the
sum is exactly 1." This is the auxiliary statement that the catalog page
of [[../wiki/problems/irrationality/E0251/_index|Problem 251]] attributes to
Erdős's 1988 survey, whose p. 103 reads: "It seems reasonable to expect
that if $g_n\ge2$, $g_n/p_n\to0$ then $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_n/g_1\ldots g_n$
(2) is irrational"; the survey is filed as
[[irrationality/erdos_1988_irrationality_certain_series_problems_results/_index|erdos_1988_irrationality_certain_series_problems_results]].
The problem itself, the constant sequence $g_n=2$, is not touched: the
construction produces one particular sequence with sum $1$.

**Monotonicity.** The author's comment adds: "It is possible that Erdős
also wanted $(g_n)$ to be increasing, but then it would be weird to
emphasize $g_n\geq2$ and switch the notation from $a_n$ to $g_n$ for this
particular problem in [Er88c]." The construction neither assumes nor
produces a monotone sequence, so the theorems on monotone denominators,
Section 3 of Erdős's 1958 paper
([[irrationality/erdos_1958_sur_certaines_series_valeur_irrationnelle_french/_index|card]];
$1<q_1\le q_2\le\cdots$ with a growth hypothesis, rational only when
$q_n=qp_n+1$ eventually) and Theorem 5.1 of Hančl–Tijdeman 2004, as
numbered in the authors' preprint
([[irrationality/hancl_2004_irrationality_cantor_series/theorem_5_1|card]];
monotonic $a_n$ with $p_n=o(a_n^2)$, rational only when $p_n/(a_n-1)$ is
eventually constant), are untouched; whether Erdős meant nondecreasing
$g_n$ in (2) is not settled by the sources.

**Construction.** With $m_n=1+\lfloor\log\log(n+3)\rfloor$, choose $N$ so
that $1+\sum_{k=N}^{n-1}m_k<p_n$ for $n\ge N$, put $c_1=\cdots=c_N=1$,
choose $c_{n+1}$ as the unique element of $\{m_n,\dots,m_n+c_n-1\}$ with
$c_{n+1}\equiv-p_n\pmod{c_n}$, and set $g_n=(p_n+c_{n+1})/c_n$. Then $g_n$
is an integer, $g_n\ge2$, $g_n=o(p_n)$, and
$p_n/(g_1\cdots g_n)=c_n/G_{n-1}-c_{n+1}/G_n$ with $G_n=g_1\cdots g_n$, so
the partial sums telescope to $1-c_{M+1}/G_M\to1$. The only input about
primes is the prime number theorem, used through $p_n\sim n\log n$;
$p_n/(n\log\log n)\to\infty$ would suffice.
