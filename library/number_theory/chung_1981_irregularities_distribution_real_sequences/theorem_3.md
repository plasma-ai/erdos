## Statement

Let $S_m$ be the set of permutations of $\{1,2,\ldots,m\}$ and $F_n$ the
Fibonacci numbers ($F_0=0$, $F_1=1$, $F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_n$). The
announcement defines

$$
u_m=\min_{\pi\in S_m}\max_I\sum_k|\pi(i_{k+1})-\pi(i_k)|^{-1},
$$

where $I$ ranges over all increasing subsequences
$\{i_1<i_2<\cdots<i_r\}\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,m\}$ and the sum runs over the
consecutive pairs of $I$ (the print writes $\sum_k$ without a range; the
1984 chapter prints $\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}$).

**Theorem 3** (p. 4001).

$$
u_m=\begin{cases}
1+\sum_{k=1}^{t}F_{2k}^{-1} & \text{if } F_{2t+3}\le m<F_{2t+4},\\[4pt]
1+\sum_{k=1}^{t}F_{2k}^{-1}+F_{2t+3}^{-1} & \text{if } F_{2t+4}\le m<F_{2t+5}.
\end{cases} \tag{4}
$$

The print does not state the range of $t$.

The page adds, without proof, that permutations attaining the minimum can
be generated by ordering the first $m$ terms of the sequence $\bar x^*$ of
[[number_theory/chung_1981_irregularities_distribution_real_sequences/theorem_2|Theorem 2]],
and that these are the permutations given by the sequence $\{k\tau\}$,
$k=0,1,2,\ldots$, with $\tau=(1+\sqrt5)/2$ and $\{x\}$ the fractional part
of $x$. (The print speaks of "equality in 3"; the 1984 chapter's
corresponding sentence speaks of permutations achieving (4).) It calls
Theorems 1 and 2 "intimately tied" to this result. As $t\to\infty$ both values in (4) tend to $1/\alpha$, with
$\alpha$ the constant of
[[number_theory/chung_1981_irregularities_distribution_real_sequences/theorem_1|Theorem 1]].

**Source.** F. R. K. Chung and R. L. Graham, *On irregularities of
distribution of real sequences*, Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA 78 (1981),
no. 7, 4001; the definition of $u_m$ and Theorem 3 are on the one printed
page. The edition is identified in the
[[number_theory/chung_1981_irregularities_distribution_real_sequences/_index|source digest]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the definition of $u_m$ and the two cases
of [4] were read clause by clause on the page image. The page gives no
proof.

## Proof pointer

None on the page. The proof is Theorem 3 of the 1984 chapter (p. 183
there): an upper bound from the permutation that orders $\{k\tau\}$,
$1\le k\le m$ (pp. 188--203), and a lower bound by induction
(pp. 203--210); see the
[[number_theory/chung_1984_irregularities_distribution/_index|chapter's digest]].
