## Statement

As printed on p. 237, with $\mathbb N^*$ the positive integers:

**Corollary 2.** "Let $v$ be a real number. The set of real numbers $x$
belonging to any interval $[a,b]$ such that the sequence
$(vx^n)_{n\in\mathbb N^*}$ is not everywhere dense mod 1, has Hausdorff
dimension 1."

In the corpus's words: $v$ is an arbitrary real number, with no condition
that it be non-zero, and $[a,b]$ is an arbitrary interval of the real line;
the conclusion is that the exceptional multipliers $x\in[a,b]$, those for
which the fractional parts of $vx^n$ ($n\ge1$) are not dense in $[0,1)$, form
a set of Hausdorff dimension 1. The introduction (p. 237) sets it beside the
earlier result it cites from Thomas [2], that for every real $v$ and any
interval $[a,b]$ the $x\in[a,b]$ with $(vx^n)$ not equidistributed mod 1
form a set of Hausdorff dimension 1, and presents the sequence
$(x^n)_{n\in\mathbb N^*}$ as an instance the more precise result covers.

**Source.** B. de Mathan, Numbers contravening a condition in density
modulo 1, Acta Math. Acad. Sci. Hungar. 36 (1980), 237--241; Corollary 2
and the introduction on printed p. 237 (PDF p. 1 of the publisher's scan),
read on the page image (the OCR text layer garbles the formulas). The
