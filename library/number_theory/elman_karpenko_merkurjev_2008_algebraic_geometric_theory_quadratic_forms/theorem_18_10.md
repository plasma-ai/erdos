## Statement

$D(\varphi)$ is the set of nonzero values of $\varphi$ and
$\langle D(\varphi)\rangle$ the subgroup of $F^\times$ it generates.

**Theorem 18.10** (Value Norm Principle, p. 78, quoted). "Let $\varphi$ be a
quadratic form over $F$ and $K/F$ a finite field extension. Then
$\mathrm N_{K/F}\bigl(D(\varphi_K)\bigr)\subset\langle D(\varphi)\rangle$."

No hypothesis is placed on $\varphi$ or on the characteristic. The book
attributes the theorem to Knebusch (p. 78).

**Theorem 18.11** (Bilinear Value Norm Principle, p. 78): the same inclusion
$\mathrm N_{K/F}\bigl(D(\mathfrak b_K)\bigr)\subset\langle D(\mathfrak b)\rangle$
for a symmetric bilinear form $\mathfrak b$ over $F$.

## Proof pointer

P. 78. Reduce to $\varphi$ anisotropic with trivial radical,
$\dim\varphi\ge2$ and $1\in D(\varphi)$. If $\varphi_K$ is isotropic and
$x\in D(\varphi_K)$, first take $K=F(x)$: the
[[number_theory/elman_karpenko_merkurjev_2008_algebraic_geometric_theory_quadratic_forms/theorem_18_3|Quadratic Value Theorem 18.3]]
puts the minimal polynomial $p$ of $x$ in $\langle D(\varphi_{F(t)})\rangle$,
and the Substitution Principle gives $\mathrm N_{K/F}(x)=p(0)$ in
$\langle D(\varphi)\rangle$. In general, $\mathrm N_{K/F}(x)$ is a square when
$[K:F(x)]$ is even, and when it is odd Springer's Theorem makes
$\varphi_{F(x)}$ isotropic, so the first step applies to $F(x)/F$. If
$\varphi_K$ is anisotropic, the even Clifford algebra of a binary subform
representing $x$ and $1$ is a quadratic extension $L$ of $K$ over which
$\varphi$ is isotropic, with $x$ a norm from $L$, which reduces to the first
case.
