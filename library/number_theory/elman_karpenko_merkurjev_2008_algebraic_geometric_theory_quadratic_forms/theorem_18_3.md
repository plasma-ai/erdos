## Statement

Setting (p. 75). $T=(t_1,\ldots,t_n)$, and $\mathbb Z^n$ is ordered
lexicographically. A nonzero $f\in F[T]$ has a leading term $aT^{\boldsymbol
i}$, the monomial of largest degree $\boldsymbol i\in\mathbb Z^n$ in that
order; $\deg f=\boldsymbol i$ and the leading coefficient is $f^*=a$. For an
irreducible $p\in F[T]$, $F(p)$ is the quotient field of $F[T]/(p)$. For a
quadratic form $\varphi$ over $F$, $\langle D(\varphi)\rangle$ is the
subgroup of $F^\times$ generated by the nonzero values $D(\varphi)$ (p. 76).

**Lemma 18.1** (p. 75, quoted). "Let $\varphi$ be a quadratic form on $V$ over
$F$ and $g\in F[T]$. Suppose that $g\in D(\varphi_{F(T)})$. Then
$g^*\in D(\varphi)$. If, in addition, $\varphi$ is anisotropic, then
$\deg g\in2\mathbb Z^n$."

**Theorem 18.3** (Quadratic Value Theorem, p. 76, quoted). "Let $\varphi$ be an
arbitrary anisotropic quadratic form on $V$ and $f\in F[T]$ a nonzero
polynomial. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1) $f^*f\in\langle D(\varphi_{F(T)})\rangle$.

(2) There exists an $a\in F^\times$ such that
$af\in\langle D(\varphi_{F(T)})\rangle$.

(3) $\varphi_{F(p)}$ is isotropic for each irreducible divisor $p$ occurring
to an odd power in the factorization of $f$."

**Theorem 18.4** (Bilinear Value Theorem, p. 77): the same equivalence for an
anisotropic symmetric bilinear form $\mathfrak b$ on $V$, with
$D(\mathfrak b_{F(T)})$ and $\mathfrak b_{F(p)}$ in place of
$D(\varphi_{F(T)})$ and $\varphi_{F(p)}$.

The book bases the section on ideas of Knebusch (p. 75).

## Proof pointer

Lemma 18.1 (pp. 75--76): clear denominators to get a polynomial representing
vector $v$; the leading term of $\varphi(v)$ is $\varphi(v^*)T_v^2$, nonzero
when $\varphi$ is anisotropic. Theorem 18.3 (pp. 76--77): (1) to (2) is
trivial; (2) to (3) reduces a product of values modulo $p$ after dividing out
the largest power of $p$; (3) to (1) goes by induction on $n$ and on
$\deg f$, using the division relation (18.2) to write $\varphi(r)=fg$ with
$\deg g<\deg f$ and Lemma 18.1 for $(fg)^*$. Theorem 18.4 follows from 18.3
with $\varphi=\varphi_{\mathfrak b}$.
