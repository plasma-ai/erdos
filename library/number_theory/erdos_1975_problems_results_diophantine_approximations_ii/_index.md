# number_theory/erdos_1975_problems_results_diophantine_approximations_ii

[[number_theory/_index|..]]

[[number_theory/erdos_1975_problems_results_diophantine_approximations_ii/question_p96_generalized_progression|question_p96_generalized_progression]]: Erdős's 1975 definition of a generalized arithmetic progression as the
sequence of integer parts of t alpha + beta with real alpha > 1 and beta,
and his question whether the complement of every sufficiently fast-growing
sequence contains an infinite one.

[[number_theory/erdos_1975_problems_results_diophantine_approximations_ii/question_p96_lacunary_density|question_p96_lacunary_density]]: Erdős's 1975 question whether every integer sequence with ratios
n_{k+1}/n_k > c > 1 admits an irrational alpha for which the fractional
parts of n_k alpha are not everywhere dense, with the Erdős--Taylor
dimension-one result he reports beside it; the printed origin of Problem
464.

## Contents

- Opening (p. 89, page image). Erdős says that the chapter first reports
  the progress made on the problems of his earlier paper, which he refers
  to as (I), and then states a few new questions at the end; he adds that
  he has not worked on the subject recently and asks forgiveness for any
  omitted reference. Six numbered sections follow, each closing with its
  own references, and the new questions form an unnumbered tail.
- § 1 (p. 89, page image). For a sequence $x_1,x_2,\ldots$ in $(0,1)$ and
  the exponential sums $\sum_{j\le n}e^{2\pi ikx_j}$, Erdős's conjecture
  that the limiting moduli $A_k$ satisfy $\limsup_k A_k=\infty$, which he
  proved, and Clunie's stronger result that $|A_k|>c\sqrt k$ for infinitely
  many $k$; whether $|A_k|>ck$ holds for infinitely many $k$ is asked, with
  Clunie's example of a sequence with $|A_k|\le k$ for all $k$.
- § 2 (pp. 89--91, page images). The discrepancy $D(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ of
  van der Corput; Schmidt's $\max_{m\le n}D(x_1,\ldots,x_m)>10^{-2}\log n$,
  sharpening van Aardenne-Ehrenfest and Roth; Erdős's question (1) whether
  some sequence has $\limsup_n|N_n(a,b)-(b-a)n|<\infty$ for every interval
  (the display prints $\limsup_nN_n(a,b)<\infty$, without the term
  $(b-a)n$), answered in the negative by Schmidt, with Schmidt's bounds (2)
  and (3) and the gap between them; the Hecke--Ostrowski boundedness for
  $x_n=\{n\beta\}$, $\beta$ irrational, when the interval length is
  $\{m\beta\}$, and the converse conjectured by Erdős and Szüsz and proved
  by Kesten; the discrepancy questions of (I) in higher dimensions resolved
  by Schmidt, and Philipp's results on $\{n_k\alpha\}$ for
  $n_{k+1}/n_k>c>1$. References to van Aardenne-Ehrenfest 1949, Roth 1955,
  Schmidt's "On irregularities of distribution I to IX", Kesten 1968,
  Kesten and Sós 1968 and Philipp.
- § 3 (pp. 91--92, page images). The notation $(k\alpha)=k\alpha-[k\alpha]$
  for the fractional part is set here. Khintchine's conjecture that
  $f_n(E,\alpha)/n\to m(E)$ for almost all $\alpha$ and every measurable
  $E$, disproved by Marstrand (1970) with a closed set $E$ for which the
  upper and lower limits are $1$ and $0$ for almost all $\alpha$;
  well-distributed sequences after Hlawka and Petersen; Erdős's claim in
  (I) that $(p_n\alpha)$, $p_n$ the $n$th prime, is not well distributed
  for some irrational $\alpha$, whose proof he "was not able to
  reconstruct" (p. 92), and his belief that it fails for every $\alpha$.
- § 4 (pp. 92--94, page images). Erdős's necessary and sufficient condition
  for almost all $\alpha$ to have infinitely many solutions of
  $|\alpha-a/n_k|<\varepsilon/n_k^2$ with $(a,n_k)=1$ along a sequence
  $n_1<n_2<\cdots$; the "old conjecture" (2),
  $\sum_{q=1}^\infty\varepsilon_q\varphi(q)/q=\infty$, which contains that
  theorem and which Erdős hopes his method will prove (the scan states
  neither the theorem's condition nor the conjecture in full: p. 92 ends at
  "is that" and p. 93 opens with display (2)); Cassels's property of
  sequences, Haight's construction (in his thesis) of a sequence for which
  the averaged ratios tend to $0$, and Erdős's results from (I) on
  $\varphi(n_1,\ldots,n_{k-1};n_k)/n_k$. References to Erdős, J. Number
  Theory 2 (1970), and Philipp's Memoir 114.
- § 5 (pp. 94--95, page images). LeVeque's uniform distribution modulo a
  sequence $A$ with $a_{n+1}/a_n\to1$: whether $n\alpha$ is uniformly
  distributed modulo $A$ for almost all $\alpha$, proved by Davenport and
  Erdős for $a_n>n^{1/2+\epsilon}$ and disproved in general by Schmidt
  (1969); Schmidt's question of a set of infinite measure in which no
  quotient of two elements is an integer, answered by Haight and by
  Szemerédi, with Erdős's question how fast its measure up to $x$ can grow;
  a question of Haight on the integer multiples of a set of positive
  measure, and Haight's bound $m(S)<9$ for a set $S$ of positive numbers
  containing at most one multiple of each $x>1$, which Erdős asks whether
  to improve to $m(S)\le3/2$, best possible if true. References to Schmidt
  1969, Erdős and Davenport 1963, Haight 1970, Szemerédi 1971 and
  Lekkerkerker 1958.
- § 6 (pp. 95--96, page images). Heilbronn's problem of estimating
  $f_3(n)$, with $f_k(n)$ the largest minimum area of a $k$-gon among $n$
  points in the unit circle: Roth's and Schmidt's upper bounds for
  $f_3(n)$, ending with Roth's $f_3(n)<c/n^{1+\alpha}$,
  $\alpha=(9-65^{1/2})/8$, and the question whether $f_3(n)<c/n^2$;
  Schmidt's $f_4(n)>c/n^{3/2}$ and conjecture $f_k(n)=o(1/n)$; an
  observation with Roth that for $n$ points in the unit sphere the
  smallest-angle method gives $f_3^{(3)}(n)<c/n^{1/2}$ and the
  smallest-diameter method $c/n^{2/3}$, and the question whether
  $f_3^{(3)}(n)=o(1/n^{2/3})$. References to Roth 1972 and Schmidt
  1971--1972.
- The disconnected problems (pp. 96--98, page images). The tail opens on
  p. 96 by announcing a few disconnected problems and poses the first in
  these words: "Let $n_1<n_2<\cdots$ be an infinite sequence of integers
  satisfying $n_{k+1}/n_k>c>1$. Is it true that there always is an
  irrational $\alpha$ for which the sequence $(n_k\alpha)$ is not
  everywhere dense?" Erdős adds that he and Taylor proved that the set of
  $\alpha$ for which $(n_k\alpha)$ is not uniformly distributed is a set
  of Hausdorff dimension $1$. The parentheses are the chapter's fractional
  part notation of § 3; the Erdős--Taylor result is the paper listed as
  reference [1] on p. 99, and the text prints no citation marker. The
  next paragraph, also p. 96, calls the sequence $[t\alpha+\beta]$, $t$
  an integer, a generalized arithmetic progression for real $\alpha>1$
  and $\beta$, and asks: "Let $\{n_k\}$ tend to infinity sufficiently
  fast. Is it true that the complement of $\{n_k\}$ contains an infinite
  generalized arithmetic progression?" Then (pp. 96--97) the density $f(x)$
  of the integers with $\varphi(n)/n<x$, shown to exist by Schoenberg and
  purely singular by Erdős (the p. 99 references [4] and [2]), and whether
  its derivative can exist and differ from $0$, or take any given value;
  (p. 97) for points $z_n$ on the unit circle, whether
  $A_n=\max_{|z|=1}\prod_{i\le n}|z-z_i|$ is unbounded, with Hayman's
  example showing $A_n\le n$ is possible, and the general version for a
  closed set $D$ without interior points comparing $A_n(D)$ with the
  Chebyshev quantity $E_n(D)$ over monic polynomials with roots in $D$;
  (pp. 97--98) for a primitive sequence $1<a_1<\cdots$ of integers,
  Erdős's $\sum_k1/(a_k\log a_k)<c$ (1) and Behrend's bound (2), sharpened
  with Sárközy and Szemerédi (the p. 99 reference [3]), and the question
  whether for real $1<a_1<\cdots$ with $|a_j-\ell a_i|\ge1$ for all
  integers $i,j,\ell$ (3), the bounds (1) and (2) still follow; Erdős could
  not prove that (3) gives $\liminf A(x)/x=0$, Haight proved
  $\lim A(x)/x=0$ under rational independence, and Besicovitch's result
  shows (3) does not force it for integers.
- References (p. 99, page image). Four items: [1] Erdős and Taylor, Proc.
  London Math. Soc. 7 (1957), 598--615; [2] Erdős, Amer. J. Math. 61
  (1939), 722--725; [3] Erdős, Sárközy and Szemerédi, Number Theory
  Colloquium, János Bolyai Math. Soc., North Holland (1968), 36--49; [4]
  Schoenberg, Math. Zeitschrift 28 (1928), 171--190. The author's address
  is the Mathematical Institute of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/number_theory/E0464/_index|#464]]: p. 96 poses the
question in Erdős's own words
([[number_theory/erdos_1975_problems_results_diophantine_approximations_ii/question_p96_lacunary_density|the lacunary density question]]):
for a sequence with $n_{k+1}/n_k>c>1$, whether some irrational $\alpha$
makes $(n_k\alpha)$ "not everywhere dense", with $(x)$ the fractional part
(§ 3, p. 91). It is the printed origin that the site's key Er75i names,
that Pollington's introduction quotes with the ratio condition written
$\ge\alpha>1$, and that Erdős's 1982 paper restates as "not everywhere
dense". The printed wording carries the irrationality clause and asks about
density of the fractional parts modulo $1$, not of the distances to the
nearest integer, as the problem page's corrected Statement does; the strict
ratio bound $>c>1$ and the site's $n_{k+1}\ge(1+\epsilon)n_k$ describe the
same class of sequences. The same page reports the Erdős--Taylor theorem
that the multipliers $\alpha$ for which $(n_k\alpha)$ is not uniformly
distributed form a set of Hausdorff dimension one, which does not answer
the question. The next paragraph poses
[[number_theory/erdos_1975_problems_results_diophantine_approximations_ii/question_p96_generalized_progression|the generalized arithmetic progression question]],
not a statement of Problem 464, beside which the problem page records the
results of Graham and Sós and of Pollington from Erdős and Graham's 1980
book (its printed p. 18). The chapter proves nothing about either question.
