## Statement

**Definition** (p. 96), as printed: "Let $\alpha>1$ and $\beta$ be real
numbers. We call the sequence $[t\alpha+\beta]$, $t$ integer a generalized
arithmetic progression." Here $[x]$ is the integer part, so the progression
is the set of integer parts of the points of an arithmetic progression of
real numbers with common difference $\alpha>1$; the restriction $\alpha>1$
makes the integer parts distinct.

**The question** (unnumbered, p. 96), in the paragraph that follows the
[[number_theory/erdos_1975_problems_results_diophantine_approximations_ii/question_p96_lacunary_density|lacunary density question]],
as printed: "Let $\{n_k\}$ tend to infinity sufficiently fast. Is it true
that the complement of $\{n_k\}$ contains an infinite generalized arithmetic
progression?"

The growth condition is left unquantified: the chapter does not say how fast
"sufficiently fast" is, and it states no lacunarity hypothesis here.

**Source.** P. Erdős, Problems and results on diophantine approximations
(II), in Répartition modulo 1 (Actes du Colloque de Marseille-Luminy 1974),
Lecture Notes in Mathematics 475, Springer, 1975, pp. 89--99; the definition
and the question on p. 96. The edition read is identified on the
[[number_theory/erdos_1975_problems_results_diophantine_approximations_ii/_index|source card]].
