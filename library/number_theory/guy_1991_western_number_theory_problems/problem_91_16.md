## Statement

**Problem 91:16** (p. 15), attributed "(Paul Erdős)", quoted as printed:
"Is it true that if
$\frac1{x_1}+\frac1{x_2}+\ldots+\frac1{x_n}=1$, $x_1<x_2<\ldots<x_n$ then
$\liminf\frac{x_n}{x_1}>e$ ? It is trivial that the limit is $\ge e$. In
fact perhaps it is infinite."

The print does not say over what the lim inf is taken; the natural reading
is $n\to\infty$, with $x_n/x_1$ for each $n$ the least ratio over the
representations of $1$ by $n$ distinct unit fractions, and the "limit" of
the second sentence is that lim inf.

**Source.** *Western Number Theory Problems, 1991-12-19 & 22*, edited by
Richard K. Guy (Department of Mathematics and Statistics, The University of
Calgary; dated 92-08-20), problem 91:16, printed p. 15. The edition is
identified in the
[[number_theory/guy_1991_western_number_theory_problems/_index|source digest]].
