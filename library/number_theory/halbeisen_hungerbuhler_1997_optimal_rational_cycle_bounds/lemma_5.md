## Setting

The paper's notation (pp. 1--3). On the rationals with odd denominator,
written $\mathbb Q[(2)]$, the Collatz sequence is $x_n=g_0(x_{n-1})=x_{n-1}/2$
when the numerator of $x_{n-1}$ is even and
$x_n=g_1(x_{n-1})=(3x_{n-1}+1)/2$ when it is odd (1). $S_{l,n}$ is the set of
0-1 sequences of length $l$ with exactly $n$ ones, $S_l$ the union over $n$,
and $S$ the union over $l$; $l(s)$ and $n(s)$ are the length and the number of
ones of $s$. The function $\varphi:S\to\mathbb N$ is defined by
$\varphi(\{\})=0$, $\varphi(s0)=\varphi(s)$ and
$\varphi(s1)=3\varphi(s)+2^{l(s)}$ (2), so that

$$
\varphi(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{l(s)}s_j\,3^{s_{j+1}+\cdots+s_{l(s)}}\,2^{j-1}
$$

(3). By Lemma 2 (p. 3, credited to Lagarias), each $s\in S$ determines one
cycle in $\mathbb Q[(2)]$ of length $l(s)$, the one through
$x_0=\varphi(s)/(2^{l(s)}-3^{n(s)})$ whose parity sequence is $s$. With
$\lambda_l$ the left shift $(s_1,\ldots,s_l)\mapsto(s_2,\ldots,s_l,s_1)$,
$\sigma(s)$ is the set of the rotations $\lambda_l^k(s)$, $k=1,\ldots,l$, and

$$
M_{l,n}:=\max_{s\in S_{l,n}}\Bigl\{\min_{t\in\sigma(s)}\varphi(t)\Bigr\}
$$

(p. 3). The rotations of $s$ are the parity sequences read from the other
elements of the same cycle, so for $2^l>3^n$ the quotient
$M_{l,n}/(2^l-3^n)$ is the largest minimum a cycle with parity data $(l,n)$
can have; the paper uses it this way in (5) (p. 3) and (11) (p. 10).

## Statement

**Lemma 5** (p. 5): "Let $n\le l$ be natural numbers. Let
$\tilde s_i:=\lceil i\frac nl\rceil-\lceil(i-1)\frac nl\rceil$ (for
$1\le i\le l$), then $\varphi(\tilde s)=\min_{t\in\sigma(\tilde s)}\{\varphi(t)\}=M_{l,n}$."

The paper writes $\tilde s(l,n)$ for this sequence. Combined with (3) it
gives **Corollary 1** (p. 6): for every $l$ and $n\le l$,

$$
M_{l,n}=\sum_{j=1}^{l}\Bigl(\Bigl\lceil j\frac nl\Bigr\rceil-\Bigl\lceil(j-1)\frac nl\Bigr\rceil\Bigr)2^{j-1}3^{\,n-\lceil j\frac nl\rceil}.
$$

So when $2^l>3^n$ the cycle generated by $\tilde s(l,n)$ is, among the
cycles with $l$ steps and $n$ odd steps, one whose minimum is as large as
possible. This is the
sense in which the paper calls the criterion of
[[number_theory/halbeisen_hungerbuhler_1997_optimal_rational_cycle_bounds/theorem_4|Theorem 4]]
optimal (p. 11).

**Source.** Lorenz Halbeisen and Norbert Hungerbühler, *Optimal bounds for
the length of rational Collatz cycles*, Acta Arith. 78 (1997), 227--239;
Lemma 5 on p. 5 and Corollary 1 on p. 6 of the authors' preprint named on
the
[[number_theory/halbeisen_hungerbuhler_1997_optimal_rational_cycle_bounds/_index|source card]],
numbered 1--13 rather than by the journal's pagination.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the statements and the definitions on
pp. 1--6 were read on the print, and the proof on p. 5 was read. Nothing
here is independently reviewed.

## Proof pointer

Page 5. Lemma 4 (p. 4) shows that among distinct sequences of $S_{l,n}$, one
whose partial sums are everywhere at most another's has the larger value of
$\varphi$. Any $t\in S_{l,n}$ has a rotation whose partial sums all lie on or
above the line $k\,n/l$, starting where $t$'s staircase falls furthest below
that line, and $\tilde s$ lies between that rotation and the line, so
$\varphi(\tilde s)$ is at least the least value of $\varphi$ on $\sigma(t)$.
Every rotation of $\tilde s$ has partial sums at most those of $\tilde s$,
so by Lemma 4 again $\tilde s$ is the minimizer within its own class.

## Dependencies

Lemma 4 (p. 4) and formula (3) (p. 3).
