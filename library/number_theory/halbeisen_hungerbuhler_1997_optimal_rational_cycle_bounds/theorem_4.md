## Setting

The notation of
[[number_theory/halbeisen_hungerbuhler_1997_optimal_rational_cycle_bounds/lemma_5|Lemma 5]]:
Collatz cycles in $\mathbb Q[(2)]$ under $g_0(x)=x/2$ and
$g_1(x)=(3x+1)/2$, and $n(l):=\lfloor l\log_32\rfloor$ (Proposition 1, p. 6).

## Statement

**Theorem 4** (p. 11). Let $L(m)$ be the smallest integer $L$ such that,
with $n=n(L)$,

$$
\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{L}\bigl(\lceil j\frac nL\rceil-\lceil(j-1)\frac nL\rceil\bigr)2^{j-1}3^{\,n-\lceil j\frac nL\rceil}}{2^L-3^n}\ge m .
$$

Then every positive Collatz cycle $C$ in $\mathbb Q[(2)]$ satisfies
$|C|\ge L(\min C)$ (12).

By Corollary 1 (p. 6) the numerator is $M_{L,n(L)}$. The paper calls the
criterion optimal (p. 11), by Lemma 5: for every cycle length, it says, the
cycle generated by the sequence $\tilde s$ of Lemma 5 gives equality in
(12). It also notes that the criterion is costly to
check, and its worked example is recorded on
[[number_theory/halbeisen_hungerbuhler_1997_optimal_rational_cycle_bounds/example_p11|the example's page]].

**Source.** Lorenz Halbeisen and Norbert Hungerbühler, *Optimal bounds for
the length of rational Collatz cycles*, Acta Arith. 78 (1997), 227--239;
Theorem 4 on p. 11 of the authors' preprint named on the
[[number_theory/halbeisen_hungerbuhler_1997_optimal_rational_cycle_bounds/_index|source card]],
numbered 1--13 rather than by the journal's pagination.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the statement and the remark on optimality
were read on the print. The paper prints no separate proof. Nothing here is
independently reviewed.

## Proof pointer

The paper gives no proof under the theorem; the argument of (11) (p. 10)
gives it. A positive cycle with $l$ steps and $n$ odd
steps has $2^l>3^n$, so $n\le n(l)$, and its minimum is at most
$M_{l,n}/(2^l-3^n)$, which by Lemma 7 (p. 9) is at most
$M_{l,n(l)}/(2^l-3^{n(l)})$; Corollary 1 makes this the quotient in the
display at $L=l$, so $l\ge L(\min C)$.
