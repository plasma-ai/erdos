## Statement

Setting (pp. 171--172). $(W,S)$ is a finite Coxeter system. For $J\subseteq
S$, $W_J$ is the subgroup generated by $J$; each coset $wW_J$ contains a unique
element $w_J$ of minimal length, and $W^J$ is the set of these
representatives, ordered by the restriction of the Bruhat order of $W$. It is
graded with the restricted length as rank function, and its rank-generating
function is $F(W,q)/F(W_J,q)=\prod_{i=1}^m(1+q+\cdots+q^{e_i})\big/\prod_{i=1}^l(1+q+\cdots+q^{f_i})$,
with $e_1,\ldots,e_m$ the exponents of $W$ and $f_1,\ldots,f_l$ those of $W_J$
(p. 171, display (2)). Rank-symmetric, rank-unimodal and property S (the
$k$-Sperner property for all $k$) are as defined on p. 168; see
[[number_theory/stanley_1980_weyl_groups_hard_lefschetz_theorem_sperner/theorem_2_4|Theorem
2.4]].

**Theorem 3.1** (p. 172, quoted). "Let $(W,S)$ be a Coxeter system for which
$W$ is a Weyl group. Let $J\subset S$ and let $W^J$ be the poset defined
above. Then $W^J$ is rank-symmetric, rank-unimodal, and has property S."

**Remarks** (p. 172). The paper states that Theorem 3.1 is easily checked to
hold also for the dihedral groups $I_2(p)$ and for $H_3$, that the remaining
case $H_4$ can presumably also be checked directly, and that by Proposition 2.5 a reducible
Coxeter system reduces to its irreducible factors. Instances used later: in
type $A_{n-1}$ with $J=S-\{(n-k,n-k+1)\}$, $W^J$ is the lattice $L(k,n-k)$ of
partitions with at most $k$ parts, each at most $n-k$, ordered by inclusion of
Ferrers diagrams, with rank-generating function the $q$-binomial coefficient
(pp. 173--174); in type $B_n$ with $J=\{s_1,\ldots,s_{n-1}\}$, $W^J$ is the
distributive lattice $M(n)$ of subsets of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, with
$\{a_1<\cdots<a_j\}\le\{b_1<\cdots<b_k\}$ when $j\le k$ and $a_{j-i}\le
b_{k-i}$ for $0\le i\le j-1$, and rank-generating function
$(1+q)(1+q^2)\cdots(1+q^n)$ (pp. 177--178).

## Proof pointer

Pages 171--172. For $G$ a complex semisimple algebraic group with Weyl group
$W$ and $P$ a parabolic subgroup, $G/P$ is a nonsingular irreducible complex
projective variety, and the Bruhat decomposition gives a cellular
decomposition into generalized Schubert cells (p. 171, cited to [5]). The
parabolic subgroups containing a fixed Borel subgroup correspond to the
subgroups $W_J$, and the poset $Q^{G/P}$ is isomorphic to $W^J$ (p. 172,
cited); Theorem 2.4 then applies.
