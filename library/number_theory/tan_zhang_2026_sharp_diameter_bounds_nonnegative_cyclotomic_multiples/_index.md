## Research digest

Tan and Zhang prove sharp support-diameter bounds for polynomials with
nonnegative real coefficients divisible by a cyclotomic polynomial. Their
Theorem 1.1 (p. 2): for $N\ge2$ with least prime divisor $p$, every nonzero
$F\in\mathbb R[X]$ with nonnegative coefficients and $\Phi_N\mid F$ has
$\max\operatorname{supp}F-\min\operatorname{supp}F\ge(p-1)N/p$, with
equality exactly for $F=cX^b(1+X^{N/p}+\cdots+X^{(p-1)N/p})$, $c>0$,
$b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$; this proves Steinberger's conjecture, which
Steinberger had proved for even $N$, for prime powers, and when the distinct
prime factors $p=p_1<\cdots<p_s$ of $N$ satisfy
$2/p_1>\sum_{i=2}^s1/p_i$ (1.4).
Evaluated at a primitive $N$th root of unity, such a polynomial gives a
vanishing sum of $N$th roots of unity with nonnegative weights, its exponent
support recording where the mass lies.  The extremal constructions and sharp
bounds provide another way to constrain the geometry of positive relations.

For E0774 this is background rather than a direct solution method.  It can help
when a roots-of-unity construction is organized through positive relations or
cyclotomic multiples, but dissociation concerns signed, square-free relations.
Do not infer a signed circuit bound from a nonnegative coefficient theorem
without an explicit reduction.
