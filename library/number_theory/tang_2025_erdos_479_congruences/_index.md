# Tang (2025): A note on Erdős Problem 479

[[number_theory/_index|..]]

[[number_theory/tang_2025_erdos_479_congruences/theorem_3_1|theorem_3_1]]: Tang's explicit proof that, for every integer i at least 1, infinitely many
positive integers n satisfy 2^n ≡ 2^i (mod n), by taking n = ip for primes
p in a progression given by multiplicative orders.

***

Quanyu Tang's *A Note on Erdős Problem #479: Infinitude of the Sets
$A(2^i)$ and Related Results* (2 December 2025) is an unpublished author
manuscript. Its p. 1 explicitly disclaims novelty of the underlying
number-theoretic statements and describes the power-of-two argument as
expository and independent; it may or may not coincide with the unpublished
Graham–Lehmer–Lehmer proof. No accepted or published version was located by
the searches recorded on the problem page.

Theorem 3.1, stated on p. 4 and proved on pp. 4–5, states that for every
integer $i\geq1$ there are infinitely many positive integers $n$ with
$2^n\equiv2^i\pmod n$. Section 2 (pp. 1–3) surveys the values of $k$ for
which $A(k)=\{n\ge1:2^n\equiv k\pmod n\}$ is known to be infinite,
and ends (p. 3) by saying that the
question remains open for every fixed $k$ other than
$k\in\{0,-1,-2,2^i:i\geq1\}$.

Read status: claims checked for Theorem 3.1 (p. 4), read clause by clause
on the page images with its proof (pp. 4–5) read step by step; the p. 1
disclaimer and the Section 2 survey (pp. 1–3) were read on the page images.
Nothing is independently reviewed.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/diophantine_problems/E0479/_index|#479]]:
Theorem 3.1 proves the problem's assertion for the values $k=2^i$ with
$i\geq1$ and for no other $k$. As the note reports on p. 1, Erdős and
Graham (1980, p. 96) attribute to Graham, Lehmer and Lehmer the partial
result for these $k$ and for $k=-1$; Theorem 3.1 covers its cases $k=2^i$
only. The Section 2 survey (p. 3) lists $k\in\{0,-1,-2\}$ as the
other values for which infinitude is known: $k=0$ as immediate ($A(0)$ is
the set of powers of $2$), $k=-1$ with Kalmynin and the OEIS, and $k=-2$
with Kin Y. Li et al. Paged
at [[number_theory/tang_2025_erdos_479_congruences/theorem_3_1|theorem_3_1]].

**Extracted result.** [[number_theory/tang_2025_erdos_479_congruences/theorem_3_1|Theorem 3.1]]
(p. 4): for every integer $i\geq1$, infinitely many positive integers $n$
satisfy $2^n\equiv2^i\pmod n$.
