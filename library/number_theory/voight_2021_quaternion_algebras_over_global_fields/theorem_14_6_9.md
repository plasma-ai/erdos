## Statement

**Theorem 14.6.9** (Hasse--Minkowski, p. 233, quoted). "Let $F$ be a global
field and let $Q$ be a quadratic form over $F$. Then $Q$ is isotropic over $F$
if and only if $Q_v$ is isotropic over $F_v$ for all places $v$ of $F$."

The book calls this local-global principle for isotropy the Hasse principle
(p. 233). Over $F=\mathbb Q$ it is
[[number_theory/voight_2021_quaternion_algebras_over_global_fields/theorem_14_3_3|Theorem 14.3.3]],
which the chapter proves.

**Source.** John Voight, "Quaternion algebras over global fields," Chapter 14
of *Quaternion Algebras*, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 288, Springer, 2021,
pp. 217--240, doi:10.1007/978-3-030-56694-4_14. The statement is on p. 233.
The edition read is identified on the
[[number_theory/voight_2021_quaternion_algebras_over_global_fields/_index|source card]].

## Proof pointer

Not proved in the chapter. The book gives a proof in its Section 26.8, under
the same comments as for
[[number_theory/voight_2021_quaternion_algebras_over_global_fields/main_theorem_14_6_1|Main Theorem 14.6.1]],
and points to O'Meara's *Introduction to Quadratic Forms*, §§65--66, for a
standalone class field theory proof when $F$ is a number field.

## Dependencies

The book's Section 26.8 (outside this chapter).

## Bears on

The theorem concerns isotropy of quadratic forms and bears on no Erdős problem
directly. The card's note on
[[../wiki/problems/diophantine_problems/E0940/_index|Problem 940]] records that
it, like its rational case, could reduce a rational solvability question met
in that problem to local ones, while giving no integral or counting control.
