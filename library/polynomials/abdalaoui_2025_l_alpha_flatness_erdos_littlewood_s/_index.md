# polynomials/abdalaoui_2025_l_alpha_flatness_erdos_littlewood_s

[[polynomials/_index|..]]

***

el Houcein el Abdalaoui, On L^alpha-flatness of Erdős-Littlewood's polynomials.
arXiv preprint (2025). arXiv:2504.21499, doi:10.48550/arXiv.2504.21499. The
arXiv record (https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21499, read 2026-10-02) names the
Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 license.

The copy read for this card is arXiv version 1 of 30 April 2025, the PDF beside
this card. The preprint asserts in Theorem 1 (p. 2) that no sequence of
polynomials with plus-or-minus-one coefficients, normalized by 1/sqrt(q), is
L^(2p)-flat for any integer p > 1, and in Corollary 1 (p. 2) that none is
L^alpha-flat for any alpha >= 4; here a sequence is L^alpha-flat when the
moduli converge in L^alpha to 1 (p. 2). The abstract presents this as a
partial answer to an old problem of Littlewood, and Corollary 2 (p. 3) as a
positive answer to the Erdos-Newman conjecture that no ultraflat sequence of
such polynomials exists. The abstract names three inputs: the classical L^p
estimate for the Dirichlet kernel (Lemma 1), the interpolation inequalities of
Marcinkiewicz and Zygmund (Lemma 3), and the p-concentration theorem of Bonami
and Revesz for idempotent polynomials (Lemma 5). If true, Theorem 1 would
answer problem 1150 affirmatively, since a sequence of such polynomials with
maximum modulus at most (1+o(1)) sqrt(n) would be L^alpha-flat for every finite
alpha. In the problem's discussion thread Thomas Bloom reported that the final
step of the argument on page 9 does not in fact contradict the paper's own
Lemma 5 and that it did not look fixable, and Terence Tao asked that its claims
be treated as unconfirmed. Theorem 1.1 of the OpenAI release of 23 September
2026
([[polynomials/openai_2026_asymptotically_minimal_maxima_real_littlewood_polynomials/_index|card]])
gives, for every eta > 0 and every large N, sign polynomials of length N with
maximum modulus at most (1+eta) sqrt N on the unit circle, hence L^alpha-flat
for every finite alpha, so the conclusion of Theorem 1 is false as stated;
Appendix A of that release disputes the preprint's argument. The problem's
rejected claim page is
[[../wiki/problems/polynomials/E1150/claims/2025_04_30_el_abdalaoui|2025_04_30_el_abdalaoui]].
The card records the preprint's statements as claimed; none is verified.
