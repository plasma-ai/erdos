# polynomials/basu_pollack_roy_2006_algorithms_real_algebraic_geometry

[[polynomials/_index|..]]

[[polynomials/basu_pollack_roy_2006_algorithms_real_algebraic_geometry/theorem_1_22|theorem_1_22]]: Basu, Pollack and Roy's projection theorem for constructible sets: over an
algebraically closed field C, the projection to C^k of a constructible set
in C^{k+1} defined by polynomials with coefficients in a subring D is a
constructible set defined by polynomials with coefficients in D.

[[polynomials/basu_pollack_roy_2006_algorithms_real_algebraic_geometry/theorem_1_23|theorem_1_23]]: Basu, Pollack and Roy's quantifier elimination over an algebraically closed
field C: every formula of the language of fields with free variables
Y_1, ..., Y_l and coefficients in a subring D of C is C-equivalent to a
quantifier-free formula in the same variables with coefficients in D.

***

Saugata Basu, Richard Pollack, and Marie-Françoise Roy, *Algorithms in Real
Algebraic Geometry*, 2nd ed., Springer, 2006, Algorithms and Computation in
Mathematics 10, X+662 pages, DOI
[10.1007/3-540-33099-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-33099-2).

Chapter 1 works over an algebraically closed field \(\mathrm C\) (p. 19),
and Section 1.2 fixes a subring \(D\) of \(\mathrm C\) with quotient field
\(K\) (p. 22); the field \(\mathbb C\) of complex numbers is the typical
example, not the standing hypothesis. Constructible sets are the smallest
family containing the algebraic sets and closed under complement, finite
union and finite intersection (p. 20).
[[polynomials/basu_pollack_roy_2006_algorithms_real_algebraic_geometry/theorem_1_22|Theorem 1.22]]
(p. 33) states that a constructible set in \(\mathrm C^{k+1}\) defined by
polynomials with coefficients in \(D\) has a projection to
\(\mathrm C^k\) that is constructible and defined by polynomials with
coefficients in \(D\).
[[polynomials/basu_pollack_roy_2006_algorithms_real_algebraic_geometry/theorem_1_23|Theorem 1.23]]
on the same page states quantifier elimination over the algebraically
closed field \(\mathrm C\): for a subring \(D\) of \(\mathrm C\), every
formula \(\Phi(Y_1,\ldots,Y_\ell)\) of the language of fields whose free
variables are \(\{Y_1,\ldots,Y_\ell\}\) and whose coefficients lie in
\(D\) is \(\mathrm C\)-equivalent to some quantifier-free formula
\(\Psi(Y_1,\ldots,Y_\ell)\) with coefficients in \(D\). The book
derives from it the Lefschetz principle (Theorem 1.26, p. 34).
