# Erdős and Turán (1940), On interpolation III

[[polynomials/_index|..]]

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/evidence/_index|evidence/]]: Retains the independent review of the Lemma IV reconstruction and its
increasing-node interface.

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/lemma_iv_adjacent_fundamental_polynomials|lemma_iv_adjacent_fundamental_polynomials]]: Proves that the sum of the two Lagrange fundamental polynomials belonging
to adjacent real nodes is at least one throughout their intervening interval.

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_i|theorem_i]]: Erdős and Turán's bound for strongly normal node matrices: the monic node
polynomial satisfies |ω_n(x)| ≤ (8/√c_1)·√n/2^n throughout [−1,1], with
c_1 the constant of strong normality.

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_ii|theorem_ii]]: Erdős and Turán's bounds for the monic orthogonal polynomial of a weight
that is at least m on [a,b]: |ω_n(x)| is O((2n+1)/2^n) on [a,b] and
O(√(2n+1)/2^n) on [a+ε,b−ε], with explicit constants.

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_iii|theorem_iii]]: Erdős and Turán's lower bound |ω_n(x)| ≥ [c_34 m/((b−a)∫p)]^{1/2}((b−a)/4)^n
|x − x_d| for real x, where the weight is at least m on [a,b] and x_d is
the root of ω_n nearest to x; the proof uses Lemma IV.

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_iv|theorem_iv]]: Erdős and Turán's refinement of Theorem III: where also m ≤ p(x) ≤
M/√(1−x²) on [c,d], |ω_n(x)| exceeds a constant times |x − x_d|((b−a)/4)^n √n
on [c+ε,d−ε] for n > n_0(ε,c,d,p).

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_ix|theorem_ix]]: Erdős and Turán's asymptotic formula for Christoffel numbers: when
p(x)√(1−x²) is continuous and at least m > 0 on [−1,1], k_ν ~ π p(x_ν)
√(1−x_ν²)/n for the nodes with |x_ν| ≤ [1 − (log n)/n²]^{1/2}.

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_v|theorem_v]]: Erdős and Turán's Fejérian theorem for external behavior: if the nth roots
of the fundamental functions are at most 1+ε on [−1,1] for every small ε
and n > n_2(ε), then ω_n(z)^{1/n} tends to (z+√(z²−1))/2 off [−1,1].

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_vi|theorem_vi]]: Erdős and Turán's theorem that for a non-negative L-integrable weight on
[−1,1] whose zeros form a set of measure 0, the orthogonal polynomials
satisfy ω_n(z)^{1/n} → (z+√(z²−1))/2 uniformly in each interior domain
of the cut plane.

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_vii|theorem_vii]]: Erdős and Turán's Fejérian theorem for the fine distribution of nodes: if
the fundamental functions of the nodes in [cos β, cos α] are bounded by
c_49 there and the others grow at most polynomially, consecutive angles
in [α+ε, β−ε] differ by between constants times 1/n.

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_viii|theorem_viii]]: Erdős and Turán's theorem that where a weight satisfies 0 < m ≤ p(x) ≤
M/√(1−x²) on [cos β, cos α], consecutive root angles of its orthogonal
polynomials in [α+ε, β−ε] differ by between c_56/n and c_57/n.

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_x|theorem_x]]: Erdős and Turán's theorem that when p(x)√(1−x²) is continuous and at least
m > 0, each fundamental function with |x_ν| ≤ [1 − (log n)/n²]^{1/2} is
within ε on [−1,1] of an explicit Chebyshev expression φ_{n−1}, for
n > n_0(ε).

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_xi|theorem_xi]]: Erdős and Turán's theorem that when p(x)√(1−x²) is continuous and at least
m > 0 on [−1,1], consecutive root angles in [C(n)/n, π − C(n)/n] satisfy
n(ϑ_{k+1} − ϑ_k) → π uniformly in k, for any C(n) → ∞.

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_xii|theorem_xii]]: Erdős and Turán's Fejérian theorem that if [|l_k(x)|]^{1/n} ≤ 1+ε on
[−1,1] for every k and all n > n_10(ε), the node angles are uniformly
distributed: the proportion in [α,β] tends to (β−α)/π.

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_xiii|theorem_xiii]]: Erdős and Turán's theorem that for a non-negative L-integrable weight on
[−1,1] whose zeros form a set of measure 0, the proportion of root angles
of the nth orthogonal polynomial in a fixed [α,β] ⊂ [0,π] tends to
(β−α)/π.

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_xiv|theorem_xiv]]: Erdős and Turán's Fejérian theorem with error term: if |l_ν(x)| ≤ D on
[−1,1] for all ν and n, the number of node angles in [α,β] differs from
(β−α)n/π by less than c_70(D,ε){(β−α)n}^{1/2+ε} once (β−α)n ≥ c_69(D,ε).

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_xv|theorem_xv]]: Erdős and Turán's Fejérian theorem that if |l_k(x)| ≤ c_87 n^{c_88} on
[−1,1] for all k and n, the number of node angles in any [α,β] ⊂ [0,π]
differs from (β−α)n/π by less than c_89(c_87,c_88,ε) n^{1/2+ε}.

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_xvi|theorem_xvi]]: Erdős and Turán's theorem that for an L-integrable weight with p(x) ≥ m >
0 on [−1,1], the number of root angles of the nth orthogonal polynomial
in any [α,β] ⊂ [0,π] is (β−α)n/π up to less than c_90(p,ε) n^{1/2+ε}.

[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_xvii|theorem_xvii]]: Erdős and Turán's theorem that for an L-integrable weight with 0 < m ≤
p(x)√(1−x²) ≤ M on [−1,1], the number of root angles in [α,β] is
(β−α)n/π up to less than c_91(p,ε){(β−α)n}^{1/2+ε} once n(β−α) >
c_92(p,ε).

***

Paul Erdős and Paul Turán, *On interpolation. III. Interpolatory theory of
polynomials*, Annals of Mathematics (2) **41** (3) (1940), 510--553,
DOI [10.2307/1968733](https://doi.org/10.2307/1968733).
[The primary scan in the Rényi Institute Erdős
archive](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1940-05.pdf), a complete 44-page PDF,
is the copy read for this card. The scan's
first page identifies the journal, volume, issue, month, year, title, authors,
received date, and printed page 510; this record does not assert that the
archive scan is a separately authenticated publisher facsimile. The
scan prints the journal header and no copyright or license line; the article's
Crossref record (DOI 10.2307/1968733, read 2026-10-02) names no license and its
JSTOR page was not read, and the journal's site shows the footer "Copyright ©
2026 Annals of Mathematics" and names no license
(https://annals.math.princeton.edu/, read 2026-10-02), every other right
reserved.

The article uses, on printed p. 510, a descending row of distinct nodes

$$
1\ge x_1^{(n)}>x_2^{(n)}>\cdots>x_n^{(n)}\ge-1,
\qquad x_\nu^{(n)}=\cos\vartheta_\nu^{(n)},
$$

and the ordinary Lagrange fundamental polynomials

$$
l_{\nu,n}(x)
=\frac{\omega_n(x)}
       {\omega_n'(x_\nu^{(n)})(x-x_\nu^{(n)})},
\qquad
\omega_n(x)=\prod_{j=1}^n(x-x_j^{(n)}).
$$

Its two model classes (pp. 511--512) are the roots of the monic orthogonal
polynomials of a non-negative Lebesgue-integrable weight $p$ on $[-1,1]$
and Fejér's strongly normal matrices. Following what the paper calls Fejér's
principle, it derives properties of the nodes from properties of the
fundamental functions ("Fejérian theorems") and applies them to both classes.
The numbered sections of the body are printed "1." (p. 521) to "4."
(p. 545), while the introduction refers to them as §§2--5.

**Results.**

- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_i|Theorem I]] (p. 521): $|\omega_n(x)|\le(8/\sqrt{c_1})\sqrt n/2^n$
  on $[-1,1]$ for strongly normal matrices; Lemma I,
  $\sum_\nu1/|\omega_n'(x_\nu)|\ge2^{n-2}$, is stated on the same page.
- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_ii|Theorem II]] (p. 522): upper bounds for the monic
  orthogonal polynomial on a subinterval where $p\ge m>0$.
- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_iii|Theorem III]] (p. 528): a lower bound in terms of
  the distance to the nearest root, proved with Lemma IV.
- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_iv|Theorem IV]] (p. 530): the lower bound gains a factor
  $\sqrt n$ where also $p\le M/\sqrt{1-x^2}$.
- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/lemma_iv_adjacent_fundamental_polynomials|Lemma IV]] (p. 529):
  $l_{k,n}(x)+l_{k+1,n}(x)\ge1$ for $x_{k+1}^{(n)}\le x\le x_k^{(n)}$, for any
  node matrix.
- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_v|Theorem V]] (p. 532): if
  $[|l_k(x)|]^{1/n}\le1+\epsilon$ on $[-1,1]$ for all $k$ and $n>n_2(\epsilon)$,
  for every sufficiently small $\epsilon$, then
  $\omega_n(z)^{1/n}\to(z+\sqrt{z^2-1})/2$ off $[-1,1]$.
- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_vi|Theorem VI]] (p. 535): the same limit for weights whose
  zero set has measure $0$, through Lemma VII (p. 534).
- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_vii|Theorem VII]] (p. 536): fundamental functions of the
  nodes in a subinterval bounded there, and the others at most polynomially
  large there, give root gaps of order $1/n$ inside it.
- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_viii|Theorem VIII]] (p. 538): gaps of order $1/n$ where
  $0<m\le p(x)\le M/\sqrt{1-x^2}$.
- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_ix|Theorem IX]] (p. 542): Christoffel numbers
  $k_\nu\sim\pi p(x_\nu)\sqrt{1-x_\nu^2}/n$ for the nodes with
  $|x_\nu|\le[1-\log n/n^2]^{1/2}$, when $p(x)\sqrt{1-x^2}$ is continuous
  and at least $m>0$.
- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_x|Theorem X]] (p. 543): uniform asymptotic form of the
  fundamental functions under the same hypothesis.
- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_xi|Theorem XI]] (p. 544): $n(\vartheta_{k+1}-\vartheta_k)\to\pi$
  for the roots in $[C(n)/n,\pi-C(n)/n]$.
- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_xii|Theorem XII]] (p. 545): the $n$th-root condition of
  Theorem V forces uniform distribution of the node angles.
- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_xiii|Theorem XIII]] (p. 547): uniform distribution of the
  root angles for weights whose zero set has measure $0$.
- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_xiv|Theorem XIV]] (pp. 547--548): uniformly bounded
  fundamental functions give discrepancy $c_{70}(D,\epsilon)\{(\beta-\alpha)n\}^{1/2+\epsilon}$.
- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_xv|Theorem XV]] (p. 552): polynomially bounded fundamental
  functions give discrepancy $c_{89}n^{1/2+\epsilon}$.
- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_xvi|Theorem XVI]] (p. 553) and
- [[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/theorem_xvii|Theorem XVII]] (p. 553): the two discrepancy bounds
  for orthogonal polynomials, under $p\ge m>0$ and under
  $0<m\le p(x)\sqrt{1-x^2}\le M$ respectively.

The introduction also announces, without proof, that
$|\omega_n(x)|\le A(n)/2^n$ on $[-1,1]$ with $A(n)\ge2$ gives discrepancy
at most $8[n\log A(n)]^{1/2}$ on a fixed $[\alpha,\beta]$ (p. 520), and
several further results whose proofs it omits (pp. 516, 518--520); no
page is written for them.

**Lemma IV.** Lemma IV, together with its proof, occupies printed p. 529 /
physical p. 20. The paper proves the indices $2<k<n-2$ by counting zeros of
the derivative and says that the cases $k=1,2,n-2,n-1$ run analogously. Its
final contradiction literally considers an interior point where
$\phi(\xi_0)=1$; that sentence does not itself state the needed
contradiction when $\phi(\xi)<1$. The
[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/lemma_iv_adjacent_fundamental_polynomials|Lemma IV page]]
preserves this source detail and completes the same derivative-root
argument, including the boundary cases and the translation to increasing
nodes. Lemma IV does not depend on the preceding orthogonal-polynomial
lemmas; its first use is the proof of Theorem III (p. 530).

**Read status.** Claims checked for Theorems I to XVII and for the lemmas
stated on their pages (I, III, V, VI, VII, VIII, IX, X, XI), read clause by
clause on the page images of the print; the proofs were followed for
structure only, except Lemma IV, whose proof is rewritten in full on its
page with an independent review retained under `evidence/`. Theorems XI and
XV are deduced in the paper without written details, and those deductions
were not reconstructed. Nothing else here is independently reviewed.

**Used by.** P. Erdős and J. Szabados cite Lemma IV on p. 194 of their 1978
paper on the integral of the Lebesgue function
([[polynomials/erdos_szabados_1978_integral_lebesgue_function_interpolation/integral_lower_bound|its
integral lower bound]]), and Erdős's 1989 paper on convergent interpolatory
polynomials uses it
([[polynomials/erdos_1989_convergent_interpolatory_polynomials/theorem|its
theorem]]).

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/polynomials/E1153/_index|Problem 1153]]:
the paper proves no lower bound for the Lebesgue function
$\lambda(x)=\sum_k|l_k(x)|$. The sum appears only in an upper bound,
$\sum_\nu|l_\nu(x)|\le\sqrt{n/c_1}$ for strongly normal matrices, inside
the second proof of Theorem I (p. 522), and in Pólya's condition quoted on
p. 517. Its
[[polynomials/erdos_turan_1940_on_interpolation_iii/lemma_iv_adjacent_fundamental_polynomials|Lemma IV]] is an input
that Erdős and Szabados (1978) cite for their integral lower bound, whose
own relation to the problem is stated on that result's page; the paper
itself proves nothing about the problem.
