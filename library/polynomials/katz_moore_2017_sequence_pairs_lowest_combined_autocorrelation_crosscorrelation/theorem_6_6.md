## Statement

Sequences are identified with Laurent polynomials
$f(z)=\sum_jf_jz^j\in\mathbb C[z,z^{-1}]$ (p. 7); $\operatorname{ord}f$ is
the least exponent with a nonzero coefficient, with
$\operatorname{ord}0=\infty$ (p. 8). A pair is isoenergetic when its two
sequences have the same Euclidean norm (p. 2).

- Construction 6.1 (p. 23):
  $\operatorname{GRS}((a,b),m)=(a+z^mb,\ a-z^mb)$ for an integer $m$.
- Construction 6.2 (pp. 23–24): for a sequence
  $\gamma=(\gamma_0,\gamma_1,\ldots)$ of transformations,
  $\operatorname{GRS}^0_\gamma((a,b),m)=\gamma_0(a,b)$ and
  $\operatorname{GRS}^{n+1}_\gamma((a,b),m)=\gamma_{n+1}\bigl(\operatorname{GRS}(\operatorname{GRS}^n_\gamma((a,b),m),2^nm)\bigr)$.
- Definition 4.10 (p. 17): the stationary Golay group $\mathrm{SGol}$ is the
  group of maps of pairs generated by swapping the two entries, conjugate
  reversal of either entry, simultaneous reversal $\mathrm{srev}$, the
  scalings $(f,g)\mapsto(uf,vg)$ with $u,v$ nonzero complex and $|u|=|v|$, and
  the substitutions $z\mapsto wz$ with $|w|=1$.

**Theorem 6.6** (p. 27). Let $(f,g)$ be an isoenergetic Golay pair with
$\operatorname{ord}f=\operatorname{ord}g\ne\infty$, let $m$ be an integer
with $\max(\operatorname{len}f,\operatorname{len}g)\le m$, and let
$\gamma=(\gamma_0,\gamma_1,\ldots)$ be a sequence of transformations from
$\mathrm{SGol}$. Put
$(f^{(n)},g^{(n)})=\operatorname{GRS}^n_\gamma((f,g),m)$. Then for each
$n\in\mathbb N=\{0,1,\ldots\}$ the pair $(f^{(n)},g^{(n)})$ is an
isoenergetic Golay pair with
$\operatorname{ord}(f^{(n)},g^{(n)})=\operatorname{ord}(f,g)\ne\infty$,
$\max(\operatorname{len}f^{(n)},\operatorname{len}g^{(n)})\le2^nm$ and
$\operatorname{ADF}(f^{(n)})=\operatorname{ADF}(g^{(n)})$, and

$$
\operatorname{ADF}(f^{(n)})-\frac13=\Bigl(-\frac12\Bigr)^n\Bigl(\operatorname{ADF}(f)-\frac13\Bigr),
\qquad
\operatorname{CDF}(f^{(n)},g^{(n)})-\frac23=\Bigl(-\frac12\Bigr)^n\Bigl(\operatorname{CDF}(f,g)-\frac23\Bigr).
$$

Hence $\operatorname{ADF}(f^{(n)})$ and $\operatorname{ADF}(g^{(n)})$ tend to
$1/3$ and $\operatorname{CDF}(f^{(n)},g^{(n)})$ tends to $2/3$.

Remark 6.8 (p. 28) records that a binary Golay pair of length $m$ with
$\operatorname{ord}f=\operatorname{ord}g$, together with transformations from
the binary Golay group $\mathrm{BGol}$ (Definition 4.14, p. 17), meets these
hypotheses and gives binary pairs of length $2^nm$; Remark 6.7 says the same
for unimodular pairs and $\mathrm{UGol}$.

## Proof pointer

Induction on $n$ (pp. 27–28). The case $n=0$ uses Lemma 2.5 and the
invariance Lemmas 4.8 and 4.9 with Remark 4.11. The step is Proposition 6.5
(p. 26): one application of $\operatorname{GRS}$ followed by an element of
$\mathrm{SGol}$ multiplies $\operatorname{ADF}-\frac13$ and
$\operatorname{CDF}-\frac23$ by $-\frac12$, using Lemma 6.4 and
[[polynomials/katz_moore_2017_sequence_pairs_lowest_combined_autocorrelation_crosscorrelation/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]]
in the form $\operatorname{ADF}+\operatorname{CDF}=1$ for Golay pairs with
equal autocorrelation demerit factors.

**Depends on.** Proposition 6.5, Lemma 6.4, Lemmas 2.5, 4.8 and 4.9;
[[polynomials/katz_moore_2017_sequence_pairs_lowest_combined_autocorrelation_crosscorrelation/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]].
