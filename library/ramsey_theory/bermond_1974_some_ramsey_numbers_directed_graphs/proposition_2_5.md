## Statement

Notation (printed p. 313): $K_p^*$ is the complete symmetric directed graph
on $p$ vertices, $TT_p$ the transitive tournament on $p$ vertices, and the
Ramsey number $R(G_1,\ldots,G_k)$ of directed graphs "is the smallest
integer $n$ such that for any partition $(U_1,\ldots,U_k)$ of the arcs of
$K_n^*$ into $k$ sets, some of which may be empty, there exists an integer
$i$, $1\le i\le k$, such that $G_i$ is a subgraph of the partial graph of
$K_n^*$ generated by $U_i$". Section 2 (p. 314) defines $\nu(n)$ as "the
smallest integer $\nu$, such that every tournament $T_\nu$ contains a
transitive subtournament $TT_n$", records $\nu(3)=4$, and writes
$r(n_1,\ldots,n_{k-1})$ for the classical Ramsey number
$R(K_{n_1},\ldots,K_{n_{k-1}})$.

**Proposition 2.5** (printed p. 316). "$R(TT_3,K_3^*)=9$."

**In the problem's notation.** For a 2-coloring $(U_1,U_2)$ of the arcs of
$K_n^*$, $U_1$ is an arbitrary directed graph on $n$ vertices and $U_2$
contains a $K_3^*$ exactly on the triples of vertices no two of which are
joined by an arc of $U_1$. So the proposition says: every directed graph on
9 vertices contains a transitive tournament on 3 vertices as a subgraph or
an independent set of size 3, and some directed graph on 8 vertices
contains neither. In the letters of Problem 112, $k(3,3)=9$; in those of
Ihringer, Rajendraprasad and Weinert, $r(I_3,L_3)=9$, which their 2021
paper attributes to this one.

**Source.** J.-C. Bermond, Some Ramsey numbers for directed graphs,
Discrete Math. 9 (1974), 313--321; Proposition 2.5 with its proof on
printed p. 316 (PDF p. 4 of the publisher scan), the definitions
on p. 313 (PDF p. 1) and $\nu(n)$, its values and Theorem 2.2 on p. 314
(PDF p. 2), read on the page images (the text layer garbles the star of
$K_3^*$ and the congruences). The edition is identified in the
[[ramsey_theory/bermond_1974_some_ramsey_numbers_directed_graphs/_index|source digest]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the statement, the definitions of p. 313

## Proof pointer

Page 316. Upper bound: Theorem 2.2 (p. 314) at $k=2$ gives
$R(TT_3,K_3^*)\le r(\nu(3),3)=r(4,3)=9$; the paper states $r(4,3)=9$, the
classical Ramsey number $R(3,4)=9$, without citation. The argument behind
Theorem 2.2 for two colors: given $(U_1,U_2)$ on $K_9^*$, let $E_1$ be the
underlying graph of $U_1$ and $E_2$ its complement, so that $\{x,y\}\in E_2$
iff both arcs $xy$ and $yx$ lie in $U_2$; since $9\ge r(4,3)$, either $E_1$
contains a $K_4$, whose vertices carry a tournament $T_4$ inside $U_1$
(one arc chosen from each pair), which contains a $TT_3$ because
$\nu(3)=4$, or $E_2$ contains a $K_3$, that is, $U_2$ contains a $K_3^*$.

Lower bound (p. 316). The paper 2-colors the arcs of $K_8^*$ on the
vertices $1,\ldots,8$: $U_1$ consists of the arcs $ij$ with $j-i\equiv2$ or
$3\pmod8$, and $U_2$ is its complement. It asserts, without printing an
argument, that $U_1$ contains no $TT_3$. For $U_2$ it passes to the
2-coloring $(E_1,E_2)$ of $K_8$ in which $E_1$ is the underlying graph of
$U_1$, cites Graver and Yackel [8, p. 148] for the fact that $E_2$ has no
triangle, and concludes that $U_2$ contains no $K_3^*$.

Filing checks of the two unprinted steps, carried out here: (a) if $ab$ and
$bc$ are arcs of $U_1$ then $c-a\equiv(b-a)+(c-b)\in\{4,5,6\}\pmod8$, which
is neither 2 nor 3, so $ac$ is not an arc and $U_1$ contains no $TT_3$;
$U_1$ is moreover an oriented graph, since $\{2,3\}$ and $\{-2,-3\}\equiv
\{6,5\}$ are disjoint mod 8. (b) $E_1$ is the circulant on $\mathbb Z_8$
with differences $\pm2,\pm3$, so $E_2$ is the circulant with differences
$\pm1$ and $4$; three differences from $\{1,7,4\}$ never sum to $0\pmod8$
(the ten sums, over the multisets of size three, are $3,1,6,7,4,1,5,2,7$
and $4$, none $0$), so $E_2$ has no triangle. Both checks are one line each
and are not a review of the paper.

## Dependencies

Within the paper: Theorem 2.2 (p. 314) and the value $\nu(3)=4$ (p. 314),
itself immediate (a tournament on 4 vertices has a vertex of outdegree at
least 2, and the two out-neighbors with it form a $TT_3$; the cyclic
triangle shows $\nu(3)>3$). Outside it: the classical $R(3,4)=9$, stated
without citation, and Graver and Yackel, Some graph theoretic results
associated with Ramsey's theorem, J. Combin. Theory 4 (1968), 125--175, at
p. 148, for the triangle-freeness of the 8-vertex complement, filed as
[[graph_coloring/graver_yackel_1968_graph_theoretic_results_associated_ramsey_theorem/_index|graver_yackel_1968_graph_theoretic_results_associated_ramsey_theorem]];
the cited passage is Computation F on printed p. 148 (PDF p. 24), the
cyclic graph on eight points with $i_1=1$, $i_2=4$, the octagon with
antipodal points joined, "Clearly there are no triangles", read clause by
clause on the page image on 2026-09-22; it is the graph $E_2$ above, and
the folder has no result page for it. The triangle-freeness is also
checked here directly. The note added in proof (p. 320) says the result is
also in Harary and Hell, Generalised Ramsey theory for graphs V, The Ramsey
number of a digraph, then to appear.
