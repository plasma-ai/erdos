## Statement

Section 5, "Question" (p. 202), says that the bounds of Theorems 2 and 3
are far from satisfactory and leave many questions open, and singles out
one question that the authors found "particularly annoying" not to have
answered: "Does $f(n)/n\to\infty$ as $n\to\infty$?"

Here $f(n)$ is the paper's all-graphs function, the largest $q$ such that
every connected graph of order $n$ and size $q$ satisfies $r(K_3,G)=2n-1$
(printed p. 193), which is the site's $F(n)$ in Problem 1182. Theorem 1 of
the paper brackets it between $(17n+1)/15$ and $(27/4+\varepsilon)n(\log n)^2$,
so the question is whether $f(n)$ eventually exceeds every constant
multiple of $n$.

**Source.** S. A. Burr, P. Erdős, R. J. Faudree, C. C. Rousseau and R. H.
Schelp, An extremal problem in generalized Ramsey theory, Ars Combin. 10
(1980), 193--203; Section 5 on printed p. 202 (PDF p. 10 of the
Rényi scan), read on the rendered page image (the text layer prints the
infinity signs as dashes).

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the passage was read clause by clause on
the page image on 2026-09-18. There is nothing to prove; the section states
a question.

## Proof pointer

None; a question. Brandt's 1996 preprint claims the
[[ramsey_theory/brandt_1996_expanding_graphs_ramsey_numbers/bound_p7|bound]]
$f(n,3)/n<84$ for large $n$, which would make the answer no; the problem
page records the claim as pending.
