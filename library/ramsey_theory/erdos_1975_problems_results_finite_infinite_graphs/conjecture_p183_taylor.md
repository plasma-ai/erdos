## Statement

$K(G)$ is the chromatic number of $G$ (the paper's notation).

**Conjecture** (Walter Taylor; p. 183, quoted). "Let $K(G)=\aleph_1$
($K(G)$ is the chromatic number of $G$). Then to every cardinal number
$m$ there is a graph $G'$ with $K(G')=m$ and every finite subgraph of $G'$
also occurs in $G$."

**Further problem** (p. 184). Erdős asks for a characterisation of the
families $F$ of finite graphs for which there are graphs of arbitrarily
large chromatic number all of whose finite subgraphs lie in $F$, and
remarks that "finite" can be replaced by "power $\le m$".

**Source.** P. Erdős, *Problems and results on finite and infinite graphs*,
Recent advances in graph theory (Proc. Second Czechoslovak Sympos., Prague,
1974), Academia, Prague, 1975, pp. 183--192; Section II, pp. 183--184. The
edition read is identified on the
[[ramsey_theory/erdos_1975_problems_results_finite_infinite_graphs/_index|source card]].
