## Statement

$K(G)$ is the chromatic number of $G$; $K(\aleph_0,\aleph_0)$ is the
complete bipartite graph with $\aleph_0$ white and $\aleph_0$ black
vertices, and $K(n;\aleph_1)$ the one with $n$ and $\aleph_1$ (the paper's
notation).

**Theorem** (Erdős and Hajnal, the paper's reference [8]; p. 184, reported
without proof). Assuming the continuum hypothesis, there is a graph $G$ on
$\aleph_1$ vertices with $K(G)=\aleph_1$ that contains no
$K(\aleph_0,\aleph_0)$.

**Theorem** (Hajnal, the paper's reference [9]; p. 184). There is such a
graph which in addition contains no triangle. The sentence does not repeat
the continuum hypothesis.

**Question** (p. 184, quoted). "Is there such a graph the smallest odd
circuit of which has size $2\ell+1$?"

**Theorem** (Erdős and Hajnal, [8]; p. 184, in parentheses).
$K(G)=\aleph_1$ implies that $G$ contains a $K(n;\aleph_1)$ for every
finite $n$.

**Source.** P. Erdős, *Problems and results on finite and infinite graphs*,
Recent advances in graph theory (Proc. Second Czechoslovak Sympos., Prague,
1974), Academia, Prague, 1975, pp. 183--192; Section II, p. 184. The
edition read is identified on the
[[ramsey_theory/erdos_1975_problems_results_finite_infinite_graphs/_index|source card]].
Reference [8] is P. Erdős and A. Hajnal, On the chromatic number of graphs
and set systems, Acta Math. Acad. Sci. Hungar. 17 (1966), 61--99;
reference [9] is A. Hajnal, A negative partition relation, Proc. Nat. Acad.
Sci. USA 68 (1971), 142--144.
