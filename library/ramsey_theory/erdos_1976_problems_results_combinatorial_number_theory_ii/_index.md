[[ramsey_theory/erdos_1976_problems_results_combinatorial_number_theory_ii/problem_p289|problem_p289]]: Erdős's 1976 statement of Cohen's question, a function F(d) such that every
two-class split of the integers has, for infinitely many d, a progression of

[[ramsey_theory/erdos_1976_problems_results_combinatorial_number_theory_ii/problem_p290|problem_p290]]: Erdős's 1976 question whether every two-class split of the integers (or
reals) has an infinite sequence all of whose multilinear expressions lie in
one class, his much weaker conjecture for r integers with their pairwise sums
and products, and Graham's computer result for x, y, x+y, xy up to 252; the
questions behind Problems 1198 and 172.

***

Math. Soc. (N.S.) **40** (1976), 285--298. Received April 25, 1975; a
footnote to the title records that the paper is the text of Erdős's lecture to
the Indian Mathematical Society's annual meeting (Powai, Bombay, December
1974).

## Contents

- Introduction (pp. 285--286): Erdős refers to his 1973 survey of the same
  title, "Problems and results on combinatorial number theory", as I,
  avoiding overlap with it except where its problems have seen progress, and
  lists, as II--XI, papers, books and conference proceedings where related
  problems and further literature can be found.
- Section 1 (pp. 286--288): $r_k(n)$, the Erdős--Turán conjecture
  $r_k(n)=o(n)$ with Szemerédi's proof announced for Acta Arithmetica, the
  Behrend--Roth bounds for $k=3$, van der Waerden's function $f(k)$, and a
  density version of the Gallai--Witt theorem (Ajtai--Szemerédi for the
  isosceles right triangle, Szemerédi for the square).
- Section 2 (pp. 288--290): the conjecture that a sequence with
  divergent reciprocal sum contains $k$-term progressions for every $k$, its
  finite forms $g_k(n)$ and $G(k)$ with $G(k)>\frac12\log f(k)$ (Gerver's
  $G(k)\ge(1+o(1))k\log k$ added in proof), $h_k(n)$ for three-term
  progressions without $k$-term ones, then Cohen's problem (below), Hindman's
  theorem with Baumgartner's shorter proof, the multilinear extension, and
  Graham's computation that any two-class split of the integers up to $252$
  has $x,y,x+y,xy$ in one class, failing for $251$.
- Section 3 (pp. 291--293): covering systems with least modulus $n_1$
  arbitrarily large (Choi's record $n_1=20$), $g(t)$, exact covering systems
  (Mirsky--Newman, the Herzog--Schönheim question), integers not of the form
  $2^k+p$, Gallagher's and Linnik's results, $2^k+Q$ with $Q$ squarefree, and
  Cohen--Selfridge on sums and differences of two prime powers.
- Section 4 (pp. 293--294): Sidon $B_2$ sequences, $\max B_2(x)=x^{1/2}+O(1)$
  conjectured, Szemerédi's result printed as $B_2(x)=x^{1/2}+0(x^{1/4})$ (the
  same 0 means $O$ in the conjecture and $o$ in the Erdős--Turán lower bound
  $(1+o(1))x^{1/2}$), and the question whether an infinite $B_2$ sequence can
  have $a_k/k^3\to0$.
- Section 5 (pp. 295--296): primitive sequences, then extremal problems on
  $B_2$ and $B_\infty$ sequences (all subset sums distinct) and on sequences
  in which no term is a sum of distinct other terms.
- Section 6 (pp. 296--298): miscellaneous problems.

## The Cohen passage (pp. 289--290)

The passage runs from the foot of p. 289 to the head of p. 290 (PDF
pp. 5--6, page images). Erdős calls it an old problem of Cohen and states it
as: "Determine or estimate a function $F(d)$ so that if we split the integers
into two classes at least one class contains, for infinitely many values of
$d$, an arithmetic progression of difference $d$ and length $F(d)$" (p. 289).
He adds in parentheses that the 1973 survey (I, p. 121) states the problem
incorrectly. He then records his own bound $F(d)<cd$ and announces that
Petruska and Szemerédi had improved it to $F(d)<cd^{1/2}$, were sure their
proof would give $F(d)=O(d^\varepsilon)$, and would publish soon. On the
other side he knows of no explicit lower bound: van der Waerden's theorem
gives $F(d)\to\infty$, and he says the true order of $F(d)$ may be very hard
to determine. No proof or reference is given for $F(d)<cd$ here (the 1973
survey's p. 121 gives the quadratic-irrationality coloring), and the
Petruska--Szemerédi result is announced, not cited.
