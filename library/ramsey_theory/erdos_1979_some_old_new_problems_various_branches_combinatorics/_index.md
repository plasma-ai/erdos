[[ramsey_theory/erdos_1979_some_old_new_problems_various_branches_combinatorics/problem_p15|problem_p15]]: Erdős's 1979 question on the densest m-vertex induced subgraph of a graph
on n vertices with no independent set of m vertices: the two-sided bound
(1) and the case m = [n^{1/2}], the origin of Problem 801.

***

Theory and Computing (Florida Atlantic Univ., Boca Raton, Fla., 1979),

## Contents

Opening (pp. 1--3): a numbered list of eighteen earlier problem papers,
referred to by number in the text, and the plan of the paper: first a
progress report on some of Erdős's favorite problems, then a few recent
problems with proofs of some new results.

1. Chromatic hypergraphs (pp. 3--4): $m_k(r)$, the smallest number of edges
   of a $k$-chromatic $r$-uniform hypergraph (property B); display (1)
   $\log2\cdot2^n<m_3(n)<c_1n^22^n$, Schmidt's improvement of the lower bound,
   the conjectures $m_3(n)/2^n\to\infty$ and (2) $\sum_i2^{-|A_i|}>c_r$ for a
   three-chromatic family with $|A_i|\ge r$, where $c_r\to\infty$ as
   $r\to\infty$, both proved by Beck, who in fact showed
   $m_3(n)>cn^{1/3}2^n$; critical families; $m_2(k)=\binom k2$ and the
   conjecture $m_r(k)=\binom{(k-1)r+1}{r}$ for $k>k_0(r)$, "still open even
   for $r=3$" (p. 4).
2. Random graphs (pp. 4--6): the Erdős--Rényi Hamiltonicity conjectures,
   proved by Pósa and by Komlós and Szemerédi; the longest path in
   $G(n;cn)$; matchings in random graphs and Shamir's problem on perfect
   matchings in random $r$-uniform hypergraphs; the Erdős--Spencer proof of
   Burtin's conjecture on random subgraphs of the cube; Füredi's random
   subgraphs of the plane lattice; the giant component and the second
   largest component of the evolving random graph.
3. Extremal graph theory (pp. 6--7, continued on the absent page 8): display
   (1) $f(n;C_4)=(\frac12+o(1))n^{3/2}$; the conjecture (2)
   $f(p^2+p+1;C_4)=\frac12p(p+1)^2+1$, proved for infinitely many $p$ by
   Füredi; graphs with no $C_3$ and no $C_4$ (open for more than ten years)
   and the $C_4,C_5$ case settled with Simonovits; the bipartite $C_6$
   conjecture; Turán's problem for $K^{(r)}(t)$, display (3) and the prize
   offer for the constants $c_{r,t}$; the edge density of a sequence
   of $r$-graphs, where the text breaks off at the foot of the page headed 7.
4. Intersecting families (pp. 9--10): $f(n;k,m)$, display (1)
   $f(n;k,2)=\binom{n-1}{k-1}$ for $k\ge3$, $n\ge\frac32k$; Chvátal's results
   and conjecture; Frankl's announcement; the Erdős--Hajnal arrow relation for
   $\binom43$ and its remaining $\log\log n$ gap.
5. Ramsey-type problems (pp. 10--12): the Erdős--Hajnal conjecture on
   $K(4)$-free graphs forcing a monochromatic triangle under $k$ colors,
   proved by Folkman for $k=2$ and by Nešetřil and Rödl; its infinite version;
   the offer for a proof or disproof that such a graph exists for
   two colors on at most $10^{10}$ vertices; the $G_{k,r}$ conjecture proved
   by Nešetřil and Rödl; the Dowker--Erdős directed-circuit question; the
   Erdős--Hajnal girth-and-chromatic-number function $f(k,r)$; Rosenfeld's
   question on triangle-free subgraphs of the unit-sphere graph of Hilbert
   space and Larman's disproof; Spencer's property R (a rainbow circuit under
   every proper edge coloring) and the question of graphs of arbitrarily large
   girth with property R.
6. Block designs and finite geometries (pp. 12--14): blocking sets meeting
   every line in at most $C$ points and its generalization (1); the
   Freeman--Bruen result; whether every family of sets with pairwise
   intersections at most one is contained in the lines of a finite geometry;
   the Sidon-set and perfect-difference-set conjecture; the Erdős--Larson
   family with $|A_i|=(1+o(1))n^{1/2}$; older questions on systems in which
   every pair lies in exactly one set.
7. Miscellaneous recent problems (pp. 14--19): I, Dinitz's problem on
   incomplete Latin squares from $n^2$ sets of size $n$; II, the
   Hanani--Erdős theorem on the graph of $n$ edges with the most $K(r)$'s and
   Alon's hypergraph question; III, the dense induced subgraphs of graphs
   with small independence number (the page headed 15; page
   [[ramsey_theory/erdos_1979_some_old_new_problems_various_branches_combinatorics/problem_p15|problem_p15]]);
   IV, the triangle-forcing density questions from paper 1, the function
   $F_{n/k}(n)$ with its display (1), and the "final remark" on graphs whose
   induced subgraphs all have edge density about $c$; V, $A(n;k)$, the least
   common multiple of $n+1,\dots,n+k$, with the proof that $\lim n_k/k=\infty$
   for the least $n_k$ with $A(n_k;k)>A(n_k+k;k)$ (displays (1)--(5)), the
   conjecture (6) $\lim n_k/k^2=0$, the old conjecture (7) $A(n,k)\ne A(m,k)$
   for $n+k<m$, Selfridge's solutions of (8) and the closing questions (9).

## Compiled scope

Read on page images: all eighteen pages for structure and for the
statements named above; the page headed 15 clause by clause. Statements only;
the short proof in item 7.V was not checked. The results quoted are Erdős's
summaries of other authors' work and are not verified against those papers.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/ramsey_theory/E0801/_index|#801]] (item 7.III, the page
headed 15, PDF p. 14: the definition of $f(n;m)$, display (1)
$c_1m<f(n;m)<c_2m\log n$, and "the most interesting open question is whether
the upper bound in (1) is best possible for $m=[n^{1/2}]$", the problem's
origin and the site's key Er79g).
[[../wiki/problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1080/_index|#1080]] (item 3, the page headed 7,
PDF p. 7, page image; the site's key Er79g): Erdős recalls "an old and
nearly forgotten conjecture of mine", that a bipartite graph "of $[n^{2/3}]$
white and $n$ black vertices and more than $cn$ edges" contains a $C_6$; he
adds that such a graph is easily seen to contain a $C_8$, and that "many
generalizations and extensions are possible". The 1975 survey's form of the
question has $n$ vertices in all, $[n^{2/3}]$ black and $n-[n^{2/3}]$ white,
and the site follows the survey; the problem page records why the two forms
have the same answer.

**Results.**

- [[ramsey_theory/erdos_1979_some_old_new_problems_various_branches_combinatorics/problem_p15|Problem (p. 15)]]
  (item 7.III): with $f(n;m)$ the largest integer such that every graph on
  $n$ vertices with no independent set of $m$ vertices has an $m$-vertex
  subgraph with $f(n;m)$ edges, $c_1m<f(n;m)<c_2m\log n$; is the upper bound
  best possible for $m=[n^{1/2}]$?
