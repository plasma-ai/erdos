## Statement

The introduction (p. 156) states the problem as: let
$1\le a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_n<\cdots$ be integers with
$\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n^{-1}=\infty$; show that $\{a_1,a_2,\ldots\}$ contains
arithmetic progressions of arbitrary length. The paper calls it a more general
problem than whether the primes contain arbitrarily long arithmetic
progressions, since Euler showed $\sum p_n^{-1}=\infty$, says the conjecture
is hard, and offers a prize to whoever can prove or disprove it.

Before it (p. 155) the paper recalls van der Waerden's theorem and the
conjecture of Turán and Erdős that for every $\varepsilon>0$ and every $k$
there is $n_0$ such that, for $n\ge n_0$, any integers
$1\le a_1<\cdots<a_l\le n$ with $l\ge\varepsilon n$ contain an arithmetic
progression of length $k$; it was still unsolved in 1972, when Erdős offered
a prize for a proof or disproof, and Szemerédi then proved it, with a second
proof by Furstenberg by ergodic methods (p. 156).

**Source.** P. Erdős, Noen mindre kjente problemer i kombinatorisk tallteori,
Normat 28 (1980), no. 4, 155--164, 180; the introduction, printed pp. 155--156, read on the page
images; the edition is identified in the [[ramsey_theory/erdos_1980_noen_mindre_kjente_problemer_i_kombinatorisk/_index|source digest]].
