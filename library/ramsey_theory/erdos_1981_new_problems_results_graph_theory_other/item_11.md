## Statement

As printed on p. 12: "Denote by $r(C_{2n+1},k)$ the largest integer $t$ for
which the edges of $K(t)$ can be colored by $k$ colors so that there should
be no monochromatic $C_{2n+1}$. Graham and I conjectured that

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty\ [\mathrm{sic}]}r(C_{2n+1},k)/r(C_3,k)=0\qquad\ldots\ (11)
$$

(11) is open even for $n=2$. Perhaps the proof of $r(C_5,k)<C^k$ will not
be too hard."

Two points of reading. The quantity is the largest order admitting a good
coloring, one less than the least forcing order $R_k(C_{2n+1})$ of the
problem page; the ratio question is unaffected. The limit is printed with
the subscript $n\to\infty$; the surrounding text ("open even for $n=2$", the
remark on $r(C_5,k)$) fixes $n$ and varies $k$, as does the 1975 source of
the conjecture (question (v) there has $k\to\infty$), so the printed
subscript is read as a misprint for $k\to\infty$ and is recorded here as
printed, marked [sic].

The same page continues with the Erdős--Graham problem on the shortest
monochromatic odd cycle forced in an $r$-coloring of $K(2^r+1)$ (the
function $f(r)$), and p. 10 attributes to Schur the bound
$r_k(C_3)=r_k(3,\ldots,3)<e\cdot k!$ and asks whether $r_k(C_3)<C^k$ (its
item (1)).

**Source.** P. Erdős, *Some new problems and results in graph theory and
other branches of combinatorial mathematics*, Lecture Notes in Math. 885
(1981), 9--17; item (11) on printed p. 12 (PDF p. 4 of the scan)
and the Schur attribution on printed p. 10 (PDF p. 2), read on the page
images (the OCR text layer garbles the formulas).

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the passage was read clause by clause on the
page image, including the printed subscript. There is nothing to prove; the
item is a conjecture.

## Proof pointer

None; a conjecture, stated with no argument.
