## Statement

As printed on p. 65: "Let $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ be a convex polygon in the
plane. Consider the $\binom n2$ distances $d(x_i,x_j)$ and assume that the
distance $u_i$ occurs $s_i$ times. Clearly

$$
\sum_is_i=\binom n2\,.
$$

I conjectured and Fishburn proved that

$$
\sum_is_i^2<cn^3\,.
$$

I also conjectured that $\sum_is_i^2$ is maximal for the regular $n$-gon
for $n>n_0$. If convexity is not assumed then I conjectured that

$$
\sum_is_i^2<n^{3+\epsilon} \tag{5.3}
$$

for any $\epsilon>0$ and $n>n_0(\epsilon)$. I offer \$500 for a proof or
disproof of (5.3)."

The first inequality is the statement of Problem 94, in the site's
notation $f(u_i)=s_i$ and $\sum_if(u_i)^2\ll n^3$. It is reported as
Fishburn's theorem without proof or reference; Fishburn's chapter
"Distances in Convex Polygons" is in Volume II of the same work (contents,
p. XV of the eBook); Volume II is not read here. No prize is printed for
it; the prize is for (5.3).

**Source.** P. Erdős, *Some of My Favorite Problems and Results*, The
Mathematics of Paul Erdős I (1997), 47--67; printed p. 65 (PDF p. 80 of
the eBook), read on the page image. The copy read is identified
in the
[[ramsey_theory/erdos_1997_some_my_favorite_problems_results/_index|source digest]].
