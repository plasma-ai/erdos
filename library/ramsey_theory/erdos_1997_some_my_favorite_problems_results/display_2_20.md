## Statement

As printed on p. 57, after the recollection "I also proved that if
$\sum_i\frac1{a_i\log a_i}$ is large then the sequence cannot be primitive,
i.e., $a_i\nmid a_j$ cannot hold": "Now the following problem is annoying:
Let $1<\alpha_1<\alpha_2<\cdots$ be a sequence of real numbers and assume
that for all $i\ne j,k$ we have

$$
|k\alpha_i-\alpha_j|\ge1\,. \tag{2.20}
$$

Note that if the $\alpha_i$'s are integers then (2.20) implies that no
$\alpha_i$ divides any $\alpha_j$, $i\ne j$. Does (2.20) imply

$$
\sum_i\frac1{\alpha_i\log\alpha_i}=\infty\quad\text{or}\quad
\frac1{\log x}\sum_{\alpha_i<x}\frac1{\alpha_i}\to\infty\ \text{ as }x\to\infty\
?\ \text{[sic]}
$$"

and, at the top of p. 58: "I offer \$500 for settling this annoying
diophantine problem."

A filing observation, not a review verdict: the printed question asks
whether (2.20) forces the two sums to diverge, while the site's Problem
143 asks whether it forces $\sum1/(x\log x)<\infty$ or
$\sum_{x<n}1/x=o(\log n)$, the sparseness that the integer case (a
primitive sequence, whose $\sum1/(a_i\log a_i)$ Erdős bounded in 1935)
suggests and that the preceding sentence recalls. The printed signs are
read here as reversed by a misprint, and the site's form is the one the
problem page carries; the source is recorded as printed.

**Source.** P. Erdős, *Some of My Favorite Problems and Results*, The
Mathematics of Paul Erdős I (1997), 47--67; printed pp. 57--58 (PDF
pp. 72--73 of the eBook), read on the page images, the displayed
question on a 300 dpi crop. The copy read is identified in the
[[ramsey_theory/erdos_1997_some_my_favorite_problems_results/_index|source digest]].
