## Statement

As printed on p. 58, after the prime-gap offers, with $d_n=p_{n+1}-p_n$:
"Ricci and I proved that the set of limit points of $d_n/\log_n$ [sic] has
positive measure. No doubt they are everywhere dense. Maier has the
sharpest partial results and he has collected \$250 more than once for
some of these."

The quotient is printed "$d_n/\log_n$", a misprint for $d_n/\log n$. The
sentence "No doubt they are everywhere dense" is the expectation behind
Problem 5, which asks whether every $C\ge0$ is a limit point; no prize is
attached to it here, the money named being payments to Maier for partial
results.

**Source.** P. Erdős, *Some of My Favorite Problems and Results*, The
Mathematics of Paul Erdős I (1997), 47--67; printed p. 58 (PDF p. 73 of
the eBook), read on the page image. The copy read is identified
in the
[[ramsey_theory/erdos_1997_some_my_favorite_problems_results/_index|source digest]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the sentences were read clause by clause on
the page image on 2026-09-22. No proof or reference is printed; the
positive-measure theorem is an author's statement here. Nothing is
independently reviewed.

## Proof pointer

None printed. The Problem 5 page lists Erdős 1955 and Ricci 1956 as the
theorem's papers and Banks, Freiberg and Maynard 2016 and Merikoski 2020
for the later results; none is consulted here.
