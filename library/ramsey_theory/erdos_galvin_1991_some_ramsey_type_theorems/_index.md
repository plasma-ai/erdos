# ramsey_theory/erdos_galvin_1991_some_ramsey_type_theorems

[[ramsey_theory/_index|..]]

[[ramsey_theory/erdos_galvin_1991_some_ramsey_type_theorems/corollary_2_2|corollary_2_2]]: For positive integers r and k there is c > 0 such that every k-coloring of
the r-subsets of N has a set A whose r-subsets take at most 2^(r-1) colors
and which has at least c log_{r-1} n points below n for infinitely many n.

[[ramsey_theory/erdos_galvin_1991_some_ramsey_type_theorems/problem_4_2|problem_4_2]]: Erdős and Galvin's question whether for some k a function φ bounds, infinitely
often, a sequence whose finite sums miss one of k + 1 classes in every
partition of N: Problem 948 (first asked by Erdős in 1977), negative for
k = 1 by Theorem 4.1 and left open from k = 2.

[[ramsey_theory/erdos_galvin_1991_some_ramsey_type_theorems/theorem_2_1|theorem_2_1]]: Erdős and Galvin's main result: if n → (φ(n))^r_{k+1} for all large n, then
every k-coloring of the r-subsets of N has a set A whose r-subsets take at
most 2^(r-1) colors and which has at least φ(n) points below n for
infinitely many n.

[[ramsey_theory/erdos_galvin_1991_some_ramsey_type_theorems/theorem_2_3|theorem_2_3]]: For every r and every φ: N → N there is a coloring of the r-subsets of N
with 2^(r-1) colors such that every infinite set either shows all colors on
its r-subsets or has a_n > φ(n) for all but finitely many n, so 2^(r-1) is
best possible in Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.2.

[[ramsey_theory/erdos_galvin_1991_some_ramsey_type_theorems/theorem_3_1|theorem_3_1]]: For every φ: N → N there is a partition of the pairs of N into two classes
such that any increasing infinite path whose consecutive pairs lie in one
class uses the second class and has x_n > φ(n) for all n.

[[ramsey_theory/erdos_galvin_1991_some_ramsey_type_theorems/theorem_3_3|theorem_3_3]]: For k ≥ 2, every coloring of the pairs of N with k colors has an increasing
infinite path whose edges use at most two colors and which has more than
n^(1/k) vertices in [1, n] for infinitely many n.

[[ramsey_theory/erdos_galvin_1991_some_ramsey_type_theorems/theorem_4_1|theorem_4_1]]: Galvin's two-class partition of N under which every sequence whose sums of
consecutive terms lie in one class has x_n > φ(n) for all n: the negative
answer to Erdős's 1977 monochromatic growth question and to Problem 4.2 for
k = 1.

[[ramsey_theory/erdos_galvin_1991_some_ramsey_type_theorems/theorem_4_3|theorem_4_3]]: For every k a constant c such that any partition of N into k classes has a
set X whose sums of consecutive elements lie in two classes and which has at
least c log log n points below n for infinitely many n: the interval-sums
theorem the site quotes on Problem 948.

## Contents

- Abstract and § 1, Introduction (pp. 261--262, page images). The abstract
  observes that the infinite homogeneous set $A$ of Ramsey's theorem can be
  arbitrarily sparse, and describes the paper as studying "versions of
  Ramsey's theorem in which the homogeneity requirement is weakened so
  that the rate of growth of the elements of $A$ can be bounded from
  above" (p. 261). With $\mathbb{N}$ the positive integers, the infinite
  Ramsey theorem gives, for any coloring of $[\mathbb{N}]^r$ with $k$
  colors, an infinite homogeneous $A=\{a_n:n\in\mathbb{N}\}$; for $r=1$
  the set can be chosen with
  $a_n\le k(n-1)+1$ for infinitely many $n$ (upper density at least
  $1/k$), while for $r\ge2$ the growth of $a_1,a_2,\ldots$ admits no bound
  at all, by Theorem 2.3. The sections are announced: § 2, at
  most $2^{r-1}$ colors on $[A]^r$ with $|A\cap\{1,\ldots,n\}|\ge
  c\log_{r-1}n$ for infinitely many $n$ (Corollary 2.2); § 3, for $k\ge2$
  colors on the edges of the complete graph on $\mathbb{N}$, an increasing
  infinite path with at most two edge colors and
  $|V(P)\cap\{1,\ldots,n\}|>n^{1/k}$ for infinitely many $n$ (Theorem 3.3);
  § 4, "the analogous questions regarding Hindman's finite sums theorem"
  (p. 262). Notation (p. 262): $\omega=
  \mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}$; $[A]^r$ the $r$-element subsets of $A$; $X\subseteq
  A$ is $f$-homogeneous if $f$ is constant on $[X]^r$; the partition symbol
  $a\to(x)^r_k$ asserts that every $k$-coloring of $[A]^r$ with $|A|=a$ has
  an $f$-homogeneous $X\subseteq A$ with $|X|\ge x$, where $x$ need not be an
  integer.
- § 2, Ramsey's theorem (pp. 262--265; statements on the page images, the
  proof of Theorem 2.1 in the text layer). The paper names Theorem 2.1 as
  its main result. Theorem 2.1 (p. 262, quoted): "Let $r$ and $k$ be
  positive integers, and let the function $\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}$
  be such that $n\to(\varphi(n))^r_{k+1}$ holds for all sufficiently large
  $n$. Given any coloring $f:[\mathbb{N}]^r\to\{1,\ldots,k\}$, there is a set
  $A\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ such that: (1) $|\{f(X):X\in[A]^r\}|\le2^{r-1}$;
  (2) $|A\cap\{1,\ldots,n\}|\ge\varphi(n)$ for infinitely many $n$." Since
  $n\to(c\log_{r-1}n)^r_s$ holds for large $n$ with $c=c(r,s)>0$ ([5,
  Theorem 26.6, p. 153]; $\log_{r-1}$ the $(r-1)$-times iterated logarithm),
  Corollary 2.2 (p. 262) restates the theorem with
  $|A\cap\{1,\ldots,n\}|\ge c\log_{r-1}n$ for infinitely many $n$, $c$
  depending only on $r$ and $k$, a form the paper says was stated in [1],
  for $r=2$ and $k=3$, and in [4]. Theorem 2.3 (pp. 262--263, quoted): "Let a
  positive integer $r$ and a function $\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ be
  given. There is a coloring $f:[\mathbb{N}]^r\to I$, with $|I|=2^{r-1}$
  colors, such that, for any infinite set $A=\{a_1,a_2,\ldots\}\subseteq
  \mathbb{N}$, $a_1<a_2<\cdots$, either $\{f(X):X\in[A]^r\}=I$, or else
  $a_n>\varphi(n)$ for all but finitely many $n$", so $2^{r-1}$ is best
  possible; its coloring records, for $x_1<\cdots<x_r$, which of the $r-1$
  gaps straddle a threshold $m_k=\varphi(rk)$, with $\varphi$ first taken
  strictly increasing. The proof of Theorem 2.1
  (pp. 263--265) reduces to $r\ge2$, calls a set $S\subseteq[\mathbb{N}]^{r-1}$
  large if for every $m,p$ and every $p$-coloring $g$ of $[\mathbb{N}]^{r-1}$
  there are $n>m$ and $Y\subseteq(m,n]$ with $|Y|\ge\varphi(n)$, $Y$ both
  $f$- and $g$-homogeneous and $[Y]^{r-1}\subseteq S$, shows in Claims 1--3
  that the large sets support an ultrafilter $\mathcal{U}$ on
  $[\mathbb{N}]^{r-1}$, derives ultrafilters $\mathcal{U}_p$ on
  $[\mathbb{N}]^p$ (Claim 4), defines colorings $f_{s,r_1,\ldots,r_t}$ by
  induction along admissible sequences and finds, for each finite $W$, a
  set $S(W)\in\mathcal{U}$ such that $f_{s+r_1,r_2,\ldots,r_t}(Z\cup X)=
  f_{s,r_1,\ldots,r_t}(Z)$ for $Z\in[W]^s$ and $X\in[Y]^{r_1}$ whenever
  $[Y]^{r-1}\subseteq S(W)$ and $|Y|\ge r-1$ (Claim 5), and builds $A$ as
  a union of blocks $Y_p\subseteq(n_{p-1},n_p]$ with
  $|Y_p|\ge\varphi(n_p)$ whose mixed $r$-sets take at most $2^{r-1}-1$
  colors and whose pure $r$-sets take one more.
- § 3, Increasing paths (pp. 265--267; statements on the page image of
  p. 266, the proof of Theorem 3.3 in the text layer). Theorem 3.1 (p. 266,
  quoted): "For any function $\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$, there is a
  partition $[\mathbb{N}]^2=C_1\cup C_2$ such that, for any infinite sequence
  $x_1<x_2<x_3<\cdots$ of positive integers, if $\{\{x_n,x_{n+1}\}:n\in
  \mathbb{N}\}\subseteq C_i$ for some $i\in\{1,2\}$, then $i=2$ and
  $x_n>\varphi(n)$ for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$", with the two-line coloring,
  for $\varphi$ first taken strictly increasing: $\{x,y\}\in C_2$, $x<y$,
  iff $\varphi(1)<x\le\varphi(n)<y$ for some $n$. Lemma 3.2
  (p. 266): a $k$-coloring of the complete graph on a linearly ordered $V$
  with $|V|>n^k$ has a monochromatic increasing path with $n+1$ vertices
  (Seidenberg's argument). Theorem 3.3 (p. 266, quoted): "Let $2\le k\in
  \mathbb{N}$. For any partition $[\mathbb{N}]^2=C_1\cup\cdots\cup C_k$, there
  is an increasing infinite path $P$, with vertex-set $V(P)=\{x_r:r\in
  \mathbb{N}\}\subseteq\mathbb{N}$, $x_1<x_2<\cdots$, and edge-set
  $E(P)=\{\{x_r,x_{r+1}\}:r\in\mathbb{N}\}$, such that $E(P)\subseteq
  C_i\cup C_j$ for some $i,j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}$ and $|V(P)\cap[1,n]|>n^{1/k}$
  for infinitely many $n$." Its proof partitions $\mathbb{N}$ into intervals
  $V_r$ of length $p_r^k+1$, takes a monochromatic increasing path in each
  by Lemma 3.2 and joins infinitely many by Ramsey's theorem. A closing
  remark (p. 267) announces, for paths not required to be increasing,
  monochromatic infinite paths of positive upper density, deferred to
  another paper [3].
- § 4, Finite sums (pp. 267--269, page images). Definitions (p. 267): for
  $X\subseteq\mathbb{N}$, $\mathrm{FS}(X)$ is the set of positive integers
  that are sums of distinct elements of $X$, that is, the numbers
  $\sum\{x:x\in F\}$ over the nonempty finite subsets $F\subseteq X$; and
  $\mathrm{CFS}(X)$ is the set of positive integers that are sums of
  consecutive elements of $X$, that is, the numbers
  $\sum\{x:x\in X,\ a\le x\le b\}$ with $a,b\in X$ and $a\le b$; so
  $\mathrm{CFS}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{FS}(X)$. Hindman's theorem [7] is
  recalled: every partition $\mathbb{N}=C_1\cup\cdots\cup C_k$ into finitely
  many classes has an infinite $X=\{x_1<x_2<\cdots\}$ with
  $\mathrm{FS}(X)\subseteq C_i$ for some $i$. Theorem 4.1 is introduced as
  the example showing that already for $k=2$ the growth of $x_1,x_2,\ldots$
  in Hindman's theorem has no bound that holds uniformly over the
  partitions $\mathbb{N}=C_1\cup C_2$, nor does it in the weakening with
  $\mathrm{FS}(X)$ replaced by $\mathrm{CFS}(X)$. Theorem 4.1
  (pp. 267--268): for any $\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ there is a
  partition $\mathbb{N}=C_1\cup C_2$ such that every infinite sequence
  $x_1<x_2<\cdots$ satisfies (1) for every sufficiently large $u$, $u\in C_2$ or
  $x_1+u\in C_2$; (2) $x_m+\cdots+x_n\in C_2$ for some $m\le2<n$; (3) if
  $\mathrm{CFS}(\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\})\subseteq C_2$ then $x_n>\varphi(n)$; (4)
  if $\mathrm{CFS}(\{x_1,x_2,\ldots\})\subseteq C_i$ for some $i$, then
  $i=2$ and $x_n>\varphi(n)$ for all $n$. The partition (p. 268), with
  $\varphi$ first taken strictly increasing: write $x=2^ry$ with $y$ odd,
  and put $x$ in $C_2$ if $y\ge\varphi(2^{r+1})$, in $C_1$ otherwise. Then
  (p. 268) the authors say they do not know whether
  some theorem stands to Hindman's theorem as Theorem 2.1 stands to
  Ramsey's, and Problem 4.2 asks, for some positive integer $k$, for a
  $\varphi$ such that every partition of $\mathbb{N}$ into $k+1$ classes has
  an infinite sequence whose finite sums miss a class and which has
  $x_n\le\varphi(n)$ for infinitely many $n$. Theorem 4.1 answers it
  negatively for $k=1$; of the next case they write, quoted, "We know
  nothing about the case $k=2$", and turn to the positive result that
  replacing FS with CFS allows. Theorem 4.3 (p. 268): for every $k$ there
  is $c>0$ such that any
  partition $\mathbb{N}=C_1\cup\cdots\cup C_k$ has $X\subseteq\mathbb{N}$
  with $\mathrm{CFS}(X)\subseteq C_i\cup C_j$ for some $i,j$ and
  $|X\cap\{1,\ldots,n\}|\ge c\log\log n$ for infinitely many $n$; proved
  (pp. 268--269) from Corollary 2.2 with $r=2$ applied to the coloring of
  $\{a,b\}$, $a<b$, by the class of $2^b-2^a$, taking
  $x_s=2^{a_{s+1}}-2^{a_s}$.
- References (p. 269), nine items, listed above.

## Compiled scope

consumes, with result pages for Theorem 4.1, Problem 4.2 and Theorem 4.3;
the proofs of Theorems 4.1 and 4.3 were followed on the page images. The
main results of § 2 and § 3 have result pages at statement depth: Theorem

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/ramsey_theory/E0948/_index|#948]]: the problem is the
paper's Problem 4.2 (printed p. 268, PDF p. 8): "Does there exist, for some
positive integer $k$, a function $\varphi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}$ such
that, for any partition of $\mathbb{N}$ into $k+1$ disjoint classes
$C_1,\ldots,C_{k+1}$, there is an infinite sequence $x_1<x_2<\cdots$ of
positive integers with $\mathrm{FS}(\{x_1,x_2,\ldots\})\cap C_i=\emptyset$
for some $i\in\{1,\ldots,k+1\}$ and $x_n\le\varphi(n)$ for infinitely many
$n$?" The site's $k$ colors are the paper's $k+1$ classes, its "$a_n<f(n)$"
the paper's "$x_n\le\varphi(n)$", and "does not contain all colours" the
paper's "$\cap\,C_i=\emptyset$ for some $i$"; the quantifier "for infinitely
many $n$" is printed here, where Erdős's 1977 wording of the same question
has none. Theorem 4.1 (pp. 267--268) is Galvin's coloring from the site's
commentary with its proof, and its clause (4) answers the two-class case
negatively in the strong form: under the partition, any sequence whose
consecutive sums lie in one class has $x_n>\varphi(n)$ for every $n$. The
paper leaves the question open from three classes on ("We know nothing about
the case $k=2$", p. 268) and asks it for finitely many classes only; the
$\aleph_0$ version of Erdős's 1977 paper does not appear. Theorem 4.3
(p. 268) is the interval-sums result the site quotes: two classes among $k$
for $\mathrm{CFS}(X)$ with $|X\cap\{1,\ldots,n\}|\ge c\log\log n$ for
infinitely many $n$, that is, $x_n<2^{2^{O(n)}}$ for infinitely many $n$. A
filing observation, not a review verdict: the site's commentary writes this
bound "for all $n$", while the printed theorem asserts it for infinitely
many $n$. Theorem 2.1 (p. 262), which the paper calls "the main result of
our paper" (p. 262), is context only: the paper poses Problem 4.2 as the
question whether some theorem stands to Hindman's theorem as Theorem 2.1
stands to Ramsey's, and the problem page states Theorem 2.1 as the paper's
main result. Corollary 2.2, its concrete form, is with $r=2$ the input to
Theorem 4.3.

**Results.**

- [[ramsey_theory/erdos_galvin_1991_some_ramsey_type_theorems/theorem_2_1|Theorem 2.1]]
  (p. 262): the main result; if $n\to(\varphi(n))^r_{k+1}$ for all large
  $n$, every $k$-coloring of $[\mathbb{N}]^r$ has $A$ with at most $2^{r-1}$
  colors on $[A]^r$ and $|A\cap\{1,\ldots,n\}|\ge\varphi(n)$ for
  infinitely many $n$.
- [[ramsey_theory/erdos_galvin_1991_some_ramsey_type_theorems/corollary_2_2|Corollary 2.2]]
  (p. 262): the same with $|A\cap\{1,\ldots,n\}|\ge c\log_{r-1}n$ for
  infinitely many $n$, $c$ depending only on $r$ and $k$.
- [[ramsey_theory/erdos_galvin_1991_some_ramsey_type_theorems/theorem_2_3|Theorem 2.3]]
  (pp. 262--263): a $2^{r-1}$-coloring under which every infinite set either
  shows all colors or has $a_n>\varphi(n)$ for all but finitely many $n$; so
  $2^{r-1}$ is best possible.
- [[ramsey_theory/erdos_galvin_1991_some_ramsey_type_theorems/theorem_3_1|Theorem 3.1]]
  (p. 266): a partition $[\mathbb{N}]^2=C_1\cup C_2$ under which every
  increasing path in one class lies in $C_2$ and has $x_n>\varphi(n)$ for
  all $n$.
- [[ramsey_theory/erdos_galvin_1991_some_ramsey_type_theorems/theorem_3_3|Theorem 3.3]]
  (p. 266): for $k\ge2$ colors, an increasing infinite path in at most two
  colors with $|V(P)\cap[1,n]|>n^{1/k}$ for infinitely many $n$.
- [[ramsey_theory/erdos_galvin_1991_some_ramsey_type_theorems/theorem_4_1|Theorem 4.1]]
  (pp. 267--268): Galvin's partition $\mathbb{N}=C_1\cup C_2$ by the odd
  part of $x=2^ry$ against $\varphi(2^{r+1})$, $\varphi$ taken strictly
  increasing; every sequence with monochromatic consecutive sums has
  $x_n>\varphi(n)$ for all $n$.
- [[ramsey_theory/erdos_galvin_1991_some_ramsey_type_theorems/problem_4_2|Problem 4.2]]
  (p. 268): the $(k+1)$-class question with $x_n\le\varphi(n)$ for
  infinitely many $n$; negative for $k=1$, unknown from $k=2$.
- [[ramsey_theory/erdos_galvin_1991_some_ramsey_type_theorems/theorem_4_3|Theorem 4.3]]
  (p. 268): $\mathrm{CFS}(X)$ in two of $k$ classes with
  $|X\cap\{1,\ldots,n\}|\ge c\log\log n$ for infinitely many $n$, from
  Corollary 2.2.
