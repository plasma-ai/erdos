## Statement

Notation as in
[[ramsey_theory/faudree_1993_conjecture_erdos_ramsey_number_r_w6/theorem_1|Theorem 1]];
$W_5=K_1+C_4$ is the wheel with five vertices and four spokes, and
$\chi(W_5)=3$ (p. 2).

**Theorem 3** (p. 2). $r(W_5)=15$.

The paper presents it as confirming the expectation $r(W_5)<r(K_4)$ for a
graph of chromatic number 3 (p. 2). It is not new: Table 1 (p. 3) lists
$r(W_5,W_5)=15$ without an asterisk, and the paper attributes the value to
Hendry's table, which covers most pairs of graphs with at most five vertices
(p. 3);
the paper re-verified it with its own search (p. 5).

**Source.** R. J. Faudree and B. D. McKay, A Conjecture of Erdős / the
Ramsey Number $r(W_6)$, Journal of Combinatorial Mathematics and
Combinatorial Computing 13 (1993), 23--31; reprint pages as identified in the
[[ramsey_theory/faudree_1993_conjecture_erdos_ramsey_number_r_w6/_index|source digest]]:
Theorem 3 on p. 2, Table 1 and the attribution on p. 3.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the statement and its attribution were read on
the page images. The computation was not rerun.

## Proof pointer

Prior literature (Hendry, J. Graph Theory 13 (1989), cited p. 3), re-verified
by the exhaustive search of Section 3; Table 5 (p. 7) counts the
$(W_5,W_5)$-free graphs by order up to 14.

## Dependencies

Hendry's table (cited p. 3); the computation of Section 3.
