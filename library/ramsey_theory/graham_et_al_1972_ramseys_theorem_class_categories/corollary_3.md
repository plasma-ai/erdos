## Statement

Notation as on the
[[ramsey_theory/graham_et_al_1972_ramseys_theorem_class_categories/theorem_1|Theorem 1 page]]
and the
[[ramsey_theory/graham_et_al_1972_ramseys_theorem_class_categories/corollary_2|Corollary 2 page]].
$C_1$ is the category, defined on p. 428, whose morphisms $k\to l$ are the
pairs $(w,\varphi)$ with $w\in A_1\otimes V_l$ and $\varphi$ a linear
monomorphism $V_k\to V_l$, where $A_1=\langle a_1\rangle$; the paper says
(p. 430) that its subobjects of an object $l$ can be considered to be affine
subspaces of $V_l$.

**Corollary 3 (Affine Analog)** (p. 430). For $C=C_1$,
$C(k;l_1,\ldots,l_r)$ is true in general.

## Proof pointer

P. 430. It is the case $m=1$ of the statement, proved for Corollary 2, that
$C_m(k;l_1,\ldots,l_r)$ holds in general for every $m$.

The paper adds two remarks on p. 430. Theorem 1 with $A=C_1$ and $B=C_0$ is
the known implication from the affine analog for $k$ to the vector space
analog for $k+1$. A second known argument, deriving $C_0(k;\ldots)$ from
$C_1(k;\ldots)$, is also a case of Theorem 1, with $C_0$ replaced by an
equivalent category shifted by one and with $t=0$.
