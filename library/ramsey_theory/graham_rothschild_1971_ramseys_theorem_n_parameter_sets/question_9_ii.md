**Source.** R. L. Graham and B. L. Rothschild, Ramsey's theorem for
$n$-parameter sets, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 159 (1971), 257--292; question
(ii) of Section 9, "Concluding remarks", on printed p. 291. The edition read
is identified in the
[[ramsey_theory/graham_rothschild_1971_ramseys_theorem_n_parameter_sets/_index|source digest]].

## Statement

**Question (ii)** (p. 291, quoted). "Are the various infinite versions of
certain of the corollaries valid? A specific simple case would be: If the
positive integers are 2-colored, is it true that there always exists an
infinite subset $A$ such that all sums $\sum_{b\in B}b$, $\varnothing\neq
B\subseteq A$, $B$-finite, have one color?"

The specific case is the infinite form of
[[ramsey_theory/graham_rothschild_1971_ramseys_theorem_n_parameter_sets/corollary_4|Corollary 4]]
for two colors: the finite corollary gives, for each $l$, $l$ integers whose
nonempty subset sums have one color, and the question asks for one infinite
set with that property. The paper states it as a question, not as a
conjecture; later sources call it the conjecture of Graham and Rothschild,
and Hindman (1974, p. 1) records that Erdős attributed it to them as a
conjecture.

**Answer.** Yes, for every finite number of colors: Hindman's theorem,
[[ramsey_theory/hindman_1974_finite_sums_sequences_within_cells_partition_n/theorem_3_1|Theorem 3.1]]
of Hindman 1974, with Baumgartner's short proof in
[[ramsey_theory/baumgartner_1974_short_proof_hindman_theorem/_index|Baumgartner 1974]].

page image of p. 291. Nothing here is independently reviewed.
