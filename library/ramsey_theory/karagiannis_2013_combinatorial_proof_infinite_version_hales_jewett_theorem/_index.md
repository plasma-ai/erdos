# A combinatorial proof of an infinite version of the Hales–Jewett theorem

[[ramsey_theory/_index|..]]

[[ramsey_theory/karagiannis_2013_combinatorial_proof_infinite_version_hales_jewett_theorem/theorem_2|theorem_2]]: For a finite alphabet, every finite coloring of the words over it admits an
infinite sequence of variable words all of whose ordered products, with
each variable replaced by a letter, have one color; the paper attributes
the theorem to Carlson and to Furstenberg and Katznelson and proves it
combinatorially from the Hales–Jewett theorem.

[[ramsey_theory/karagiannis_2013_combinatorial_proof_infinite_version_hales_jewett_theorem/theorem_3|theorem_3]]: For an increasing sequence of finite alphabets A_n with union A, every
finite coloring of the words over A admits an infinite sequence of variable
words t_n(x) such that all ordered products in which the factor t_m(x)
receives a letter of A_m have one color.

## Research digest

Karagiannis gives a combinatorial proof, resting on the classical
Hales–Jewett theorem (recalled as Theorem 1, p. 273), of the infinite
extension due to Carlson and, independently, Furstenberg and Katznelson
(Theorem 2, p. 274): for a finite alphabet $A$, every finite coloring of the
words over $A$, the empty word included, admits a sequence $(t_n(x))$ of
variable words such that all products $t_{m_0}(a_0)\cdots t_{m_n}(a_n)$ with
$m_0<\cdots<m_n$ and $a_i\in A$ have one color. Theorem 3 (p. 274) extends
this to an increasing sequence of finite alphabets $A_n$, with
$a_i\in A_{m_i}$; the paper notes that it is a consequence of a more general
result of Carlson and that a direct extension of Theorem 2 to one infinite
alphabet is false. Section 2 (pp. 275--279) proves Theorem 2 and Section 3
(pp. 280--289) Theorem 3; Remark 1 (p. 289) records a left-variable version
of Theorem 2 from the literature (Theorem 25) and leaves open whether the
method proves it.

The paper concerns colorings of words only; it states nothing about sums of
integers or dissociated sets. An application to E0774 would need its own
translation of variable words into additive relations, and none is recorded
here.
