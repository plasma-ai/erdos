# Canonizing partition theorems: Diversification, products, and iterated versions

[[ramsey_theory/_index|..]]

[[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_10|theorem_10]]: Voigt's abstract iterated canonizing theorem: in a locally finite category
with conditions (A), (B), (C), for an object B with finitely many objects
mapping into it, any coloring of all morphisms into a large A becomes, on
a copy of B, canonical on each level and either disjoint or comparable
through the chosen attribute functions across levels.

[[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_11|theorem_11]]: Voigt's canonizing functor theorem: under a full and dense functor Phi, the
attribute functions of a canonizing set that are constant on the fibres of
Phi push forward to attribute functions satisfying the canonizing condition
(CAN) in the image category.

[[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_13|theorem_13]]: The category of finite-dimensional vector spaces over a fixed finite field
GF(q) has the diversification property: every canonizing set M(k,m) of the
vector-space canonization theorem has it, by Voigt's general
diversification theorem.

[[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_14|theorem_14]]: The q-analogue of the iterated Erdős–Rado theorem: a coloring by natural
numbers of all subspaces of GF(q)^n, n large in terms of m, has an
m-dimensional subspace and attribute functions m(k) in M(k,m), one per
dimension k <= m, such that for every pair of dimensions the colors are
either disjoint or compared through the attribute functions.

[[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_15|theorem_15]]: The q-analogue of Voigt's canonizing theorem for injections: a coloring of
the rank-k matrices of size k by n over GF(q), n large, has an
m-dimensional subspace A and, for each invertible k by k matrix T, an
attribute function m^T in M(k,m) such that colors of A B S and A C T are
either always different or equal exactly when m^S(B) = m^T(C).

[[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_16|theorem_16]]: Voigt's factorization theorem: a lattice embedding of a product of
subspace lattices L_{q_i}(m_i), with m_i > 2 and the q_i powers of distinct
primes, into a larger such product acts coordinatewise, by an embedding on
each matching factor and by a constant on each extra factor.

[[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_18|theorem_18]]: For a product of subspace lattices L_{q_i}(m_i), the q_i powers of
mutually distinct primes and every m_i > 2, Voigt shows that the products
of the attribute functions constant, one-to-one and rank form a canonizing
set for colorings of points under lattice embeddings.

[[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_3|theorem_3]]: Voigt's product version of the Erdős–Graham canonization theorem: every
coloring of the t-fold grid {0,...,n-1}^t by natural numbers, n large in
terms of t and m, becomes on some product of t m-term arithmetic
progressions, with possibly different differences, a coloring that records
exactly the positions in a fixed set J of coordinates.

[[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_4|theorem_4]]: For any two one-to-one maps from {0,...,n-1} to the natural numbers, n
large in terms of m, some m-term arithmetic progression carries either
identical values of the two maps or disjoint sets of values.

[[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_5|theorem_5]]: For any two colorings of the k-subsets of {0,...,n-1} by natural numbers, n
large in terms of k and m, some m-set X and index sets J_0, J_1 make the two
colorings on the k-subsets of X either use disjoint sets of colors or agree
on A and B exactly when the J_0-subset of A equals the J_1-subset of B.

[[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_6|theorem_6]]: Voigt's iterated Erdős–Rado theorem: a coloring by natural numbers of all
subsets of {0,...,n-1} of size at most m, n large in terms of m, has an
m-set X and patterns J_k such that, for every pair of sizes k <= l, the
colors on the k- and l-subsets of X are either disjoint or compared
through the J_k- and J_l-subsets.

[[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_8|theorem_8]]: Voigt's canonizing product theorem: if M is a canonizing set of attribute
functions in a locally finite category where the object C has the
partition property, and M-hat is a finite canonizing set with the
diversification property, then the products of their attribute functions
form a canonizing set for the product category.

[[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_9_p355|theorem_9_p355]]: Voigt's canonizing theorem for arbitrary injections between finite sets,
printed as the first of two theorems labelled 9: a coloring of the
injections from a k-set into an n-set, n large, is canonical on some m-set,
each ordering pattern of a k-subset being compared through a subset h(s).

[[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_9_p364|theorem_9_p364]]: Voigt's diversification theorem, printed as the second of two theorems
labelled 9: a locally finite category that is completely hereditary
canonizing, upper bound Ramsey and has amalgamated unions has the
diversification property for every one of its canonizing sets.

## Research digest

Voigt sets up an abstract framework for canonizing partition theorems, built
on attribute functions and diversification, and uses it to derive a
canonizing product theorem generalizing results of Rado for Ramsey's theorem,
canonizing product theorems for arithmetic progressions and finite geometric
arguesian lattices, and iterated versions of the Erdős–Rado canonization
theorem and of its $q$-analogue for finite vector spaces (abstract, p. 349).
Instead of finding only a monochromatic substructure, one passes
to a structured subsystem on which a coloring with arbitrarily many colors
induces one of a controlled list of canonical equivalence relations.

**Labels.** The print has two theorems labelled 9 and none labelled 7: the
canonizing theorem for injections (p. 355) and the diversification theorem
of Section 2 (p. 364). The canonizing product theorem is printed as
Theorem 8 (p. 361) and cited as Theorem 8 on p. 351 but as Theorem 7 on
p. 370. The result pages below take the printed labels, adding the page to
the two Theorems 9.

Read status: claims checked for the results linked below, statements read
clause by clause on the printed pages; no proof is checked step by step.
Theorems 1, 2, 12 and 17 are quoted from earlier work (Erdős–Rado, Rado, and
the author's references [24] and [25]) and are recorded only where a page
uses them.

**Results.**

- [[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_3|Theorem 3 (pp. 351--352)]]: product version of the
  Erdős–Graham canonization theorem for $t$ arithmetic progressions, with
  possibly different differences.
- [[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_4|Theorem 4 (p. 352)]]: two one-to-one maps on
  $\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$ agree, or have disjoint images, on some $m$-term
  progression.
- [[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_5|Theorem 5 (p. 353)]]: diversification for $k$-subsets,
  with the two unnumbered Lemmas of pp. 353--354.
- [[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_6|Theorem 6 (p. 355)]]: iterated Erdős–Rado canonization
  theorem for all subsets of size at most $m$.
- [[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_9_p355|Theorem 9 (p. 355)]]: canonizing theorem for
  injections between finite sets.
- [[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_8|Theorem 8 (p. 361)]]: the canonizing product theorem, with
  the definitions of attribute functions, canonizing sets and the
  diversification property.
- [[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_9_p364|Theorem 9 (p. 364)]]: a locally finite category with
  conditions (A), (B), (C) has the diversification property; with Lemmas 2
  and 3.
- [[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_10|Theorem 10 (pp. 364--365)]]: the abstract iterated
  canonizing theorem.
- [[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_11|Theorem 11 (p. 365)]]: the canonizing functor theorem.
- [[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_13|Theorem 13 (p. 370)]]: finite vector spaces over $GF(q)$
  have the diversification property.
- [[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_14|Theorem 14 (pp. 370--371)]]: iterated canonization for
  subspaces of $GF(q)^n$, the $q$-analogue of Theorem 6.
- [[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_15|Theorem 15 (p. 371)]]: canonizing theorem for linear
  embeddings, the $q$-analogue of Theorem 9 (p. 355).
- [[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_16|Theorem 16 (p. 374)]]: embeddings between products of
  linear lattices act factor by factor.
- [[ramsey_theory/voigt_1985_canonizing_partition_theorems_diversification_products_iterated_versions/theorem_18|Theorem 18 (pp. 375--376)]]: the products of
  {constant, one-to-one, rank} form a canonizing set for points of a product
  of linear lattices $\mathcal L_{q_i}(m_i)$, $q_i$ powers of distinct primes
  and $m_i>2$.
