# ramsey_theory/zhang_2017_some_values_ramsey_numbers_c_4_versus_stars

[[ramsey_theory/_index|..]]

[[ramsey_theory/zhang_2017_some_values_ramsey_numbers_c_4_versus_stars/lemma_2|lemma_2]]: Zhang, Chen and Cheng's parity upper bound R(C_4, K_{1,(l+1)l-t}) <= (l+1)^2 - t
for every even l at least 4 and t = 2, 4, ..., l - 2, one below Parsons's
general bound at these n, with no prime-power hypothesis.

[[ramsey_theory/zhang_2017_some_values_ramsey_numbers_c_4_versus_stars/question_p76|question_p76]]: Zhang, Chen and Cheng's 2017 question whether R(C_4, K_{1,n}) equals
n + floor(sqrt(n-1)) + 1 or n + floor(sqrt(n-1)) + 2 for every n at least
2, posed from the known values, with the remark that an affirmative answer
would refute Burr et al.'s Conjecture 1.

[[ramsey_theory/zhang_2017_some_values_ramsey_numbers_c_4_versus_stars/theorem_6|theorem_6]]: Zhang, Chen and Cheng's exact values R(C_4, K_{1,(q-1)^2+t}) = (q-1)^2 + q + t
for even prime powers q at least 4 and t = 1, 0, -2, from the C_4-free graph
Gamma_q on q^2 - 1 vertices with q - 1 - t vertices deleted.

[[ramsey_theory/zhang_2017_some_values_ramsey_numbers_c_4_versus_stars/theorem_7|theorem_7]]: Zhang, Chen and Cheng's exact values R(C_4, K_{1,q(q-1)-t}) = q^2 - t for
odd prime powers q at least 5 and even t from 2 to 2 ceil(q/4), from the
C_4-free graph Gamma_q with t vertices deleted and a parity upper bound.

## Contents

- Abstract and § 1, Introduction (pp. 73--76, page images). Notation: $C_m$
  the cycle of length $m$, $K_{1,n}$ "a star of order $n+1$", $W_n$ the
  wheel obtained from $C_n$ and a new vertex joined to all of it;
  $R(G_1,G_2)$ the smallest $N$ such that every graph $G$ of order $N$ has
  $G_1\subseteq G$ or $G_2\subseteq\overline G$; a $(G_1,G_2)$-Ramsey graph
  is a graph on $R(G_1,G_2)-1$ vertices with neither. Recalled results, each
  quoted with its attribution: Theorem 1 (p. 74, Parsons [6])
  "$R(C_4,K_{1,n})\le n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+2$ for all $n\ge2$, and if
  $n=\ell^2+1$ and $\ell\ge1$, then
  $R(C_4,K_{1,n})\le n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+1$"; Theorem 2 (p. 74,
  Parsons [6]) "Let $q$ be a prime power. Then $R(C_4,K_{1,q^2+1})=q^2+q+2$
  and $R(C_4,K_{1,q^2})=q^2+q+1$"; Theorem 3 (p. 74, Burr et al. [2])
  "$R(C_4,K_{1,n})>n+\lfloor\sqrt n-6n^{11/40}\rfloor$ for sufficiently
  large $n$", stated without the prime-gap hypothesis under which that paper
  proves it; Conjecture 1 (p. 74, Burr et al. [2])
  "$R(C_4,K_{1,n})<n+\sqrt n-c$ holds infinitely often, where $c$ is an
  arbitrary constant", for which the paper records Erdős's prize for a
  proof or disproof; Theorem 4 (p. 75, Parsons [5], the 1976 Aequationes
  Math. paper "Graphs from projective planes")
  "$R(C_4,K_{1,q^2-t})=q^2+q-(t-1)$ if $q$ is an even prime power,
  $1\le t\le q+1$ and $t\ne q$, or $q$ is an odd prime power,
  $0\le t\le2\lceil\frac q4\rceil$ and $t$ is even"; Theorem 5 (p. 75, Zhang
  et al. [10]) "Let $q$ be an odd prime power,
  $1\le t\le2\lceil\frac q4\rceil$ and $t\ne2\lceil\frac q4\rceil-1$. Then
  $R(C_4,K_{1,q^2-t})=q^2+q-(t-1)$." The paper notes that all these values
  are $n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+1$ when $n=q^2+1$ and
  $n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+2$ otherwise, that every such $n$ lies near
  the square of a prime power, and that, Conjecture 1 being open, it turns
  to values of $R(C_4,K_{1,n})$ for $n$ of another kind. The main results
  (p. 75), quoted in full: Theorem 6, "Let $q\ge4$ be an even prime power and
  $t=1,0,-2$. Then $R(C_4,K_{1,(q-1)^2+t})=(q-1)^2+q+t$." Theorem 7, "Let
  $q\ge5$ be an odd prime power, $t=2,4,\ldots,2\lceil\frac q4\rceil$. Then
  $R(C_4,K_{1,q(q-1)-t})=q^2-t$." The paragraph after them (p. 75) places
  the values on the two lines: in Theorem 6, $n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+1$
  for $t=1$ and $n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+2$ for $t=0,-2$; in Theorem 7,
  $n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+1$ for every $n=q(q-1)-t$ except $q=5$, $t=4$,
  $n=16$, where $R(C_4,K_{1,16})=21=n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+2$. The
  summary of known values for $2\le n\le50$ (pp. 75--76): $n=2,3$ from
  Chvátal and Harary [11]; $n=4,5,9,10,16,17,25,26,49,50$ from Theorem 2;
  $6\le n\le20$, $34\le n\le36$ and $n=43$ from the computer determinations
  of $R(C_4,W_n)$ by Tse [7], Dybizbański and Dzido [3] and Wu et al. [8],
  through $R(C_4,W_n)=R(C_4,K_{1,n})$ for $n\ge6$ (Zhang, Broersma and Chen
  [9]); $n=21,23,24,45,47,48$ from Theorems 4 and 5; and from Theorem 7,
  $R(C_4,K_{1,40})=7^2-2=47$ and $R(C_4,K_{1,38})=7^2-4=45$, the two points
  marked "×" in Fig. 1 (p. 76), which plots the known values against
  $f(n)=n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+2$ and
  $g(n)=n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+1$. The question (p. 76): every value the
  paper lists as known with $n\ge2$ is $n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+1$ or
  $n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+2$, and the paper asks, quoted, "whether
  $R(C_4,K_{1,n})=n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+1$ or
  $n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+2$ for all $n\ge2$", adding that an
  affirmative answer would refute Conjecture 1. A filing observation: for
  every integer $n\ge2$, $\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+1=\lceil\sqrt n\rceil$,
  so the paper's two lines are the site's $n+\lceil\sqrt n\rceil$ and
  $n+\lceil\sqrt n\rceil+1$.
- § 2, Graph $\Gamma_q$ and its properties (pp. 76--79; p. 78 on the page
  image, the rest in the text layer). Construction: for a prime power $q$,
  $\widehat\Gamma_q$ has vertex set $F_q^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}$ with $(a,b)$
  and $(x,y)$ adjacent if and only if $ax-by=1$, and $\Gamma_q$ is
  $\widehat\Gamma_q$ with its loops deleted. Proposition 1 (the solution
  sets of two linear equations $a_ix-b_iy=1$ over $F_q$); Proposition 2
  ($(a,b)$ has degree $q-1$ if $a^2-b^2=1$ and $q$ otherwise); Proposition
  3 ($\Gamma_q$ contains no $C_4$); Proposition 4 (no two
  $(q-1)$-vertices are adjacent); Proposition 5 (the $(q-1)$-vertices are
  $N((1,1))$, $q$ of them, for even $q$, and $q-1$ of them for odd $q$).
  Closing paragraph (p. 78): the propositions make $\Gamma_q$ a $C_4$-free
  graph on $q^2-1$ vertices with $\delta(\Gamma_q)=q-1$ and
  $\Delta(\Gamma_q)=q$, so $\Delta(\overline{\Gamma_q})=q(q-1)-1$ and
  $R(C_4,K_{1,q(q-1)})\ge q^2$, while Theorem 1 gives
  $R(C_4,K_{1,q(q-1)})\le q^2+1$; the value is therefore $q^2$ or $q^2+1$,
  and $\Gamma_q$ is a $(C_4,K_{1,q(q-1)})$-Ramsey graph for $q=2,3,5$ but
  not for $q=4$, where the value is $q^2+1$ (p. 79, from Fig. 1).
- § 3, Some lemmas (pp. 79--81, text layer). Lemma 1 (deleting a set $A$
  from $\Gamma_q$ that meets each outside $q$-vertex's neighborhood in at
  most one vertex and no outside $(q-1)$-vertex's neighborhood leaves
  minimum degree at least $q-1$). Lemma 2 (p. 79), quoted: "Let $\ell\ge4$
  be even and $t=2,4,\ldots,\ell-2$. Then
  $R(C_4,K_{1,(\ell+1)\ell-t})\le(\ell+1)^2-t$", proved by showing that a
  $C_4$-free graph on $(\ell+1)^2-t$ vertices with minimum degree at least
  $\ell+1$ would be $(\ell+1)$-regular, an odd degree on an odd number of
  vertices; its Remark restates it as
  $R(C_4,K_{1,n})\le n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+1$ for $n=\ell(\ell+1)-t$.
  Lemma 3 (when two of the systems $y-x=g^k$, $y+x=g^{-k}$ share their
  $x$-coordinate) and Lemma 4 (the number of $a\in F_q$ with $1+a^2$ a
  square is $(q-1)/2$ for $q\equiv3\pmod4$ and $(q+1)/2$ for $q\equiv1\pmod4$).
- § 4, Proof of main results (pp. 81--84, text layer). Theorem 6: the upper
  bound is Theorem 1; for the lower bound, Claims 1--3 delete from
  $\Gamma_q$ ($q$ even) the sets $A_{q-2}=\{(x,x):x\ne1\}$,
  $A_{q-1}=\{(1,y):y\ne1\}$ and $A_{q+1}=N[(1,1)]$, of sizes $q-2$, $q-1$
  and $q+1$, leaving $C_4$-free graphs on $(q-1)^2+q+t-1$ vertices with
  minimum degree at least $q-1$, hence complements without
  $K_{1,(q-1)^2+t}$. Theorem 7: for $q=5$ the values $R(C_4,K_{1,18})=23$
  and $R(C_4,K_{1,16})=21$ are taken from Fig. 1; for $q\ge7$ the set
  $A=\{(0,a):a\in NS\}$, $NS$ the set of $a\in F_q$ with $1+a^2$ a
  non-square, joined by the two square roots of $-1$ when $q\equiv1\pmod4$,
  of size $2\lceil q/4\rceil$ by Lemma 4, has pairwise disjoint
  neighborhoods (Claim 4) missing every $(q-1)$-vertex (Claim 5), so
  deleting any $t$ of its vertices leaves a $C_4$-free graph on $q^2-1-t$
  vertices with $\Delta(\overline{G_t})\le q(q-1)-1-t$, giving
  $R(C_4,K_{1,q(q-1)-t})\ge q^2-t$, and Lemma 2 with $\ell=q-1$ gives the
  matching upper bound since $2\lceil q/4\rceil\le q-3$ for $q\ge7$.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/ramsey_theory/E0552/_index|#552]]:
Theorems 6 and 7 (printed p. 75, PDF p. 3) are this paper's exact families:
"Let $q\ge4$ be an even prime power and $t=1,0,-2$. Then
$R(C_4,K_{1,(q-1)^2+t})=(q-1)^2+q+t$" and "Let $q\ge5$ be an odd prime
power, $t=2,4,\ldots,2\lceil\frac q4\rceil$. Then
$R(C_4,K_{1,q(q-1)-t})=q^2-t$", with the paper's own placement of every
value on the lines $n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+1$ and
$n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+2$ (p. 75), which are $n+\lceil\sqrt n\rceil$
and $n+\lceil\sqrt n\rceil+1$. The site (page last edited 1 February 2026)
names no family of the paper: it refers to it and to [ZCC17b], with the
papers of Parsons and of Wu et al., for a precise description of the
extensions of Parsons's values, which it places at $n=q^2\pm t$ with $q$ a
prime power and $0\le t\le q$; neither theorem fits that description, since
Theorem 7's new values at $n=40$ and $38$ ($q=7$) and Theorem 6's values at
$n=226$, $225$ and $223$ ($q=16$) lie at no such $n$. The speculation
the site reports is the question of printed p. 76 (PDF p. 4): "Our question
is whether $R(C_4,K_{1,n})=n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+1$ or
$n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+2$ for all $n\ge2$", with the remark that an
affirmative answer would refute Conjecture 1, where Conjecture 1 (p. 74) is
the problem's displayed inequality in the form "$R(C_4,K_{1,n})<n+\sqrt n-c$
holds infinitely often, where $c$ is an arbitrary constant", attributed to
Burr et al. The paper poses the question and does not answer it; it settles
nothing the problem leaves open. Lemma 2 (printed p. 79) is an upper bound
on the same problem: $R(C_4,K_{1,(\ell+1)\ell-t})\le(\ell+1)^2-t$ for every
even $\ell\ge4$ and $t=2,4,\ldots,\ell-2$, which the Remark (p. 80) writes
as $R(C_4,K_{1,n})\le n+\lfloor\sqrt{n-1}\rfloor+1$ for
$n=(\ell+1)\ell-t$, that is $n+\lceil\sqrt n\rceil$, one below
Parsons's general bound at those $n$; it gives no value by itself. The problem page reads the theorems on the
page image at statement depth; no proof was read.

**Results.**

- [[ramsey_theory/zhang_2017_some_values_ramsey_numbers_c_4_versus_stars/theorem_6|Theorem 6]]

  $\ell\ge4$ and $t=2,4,\ldots,\ell-2$, no prime-power hypothesis.
