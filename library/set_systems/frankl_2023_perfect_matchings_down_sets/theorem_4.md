## Statement

**Setting** (p. 2). The Kneser graph $KG(\mathcal F)$ of a family
$\mathcal F$ has vertex set $\mathcal F$, two members being joined when they
are disjoint.

**Theorem 4** (Berge, p. 2). Let $\mathcal B$ be a down-set. Then one of the
following holds:

- (i) $|\mathcal B|$ is even and $KG(\mathcal B)$ has a perfect matching;
- (ii) $|\mathcal B|$ is odd and $KG(\mathcal B\setminus\{\emptyset\})$ has a
  perfect matching.

The paper attributes Theorem 4 to an unpublished 1980 manuscript of Berge (its
reference [1]) and proves it at the end of Section 3, restated as **Theorem
13** (p. 7): a down-set $\mathcal A\subset2^{[n]}$ "can be matched to itself",
meaning that $\mathcal A$ or $\mathcal A\setminus\{\emptyset\}$ can be
partitioned into pairs of disjoint sets.

**Theorem 3** (p. 2). If $\mathcal B\subset2^{[n]}$ is a down-set and
$\mathcal F\subset\mathcal B$ is intersecting, then

$$
|\mathcal F|\le|\mathcal B|/2. \tag{4}
$$

The paper proves (4) from the non-uniform Erdős–Ko–Rado bound
$|\mathcal F|\le2^{n-1}$ for intersecting $\mathcal F\subset2^{[n]}$ (its
Theorem 1, p. 1) and the Harris–Kleitman inequality (its Theorem 2, p. 2),
calls it a weaker result than Chvátal's conjecture, and notes that Berge's
Theorem 4 implies it. The reason, an observation of this page: an
intersecting family takes at most one set from each disjoint pair and never
the empty set.

## Proof pointer

Theorem 3, p. 2: the up-set $\mathcal A=\mathcal F^\uparrow$ generated by
$\mathcal F$ is intersecting, so $|\mathcal A|\le2^{n-1}$, and Harris–Kleitman
applied to $\mathcal A\cap\mathcal B\supset\mathcal F$ gives (4).

Theorem 13, pp. 7--8, by induction on $n$. Split $\mathcal A$ into the
traces $\mathcal A(n)\subset\mathcal A(\bar n)\subset2^{[n-1]}$ of its
members containing and avoiding $n$, both down-sets, and match each to itself
by induction. Observation 1 (p. 7): the union of two matchings is a bipartite
graph, so the pairs of the first matching can be oriented so that their first
members form an independent set $\mathcal I$ in the union. Adding $n$ to the
second member of each first-matching pair and to the members of
second-matching pairs that lie in $\mathcal I$ gives disjoint pairs covering
nearly all of $\mathcal A$; the parity cases of $|\mathcal A(n)|$ and
$|\mathcal A(\bar n)|$ are closed by pairing $\{n\}$ with $\emptyset$ or with a
maximal set of $\mathcal A(\bar n)\setminus\mathcal A(n)$ (p. 8).

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/set_systems/E0701/_index|Problem 701]]: Theorem 3, which
  Theorem 4 also implies, bounds every intersecting subfamily of a down-set
  $\mathcal B$ by $|\mathcal B|/2$. The paper calls this weaker than
  Chvátal's conjecture (the problem's corrected Statement), whose bound is the
  largest degree $\Delta(\mathcal B)$; for a down-set
  $\Delta(\mathcal B)\le|\mathcal B|/2$, since removing $x$ maps the members
  containing $x$ injectively to members avoiding it (an observation of this
  page).
