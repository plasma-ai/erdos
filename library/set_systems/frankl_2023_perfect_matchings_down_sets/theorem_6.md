## Statement

**Setting** (pp. 2--3). For $\mathcal F\subset2^X$, $\mathcal F^\downarrow$
is the down-set generated by $\mathcal F$, the sets contained in some member
of $\mathcal F$; it does not depend on $X$ (p. 2). Families $\mathcal F$ and
$\mathcal G$ are cross-intersecting when $A\cap B\ne\emptyset$ for all
$A\in\mathcal F$, $B\in\mathcal G$ (p. 3).

**Theorem 6** (p. 3). If $\mathcal F,\mathcal G\subset2^{[n]}$ are
cross-intersecting, then

$$
|\mathcal G|+|\mathcal F|\le\max\{|\mathcal F^\downarrow|,|\mathcal G^\downarrow|\}. \tag{5}
$$

The paper notes (p. 3) that when $\mathcal F$ and $\mathcal G$ are moreover
both union families (no two members have union $[n]$), so are
$\mathcal F^\downarrow$ and $\mathcal G^\downarrow$, the right side of (5) is
then at most $2^{n-1}$, and $\mathcal F=\mathcal G$ gives the bound
$2^{n-2}$ for IU families of
[[set_systems/frankl_2023_perfect_matchings_down_sets/theorem_9|Theorem 8]].

## Proof pointer

Section 3, p. 5. Assume $|\mathcal F^\downarrow|\le|\mathcal G^\downarrow|$.
[[set_systems/frankl_2023_perfect_matchings_down_sets/theorem_5|Theorem 5]]
matches $\mathcal F^\downarrow$ into $\mathcal G^\downarrow$ by disjoint
pairs. Cross-intersection lets at most one set of each pair lie in its
family ($\mathcal F$ or $\mathcal G$), which gives
$|\mathcal F|+|\mathcal G|\le|\mathcal G^\downarrow|$.
