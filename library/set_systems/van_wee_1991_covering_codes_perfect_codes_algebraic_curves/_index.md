## Covering-radius conventions

For an alphabet $Q$ of size $q\ge2$, the Hamming space is $Q^n$, and a code is
a nonempty subset. Its covering radius is the exact maximum

$$
\operatorname{CR}(C)=\max_{x\in Q^n}d(x,C),
\qquad d(x,C)=\min_{c\in C}d(x,c).
$$

The introduction defines $K_q(n,R)$ as the least size of a $q$-ary code of
length $n$ with $\operatorname{CR}(C)=R$ (printed p. 4; PDF p. 12). Chapter 1
likewise calls a binary code an $(n,M)R$ code when its covering radius is
exactly $R$ and defines $K(n,R)$ from that notation (printed p. 34; PDF p. 42).
The statements below retain the notation printed in their respective sections.

## Selected binary covering bounds

For $n\in\mathbb N$ and $R\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}$, Chapter 1 defines
$V_2(n,R)$ on printed p. 33 (PDF p. 41), and Theorem 1 (printed p. 34; PDF p.
42) gives the binary sphere bound

$$
K(n,R)\ge\frac{2^n}{V_2(n,R)},
\qquad V_2(n,R)=\sum_{i=0}^{R}\binom ni.
$$

Theorem 1 adds: "Equality holds in (6) if and only if a perfect
$R$-error-correcting code of length $n$ exists" (printed p. 34), where (6) is
the displayed bound. Theorem 2 on the same page records

$$
K(R,R)=1\quad(R=1,2,\ldots),
$$

$$
K(n,R)=2\quad(n,R\in\mathbb N,\ R+1\le n\le2R+1),
$$

and $K(n,0)=2^n$ for $n\in\mathbb N$.

For $n_1,n_2\in\mathbb N$ and
$R_i\in\{0,1,\ldots,n_i\}$, Theorem 3 (printed p. 35; PDF p. 43) gives

$$
K(n_1+n_2,R_1+R_2)
\le K(n_1,R_1)K(n_2,R_2).
$$

Its displayed special case is

$$
K(n+1,R)\le2K(n,R)
\qquad(n\in\mathbb N,\ 0\le R\le n).
$$

## Taussky–Todd group formulation

The introduction quotes Taussky and Todd's problem (printed p. 12; PDF p.
20): "Let $G$ be an abelian group with $n$ base elements $g_1,g_2,\ldots,g_n$,
each of order $p$ (not necessarily a prime). Let $S$ denote the set of the
$\nu=n(p-1)+1$ distinct powers of the base elements." The count $\nu$ takes
the identity once, together with the $p-1$ nonidentity powers of each base
element. Their Problem 1 asks for the least integer $\sigma=\sigma(n,p)$ for
which some set $H\subseteq G$ of $\sigma$ elements satisfies

$$
G=HS,
$$

where $HS$ is the set of all products $hs$ with $h\in H$ and $s\in S$.

## Normal binary codes

Let $C\subseteq\mathbb F_2^n$ have covering radius
$R=\operatorname{CR}(C)$. For $a\in\mathbb F_2$, its unpunctured coordinate
slice is

$$
C_a^{(i)}=\{c\in C:c_i=a\}\subseteq\mathbb F_2^n.
$$

Printed p. 75 (PDF p. 83) defines

$$
N^{(i)}(C)=
\max_{x\in\mathbb F_2^n}
\bigl(d(x,C_0^{(i)})+d(x,C_1^{(i)})\bigr),
$$

with the convention $d(x,\varnothing)=n$. Coordinate $i$ is *acceptable* if
$N^{(i)}(C)\le2R+1$, and $C$ is *normal* if it has at least one acceptable
coordinate.

The punctured slice $C_a^{(i)\prime}\subseteq\mathbb F_2^{n-1}$ is obtained
by deleting coordinate $i$ from every word in $C_a^{(i)}$. In this chapter,
optimal means $M=K(n,R)$. Chapter 4, Theorem 1 (printed p. 76; PDF p. 84)
says that if $C$ is an optimal binary $(n,M)R$ code with $R=1$ and $n\ge3$,
then for every coordinate $i$ and $a\in\mathbb F_2$,

$$
C_a^{(i)\prime}\ne\varnothing
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\operatorname{CR}\bigl(C_a^{(i)\prime}\bigr)\le2.
$$

Corollary 1 (printed p. 79; PDF p. 87) concludes that every optimal binary
radius-one code is normal and every coordinate is acceptable. Chapter 4,
Theorem 2 on that page gives a broader binary criterion: if $|C|>1$ and

$$
d_{\min}(C)\ge2\operatorname{CR}(C),
$$

then $C$ is normal and every coordinate is acceptable.

## Binary/ternary radius-one bound

Let $t,b$ be nonnegative integer coordinate counts. The premise of the result
below has covering radius one, so it forces $t+b>0$. Put

$$
\mathcal H=\mathbb F_3^t\times\mathbb F_2^b.
$$

The mixed-space notation is introduced on printed p. 11 (PDF p. 19), and
Chapter 5, Section V restates this space on printed p. 96 (PDF p. 104).
Theorem 16 (printed p. 97; PDF p. 105) says that a code
$C\subseteq\mathcal H$ with exact covering radius
$\operatorname{CR}(C)=1$ satisfies

$$
|C|\ge
\frac{(2t+b)3^t2^b}
{(2t+b)(1+2t+b)-b}
\qquad\text{if $b$ is even},
$$

and

$$
|C|\ge
\frac{(2t+b)3^t2^b}
{(2t+b)(1+2t+b)-2t}
\qquad\text{if $b$ is odd}.
$$

The surrounding text says that this section treats only radius-one coverings
in detail; higher-radius generalizations are attributed to separate joint
work.
