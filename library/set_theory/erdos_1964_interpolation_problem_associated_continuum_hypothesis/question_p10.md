## Statement

**Question** (p. 10, unnumbered, quoted). "Can one construct a family of
distinct entire functions $f_\alpha$ $(1\leq\alpha<\Omega_{\mathfrak c})$ such
that for every $z$ the set $\{f_\alpha(z)\}$ has power less than
$\mathfrak c$?"

Erdős says he is unable to decide it. He notes that the construction is
possible if $\mathfrak c=\aleph_1$, by the second part of
[[set_theory/erdos_1964_interpolation_problem_associated_continuum_hypothesis/theorem_p9|the theorem]],
whose value sets are countable, and that for $\mathfrak c>\aleph_1$ his proof
breaks down. He adds that Cohen's recent proof of the independence of the
continuum hypothesis gives the problem added interest.

**Source.** P. Erdős, An interpolation problem associated with the continuum
hypothesis, Michigan Math. J. 11 (1964), 9--10, doi:10.1307/mmj/1028999028,
p. 10; the edition read is named on the
[[set_theory/erdos_1964_interpolation_problem_associated_continuum_hypothesis/_index|source card]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the question was read clause by clause on the
page image of p. 10. Nothing here is independently reviewed.

## Proof pointer

None: the paper poses the question without an answer.

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/set_theory/E1119/_index|Problem 1119]]: the question
  bounds each value set below $\mathfrak c$ rather than by a fixed cardinal.
  When $\mathfrak c=\mathfrak m^+$ with $\mathfrak m>\aleph_0$, power less than
  $\mathfrak c$ means at most $\mathfrak m$, and a family as asked is a family
  of more than $\mathfrak m$ entire functions with at most $\mathfrak m$ values
  at each point, the problem's case $\mathfrak m^+=\mathfrak c$ answered no.
  The paper decides nothing in that case; the answers are on the problem page.
