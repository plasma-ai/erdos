## Statement

**Theorem (Baumgartner–Hajnal [2])** (printed p. 271). Let $\Phi$ be an order
type with $\Phi\to(\omega)^1_{\aleph_0}$. Then $\Phi\to(\alpha)^2_k$ for every
$\alpha<\omega_1$ and every $k<\omega$.

The hypothesis $\Phi\to(\omega)^1_{\aleph_0}$ says that whenever a set of type
$\Phi$ is split into countably many pieces, some piece contains a subset of
type $\omega$. The paper's reference [2] is Baumgartner and Hajnal, A proof
(involving Martin's axiom) of a partition relation, Calgary Research Paper
122 (1971), then in print in Fund. Math.

The survey draws two consequences on p. 271: $\omega_1\to(\alpha)^2_k$ and
$\lambda\to(\alpha)^2_k$ for $\alpha<\omega_1$, $k<\omega$, where $\lambda$, which
the survey does not define, is the order type of the real line. It says these
give a positive answer to Problem 10 with $\rho=0$ and to Problems 10/A, 11
and 11/A of that list.

On p. 272 it reports, without proof, F. Galvin's combinatorial proof and his
extension to partial order types: if $\varphi\to(\eta)^1_{\aleph_0}$ then
$\varphi\to(\alpha)^2_k$ and $\varphi\to(\eta,(\alpha^*\vee\alpha)_k)^2$ for
$\alpha<\omega_1$, $k<\omega$.

**Source.** P. Erdős and A. Hajnal, Unsolved and solved problems in set
theory, Proceedings of Symposia in Pure Mathematics 25 (1974), 269--287;
printed pp. 271--272 (PDF pp. 3--4 of the Rényi archive scan identified on the
[[set_theory/erdos_1974_unsolved_solved_problems_set_theory/_index|source card]]).

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the theorem, its stated consequences and
Galvin's extension were read clause by clause on the page images. The survey
gives no proof.

## Proof pointer

None in this survey. It describes the proof in [2] as metamathematical: the
theorem is first proved under $\mathrm{MA}_{|\Phi|}$ and then obtained in
general by absoluteness arguments (p. 271).
