## Statement

**Theorem (Hajnal [15])** (printed p. 273). Assume G.C.H. and that
$\aleph_\alpha$ is regular. Then

$$
\omega_{\alpha+1}^2\not\to(\omega_{\alpha+1}^2,3)^2 .
$$

The survey says this solved the first part of Problem 13 of the 1967 list. Its
reference [15] is A. Hajnal, A negative partition relation, Proc. Nat. Acad.
Sci. U.S.A. 68 (1971), 142--144.

**Baumgartner's extension** (p. 273). The survey reports that J. Baumgartner
proved, assuming G.C.H., that $\omega_{\alpha+1}^2\not\to(\omega_{\alpha+1}^2,3)^2$
holds for every $\alpha$, with no regularity assumption, and gives no
reference.

**Question** (p. 274). The survey asks whether
$\mathrm{MA}_{\aleph_1}+2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_2$ implies
$\omega_1^2\to(\omega_1^2,3)^2$, calling it a problem of type D. It recalls
the authors' result $\omega_1^2\to(\omega_1\cdot\alpha,3)^2$ for
$\alpha<\omega_1$ from their 1970 paper [7], and says they do not know whether
$\omega_1^2\to(\omega_1\cdot\omega,4)^2$.

**Source.** P. Erdős and A. Hajnal, Unsolved and solved problems in set
theory, Proceedings of Symposia in Pure Mathematics 25 (1974), 269--287;
printed pp. 273--274 (PDF pp. 5--6 of the Rényi archive scan identified on the
[[set_theory/erdos_1974_unsolved_solved_problems_set_theory/_index|source card]]).

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the theorem, Baumgartner's extension and the
question were read clause by clause on the page images. The survey gives no
proof.

## Proof pointer

None in this survey; the proof is in Hajnal's 1971 paper, which is not held.

## Dependencies

None in this survey.

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/set_theory/E1169/_index|Problem 1169]]: the case
  $\alpha=0$ of the theorem gives the problem's relation
  $\omega_1^2\not\to(\omega_1^2,3)^2$ under G.C.H. (the theorem as printed
  assumes G.C.H., not only CH). That is a consistency result, not a ZFC proof.
  The question on p. 274 asks whether
  $\mathrm{MA}_{\aleph_1}+2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_2$ implies the positive relation
  $\omega_1^2\to(\omega_1^2,3)^2$; the survey gives no answer.
- [[../wiki/problems/set_theory/E1171/_index|Problem 1171]]: the survey says,
  on p. 274 and without proof, that the authors proved
  $\omega_1^2\to(\omega_1\cdot\alpha,3)^2$ for $\alpha<\omega_1$ in [7], their
  1970 paper in Acta Math. Acad. Sci. Hungar. 21. Its case $\alpha=\omega$ is
  the problem's case $k=1$, $\omega_1^2\to(\omega_1\omega,3)^2_2$. The survey
  says nothing on $k\ge2$; the open relation it names,
  $\omega_1^2\to(\omega_1\cdot\omega,4)^2$, is a different one.
