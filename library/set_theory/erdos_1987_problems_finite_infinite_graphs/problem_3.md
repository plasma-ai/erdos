## Statement

Problem 3 (printed pp. 223--224) has two parts.

*The triple relation (p. 223).* The print calls
$\mathfrak c\to(\omega+n,4)^3_2$ "an old result of Rado and myself" and
suggests that $\omega+n$ "can perhaps be replaced by any $\alpha<\omega_1$ and
4 by any $n<\omega$, but I know nothing about this." The print writes the
left side as a plain c and does not define it here.

*Pairs from $\omega_1^2$ (pp. 223--224).* The paper reports these results
without proof:

- Erdős and Hajnal proved $\omega_1^2\to(\omega_1\omega,3)^2$, and could
  never show $\omega_1^2\to(\omega_1\omega,4)^2_2$.
- During the meeting, Baumgartner and Hajnal proved, assuming the continuum
  hypothesis, $\omega_1^2\not\to(\omega_1\omega,4)^2$; they also proved a
  three-color relation, printed $\omega_1^2\to(\omega_1\omega,3,3)^2_1$.
- Erdős and Hajnal could never decide $\omega_2\omega\to(\omega_2\omega,3)^2_2$,
  and Erdős reports having heard in October 1985 that Shelah had just proved
  it.

He then asks: "Perhaps if $G$ is any graph of power $\aleph_1$ which contains
no $K_4$ then $\omega_1^2\to(\omega_1\omega,G)^2$. This is open even if $G$ is
assumed to be finite." In a parenthesis he reports that Baumgartner had just
shown $\omega_1^2\not\to(\omega_1\omega,K(\aleph_0,\aleph_0))^2$, and suggests
instead that the positive relation perhaps holds if $G$ contains no $K(4)$ and
no $K(\aleph_0,\aleph_0)$, adding that it may be necessary to restrict $G$ to
finite order.

**Source.** P. Erdős, *Some problems on finite and infinite graphs*, Logic and
Combinatorics (Arcata, Calif., 1985), Contemp. Math. 65, Amer. Math. Soc.
(1987), 223--228; Problem 3, pp. 223--224, PDF pp. 1--2 of the Rényi
archive's scan (printed p. $n$ = PDF p. $n-222$), read on the rendered page
images. The edition read is identified in the
[[set_theory/erdos_1987_problems_finite_infinite_graphs/_index|source digest]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the item was read clause by clause on the page
images. The results it reports are given without proof, and none of them was
checked here.

## Proof pointer

None in the source. The Erdős--Rado relation is Theorem 31 of their 1956
partition calculus paper, as recorded on
[[../wiki/problems/set_theory/E0070/_index|Problem 70]].
