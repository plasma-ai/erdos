## Statement

**Theorem 9** (p. 705, quoted). "Under $\Diamond$ there is an
$\aleph_1$-chromatic Hajnal-Máté graph not containing $C_3$, $C_5$, or
$K(\aleph_0,\aleph_0)$."

A Hajnal--Máté graph is a graph on $\omega_1$ in which every vertex is joined
to finitely many smaller vertices or to an $\omega$-sequence converging to it
(p. 704), and $K(\aleph_0,\aleph_0)$ is the complete bipartite graph with two
countably infinite classes (p. 703).

**Source.** Péter Komjáth and Saharon Shelah, Forcing constructions for
uncountably chromatic graphs, J. Symbolic Logic 53 (1988), 696--707: Theorem 9
and its proof on p. 705. The edition is the one identified on the
[[set_theory/komjath_1988_forcing_constructions_uncountably_chromatic_graphs/_index|source card]].

## Proof pointer

P. 705. The construction of
[[set_theory/komjath_1988_forcing_constructions_uncountably_chromatic_graphs/theorem_8|Theorem 8]]
is modified along the lines of
[[set_theory/komjath_1988_forcing_constructions_uncountably_chromatic_graphs/theorem_6|Theorem 6]]:
to exclude 4-cycles on $\alpha<\beta<\gamma<\delta$ with edges
$\{\alpha,\beta\},\{\beta,\gamma\},\{\gamma,\delta\},\{\alpha,\delta\}$, the
auxiliary class $F$ is split into two disjoint subclasses $F_1$ and $F_2$
according to the order of the vertices, and the rest of the proof is
virtually the same.
