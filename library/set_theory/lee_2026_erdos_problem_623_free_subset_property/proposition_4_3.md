**Source.** Sungchul Lee, Erdős Problem 623 and the Free-Subset Property,
preprint dated June 4, 2026; Definitions 4.1 (p. 3) and 4.2 (p. 4),
Proposition 4.3, p. 4, proof pp. 4--5. The edition read is identified on the
[[set_theory/lee_2026_erdos_problem_623_free_subset_property/_index|source card]].

## Statement

Following Koepke (p. 3), a structure $\mathcal S$ has a universe
$|\mathcal S|$, finitary functions on it and finitary relations on it.

**Definition 4.1** (p. 3). For $A\subset|\mathcal S|$, $\mathcal S[A]$ is
the substructure generated by $A$ under the function symbols of
$\mathcal S$. A set $X\subset|\mathcal S|$ is *free in $\mathcal S$* if
$x\notin\mathcal S[X\setminus\{x\}]$ for every $x\in X$.

**Definition 4.2** (p. 4). Let $\kappa$ be an infinite cardinal and
$\mu,\lambda$ cardinals. $\mathrm{Fr}_\mu(\kappa,\lambda)$ is the
assertion that for every structure $\mathcal S$ whose universe contains
every ordinal below $\kappa$ and which has at most $\mu$ functions and
relations, there is a set $X\subset\kappa$ with $|X|\ge\lambda$ that is
free in $\mathcal S$. The paper credits the notation to Koepke's 1984
paper, Section 1.

**Proposition 4.3** (p. 4). In ZFC, for all infinite cardinals
$\kappa,\mu,\lambda$,

$$
\mathsf{FS}_\mu(\kappa,\lambda)\iff\mathrm{Fr}_\mu(\kappa,\lambda).
$$

$\mathsf{FS}_\mu(\kappa,\lambda)$ is Definition 2.1 (p. 2), recorded on the
[[set_theory/lee_2026_erdos_problem_623_free_subset_property/proposition_2_2|Proposition 2.2 page]]. All three cardinals are
required to be infinite; the free set in $\mathsf{FS}$ has size exactly
$\lambda$ and the one in $\mathrm{Fr}$ size at least $\lambda$.

## Proof pointer

From $\mathsf{FS}_\mu$ (p. 4): given $\mathcal S$, put
$F(A)=\kappa\cap\mathcal S[A]$, which has size at most $\mu$ because
$\mu$ is infinite and there are at most $\mu$ terms over a finite set; an
$F$-free set is free in $\mathcal S$ because membership in a generated
substructure is witnessed by a finite set. From $\mathrm{Fr}_\mu$
(pp. 4--5): list each $F(A)$ as $e_i(A)$, $i<\mu$, and build on
$\kappa$ the structure with the $n$-ary operations
$g_{n,i}(x_0,\dots,x_{n-1})=e_i(\{x_0,\dots,x_{n-1}\})$ and the unary
operations $h_i(x)=e_i(\varnothing)$; a subset of size $\lambda$ of a free
set in it is $F$-free.
