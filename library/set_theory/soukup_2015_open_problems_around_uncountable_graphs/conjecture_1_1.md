**Source.** Conjecture 1.1 with the definition and remark around it,
Section 1 ("Connected subgraphs"), p. 1, of Daniel T. Soukup, *Open problems
around uncountable graphs*, workshop handout, Independence Results in
Mathematics and Challenges in Iterated Forcing, University of East Anglia,
November 2015, as identified on the
[[set_theory/soukup_2015_open_problems_around_uncountable_graphs/_index|source card]].

## Statement

**Definitions** (p. 1). The chromatic number of a graph $G=(V,E)$ is the
least cardinal $\kappa$ for which there is $f\colon V\to\kappa$ with
$f(x)\neq f(y)$ whenever $\{x,y\}\in E$. A graph is infinitely connected
when deleting any finitely many of its vertices leaves it connected.

**Conjecture 1.1** (p. 1; credited to Erdős and Hajnal, quoted). "There is
a graph $G$ with chromatic number $\omega_1$ without non empty infinitely
connected subgraphs."

**What the handout records** (p. 1). A graph of chromatic number $\omega_1$
with no *uncountable* infinitely connected subgraph exists, by its
reference [29], D. T. Soukup, Trees, ladders and graphs, J. Combin. Theory
Ser. B 115 (2015), 96--116 (card:
[[set_theory/soukup_2015_trees_ladders_graphs/_index|Soukup 2015]]); the
handout adds that the known examples contain many countably infinite
complete subgraphs. It traces a version of the problem to Erdős and Hajnal
(1966, its reference [6]) and to later surveys and problem lists, and
points to related results in its references [15, 16, 19].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the definition, the conjecture and the
remark were read clause by clause on p. 1 of the handout.

## Scope

A conjecture the handout records as open; the handout proves nothing.

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/set_theory/E1067/_index|Problem 1067]]: the problem
  asks whether every graph of chromatic number $\aleph_1$ has an infinitely
  connected subgraph of chromatic number $\aleph_1$. The example the
  handout cites from its reference [29] has chromatic number $\omega_1$ and
  no uncountable infinitely connected subgraph; a subgraph of chromatic
  number $\omega_1$ is uncountable, so the example has no subgraph of the
  kind the problem asks for. The handout records the example; the theorem
  is that paper's.
- [[../wiki/problems/set_theory/E1068/_index|Problem 1068]]: the problem
  asks whether every graph of chromatic number $\aleph_1$ has a countably
  infinite, infinitely connected subgraph (the reading its page adopts).
  Conjecture 1.1, if true, gives a graph of chromatic number $\omega_1$
  with no nonempty infinitely connected subgraph in the handout's sense,
  in particular no countably infinite one, and so a negative answer. The
  handout states its definition by deleting finitely many vertices, the
  problem page by vertex-disjoint paths; a subgraph infinitely connected in
  the path sense is infinitely connected in the handout's sense, since
  finitely many deleted vertices meet only finitely many of the disjoint
  paths. The handout records the conjecture as open and proves nothing
  toward it.
