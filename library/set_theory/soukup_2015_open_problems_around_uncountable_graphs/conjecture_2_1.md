**Source.** Conjecture 2.1 and the remark after it, Section 2 ("Triangle
free subgraphs"), p. 1, of Daniel T. Soukup, *Open problems around
uncountable graphs*, workshop handout, Independence Results in Mathematics
and Challenges in Iterated Forcing, University of East Anglia, November
2015, as identified on the
[[set_theory/soukup_2015_open_problems_around_uncountable_graphs/_index|source card]].

## Statement

**Conjecture 2.1** (p. 1; credited to Erdős and Hajnal, quoted). "There is
a graph $G$ with chromatic number $\omega_1$ such that every subgraph with
uncountable chromatic number contains a triangle."

**What the handout records** (p. 1). The conjecture holds consistently,
and $G$ can then even be taken with no $K_4$, whether or not CH holds; the
handout cites its reference [21], P. Komjáth and S. Shelah, Forcing
constructions for uncountably chromatic graphs, J. Symbolic Logic 53
(1988), 696--707 (card:
[[set_theory/komjath_1988_forcing_constructions_uncountably_chromatic_graphs/_index|Komjáth and Shelah 1988]]).
It asks for more constructive examples, for instance from $\diamondsuit$,
and states that the problem is still open in ZFC.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the conjecture and the remark were read
clause by clause on p. 1 of the handout.

## Scope

A conjecture the handout records as consistent and open in ZFC; the
handout proves nothing.

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/set_theory/E1175/_index|Problem 1175]]: the problem
  asks whether, for each uncountable cardinal $\kappa$, some cardinal
  $\lambda$ makes every graph of chromatic number $\lambda$ contain a
  triangle-free subgraph of chromatic number $\kappa$. Every subgraph of a
  graph of chromatic number $\omega_1$ has chromatic number at most
  $\omega_1$, so Conjecture 2.1 says that $\lambda=\omega_1$ does not work
  for $\kappa=\omega_1$, and the consistency the handout records gives this
  failure in some models of ZFC. The conjecture concerns $\lambda=\omega_1$
  only; the handout says nothing about larger $\lambda$ or other $\kappa$.
