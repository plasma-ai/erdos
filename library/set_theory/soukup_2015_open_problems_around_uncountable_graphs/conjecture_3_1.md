**Source.** Conjecture 3.1 and the remark after it, Section 3 ("On the
growth of finite subgraphs"), p. 2, of Daniel T. Soukup, *Open problems
around uncountable graphs*, workshop handout, Independence Results in
Mathematics and Challenges in Iterated Forcing, University of East Anglia,
November 2015, as identified on the
[[set_theory/soukup_2015_open_problems_around_uncountable_graphs/_index|source card]].

## Statement

**Conjecture 3.1** (p. 2; credited to Erdős, quoted). "Suppose
$f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ is increasing. Then there is a graph $G$ with
uncountable chromatic number such that every $n$ chromatic subgraph of $G$
has size at least $f(n)$."

**What the handout records** (p. 2): "Consistently yes [22] but still open
in ZFC." Its reference [22] is P. Komjáth and S. Shelah, Finite subgraphs
of uncountably chromatic graphs, J. Graph Theory 49 (2005), 28--38 (card:
[[set_theory/komjath_2002_finite_subgraphs_uncountably_chromatic_graphs/_index|Komjáth and Shelah]]).

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the conjecture and the remark were read
clause by clause on p. 2 of the handout.

## Scope

A conjecture the handout records as consistent and, as of 2015, open in
ZFC; the handout proves nothing.

## Bears on

- [[../wiki/problems/graph_coloring/E0110/_index|Problem 110]]: the
  problem asks whether some $F(n)$ makes every graph of chromatic number
  $\aleph_1$ have, for all large $n$, a subgraph of chromatic number $n$ on
  at most $F(n)$ vertices. Conjecture 3.1 for graphs of chromatic number
  exactly $\omega_1$, applied to an increasing $f$ with $f(n)>F(n)$, would
  give a graph of chromatic number $\aleph_1$ defeating $F$, so a negative
  answer. As printed, the conjecture asks only for uncountable chromatic
  number, and the handout does not bring it down to $\omega_1$. The handout
  records the 2015 status (consistent, open in ZFC) and proves nothing.
