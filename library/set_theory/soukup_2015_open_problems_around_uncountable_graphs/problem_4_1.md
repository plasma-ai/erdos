## Statement

**Problem 4.1** (p. 2; credited to Erdős, quoted). "Does every two graph
[sic] with uncountable chromatic number (chromatic number $\omega_1$)
contain a common (a) 4-chromatic subgraph? (b) $\omega$-chromatic
subgraph?"

**What the handout records** (p. 2). For 3-chromatic subgraphs the answer
is yes, since the two graphs share an odd cycle. For $\omega_1$-chromatic
subgraphs the answer is no: take $G_0,G_1$ with
$\mathrm{Chr}(G_0\times G_1)\le\omega$, where the product has vertex set
$V_0\times V_1$ and joins $(x,y)$ to $(x',y')$ when $\{x,x'\}\in E_0$ and
$\{y,y'\}\in E_1$ (p. 1). Section 8 (p. 3) states that graphs $G_0,G_1$ on
$\omega_1$ with $\mathrm{Chr}(G_0)=\mathrm{Chr}(G_1)=\omega_1$ and
$\mathrm{Chr}(G_0\times G_1)\le\omega$ exist in ZFC. The handout follows
with two related problems: Problem 4.2 (L. Soukup) asks for $G_0,G_1$ with
$\mathrm{Chr}(G_0\times G_1)=\omega_1$ and no common $\omega_1$-chromatic
subgraph, and Problem 4.3 (D. T. Soukup) asks whether a graph on
$\omega_1$ and its complement, both of uncountable chromatic number, share
an $\omega_1$-chromatic subgraph.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: Problems 4.1--4.3 and the remarks were read
clause by clause on p. 2, and the Section 8 remark on p. 3.

## Scope

A problem the handout records as open, with remarks on its 3-chromatic and
$\omega_1$-chromatic variants; the handout proves nothing.
