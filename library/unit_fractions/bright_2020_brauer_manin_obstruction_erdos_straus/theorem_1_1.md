## Statement

The paper writes the Erdős--Straus equation as

$$
\frac4n=\frac1{u_1}+\frac1{u_2}+\frac1{u_3} \tag{1.1}
$$

and the conjecture as the statement that (1.1) has a solution with
$u_1,u_2,u_3\in\mathbb N$ for every $n\ge2$ (p. 1).

**Theorem 1.1** (p. 1) reads: "Let $n\ge2$. Then there is no Brauer–Manin
obstruction to the existence of natural number solutions of the equation
(1.1)."

**Source.** Bright and Loughran, arXiv:1908.02526v2 (12 May 2020), 17 pp.;
Theorem 1.1 on p. 1, read on the page image; the geometric setting in
Section 1.2 (pp. 2--5). Published as Bull. Lond. Math. Soc. 52 (2020),
no. 4, 746--761, DOI 10.1112/blms.12374 (the arXiv listing's DOI; Crossref
record fetched); the published version was not compared.

**Read depth.** Claims checked: the statement and the setting of
Section 1.2 (pp. 2--5) were read clause by clause, pp. 1--4 on the page
images; the precise form (1.4) of Theorem 1.8 and its proof in Section 3.8
(p. 14) were followed, the local lemmas behind them were not checked.

## Setting and companion statements

- The affine cubic surface $U_n\colon 4u_1u_2u_3=n(u_1u_2+u_1u_3+u_2u_3)$
  (display (1.2), p. 2) is a singular log K3 surface; the conjecture is
  equivalent to a special case of strong approximation for $U_n$, namely
  $U_n(\mathbb Q)\cap W\ne\emptyset$ for
  $W=\{U_n(\mathbb R)_+\}\times\prod_p\mathcal U_n(\mathbb Z_p)$, where
  $U_n(\mathbb R)_+$ is the set of real points with $u_1,u_2,u_3>0$ and
  $\mathcal U_n$ is the model over $\mathbb Z$ given by the same equation
  (p. 3).
- [[unit_fractions/bright_2020_brauer_manin_obstruction_erdos_straus/theorem_1_6|Theorem 1.6]] (p. 3):
  $\operatorname{Br}U_n/\operatorname{Br}\mathbb Q\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$,
  generated by the quaternion algebra $(-u_1/u_3,-u_2/u_3)$.
  [[unit_fractions/bright_2020_brauer_manin_obstruction_erdos_straus/theorem_1_8|Theorem 1.8]] (p. 4) states, for all $n\in\mathbb N$,
  that the Brauer set of $U_n(\mathbb R)_+\times\prod_p\mathcal U_n(\mathbb Z_p)$
  is non-empty, its (1.4), which the paper calls a more precise version of
  Theorem 1.1, and that this Brauer set is a proper subset, its (1.5); the
  proof of (1.4) takes a prime dividing $n$, so it covers $n\ge2$.
- [[unit_fractions/bright_2020_brauer_manin_obstruction_erdos_straus/theorem_1_2|Theorem 1.2]] (p. 2): for odd $n$ and a natural-number
  solution, $\prod_{p\mid n}(-u_1/u_3,-u_2/u_3)_p=-1$ (Hilbert symbols);
  [[unit_fractions/bright_2020_brauer_manin_obstruction_erdos_straus/theorem_1_5|Theorem 1.5]] (p. 2): the product is $1$ for integer
  solutions that are not natural-number solutions.
  [[unit_fractions/bright_2020_brauer_manin_obstruction_erdos_straus/corollary_1_3|Corollary 1.3]] and
  [[unit_fractions/bright_2020_brauer_manin_obstruction_erdos_straus/corollary_1_4|Corollary 1.4]] (p. 2) draw the consequences at odd
  primes and at odd squares.

## Dependencies

The Brauer--Manin machinery for integral points on affine surfaces (the
paper's references [20] and the log K3 literature it cites); not examined
here.
