## Statement

**Corollary 2** (p. 2): "There exists a transcendental real number that does
not have eventually greedy best Egyptian underapproximations." The property is
defined on
[[unit_fractions/kovac_2024_eventually_greedy_best_underapproximations_egyptian_fractions/theorem_1|the Theorem 1 page]].

## Proof

The eventually greedy set is Lebesgue null by Theorem 1, and the algebraic
numbers are countable, hence null; a set of full measure remains. The paper
notes that the argument leaves no clue how to find a particular such number.

## Context and read depth

Erdős and Graham wrote in 1980 (p. 31) that it is "not difficult to construct
irrationals for which the result fails", without a proof or reference, and
Nathanson (2023, Open problem (4)) asked for a proof or disproof. The
corollary settles the existence question in the strongest sense and leaves
the construction open; the site's commentary (2026-09-17) still lists an
explicit example as open. Read depth: claims checked; the deduction was read
in full.

**Bears on.** [[../wiki/problems/unit_fractions/E0206/_index|#206]]: a
consequence of Theorem 1 that answers, non-constructively, whether an
irrational without the property exists; it names no explicit such number.
