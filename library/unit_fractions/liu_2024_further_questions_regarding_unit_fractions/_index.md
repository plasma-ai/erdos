## Results and method

[[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]]
states that, for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$ and sufficiently large
$N$, a set $A\subseteq[1,N]$ with reciprocal mass at least
$(\log N)^{4/5+\varepsilon}$ contains a subset summing to one.
It improves
[[unit_fractions/bloom_2021_density_conjecture_about_unit_fractions/theorem_3|Bloom's quantitative bound]].
The introduction (p. 1) attributes the earlier bound of order
$(\log N)(\log\log\log N)/\log\log N$ for the extremal reciprocal mass of
a set avoiding a unit subsum, and a lower bound of order $(\log\log N)^2$,
to prior work; these are cited background.
For fixed $\delta>0$ its threshold is below $\delta\log N$ for large $N$,
so it also answers [[../wiki/problems/unit_fractions/E0047/_index|Problem 47]], which
Bloom's Theorem 3 had already settled.
The paper refines the Fourier methods of Croot and Bloom. Its main
technical proposition separates reciprocal mass from divisor incidence,
and a sieve controls exceptional primes (pp. 4–6, 16–19).

The other main results have different compilation scopes. The counting
theorem has a complete rewritten proof through the restricted
Proposition 3.2. The remaining result pages below are statements and
proof pointers whose full proofs are not yet compiled; the problem each
bears on is named in parentheses:

- [[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/theorem_1_2|Theorem 1.2]]:
  the count of subsets with reciprocal sum one is
  $\exp((\gamma_*+o(1))N)$, with $\gamma_*\approx0.631573$
  defined by an integral equation (Problem 297).
- [[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/theorem_1_3|Theorem 1.3]]:
  the sharp asymptotic cardinality threshold is $(1-1/e)N$
  (Problem 300).
- [[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/proposition_1_4|Proposition 1.4]]:
  density $(\log N)^{-1/7+\varepsilon}\le\alpha\le1/2$ yields a
  positive reciprocal subsum at most one whose denominator is at most
  $\exp(C/\alpha)$ (Problem 310).
- [[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/theorem_1_5|Theorem 1.5]]:
  every proper fraction $a/b$ has a representation whose largest
  denominator is at most
  $b(\log b)(\log\log b)^3(\log\log\log b)^{O(1)}$
  (Problem 305).
- [[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/theorem_1_6|Theorem 1.6]]:
  the least forbidden first denominator is between
  $N/((\log N)(\log\log N)^3(\log\log\log N)^{O(1)})$
  and $O(N/\log N)$ (Problem 294).

The simpler Fourier argument of Proposition 3.2 is used for the counting
and denominator results, where the ambient set can be chosen; the main
Proposition 5.2 is designed for arbitrary input sets. For counting,
[[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/proposition_3_2|the actual-period, sixth-power form of Proposition 3.2]]
and [[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/lemma_3_3|Lemma 3.3]] now have complete rewritten proofs.
The full proof of Lemma 4.1 and the later denominator applications remain
to be compiled at their exact target and parameter scopes. Earlier
alternative methods and later refinements must be checked there before
calling any of their bounds current best.

## Proof coverage and source concerns

Theorem 1.1 has a complete outer reduction with parameter checks.
Its essential preliminary and technical results have separate pages,
with external prime-number, sieve and concentration inputs identified.
Local typesetting corrections are explicit, including Lemma 6.1's
missing minus sign, Lemma 6.2's unbound lowercase $n$, and the Fourier
Taylor coefficient in Fact 2.5 and Lemma 3.1.

The complete main proof now uses explicitly justified forms of its
inputs. The literal multiplicity-counting statement in
[[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/lemma_2_2|Lemma 2.2]]
is false; that page records a counterargument and a separately proved
Euler-product deduction giving the sufficient reciprocal loss
$O((\log N)^{-\beta})=o(1)$, with
$\beta=5\log(3/2)-3/2>0$. The literal unrestricted statement of
[[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/lemma_5_1|Lemma 5.1]]
also fails for very small mass parameters. Its page preserves that
limitation and proves the $H\ge2$ application form with corrected
divisor exponents and the global prime-factor bound.

[[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/proposition_5_2|Proposition
5.2]] proves that its feasible parameters satisfy $H\to\infty$ and lie in the
divisor lemma's required range. It also tracks the dyadic endpoints and
logarithmic powers, retains the missing Fourier factor, and supplies the omitted
major-arc cardinality argument. Independent reviews checked these bounded
corrections before incorporation, retained as the [source
checks](evidence/verify/source_checks_review.md) and [preliminary
review](evidence/verify/preliminary_review.md). The exact revised main-proof
pages, as they stood at 2026-09-18T07:24:04Z, passed independent blind review
on 2026-09-18, retained as the [fresh main-proof
review](evidence/verify/main_proof_review_fresh.md) with its [distinct
grade](evidence/verify/main_proof_review_grade_fresh.md), which records PASS
for the report contract and PASS for independence; the earlier [main-proof
review](evidence/verify/main_proof_review.md) of 2026-09-05 is retained as a
coordinated compilation check whose main-proof leg was ruled not independent
on 2026-09-18, because its reviewer read the sibling reports' verdicts on steps
inside its subject. These are explicit compilation
corrections, not author errata and not assertions about the uninspected
published version. They preserve Theorem 1.1's statement and do not affect
Bloom's independent resolution of Problems 298 and 299.

The counting chain is separate from that arbitrary-input proof. The
[[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/counting_period_obstruction|literal period in v1 Proposition 3.2 has a two-adic counterexample]];
its compiled replacement uses $Q=\operatorname{lcm}(A)$ and the sufficient
condition $S\le K^3/(CN^2(\log\log N)^6)$. The proof supplies the required
averaging range, set-valued complementary residue fibers, bounded prime
choices, a full
[[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/counting_carrier|integer-carrier construction]], and the cyclic frequency count.
Theorem 1.2 uses the triple-log cutoff already printed on source p. 12,
normalizes by the reciprocal of the actual mean, and tracks the uniform
atom probabilities. Its global $N$ corrects the lowercase $n$ in the
p. 13 logistic-odds display. The conclusion is the exact exponential
rate for both sum one and sum at most one; the displayed decimals remain
source approximations. The literal general proposition and its full
fifth-power range are not claimed. PNT, Bertrand's postulate and
Azuma–Hoeffding remain explicit external inputs.

A targeted literature search found no later improvement
of Theorem 1.1's exact-sum reciprocal-mass threshold, and a further check
of the citing-paper records for this paper and of the arXiv listings under
unit and Egyptian fractions found no later improvement of Theorems 1.1, 1.3
or 1.6. The 2026 preprint
[arXiv:2607.04157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04157) concerns approximate
subsum one for multisets, a different question, treated on its own
[[unit_fractions/korsky_2026_stretched_exponential_bound_erdos_graham_unit/_index|library card]].
No existing formalization of the stronger Liu–Sawhney threshold was
located; the existing Bloom formalization proves the earlier theorem.

## Relation to E301

This source bears on [[../wiki/problems/unit_fractions/E0301/_index|Problem 301]].

For Problem 301, fix $a\in A$ and put $L=\lfloor N/a\rfloor$ and
$C_a=\{m\in\{2,\ldots,L\}:am\in A\}$. The forbidden equation
$1/a=\sum_i1/b_i$ with distinct elements of $A$ holds whenever some subset
of $C_a$ has reciprocal sum $1$: if $\sum_{m\in B}1/m=1$ with
$B\subseteq C_a$, then $1/a=\sum_{m\in B}1/(am)$ with the distinct elements
$am\in A$, each exceeding $a$. Thus
[[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]]
(p. 1) implies that an E301-free $A$ satisfies
$\sum_{m\in C_a}1/m<(\log L)^{4/5+\varepsilon}$ for every fixed
$\varepsilon>0$ once $L$ is sufficiently large.
[[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/theorem_1_3|Theorem 1.3]]
(p. 2) similarly gives $|C_a|<(1-1/e+\varepsilon)L$. These are restrictions
on the multiples of each $a\in A$; neither is a global upper bound near
$N/2$ for the extremal function $f(N)$ of Problem 301. The converse fails,
since a solution need not consist of multiples of $a$ ($1/2=1/3+1/6$).

[[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/proposition_5_2|Proposition 5.2]]
(p. 16) could enter a direct forcing argument by applying it to suitable
denominators in $A\setminus\{a\}$ with target $x/Q=1/a$. This requires,
among its other hypotheses, $a\mid Q$ and the prescribed reciprocal sum
range, so an arbitrary dense $A$ is not covered.
[[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/theorem_1_2|Theorem 1.2]]
and
[[unit_fractions/liu_2024_further_questions_regarding_unit_fractions/theorem_1_6|Theorem 1.6]]
(pp. 2–3) concern representations of $1$ and do not themselves force an
E301 identity. The source is relevant because it provides strong
representation tools for the scaled sets $C_a$, but it does not prove
$f(N)=(1/2+o(1))N$ or resolve Problem 301.
