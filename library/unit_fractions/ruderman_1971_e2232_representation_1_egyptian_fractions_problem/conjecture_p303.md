## Statement

With $U_n$ the least number of distinct unit fractions with sum $1$ and
largest term at most $1/n$ (p. 302), the solution of Erdős and Straus ends
on p. 303 with the unnumbered remark: "It seems to us certain that
$U_n-(e-1)n\to\infty$ as $n\to\infty$ but we have not proved this."

The editorial note after the solution (p. 303) adds a second, separate
conjecture, credited to no one by name: "Several solvers conjecture that
$2n$ is an upper bound for $U_n$." The note lists the other solvers who
found bounds (Bohigian, Gardner, Kelly, Lossers, Reich, Schmeichel and the
proposer) without saying which of them made this conjecture.
[[unit_fractions/ruderman_1971_e2232_representation_1_egyptian_fractions_problem/inequality_1|Inequality (1)]]
gives $U_n<2n$ for all sufficiently large $n$, since $e-1<2$; this is drawn
here, not in the print, and leaves the small $n$ unchecked.

**Source.** H. D. Ruderman (proposer), P. Erdős and E. Straus (solvers),
E2232, Representation of 1 by Egyptian fractions, Amer. Math. Monthly 78
(1971), no. 3, 302--303, doi:10.2307/2317539; the remark and the editorial
note on p. 303. Edition and provenance are on the
[[unit_fractions/ruderman_1971_e2232_representation_1_egyptian_fractions_problem/_index|source card]].

**Read depth.** Claims checked: both sentences were read on the page image
of p. 303. They are conjectures; there is no proof.

## Proof pointer

None. The solution proves only the two-sided bound
[[unit_fractions/ruderman_1971_e2232_representation_1_egyptian_fractions_problem/inequality_1|(1)]],
whose lower half shows that $U_n-(e-1)n$ is bounded below.
