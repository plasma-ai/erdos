**Claim.** For every infinite set $A$ of positive integers, with
$A(n)=\lvert A\cap[1,n]\rvert$, there is a set $B$ such that $A+B$ contains
every sufficiently large integer and

$$
B(n)\le C\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\log A(k)}{A(k)},
$$

with $C$ absolute and a term with $A(k)=0$ replaced by $1$. Since
$A(k)\to\infty$, the summands tend to zero and so does their Cesàro mean, so
$B(n)=o(n)$: $B$ has density zero. This proves the statement of
[[problems/additive_bases/E0031/_index|Problem 31]], the conjecture of Erdős
and Straus. The result is Theorem 1 of Lorentz, G. G., On a problem of additive
number theory, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 5 (1954), no. 5, 838--841, received
1954-03-02 (the page's date) and published in the October 1954 issue; the card
[[../library/additive_bases/lorentz_1954_problem_additive_number_theory/_index|lorentz_1954_problem_additive_number_theory]]
records the source, and its page
[[../library/additive_bases/lorentz_1954_problem_additive_number_theory/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
reconstructs the greedy interval cover, the dyadic assembly and the Cesàro
step. If $0\in\mathbb{N}$, apply the theorem to the infinite positive part of
$A$; its complement is also one for $A$.

**Acceptance.** The `refereed` evidence is the journal publication cited
above. The `reviewed` evidence is the documented acceptance by the catalog
erdosproblems.com, whose page for the problem (the first `discussion` link)
carries the label PROVED (LEAN) and its curator, Thomas Bloom, credits
Lorentz's paper with the proof. The second `discussion` link is the catalog's

`src/v4.29.1/ErdosProblems/Erdos31.lean` of Boris Alexeev's repository
https://github.com/plby/lean-proofs, pinned above at the commit of 2026-06-24,

informal authors and Aristotle and Boris Alexeev as the formal authors, so it
is a formalization of this result and not an independent proof; the
formal-conjectures catalog tags its own statement `erdos_31` as
`research solved` and links this file. Its statement gives, for every infinite
$A\subseteq\mathbb{N}$, a set $B$ of density zero and an $n_0$ with $n\in A+B$
for all $n\ge n_0$, through the file's own `HasDensity` on $\mathbb{N}$; the
file imports only Mathlib. This corpus has not built the file, printed its
axioms or audited its definitions, so the claim carries no `formalized`
evidence and the formalization is a link, not a warrant.
