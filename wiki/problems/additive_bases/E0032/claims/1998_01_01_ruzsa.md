**Claim.** Let $B\subset\mathbb{N}$, let $S=\{p+b : p \text{ prime}, b\in B\}$,
and write $B(x)$ and $S(x)$ for the counting functions. Theorem 2 of
[[../library/additive_bases/ruzsa_1998_additive_completion_primes/_index|Ruzsa's paper]]
states that if

$$
x-S(x)\le x^{1-\log\log\log x/\log\log x}
$$

for all large $x$, and so in particular if $S$ contains all but finitely many
natural numbers, then

$$
\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{B(x)}{\log x}\ge e^{\gamma},
$$

with $\gamma$ the Euler--Mascheroni constant, so the limit inferior is at
least $1.781\ldots$. A set $A$ as in
[[problems/additive_bases/E0032/_index|Problem 32]], one for which every large
integer is $p+a$ with $p$ prime and $a\in A$, is exactly a set whose $S$ is
cofinite, so

$$
\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\lvert A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}\rvert}{\log N}\ge e^{\gamma}>1,
$$

which answers the third question of the problem yes. Ruzsa proves the theorem
in a quantitative form (Section 3 of the paper): if $\alpha<e^{\gamma}$ and
$B\subset[1,x]$ has at most $\alpha\log x$ elements, then for some $c<1$ and all
large $x$ at least $x^{1-c\log\log\log x/\log\log x}$ integers up to $x$ are
not of the form $p+b$. The same paper's Theorem 1 constructs complements
covering most integers rather than every large integer: of size $O(\log x)$
with sumset of lower density above $1-\varepsilon$ for any $\varepsilon>0$,
and of size $O(\omega(x)\log x)$ with sumset of density $1$; those results
bear on the second question without answering it.

**Covers.** The third question (the part `liminf`), answered yes with the
bound $e^{\gamma}$. Not covered: whether a set $A$ with
$\lvert A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}\rvert=o((\log N)^2)$ exists (the part `existence`)
and whether $O(\log N)$ can be achieved (the part `log`); a lower bound on the
liminf decides neither.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the claim rests on the cited paper.

Acta Arith. 86 (1998), no. 3, 269--275, the `paper` link. The site's curator
records the bound in the problem's remarks as the answer to the third
