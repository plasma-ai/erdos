***

**Claim.** Let $(A_n)_{n\ge1}$ be a sequence of Sidon sets with
$\lvert A_n\rvert=n$ and $\operatorname{diam}(A_n)=(1+o(1))n^2$, the sets the
note calls asymptotically maximum. Then, with $Q$ the quantity of
[[problems/additive_bases/E0153/_index|Problem 153]],

$$
Q(A_n)=\frac1t\sum_{1\le i<t}(s_{i+1}-s_i)^2\to\infty,
\qquad A_n+A_n=\{s_1<\cdots<s_t\}.
$$

This is Theorem 2 of the note *Erdős #153: Asymptotically maximum case* by
Yu Leon Liu, dated 2026-05-20, which entered the author's repository on
2026-05-19 (the `preprint` link is pinned to the revision linked from the
thread) and was posted to the site's discussion thread on 2026-05-20. The
proof has two inputs. Pikhurko's uniformity lemma, through the convolution
argument of the author's note on
[[problems/additive_bases/E0819/_index|Problem 819]], gives the density of
$A+A$ in a window of length $\eta D$ centered at $xD$, $D$ the diameter, as
$\varphi(x/2)+O(\eta)+o_n(1)$ with $\varphi$ the triangular density, so the
sumset thins out linearly at both ends of $[0,2D]$ (Lemma 4); a localization
inequality bounds $\sum_i(s_{i+1}-s_i)^2$ below by
$\sum_k\lvert I_k\rvert^2/(\lvert T\cap I_k\rvert+1)$ over disjoint windows
$I_k$ (Lemma 5). Windows near the ends of the sumset then contribute a Riemann
sum for $\int_\varepsilon^{1/2}du/u$, which grows without bound as
$\varepsilon\to0$. The author's thread post says that the result was found
with the help of GPT-5.5 and Rethlas and verified by hand; the note itself
names no system.

**Covers.** Every family of Sidon sets with
$\operatorname{diam}(A_n)=(1+o(1))n^2$, which by the note's footnote includes
the Bose–Chowla sets. The note's Observation 1 records, after a thread
comment of 2026-02-15, that Cauchy–Schwarz already gives $Q(A)\to\infty$ when
$\operatorname{diam}(A)/n^2\to\infty$, so the open range is
$\operatorname{diam}(A_n)=O(n^2)$; its Remark 3 leaves the case
$\liminf\operatorname{diam}(A_n)/n^2>1$ open, and the note does not claim the
general case.
[[problems/additive_bases/E0153/claims/2026_08_14_kapoor|Kapoor's logarithmic
lower bound]] of August 2026 recovers this result as the $\kappa\to1$ endpoint
of its theorem, and its thread note credits this post.

**Standing.** Claimed. The note replaced the author's withdrawn full claim of
2026-05-16 ([[problems/additive_bases/E0153/claims/2026_05_16_liu|Liu's
withdrawn proof]]), whose argument rested on a retracted bound; the corrected
note uses neither that bound nor any result of the thread. As of 2026-10-06
the result is not on the site's proof-claims tab, the site's label is OPEN, and
no comment on the post, refereed publication, outside review or Lean
development is recorded.
