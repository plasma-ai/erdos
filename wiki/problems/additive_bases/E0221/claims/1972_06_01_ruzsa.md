**Claim.** There is a set $A\subset\mathbb{N}$ with
$\lvert A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}\rvert\ll N/\log N$ for all large $N$ such that every
sufficiently large integer is $2^k+a$ with $k\ge 0$ and $a\in A$. The
two-page note
[[../library/additive_bases/ruzsajr_1972_problem_p/_index|Ruzsa 1972]] takes
for $A$ the integers of the form $5^u v$ and $5^u v+1$ with $5^u>c\log v$ for a
small absolute constant $c>0$. Representability rests on $2$ being a
primitive root modulo every power of $5$: for large $n$ choose $r$ with
$5^r\approx\log n$; some $k<5^r$ makes $n-2^k$ or $n-2^k-1$ divisible by $5^r$,
and the quotient $v$ satisfies $5^r>c\log v$. The note also observes that
every $n$ has a bounded number of such representations, and that the constant
in the counting bound must be at least $\log 2$, since the integers up to $N$ use
at most $\log_2 N+1$ powers of two. Lorentz [Lo54] had earlier given a set
with the weaker bound $\ll N\log\log N/\log N$. The site's thread records
that $c=1/(5\log 2)$ works, with $A=\{5^n m: 1\le m<2^{5^{n+1}}\}+\{0,1\}$ and
every $N\ge 32$ represented with $k\ge 1$.
