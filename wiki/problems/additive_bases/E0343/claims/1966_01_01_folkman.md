***

**Claim.**
[[../library/additive_bases/folkman_1966_representation_integers_as_sums_distinct_terms/theorem_1_3|Theorem 1.3]]
of J. Folkman, On the representation of integers as sums of distinct terms
from a fixed sequence, Canad. J. Math. 18 (1966), 643--655: a nondecreasing
sequence $A=\{a_1\le a_2\le\cdots\}$ of positive integers with
$a_n\le Mn^{\alpha}$ for all $n$, for some $M$ and some $0\le\alpha<1$ (the
paper's condition (1.1)), is subcomplete, that is, its finite subset sums
contain an infinite arithmetic progression. The paper's Theorems 1.1 and 1.2
deduce completeness from it under a residue condition; the
[[../library/additive_bases/folkman_1966_representation_integers_as_sums_distinct_terms/_index|source card]]
records the statements. The hypothesis is the case of
[[problems/additive_bases/E0343/_index|Problem 343]] in which the counting
function $\lvert A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}\rvert$ is $\gg N^{1+\epsilon}$ for some
$\epsilon>0$, which the site's remarks credit to the paper.

**Covers.** The problem's multisets whose counting function is $\gg
N^{1+\epsilon}$ for some $\epsilon>0$, equivalently $a_n\le Mn^{\alpha}$ with
$\alpha<1$; such a counting function exceeds $CN$ for all large $N$, so these
multisets are a special case of the hypothesis of the corrected Statement, and
for them the answer is yes. It does not cover a multiset with at least $CN$
terms up to $N$ for all large $N$ whose counting function is not $\gg
N^{1+\epsilon}$ for any $\epsilon>0$. The paper's companion construction, a
multiset with counting function $\gg N^{1-\epsilon}$ that is not subcomplete,
settles no instance of the question either, since its counting function is not
linear.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the paper is a journal article in Canad. J. Math.
The site's remarks credit the result, but the site's label credits Szemerédi
and Vu, so no `reviewed` evidence is listed. Crossref gives the year only, so
the page is dated to the first day of 1966. The proof is not reviewed here.
