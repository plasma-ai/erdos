***

**Claim.** For $t,a>0$ let $S_t(a)$ be the sequence with $n$th term
$\lfloor ta^n\rfloor$, each index used at most once (the paper's $P(A)$ is the
set of sums $\sum\varepsilon_ka_k$ with $\varepsilon_k\in\{0,1\}$, almost all
zero). Graham determines the set $T$ of pairs $(t,a)$ in the unit square
$0<t<1$, $1<a<2$ for which $S_t(a)$ is complete and shows that $T$ has area
approximately $0.85$, so Erdős's conjecture that $S_t(a)$ is complete for every
$t>0$ and $1<a<2$ is false. Theorem 2: for $0<t<1$ and $1<a\le5^{1/3}$ the
sequence is entirely complete (every positive integer is a sum). Theorem 3: for
$0<t<1$ and $1<a<2$ the sequence is complete if and only if it is entirely
complete. The site's remarks add the paper's consequence that for every $k$
there is $t_k\in(0,1)$ for which the set of $a$ with $S_{t_k}(a)$ complete has
at least $k$ connected components. The paper is Graham, R. L., On a conjecture
of Erdős in additive number theory, Acta Arith. 10 (1964/65), 63--70,
recorded on the card
[[../library/additive_bases/graham_nd_conjecture_erdos_additive_number_theory/_index|graham_nd_conjecture_erdos_additive_number_theory]].

**Covers.** The pairs with $0<t<1$ and $1<\alpha<2$ of the corrected
Statement of [[problems/additive_bases/E0349/_index|Problem 349]], whose sums
(each term used at most once, equal values at different indices counted
separately) and index from $n=1$ are the paper's: on that square the result
determines exactly which pairs give a complete sequence, which is what the
problem asks, so the claim's value is `answered`. It says nothing about
$t\ge1$ or about $\alpha\ge2$ or $\alpha\le1$; those regions are the subject
of [[problems/additive_bases/E0349/claims/2025_09_08_van_doorn|van Doorn's claim page]],
which builds on this one.

**Acceptance.** The `refereed` evidence is the journal publication cited
above, in Acta Arithmetica. The site's curator cites the paper in the remarks
of a problem the site labels OPEN, which credits the partial result without
settling the problem, so no `reviewed` evidence is listed. The record gives
the volume years and no finer date, so the page is dated to the first day of
1964.
