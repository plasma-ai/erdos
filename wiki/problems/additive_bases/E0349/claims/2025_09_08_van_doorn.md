**Claim.** For $t,\alpha>0$ let $S_t(\alpha)=(\lfloor t\alpha^n\rfloor)_{n\ge1}$,
each index used at most once, so that $P(S)$ is the set of sums of distinct
elements of the sequence as a multiset; $S$ is complete when $P(S)$ contains
all large integers and entirely complete when $P(S)=\mathbb N$. Wouter van
Doorn, Completeness of exponentially increasing sequences, arXiv:2602.23394
(v1 2026-02-25, 11 pages), recorded on the card
[[../library/additive_bases/doorn_2026_completeness_exponentially_increasing_sequences/_index|doorn_2026_completeness_exponentially_increasing_sequences]],
proves: (Proposition 1) for $\alpha\notin[1,2]$ the sequence is not complete
for any $t>0$, and for $\alpha=1$ it is (entirely) complete if and only if
$1\le t<2$; (Proposition 2) for $\alpha=2$ it is (entirely) complete if and
only if $t=1/2^k$ with $k\ge1$; (Proposition 3) for $5^{1/3}\le\alpha<2$ and
$t\ge1$ it is not complete; (Proposition 4) for $\varphi\le\alpha<5^{1/3}$,
$\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$, it is (entirely) complete if and only if
$t<\min(3/\alpha^2,5/\alpha^3)$; (Propositions 5 and 6) for $1<\alpha<\varphi$
it is entirely complete if and only if $t<\min(2/\alpha,3/\alpha^2)$, so that
for $1<\alpha\le5^{1/3}$ entire completeness holds exactly when
$t<\min(2/\alpha,3/\alpha^2,5/\alpha^3)$. Together with Graham's determination
of the complete pairs with $t<1$
([[problems/additive_bases/E0349/claims/1964_01_01_graham|Graham's claim page]]),
this decides completeness for every pair with $\alpha\ge\varphi$. Below
$\varphi$ the paper proves completeness regions: (Proposition 7) complete for
all $t<4/\alpha$ when $1<\alpha\le5/4$; (Proposition 8, computer-assisted,
with the search data in the linked repository) complete for all $t\le3$ when
$1.3<\alpha\le1.4$, for all $t\le5$ when $1.2<\alpha\le1.3$, for all $t\le10$
when $1.1<\alpha\le1.2$, and for all $t\le50$ when $1<\alpha\le1.1$; and
(Proposition 9) complete whenever
$1<\alpha\le1+1/(\lceil t\rceil+2\lceil\sqrt t\rceil)$, a region of infinite
area. The paper also describes a computer search that, where it succeeds,
certifies completeness on a bounded region of pairs with $1<\alpha<\varphi$
through its Lemma 5, and asserts without proof that any region $t<T$,
$\alpha<\varphi-\epsilon$ could in principle be covered this way; the author
wrote in the thread on 2026-03-02 that the author cannot prove such a covering
exists. If Geneson's claimed counterexample holds
([[problems/additive_bases/E0349/claims/2026_09_06_geneson|Geneson's claim page]]),
the conjecture fails at one base near $1.25$ for large $t$, so for large $T$ no
such search can succeed.

**Covers.** Every pair $(t,\alpha)$ of
[[problems/additive_bases/E0349/_index|Problem 349]] with $\alpha\ge\varphi$,
with $\alpha\le1$, or with $1<\alpha<\varphi$ and
$t<\min(2/\alpha,3/\alpha^2)$, and the pairs in the regions of Propositions
7--9, for the corrected Statement, whose sums and index from $n=1$ are the
paper's. On the pairs it covers, the result determines whether the
sequence is complete, which is what the problem asks, so the claim's value is
`answered`. It leaves open the pairs with $1<\alpha<\varphi$ and
$t\ge\min(2/\alpha,3/\alpha^2)$ outside those regions, which the paper
conjectures are all complete; Geneson's later counterexample at a Salem base
([[problems/additive_bases/E0349/claims/2026_09_06_geneson|Geneson's claim page]])
refutes that conjecture at one base for large $t$ without contradicting any
region proved here.

**Claimant and postings.** The author announced the results in the site's
thread on 2025-09-08 (the first `discussion` link), with the classification
above, the computer-assisted ranges and the infinite-area result, linking a
write-up; the paper was posted to the arXiv on 2026-02-25 and the thread link
updated on 2026-03-02 (the second `discussion` link), the author writing that
little had changed in the intervening months. The page is dated by the thread
announcement.

**Standing.** Claimed: a preprint with no journal reference or DOI on its
arXiv record as of 2026-10-07, no outside review and no site acceptance. The
site's remarks credit Graham's paper and do not mention this one; the problem
is labeled OPEN. Nat Sothanaphan's note of 2026-03-09
([[problems/additive_bases/E0349/claims/2026_03_09_sothanaphan|its claim page]])
reports an independent verification of the computer-assisted ranges, which the
author acknowledged in the thread, and sharpens Proposition 9 by a bounded
amount.
