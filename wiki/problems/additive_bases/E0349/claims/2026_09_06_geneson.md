**Claim.** Let $\gamma>1$ be the Salem number with minimal polynomial
$x^{18}-x^{12}-x^{11}-x^{10}-x^9-x^8-x^7-x^6+1$, so that
$6/5<\gamma<13/10<(1+\sqrt5)/2$. There are arbitrarily large real $t>0$ such
that $\lfloor t\gamma^n\rfloor$ is even for every $n\ge0$; such a sequence
represents no odd integer and is not complete. This is Theorem 9 of Geneson,
J., Deletion thresholds and exponential examples for complete sequences,
arXiv:2609.25107 (v1 2026-09-20), recorded on the card
[[../library/additive_bases/geneson_2026_deletion_thresholds_exponential_examples_complete_sequences/theorem_9|Theorem 9]],
whose one-page proof has not been independently reviewed; the ResearchGate
note of the forum claim, "Incomplete sequences from powers of a Salem number",
is the earlier posting of the same result, whose posting date is not recorded,
so the page is dated by the forum claim. Since every term is even, the sequence
is not complete under the corrected Statement of the problem page, nor under
the site's wording, which counts equal values once.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Jesse

as the AI used:

> $t>0$ and $1<\gamma<(1+\sqrt5)/2$. With an elementary sign adjustment, the

**Covers.** The conjecture, of Graham (1964) and of Erdős and Graham's 1980
monograph and repeated in the site's remarks, that $\lfloor t\alpha^n\rfloor$
is complete for every $t>0$ and every $1<\alpha<(1+\sqrt5)/2$: it is false at
one base $\gamma\approx1.25$ for unspecified large coefficients. The claim does
not classify the pairs $(t,\alpha)$ that
[[problems/additive_bases/E0349/_index|Problem 349]] asks for, says nothing at
other bases, and gives no explicit coefficient; the paper notes that van
Doorn's computer-assisted completeness region (the row of van Doorn's
Proposition 8 giving completeness for all $t\le5$ when $1.2<\alpha\le1.3$,
recorded on
[[problems/additive_bases/E0349/claims/2025_09_08_van_doorn|van Doorn's claim page]]
and on the Theorem 9 card) forces every such coefficient at this base past $5$.
The claim's value is `disproved`: the result refutes a conjectured completeness
region and proves no completeness.

**Argument.** As the paper describes it: a theorem of Dubickas (2006) gives,
for a Pisot or Salem number whose minimal polynomial $P$ has $P(1)=-q$ with
$q\ge2$, an element $\xi$ of $\mathbb Q(\gamma)$ whose multiples
$\xi\gamma^n$ all have fractional part within any prescribed distance of
$1/q$; an elementary sign adjustment (Proposition 11) makes the multiplier
positive when $q\ge3$, and doubling it makes every floor even. Here $P(1)=-5$,
and two sign checks place the root in $(6/5,13/10)$.

**Standing.** Claimed. A preprint with no journal record on the arXiv page; the
site's label is OPEN as of 2026-10-06, and no outside review is known. The
author submitted the claim to the thread on 2026-09-06 (the `discussion` link),
disclosing that the proof was found by Codex with GPT-6 Astra Ultra under a
writer-and-referee workflow the author directed, with Claude Opus 5 as a
referee, and that the author read and edited the proof and takes responsibility
for it. A forum comment of 2026-09-14 reported explicit values obtained with
ChatGPT, a coefficient $t\approx6.5\cdot10^9$ at this base and a second pair at
the Salem number $\approx1.1883$, and asked whether a smallest such base exists;
the values are a thread post. No review, refereed publication or Lean proof of
the result exists, so the claim lists no evidence.
