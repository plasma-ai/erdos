***

**Claim.** The set $\{n+1/n:n\in\mathbb{N}\}$ is strongly complete: for
every finite set $B$, every sufficiently large integer is a sum of distinct
elements of the set outside $B$. This is the case $p(x)=x$ of
[[problems/additive_bases/E0351/_index|Problem 351]]. It follows from
Theorem 3 of R. L. Graham, A theorem on partitions, J. Austral. Math. Soc. 3
(1963), no. 4, 435--441, received 17 March 1963, the date this page carries;
the library's result page
[[../library/unit_fractions/graham_1963_theorem_partitions/theorem_3|Theorem 3]]
records the statement. With $\alpha=1$ the theorem says that for every
$\beta>0$ there is $r(\beta)$ such that every integer $n>r(\beta)$ is a sum
$a_1+\cdots+a_k$ of distinct integers $a_i>\beta$ with $\sum1/a_i=1$. Then
$n+1=\sum_i(a_i+1/a_i)$ is a sum of distinct elements of the set with
indices above $\beta$. Taking $\beta$ above every index of the finite set
$B$ gives every integer above $r(\beta)+1$ as a sum of distinct elements
outside $B$, which is strong completeness.

**Covers.** The polynomial $p(x)=x$ only. The theorem decides nothing for
any other polynomial; the general case is the full claim on the
[[problems/additive_bases/E0351/claims/2026_05_03_price_barreto|Price–Barreto page]],
and the case $p(x)=x^2$ is
[[problems/additive_bases/E0351/claims/2025_09_15_van_doorn|van Doorn's claim]].

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the claim rests on the cited paper.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the paper appeared in the Journal of the
Australian Mathematical Society, volume 3 (1963), a refereed journal, and
the site's commentary records that Graham proved the statement when
$p(n)=n$. The site's label, PROVED (LEAN), settles the whole problem through
the Price–Barreto argument rather than this case, so the curator's credit is
not listed as `reviewed` evidence. The proof is not independently reviewed
in this corpus.
