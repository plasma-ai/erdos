***

**Claim.** Section 3 of Vsevolod F. Lev, *Reconstructing integer sets from their
representation functions*, Electron. J. Combin. 11 (2004), no. 1, #R78, builds a
single perfect difference set greedily. Step $n$ adds $z_n$ and $z_n+d_n$, where
$d_n$ is the least difference not yet represented and $z_n$ is chosen so that
neither number is already in the set and no nontrivial equation
$a_1-a_2=a_3-a_4$ arises among its elements. The paper observes that these
conditions exclude $O(n^3)$ values of $z_n$ and that $d_n=O(n^2)$, so the
numbers added at step $n$ are $O(n^3)$, which it states as the $n$th element of
the set being $O(n^3)$. For the $a_m$ of
[[problems/additive_bases/E1194/_index|Problem 1194]] this gives $a_m\ll m^3$:
the $d_n$ strictly increase, so a difference $m$ first represented at step $s$
satisfies $d_s\le m$ and hence $s\le m+1$, and both elements representing $m$
were added by step $s$, so $a_m=O(s^3)=O(m^3)$. The site's remarks record this
bound for the greedy construction and credit its details to Lev. The source card
is
[[../library/additive_bases/lev_2004_reconstructing_integer_sets_representation_functions/_index|lev_2004_reconstructing_integer_sets_representation_functions]].

**Covers.** An upper bound: some perfect difference set has $a_n\ll n^3$, so
$a_n/n$ need not grow faster than $n^2$. It does not determine how fast $a_n/n$
must grow.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the construction and its bound are in Section 3 of the
paper in the Electronic Journal of Combinatorics, published 2004-11-03; the step
from the paper's bound to $a_m$ is spelled out above. The site's remarks credit
the construction, but the site labels the problem OPEN, so the remarks are not
acceptance.
