**Claim.** On 2026-04-23 Liam Price posted in the thread of
[[problems/additive_bases/E1194/_index|Problem 1194]] a note in which GPT-5.4
Pro claims a partial solution. The site's curator summarized it the same day.
Write $A=\{x_1<x_2<\cdots\}$. If $a_n\ll n^c$ for every $n$, then
$x_k-x_{k-1}\gg x_k^{1/c}$, so $x_k\gg k^{c/(c-1)}$ and
$|A\cap[1,x]|\ll x^{1-1/c}$, and the counting argument in the site's remarks
then gives $a_n\gg n^{c/(2c-2)}$ infinitely often. Balancing the exponents at
$c=3/2$ gives the result as the curator states it: $a_n\gg n^{3/2}$ for
infinitely many $n$.

**Submission note.** Posted to the site's forum by Liam Price on 23 April 2026:

> GPT-5.4 pro claims a partial solution here.

**Covers.** A lower bound $a_n\gg n^{3/2}$ along infinitely many $n$. It does
not determine how fast $a_n/n$ must grow.
