***

**Claim.** [[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0140/_index|Problem 140]] is
proved by a second route. Theorem 1.1 of OpenAI, *Quasipolynomial Bounds for
Arithmetic Progressions* (release preprint dated 23 September 2026, the
claim's date; family 159 of the release; its intake card is
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/openai_2026_quasipolynomial_bounds_arithmetic_progressions/_index|openai_2026_quasipolynomial_bounds_arithmetic_progressions]]),
asserts that for each fixed integer $k\ge3$ there are constants
$C_k,c_k,\varepsilon_k>0$ with

$$
r_k(N)\le C_k\,N\exp\bigl(-c_k(\log N)^{\varepsilon_k}\bigr)
\qquad\text{for every }N\ge2,
$$

where $r_k(N)$ is the largest size of a subset of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ with no
$k$-term progression of positive common difference. At $k=3$ the factor
$\exp(-c_3(\log N)^{\varepsilon_3})$ is eventually below $(\log N)^{-C}$ for
every fixed $C>0$, so $r_3(N)\ll N/(\log N)^C$, the site's statement. For
each fixed $k\ge4$ the same theorem gives $r_k(N)\ll_{k,C}N/(\log N)^C$ for
every $C>0$. This is the extension to every $k$ that the site's commentary
records as conjectured in [ErGr80] and [Er81]. No earlier result proves it for
any $k\ge4$: the best earlier bounds are Green and Tao's $N/(\log N)^c$ for
one small $c>0$ at $k=4$ and Leng, Sah and Sawhney's
$N\exp(-(\log\log N)^{c_k})$ for $k\ge5$. The
paper's stated contribution is the all-length bound and its consequence
Corollary 1.2, Erdős's reciprocal-sum conjecture
([[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0003/_index|Problem 3]]); its
introduction (pp. 5--6) records the stronger three-term bound of Kelley and Meka
and says that no improvement of the three-term exponent is claimed. The
statement and Corollary 1.2 are on pp. 4--5 of the release PDF, and the
card's result page
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/openai_2026_quasipolynomial_bounds_arithmetic_progressions/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]]
records the statement; this corpus has not reviewed the proof (about 190
pages).

**Formal statement.** The release's `lean/` folder, at the pinned commit, proves
`OAI.Erdos3.manuscriptQuantitativeDensityTheorem`
(`OAI/Combinatorics/Progressions/Results/Conclusions.lean`): for every $k\ge3$
there are reals $C,c,\eta>0$ with
$\mathrm{extremalNumber}\,k\,N\le C\,N\exp(-c(\log\log N)^{1+\eta})$ for every
$N\ge3$, where `extremalNumber k N` (`Model.lean`) is the largest cardinality of
a subset of `Finset.Icc 1 N` with no $k$-term progression of positive
difference, that is, $r_k(N)$. This Lean bound is weaker than the paper's
Theorem 1.1 (a power of $\log\log N$ in the exponent, not of $\log N$). The
library lemma `OAI.Erdos3.QuantitativeDensityBound.logarithmic`
(`Estimates/UniformRelativePatchSource.lean`) turns the bound for one $k$ into:
for every real $B>0$ there is $C>0$ with
$\mathrm{extremalNumber}\,k\,N\le C\,N/(\log N)^B$ for every $N\ge3$. Applying
it to the theorem at $k=3$ gives exactly the problem's statement, and at any
$k\ge3$ the every-$k$ extension; that one-line combination is not a declaration
of the release and is stated here. The release's comparator challenge
`ComparatorChallenges/ErdosReciprocal.lean` pins only
`manuscriptReciprocalProgressionTheorem`, the reciprocal-sum consequence, which
does not by itself imply the problem's statement (a bound of order
$N/(\log N)^{3/2}$ would satisfy it and fail the problem at $C=2$);
`manuscriptQuantitativeDensityTheorem` is the first component of
`manuscript_main_theorems`, whose second component is the pinned
`manuscriptReciprocalProgressionTheorem`, and no challenge pins it or the lemma
`QuantitativeDensityBound.logarithmic`, which takes the bound as a hypothesis.
The release's scope note for the family (`lean/docs/159.md`) says the
quantitative bound is outside the formalized statement it selected.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki: the route is independent of Kelley and
Meka's accepted proof on
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0140/claims/2023_02_10_kelley_meka|its claim page]].

**Acceptance.** Formalized. This corpus's verification built
`OAI.Erdos3.manuscriptQuantitativeDensityTheorem` and
`OAI.Erdos3.QuantitativeDensityBound.logarithmic` at the pinned revision with
the toolchain `leanprover/lean4:v4.34.1` and checked their axioms, which for
each are exactly `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. No comparator
challenge pins either declaration, so neither has a fingerprint to match; their
statements were audited against the problem instead. `extremalNumber 3 N` is
exactly $r_3(N)$; the theorem at $k=3$ bounds it by
$C\,N\exp(-c(\log\log N)^{1+\eta})$ for every $N\ge3$, and the lemma turns that
bound into $r_3(N)\le C\,N/(\log N)^B$ for every real $B>0$ and every $N\ge3$,
which is the problem's statement. Combining the two is the one-line step stated
under **Formal statement.**, not a declaration of the release. The same
combination at each $k\ge4$ gives $r_k(N)\ll_{k,B}N/(\log N)^B$, the extension
the site's commentary records as conjectured, which lies outside the problem's
statement. The pinned `manuscriptReciprocalProgressionTheorem` gives this page
no support: it forces only $\sum_m2^{-m}r_3(2^m)<\infty$, which a bound of order
$N/(\log N)^{3/2}$ would satisfy. The acceptance is of the Lean statements so
audited, a second route to a problem already proved through Kelley and Meka, so
this page changes no standing. Not reviewed and not refereed: the preprint has
no journal record, arXiv version or published independent review, so its
Theorem 1.1, whose saving is stronger than the formalized one, and its roughly
190-page proof stay unreviewed; the release's README says its manuscripts were
produced by an internal OpenAI model and that its results are at different
stages of verification, and the site's page carried no comment or proof-claim
entry on 2026-10-07.
