***

**The claim.** The manuscript *Quasipolynomial bounds for arithmetic
progressions* of the OpenAI mathematics release, dated 23 September 2026 and
authored by OpenAI (its intake card is
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/openai_2026_quasipolynomial_bounds_arithmetic_progressions/_index|openai_2026_quasipolynomial_bounds_arithmetic_progressions]],
and the result is paged at
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/openai_2026_quasipolynomial_bounds_arithmetic_progressions/corollary_11_2|Corollary 11.2]]),
states in its Theorem 1.1 that for every fixed $k\ge3$ there are constants
$C_k,c_k,\varepsilon_k>0$ with

$$
r_k(N)\le C_kN\exp\bigl(-c_k(\log N)^{\varepsilon_k}\bigr)\qquad(N\ge2),
$$

where $r_k(N)$ is the largest size of a subset of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ with no
nonconstant $k$-term arithmetic progression, and deduces as Corollary 11.2
(Section 11.1, p. 82) that every $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ without a nonconstant
$k$-term progression satisfies

$$
\sum_{a\in A}\frac1a\le H_k:=\sum_{m\ge0}2^{-m}r_k(2^m)<\infty.
$$

The proof is a paragraph: a dyadic block $[2^m,2^{m+1})$ holds at most
$r_k(2^m)$ elements of $A$, each with reciprocal at most $2^{-m}$, and Theorem
1.1 makes the series converge. In the notation of
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0169/_index|Problem 169]] this is
$f(k)\le H_k$, so $f(k)$ is finite for every $k\ge3$. The constants
$C_k,c_k,\varepsilon_k$ are not explicit, so $H_k$ is a finite number with no
computed value; the manuscript does not name $f(k)$, $W(k)$ or the problem.
The release's README says that its manuscripts were produced by an internal
OpenAI model and that its results are at different stages of verification.

**Covers.** The finiteness of $f(k)$ for every $k\ge3$, with the explicit
form $H_k$ of a bound. Not covered: any numerical estimate of $f(k)$, which is
what the problem asks for, and the displayed question whether
$f(k)/\log W(k)\to\infty$. The site's commentary records Gerver's
observation that the finiteness of $f(k)$ for all $k$ is equivalent to
Erdős's reciprocal-sum conjecture,
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0003/_index|Problem 3]], which the same
manuscript settles through its Corollary 1.2, accepted in this corpus on
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0003/claims/2026_09_23_openai|the claim page of Problem 3]]
on Lean this corpus built and audited. By Gerver's equivalence the finiteness
of $f(k)$ for every $k\ge3$ follows from that accepted result, and it also
follows from the accepted theorem by an elementary argument: if the series
$\sum_m2^{-m}r_k(2^m)$ diverged, extremal progression-free subsets of
$\{1,\ldots,4^j\}$, translated into the windows $[2\cdot4^j,3\cdot4^j]$,
would form a progression-free set with divergent reciprocal sum. The bound
$f(k)\le H_k<\infty$ in the form this page states stays claimed, because no
accepted record derives it.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0139/claims/2026_09_23_openai|The release's r_k(N) bound on Problem 139]],
Theorem 1.1 of the same manuscript, from which the corollary is deduced; that
page is accepted on the release's Lean bound, a weaker saving than Theorem 1.1
that still makes $H_k$ finite, but the corollary's dyadic step is not formalized
and no accepted record derives it, so this page stays claimed. Gerver's lower
bound $f(k)\ge(1-o(1))k\log k$ and the other results the problem page cites are
prior work the manuscript neither uses nor cites for this corollary.

**Formalization.** The release's Lean tree at the pinned revision proves, in
`lean/OAI/Combinatorics/Progressions/Results/Conclusions.lean`, both the
reciprocal-sum theorem, the proposition that every set with divergent
reciprocal sum contains progressions of every length, pinned by the comparator
challenge `lean/ComparatorChallenges/ErdosReciprocal.lean`, and the unpinned
`OAI.Erdos3.manuscriptQuantitativeDensityTheorem`. The latter asserts
`QuantitativeDensityBound k` of `Model.lean` for every $k\ge3$: constants
$C,c,\eta>0$ with $r_k(N)\le CN\exp(-c(\log\log N)^{1+\eta})$ for every
$N\ge3$. That saving is weaker than Theorem 1.1's power of $\log N$, but it
is enough for convergence, since the terms $2^{-m}r_k(2^m)$ are eventually
below $m^{-2}$; so $f(k)\le H_k<\infty$ follows from a proved declaration
through the corollary's dyadic step, which is not formalized. No declaration
states Corollary 11.2 and no comparator pins the quantitative theorem; the
sentence in the release's page `lean/docs/159.md` that the quantitative bound
is outside the formalized statement refers to the pinned theorem. The
corpus's verification of the release accepted no declaration for this
problem, so no `formalized` evidence is listed.

**Acceptance.** None. The manuscript is a release preprint with no journal
record, no arXiv version and no published independent review; the site's label
is OPEN and its commentary (page last edited 4 April 2026) does not mention the
release. The claim is `claimed`, and the problem stays open: a partial claim
derives no standing.
