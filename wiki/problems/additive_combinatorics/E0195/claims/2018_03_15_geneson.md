***

**Claim.** There is a permutation of $\mathbb{Z}$ with no monotone six-term
arithmetic progression, that is, no subsequence $a_{i_1},\ldots,a_{i_6}$,
$i_1<\cdots<i_6$, that is an increasing or decreasing six-term progression
(Proposition 1 of the arXiv preprint). The permutation is
$0\,X_0^*X_1^*\cdots$, where $X_i^*$ arranges
$[10^i,10^{i+1})\cup(-10^{i+1},-10^i]$ with no monotone three-term
progression. So the largest $k$ of
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0195/_index|Problem 195]] is at most $5$.
J. Geneson, *Forbidden arithmetic progressions in permutations of subsets of
the integers*, Discrete Math. 342 (2019), no. 5, 1489–1491, posted as
arXiv:1803.06334 on 2018-03-15 and cited as [Ge19] on the problem page
([[../library/additive_combinatorics/geneson_2019_forbidden_arithmetic_progressions_permutations_subsets_integers/_index|source card]]).

**Covers.** The upper bound $k\le5$, improving the bound $k\le6$ that the
permutation of $\mathbb{Z}$ without monotone seven-term progressions of
Davis, Entringer, Graham and Simmons gives.
Superseded by Adenwalla's $k\le4$ on
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0195/claims/2022_11_05_adenwalla|Adenwalla's claim page]].
