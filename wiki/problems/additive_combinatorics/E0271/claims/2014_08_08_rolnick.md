***

**Claim.** Let $A(n)$ be the greedy sequence of
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0271/_index|Problem 271]], written
$S(0,n)$ in the paper's notation for Stanley sequences. Theorem 1.2 of
D. Rolnick, *On the classification of Stanley sequences*, European J. Combin.
59 (2017), 51--70 (arXiv:1408.1940; result labels are those of the arXiv
version), takes a positive integer $k$, a monotone decreasing family
$\mathcal A$ of subsets of $\{0,\ldots,k-1\}$ and the set $A$ of the sums
$\sum_{a\in B}3^a$ over $B\in\mathcal A$, and proves that $S(A\cup\{3^k\})$
and $S(A\cup\{2\cdot3^k\})$ are independent Stanley sequences, with closed-form
descriptions by ternary digits. The family $\{\emptyset\}$ gives $A=\{0\}$,
so the theorem proves the descriptions of $A(3^k)=S(0,3^k)$ and
$A(2\cdot3^k)=S(0,2\cdot3^k)$ for every $k\ge1$ that Odlyzko and Stanley had
stated without proof on
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0271/claims/1978_01_01_odlyzko_stanley|their claim page]];
the paper proves the $3^k$ case in full and describes the $2\cdot3^k$ proof
as very similar. An independent Stanley sequence is regular, and Corollary
2.9 (from Proposition 2.7, which gives $a_{2^j-\sigma}=\alpha3^j+\beta2^j$
for large $j$) proves that every regular Stanley sequence follows the first
of the two growth patterns of Odlyzko and Stanley: $a_n$ lies between
constant multiples of $n^{\log_23}$ for all large $n$.

**Covers.** The values $n=3^k$ and $n=2\cdot3^k$ for $k\ge1$: the explicit
description of the $a_k$ and their growth of order $k^{\log_23}$. Not
covered: $n=1$ and $n=2$ (the case $k=0$ is outside Theorem 1.2), every other
$n$, and the exact constants $\liminf a_k/k^{\log_23}=1/2$ and
$\limsup a_k/k^{\log_23}=1$ of Odlyzko and Stanley's Remark 2.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki: the proofs are the paper's own, and
the memorandum's unproved statements are not an input.
