**Claim.** For $C = 2$, $C = 3$, every $C = 2^s$ and every $C =
\tfrac12\binom{2s}{s}$, $s = 1, 2, \ldots$, there is a set $A \subseteq
\mathbb N$ in which every $n$ has at most $C$ representations $n = x + y$ with
$x, y \in A$, counted without regard to order and with $x = y$ counted once,
and some $n$ has exactly $C$, such that in every partition of $A$ into
finitely many parts one part again has some $n$ with exactly $C$
representations. In every construction the $C$ representations of such an $n$
are sums of two distinct elements, so dropping the sums $x + x$ leaves at most
$C$ representations of every $n$ and exactly $C$ at that $n$: the same sets
answer the corrected Statement of
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0328/_index|Problem 328]], whose count $f$
takes only sums of two distinct elements. The answer to the corrected
Statement is therefore no for these values of $C$, whether or not $t$ may
depend on $A$. The constructions take $n_1 < n_2 < \cdots$ with $n_{i+1} \geq
4 n_i$, so that an integer is a sum of distinct $n_i$ in at most one way. For
$C = 3$ (Theorem 1), $A$ is the set of sums $n_i + n_j$ with $i \neq j$: a sum
of four distinct $n_i$ has exactly three representations, and a finite
partition of $A$ colors the edges of the complete graph on the $n_i$, in which
Ramsey's theorem gives an infinite monochromatic complete subgraph, whose edge
sums lie in one part. Theorem 1' treats the other values: for $C = 2$ the sums
$n_i + n_j$ with $i \not\equiv j \pmod 2$, where a finite coloring of an
infinite complete bipartite graph has a monochromatic $C_4$; for $C = 2^s$ the
sums of $s + 1$ of the $n_i$ whose indices form a complete residue system
modulo $s + 1$; and for $C = \tfrac12\binom{2s}{s}$ the sums of $s$ distinct
$n_i$, with Ramsey's theorem for $s$-tuples. The paper states the general
conjecture, for every $k$, that Nešetřil and Rödl proved
([[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0328/claims/1985_01_01_nesetril_rodl|claim page]]),
and its note added in proof records their proof. The source is P. Erdős, *Some
applications of Ramsey's theorem to additive number theory*, European Journal
of Combinatorics **1** (1980), no. 1, 43–46, whose source card is
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/erdos_1980_applications_ramsey_s_theorem_additive_number/_index|Erdős 1980]];
the issue is dated March 1980 without a day, so the page is named by the first
of that month.

**Covers.** The values $C = 2$, $3$, $2^s$ and $\tfrac12\binom{2s}{s}$ for
$s \geq 1$. The paper says its methods should apply to other values of $k$ but
doubts that they work for every $k$, and cannot settle $k = 5$. Every integer
$C \geq 2$ is Nešetřil and Rödl's, on
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0328/claims/1985_01_01_nesetril_rodl|their claim page]].

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki: the constructions are the paper's own,
resting on Ramsey's theorem.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: European Journal of Combinatorics **1** (1980),
no. 1, 43–46, the DOI linked above. Reviewed is not listed. The site's label
settles the problem, and its commentary credits the full answer to Nešetřil
and Rödl. The commentary (page last edited 6 April 2026) credits this paper
only with the earlier answer for $C = 3$, $4$ and infinitely many other
values, and the problem lists no parts, so that credit is not acceptance.
Theorem 1′ also covers $C = 2$.
