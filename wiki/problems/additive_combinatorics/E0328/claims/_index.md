# problems/additive_combinatorics/E0328/claims

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0328/_index|..]]

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0328/claims/1980_03_01_erdos|1980_03_01_erdos]]: Erdős's 1980 sequences with at most C representations per sum that no finite
partition splits below C, for C = 2, 3, every power of two and every half
central binomial coefficient, by Ramsey's theorem; refereed.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0328/claims/1985_01_01_nesetril_rodl|1985_01_01_nesetril_rodl]]: For every integer C at least 2 there is a set of naturals whose pairwise sums
each have at most C representations and which no partition into finitely
many parts reduces to fewer than C representations in every part; answers no.

[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0328/claims/2026_06_18_axiommath|2026_06_18_axiommath]]: Answers the site's wording (ordered pairs, under which C = 2 fails
trivially), not the corrected Statement (sums of two distinct elements), so
it does not count toward the problem's standing. A Lean 4 development by
AxiomProver, published by AxiomMath, refutes that wording by the powers of
two at C = 2.
