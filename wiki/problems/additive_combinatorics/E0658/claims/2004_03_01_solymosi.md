***

**Claim.** The answer to
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0658/_index|Problem 658]] is yes. The
claimed result is Theorem 1.1 of J. Solymosi, *A note on a question of
Erdős and Graham*: for every real $\delta>0$ there is $N_0(\delta)$ such
that for $N>N_0$ every $S\subseteq[N]^2$ with $|S|\ge\delta N^2$ contains
a quadruple $\{(a,b),(a+d,b),(a,b+d),(a+d,b+d)\}$ with integer $d\ne0$,
where $[N]=\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$. The grid differs from the site's
$\{1,\ldots,N\}^2$ by a translation, and an axis-parallel square is a
square, so the theorem gives the site's conclusion in Graham's
axis-parallel form and hence in the form allowing any square. The proof
lifts $S$ to $S\times[N]\subseteq[N]^3$, where a square corresponds to a
quadruple $\{(a,b,c),(a+d,b,c),(a,b+d,c),(a+d,b+d,c+d)\}$, $d\ne0$, and
builds a four-partite $3$-uniform hypergraph whose vertices are the planes
parallel to the four faces of that configuration, with an edge for each
triple of planes from distinct classes that meets in a point of the lifted
set. Four planes, one from each class, form a complete subgraph when each
of their triples meets in the set: the four planes through one point give
a degenerate one, and a non-degenerate one is exactly a quadruple. If the
set contains no quadruple, every edge lies in exactly one complete
subgraph, so the Frankl–Rödl theorem gives $o(N^3)$ edges, while the
hypergraph has $4|S\times[N]|=4N|S|$ edges; so $|S|\ge\delta N^2$ forces a
quadruple once $N$ is large. The paper states no bound: its Remark says
that, because the Frankl–Rödl theorem rests on the regularity lemma, the
method's bound on $N_0$ is at best of tower type. The statement, the
one-page proof and the lifting are recorded on the
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/solymosi_2004_note_question_erdos_graham/_index|source card]];
the argument takes the Frankl–Rödl theorem as an external premise (claims
checked; proof not verified). The qualitative statement was known before,
from the
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0658/claims/1991_12_01_furstenberg_katznelson|density Hales–Jewett theorem]];
the paper's contribution is a combinatorial argument whose bound, though
not stated, is at best of tower type.
