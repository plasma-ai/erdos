***

**Claim.** The answer to
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0785/_index|Problem 785]] is yes. The
claimed result is the theorem of J.-H. Fang and Y.-G. Chen, *On additive
complements*, in the form the site's commentary records: if
$A,B\subseteq\mathbb N$ are infinite, $A+B$ contains every large integer and

$$
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{A(x)B(x)}{x}<\frac54,
$$

then $A(x)B(x)-x\to\infty$. The problem's hypothesis $A(x)B(x)\sim x$ gives
$\limsup=1$, so the theorem contains the problem's statement, which Sárközy
and Szemerédi had proved
([[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0785/claims/1994_09_01_sarkozy_szemeredi|their claim page]]);
its content is the weaker hypothesis. The same authors raised the threshold
to $3-\sqrt3$
([[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0785/claims/2014_08_01_fang_chen|their 2014 claim page]])
and showed in *On additive complements. II* (Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. (2011),
881--883, the site's [ChFa11]) that $3/2$ cannot be exceeded: there are
such $A,B$ with $\limsup A(x)B(x)/x=3/2$ and $A(x)B(x)-x=1$ for infinitely
many $x$. Chen's conjecture that $3/2$ is the true threshold is the subject
of the proof claim of
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0785/claims/2026_08_05_van_doorn_liu_tang|van Doorn, Liu and Tang]].
The library holds no copy; the statement follows the site's commentary and
the formal-conjectures variant `erdos_785.variants.chen_fang_limsup`, which
states the $3-\sqrt3$ form without proof, and the publication data follow
the Crossref record.
