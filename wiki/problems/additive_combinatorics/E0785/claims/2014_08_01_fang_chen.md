***

**Claim.** The answer to
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0785/_index|Problem 785]] is yes. The
claimed result is the theorem of J.-H. Fang and Y.-G. Chen, *On additive
complements. III*, in the form the site's commentary records: if
$A,B\subseteq\mathbb N$ are infinite, $A+B$ contains every large integer and

$$
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{A(x)B(x)}{x}<3-\sqrt3\approx1.268,
$$

then $A(x)B(x)-x\to\infty$. The problem's hypothesis $A(x)B(x)\sim x$ gives
$\limsup=1$, so the theorem contains the problem's statement, which Sárközy
and Szemerédi had proved
([[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0785/claims/1994_09_01_sarkozy_szemeredi|their claim page]]);
its content is the weaker hypothesis, raised from the $5/4$ of the authors'
first paper
([[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0785/claims/2010_02_05_fang_chen|their 2010 claim page]]).
The threshold cannot pass $3/2$, by the authors' construction of 2011
recorded on that page, and Chen's conjecture that $3/2$ is the true
threshold is the subject of the proof claim of
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0785/claims/2026_08_05_van_doorn_liu_tang|van Doorn, Liu and Tang]].
The library holds no copy; the statement follows the site's commentary and
the formal-conjectures variant `erdos_785.variants.chen_fang_limsup`, which
states it without proof, and the publication data follow the Crossref
record.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: J. Number Theory 141 (2014), 83--91,
doi:10.1016/j.jnt.2014.01.027; the Crossref record dates the issue to
August 2014, filled to the first of the month for this page's name.
Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas Bloom, labels the problem PROVED (LEAN)
and credits the improvement of $5/4$ to $3-\sqrt3$, as [ChFa14], in the
problem page's commentary; the curator had no part in the result. No review
by this project is recorded.
