***

**Claim.** Theorem 1.2 of T. Sanders, *The Erdős--Moser sum-free set
problem*, Canad. J. Math. 73 (2021), no. 1, 63--107, p. 2: "For every
finite set of integers $A$ we have $M(A)=\log^{1+\Omega(1)}|A|$", where
$M(A)$ is the largest size of a set $S\subset A$ whose restricted sumset
$\{s+s':s,s'\in S,\ s\ne s'\}$ is disjoint from $A$. The abstract states it
as an absolute constant $c>0$ with $M(A)\ge\log_3^{1+c}|A|$ for every
finite $A$, footnote 2 reconciling the two forms for small $|A|$. In the
notation of [[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0787/_index|Problem 787]],

$$
g(n)\ge(\log n)^{1+c}
$$

for an absolute $c>0$ and all large $n$. The theorem is stated for finite
sets of integers; Choi's observation that the real-set function equals the
integer one, which the problem page records, carries it to the site's sets
of real numbers. The proof strengthens the Sudakov--Szemerédi--Vu strategy
with Proposition 2.7, which replaces their fivefold exponential dependence
by $\exp(k^{C+o(1)})$. Cited as [Sa21] on the problem page. Library home
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/sanders_2021_erdos_moser_sum_free_set_problem/_index|sanders_2021_erdos_moser_sum_free_set_problem]];
result page
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/sanders_2021_erdos_moser_sum_free_set_problem/theorem_1_2|Theorem 1.2]].

**Covers.** The lower bound $g(n)\ge(\log n)^{1+c}$ for an absolute $c>0$,
the first lower bound a power of $\log n$ beyond the first. Not covered:
the order of growth of $g(n)$, which lies between this bound and
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0787/claims/2005_03_01_ruzsa|Ruzsa's]]
$\exp(O(\sqrt{\log n}))$.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0787/claims/1971_12_01_choi|Choi's 1971 paper]],
whose reduction of the real-set problem to sets of integers carries the
theorem to the site's formulation.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the paper is the publisher's version of record in
the Canadian Journal of Mathematics (the Crossref record gives online
publication on 23 September 2019, in the February 2021 issue); the page
is named by the first arXiv version, 1804.03356v1 of 10 April 2018. The
site's curator, Thomas F. Bloom, credits the lower bound to Sanders in the
problem page's commentary (label OPEN, page last edited 23 January 2026);
the problem is not marked settled there, so the credit is recorded here and
is not listed as `reviewed`. The statement is quoted from arXiv v3 of 31
July 2019; the proof is not checked in this corpus.
