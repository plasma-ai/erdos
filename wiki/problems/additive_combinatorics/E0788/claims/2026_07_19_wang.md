***

**Claim.** For the function $f(n)$ of
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0788/_index|Problem 788]] there are
absolute constants $c,C>0$ such that, for all sufficiently large $n$,

$$
c\sqrt{n\log n}\le f(n)\le n^{\frac12+C\left(\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}\right)^{1/3}},
$$

so that $f(n)=n^{1/2+o(1)}$. This answers the problem's particular question
yes and gives the order of growth of $f(n)$ up to the $o(1)$ in the
exponent, the exponent Choi conjectured in 1971. The claimed result is
Theorem 1.1 of S. Wang, *A Proposed Solution to Erdős Problem 788*, a
15-page manuscript in the author's public repository (the file
`788/paper.pdf`, PDF metadata dated 22 July 2026, linked above at the
repository's commit of 2 August 2026); library home
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/wang_2026_proposed_solution_erdos_problem_788/_index|wang_2026_proposed_solution_erdos_problem_788]],
result page
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/wang_2026_proposed_solution_erdos_problem_788/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]].
Its Remark 1.2 says the upper bound holds for every large $n$, not only
along a subsequence. The manuscript's $f(n)$ is the site's: the open
intervals $(n,2n)$ and $(2n,4n)$, sums of distinct elements, and the
greatest $t$ such that every $B$ admits an admissible $C$ with
$|B|+|C|\ge t$. The route, as the manuscript's plan and the author's claim
summary describe it: the identity $f(n)=\min_B(|B|+\alpha(G_B))$ over the
sum graph $G_B$ on the inner interval (Proposition 2.1); for the upper
bound, $B$ is built over $\mathbb F_p^{2r}$ as the union of the kernels of a
family of $p^{o(r)}$ surjective $\mathbb F_p$-linear maps to $\mathbb F_p^r$
that form a strong seeded extractor (Theorem 4.1), so that a $C$ avoiding
$B$ has, under each map, an image containing no pair $z,-z$ and so filling
at most about half of the target, while the extractor property makes some
map spread any large $C$ nearly evenly, a contradiction; the vectors are
then identified with base-$p$ integers and the sums produced by carries are
added to $B$; the lower bound combines a chromatic-number bound for a graph
whose edges use few label sums (Lemma 3.1) with the sparse-neighborhood
coloring theorem of Alon, Krivelevich and Sudakov. The author's note on the
site's claim tab says the proof was found by the author's AI pipeline using
GPT-5.6 Sol, and the manuscript's abstract ends "This proposed solution
was found by GPT-5."; the author is named alone.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Shouqiao
Wang (account ShouqiaoWang) on 19 July 2026, giving "GPT-5.6 Sol" as the AI
used:

> We prove the stronger version
> $$
> c\sqrt{n\log n}\le f(n)\le n^{1/2+O\left((\log\log n/\log n)^{1/3}\right)},
> $$
> and hence $f(n)=n^{1/2+o(1)}$. Think of $B$ as a small list of sums that has
> to catch a pair from every large $C$. Over $\mathbb F_p^{2r}$, we take $B$ to
> be the union of the kernels of several carefully chosen linear maps. If $C$
> avoids $B$, then the image of $C$ under any one of these maps cannot contain
> both $z$ and $-z$, since the corresponding two elements of $C$ would have a
> sum in the kernel. Thus every image occupies at most about half the target
> space. The maps are chosen so that every large $C$ is spread almost evenly by
> at least one of them, giving a contradiction. We then identify vectors with
> base-$p$ integers and include the extra sums caused by carries. The lower
> bound is a separate coloring argument for the associated sum graph. Notes: The
> proof is found by my AI pipeline using GPT-5.6 Sol. I will provide the lean
> formalisation of the proof soon!

**Formalization.** The same repository's Lake project `788/lean` (toolchain
`leanprover/lean4:v4.27.0`, Mathlib at `v4.27.0`), which the author added
to the claim thread in a comment of 23 July 2026, proves
`theorem erdos788 : MainTheorem` in `Erdos788/FinalTheorem.lean`. Its
`Definitions.lean` defines `f n` over `Finset.Ioo n (2 * n)` and
`Finset.Ioo (2 * n) (4 * n)` with distinct summands, which agrees with the
site's definition at statement level (open intervals, distinctness,
quantifier order), and `Statement.lean` defines `MainTheorem` as the
manuscript's two bounds, with the lower-bound constant $1/2000$ for every
$n\ge3$, together with the site's question in its $\varepsilon$ form.
Fifteen of the 43 modules and the root contain no `sorry`, `axiom` or
`native_decide` at the pinned commit; that check covers no other module.
The repository's workflow rejects those tokens by a text search and builds
the project, with one successful run listed (23 July 2026). The corpus has
not built the development, so this link is a posting of the claim and not
`formalized` evidence.
