***

**Claim.** E. G. Straus, *On a problem in combinatorial number theory*, J.
Math. Sci. 1 (1966), 77--80, cited as [St66] on the problem page: an
admissible subset of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$, one in which two sums of distinct
elements with different numbers of summands never coincide, has at most
$(4/\sqrt3+o(1))\sqrt N$ elements. The paper is not held and has no DOI,
and the theorem is stated as two later sources give it. Erdős, Nicolas and
Sárközy (Sém. Théor. Nombres Bordeaux (2) 3 (1991), 55--72) state Straus's
Theorem 2 as their Lemme 1 (p. 56), $P(\mathcal A,k)\ge k(|\mathcal A|-k)+1$
for the number $P(\mathcal A,k)$ of integers that are sums of exactly $k$
distinct elements of $\mathcal A$, and use Straus's Theorem 4 in the form
of their Lemme 2 (p. 57), $F(N)<\frac4{\sqrt3}N^{1/2}+1$ for the largest
size $F(N)$ of an admissible subset of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$, proved there from
Lemme 1 by following Straus's proof; Deshouillers and Freiman (Astérisque
258 (1999), p. 141) report Straus's bound as $(4/\sqrt3+o(1))\sqrt N$.
Every admissible subset of the witness $A=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ obeys this bound,
so in the notation of
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0789/_index|Problem 789]]

$$
h(n)<\frac4{\sqrt3}n^{1/2}+1,
$$

a one-line deduction the problem page records. Library result page of the
1991 reproof:
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/erdos_1991_sommes_de_sous_ensembles/lemme_2|Lemme 2]].

**Covers.** The upper bound $h(n)\ll n^{1/2}$. Not covered: the order of
growth of $h(n)$, which lies between this bound and
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0789/claims/1974_04_01_choi|Choi's]]
$(n\log n)^{1/3}$, and the sharper constant of Deshouillers and Freiman's
Theorem 1, $h(n)\le2\sqrt{n+1/4}-1$ for $n\ge N_0$.

**Depends on.**
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/erdos_1991_sommes_de_sous_ensembles/lemme_2|Lemme 2 of Erdős, Nicolas and Sárközy]],
the form of the bound in which this corpus reads it.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the paper appeared in the Journal of
Mathematical Sciences (Delhi), volume 1 (1966), as its zbMATH record (Zbl
0149.28503) gives it; the volume carries no publication day, so this page
is named by the first day of its year. The site's curator, Thomas F. Bloom,
credits $h(n)\ll n^{1/2}$ to Straus in the problem page's commentary (label
OPEN); the problem is not marked settled there, so the credit is recorded
here and is not listed as `reviewed`. The theorem is stated from the two
later sources; the proof of Lemme 2 has no independent review.
