***

**Claim.** H. Rohrbach, *Ein Beitrag zur additiven Zahlentheorie*, Math. Z.
**42** (1937), 1--30. A system of $k$ non-negative integers, the zero
counted, is a $2$-basis for $n$ if every integer $0,1,\ldots,n$ is a sum of
two of its elements; the least such $k$ is the $g(n)$ of
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0791/_index|Problem 791]] (p. 4). The
paper proves:

- Satz 3 (p. 5): a minimal $2$-basis for $n>1$ has fewer than $2\sqrt n$
  elements, that is $g(n)<2\sqrt n$, by the explicit basis (6) of Satz 2;
- the Folgerung to Satz 6 (pp. 14--15): every $2$-basis of $k$ elements for
  $n$ has $n\le k^2/2$, so $g(n)^2\ge2n$ whenever $g(n)\ge5$;
- inequality (47) (p. 18): every $2$-basis of $k$ elements for $n$ has
  $n<0.4992\,k^2$ once $k$ is large enough.

Since $g(n)\ge\sqrt{2n}\to\infty$, (47) applies to a minimal basis for all
large $n$ and gives $n<0.4992\,g(n)^2$, that is
$g(n)^2>(2.0032\ldots)n$ for large $n$. Together with Satz 3 this is the
site's $(2+c)n\le g(n)^2\le4n$, with $c=0.0032$.

**Covers.** The bounds $g(n)<2\sqrt n$ for $n>1$ and $g(n)^2>(2.0032\ldots)n$
for large $n$. Not covered: the estimate of $g(n)$ beyond these constants.
The paper's conjecture $n_2(k)=k^2/4+O(k)$ (p. 9) is the problem's "in
particular" question and is not a claim; it is refuted on
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0791/claims/1976_11_01_hammerer_hofmeister|Hämmerer and Hofmeister's claim page]]
and
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0791/claims/1979_04_01_mrose|Mrose's]].
