***

**Claim.** G. Yu, *A new upper bound for finite additive $h$-bases*, J.
Number Theory **156** (2015), 95--104. For $h=2$, with $n(k)$ the maximal
range of an additive $2$-basis of size $k$, the paper proves

$$
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{n(k)}{k^2}\le0.4585,
$$

the statement as J. Kohonen, *An improved lower bound for finite additive
2-bases*, J. Number Theory **174** (2017), p. 1, quotes it; the paper itself
is not held, and its bounds for other $h$ are not recorded here. In the
notation of [[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0791/_index|Problem 791]],
$g(n)=\min\{k:n(k)\ge n\}$ and $g(n)\to\infty$; if
$n(k)\le(0.4585+\varepsilon)k^2$ for all large $k$, then for large $n$
$n\le n(g(n))\le(0.4585+\varepsilon)g(n)^2$, so

$$
g(n)^2\ge\Bigl(\frac1{0.4585}-o(1)\Bigr)n,\qquad\frac1{0.4585}=2.1810\ldots,
$$

the lower bound the site's commentary gives as $2.181\cdots$.

**Covers.** The lower bound $g(n)^2\ge(1/0.4585-o(1))n$ for the estimate of
$g(n)$. Not covered: the value of $\lim g(n)^2/n$, or whether it exists.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the paper is published in the Journal of Number
Theory (Crossref: 2015-11), which dates this page. The site's curator,
Thomas F. Bloom, cites the bound in the problem's commentary, but the site
labels the problem OPEN, so the citation is not listed as `reviewed`. The
proof is not reviewed in this corpus.
