***

**Claim.** J. Kohonen, *An improved lower bound for finite additive
2-bases*, J. Number Theory **174** (2017), 518--524 (arXiv:1606.04770),
equation (1), p. 1. A set $A$ of non-negative integers is an additive
$2$-basis of size $k=|A|$ (the zero counted) and range $n(A)$ if $A+A$
contains $0,1,\ldots,n(A)$ but not $n(A)+1$; with $n(k)$ the maximal range
over bases of size $k$, the paper proves

$$
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{n(k)}{k^2}\ge\frac{85}{294}>0.2891,
$$

by a generalized Mrose basis built from three elementary segments placed at
multiples of $t^2$ (Theorem 1, pp. 3--4). In the notation of
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0791/_index|Problem 791]],
$g(n)=\min\{k:n(k)\ge n\}$; if $n(k)\ge(85/294-\varepsilon)k^2$ for all
$k\ge k_0(\varepsilon)$, then for large $n$ the integer
$k=\lceil\sqrt{n/(85/294-\varepsilon)}\rceil$ has $n(k)\ge n$, so
$g(n)\le k$ and

$$
g(n)^2\le\Bigl(\frac{294}{85}+o(1)\Bigr)n,\qquad\frac{294}{85}=3.4588\ldots,
$$

the upper bound the site's commentary gives as $3.458\cdots$. Since
$294/85<4$, the bound also gives $\limsup g(n)/\sqrt n<2$, so the guess
$g(n)\sim2n^{1/2}$ is false; that refutation was first in print in
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0791/claims/1976_11_01_hammerer_hofmeister|Hämmerer and Hofmeister's paper]]
and is also
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0791/claims/1979_04_01_mrose|Mrose's]].

**Covers.** The upper bound $g(n)^2\le(294/85+o(1))n$ for the estimate of
$g(n)$. Not covered: the value of $\lim g(n)^2/n$, or whether it exists.
