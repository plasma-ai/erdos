***

**Claim.** The answer to
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0818/_index|Problem 818]] is yes, with
$C=1$. The claimed result is Theorem 2.1 of J. Solymosi, *Bounding
multiplicative energy by the sumset*: for every finite set $A$ of positive
reals,

$$
\lvert AA\rvert\,\lvert A+A\rvert^2
\ge\frac{\lvert A\rvert^4}{4\lceil\log\lvert A\rvert\rceil},
$$

so if $\lvert A+A\rvert\le K\lvert A\rvert$ then

$$
\lvert AA\rvert\ge\frac{\lvert A\rvert^2}{4K^2\lceil\log\lvert A\rvert\rceil},
$$

the site's inequality with one logarithm in place of a power of the
logarithm. The bound on $\lvert AA\rvert\,\lvert A+A\rvert^2$ is sharp up to
the logarithm, as $A=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ shows. The proof covers $A\times A$ by
the lines through the origin and uses that the sumsets of points on
consecutive rays are disjoint inside $(A+A)\times(A+A)$; the digest is on
the
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/solymosi_2009_bounding_multiplicative_energy_sumset/_index|source card]].
The site asks about finite sets of integers, which may contain $0$ and
numbers of both signs; the nonzero members of one sign number at least
$(\lvert A\rvert-1)/2$, negating them changes neither the size of the sumset
nor the size of the product set, and both sizes are monotone under taking
subsets, so the theorem for positive reals gives the integer statement with
a worse absolute constant. The public Lean proof below carries this
reduction out, with the constant $324K^2$ for every real set of at least
five elements. The paper's Corollary 2.2, the sum-product exponent
$\max(\lvert A+A\rvert,\lvert AA\rvert)\gg\lvert A\rvert^{4/3}/\log^{1/3}\lvert A\rvert$,
is the result for which the paper is best known and is not the site's
question. The statement is recorded from the card's reading of the paper,
which records the read depth.

with at least two elements,

$$
\lvert A\rvert^2\le4c^2\lceil\log_2\lvert A\rvert\rceil\,\lvert AA\rvert
\quad\text{when }\lvert A+A\rvert\le c\lvert A\rvert,

and `erdos_problem_818_general` for every finite set of reals with at least
five elements, with the constant $324c^2$; its closing comment records
