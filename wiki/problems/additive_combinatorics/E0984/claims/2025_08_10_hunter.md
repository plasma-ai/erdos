***

**Claim.** The answer to
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0984/_index|Problem 984]] is yes: there
is a $2$-coloring of $\mathbb{N}$ such that every monochromatic $k$-term
arithmetic progression $\{a,a+d,\ldots,a+(k-1)d\}$ satisfies
$k\ll_\epsilon a^\epsilon$ for every $\epsilon>0$. The claimed result is the
argument Zach Hunter posted on the problem's discussion thread on 10 August
2025, which gives the stronger bound $k\le\exp((\log a)^{1/2+o(1)})$. The
argument, as the post gives it: split $\mathbb{N}$ into the intervals
$I_t=[100^t,100^{t+1})$; a progression $P$ of $k>10^4$ terms either (i)
places a $3$-term subprogression in an odd-indexed interval and another in
an even-indexed one, or (ii) has, for some $t$ with $\min P\ge c\cdot100^t$
for an absolute $c>0$, more than $k/100$ of its terms in $I_t$; color each
odd-indexed interval by a coloring with no red $3$-term progression and no
blue $k_t$-term progression, and each even-indexed interval by a coloring
of the same kind with the roles of red and blue exchanged, where $k_t$ is
subpolynomial in $\lvert I_t\rvert$. The colorings of the intervals come from Green's
construction for the off-diagonal van der Waerden numbers $w(3,k)$ (Forum
Math. Pi 10 (2022), Paper No. e18, doi:10.1017/fmp.2022.12;
[arXiv:2102.01543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01543)) as sharpened in
Hunter's own paper (Combinatorica 42 (2022), 1231--1252,
doi:10.1007/s00493-022-4925-2;
[arXiv:2111.01099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.01099)), both journal records
checked, which give
$k_t\le\exp((\log\lvert I_t\rvert)^{1/2+o(1)})$. A monochromatic
progression of the first kind is impossible, since one of its $3$-term
subprogressions has the forbidden color, and one of the second kind has
fewer than $100k_t$ terms; since $\lvert I_t\rvert=O(\min I_t)=O(a)$ in
case (ii), this gives $k<100k_t\le\exp((\log a)^{1/2+o(1)})$, and in
particular $k\ll_\epsilon a^\epsilon$. The post adds that the exponent $1/2$ is a
barrier for the present constructions. Spencer had shown the analogous
statement with three colors and a very slowly growing bound in place of
$a^\epsilon$, and Erdős ([Er80], p. 92) reports a $2$-coloring with
$k\ll a^{1-c}$ for an absolute $c>0$ and knows no nontrivial lower bound;
the source card
[[../library/number_theory/erdos_1980_survey_problems_combinatorial_number_theory/_index|erdos_1980_survey_problems_combinatorial_number_theory]]
holds that survey.

**Submission note.** Posted to the site's forum by Zach Hunter on 10 August
2025:

> This has a positive answer, using Ben's nice construction for off-diagonal van
> der Waerden numbers. Let $I_t$ denote the discrete interval
> $[100^t,100^{t+1})$.
>
> We first observe that if $P$ is any $k$-AP $P$ (with $k>10000$ say), then
> either: i) there is an odd $t_1$ and an even $t_2$ so that $P\cap I_{t_1}$ and
> $P\cap I_{t_2}$ both contain a $3$-AP; ii) there is $t$ so that $\min(P) =
> \Omega(\min(I_t))$, so that $|P\cap I_t|>k/100$.
>
> Now for $t$ odd, we fix some red-blue coloring of $I_t$ with no red
> monochromatic $3$-AP, and no blue monochromatic $k_t$-AP, where $k_t$ is
> subpolynomial in $|I_t| = O(\min(I_t))$ (such colorings exist by: this paper).
> For $t$ even, we use a red-blue coloring of $I_t$ with no blue monochroamtic
> $3$-AP, and no red monochromatic $k_t$-AP.
>
> Now consider any arithmetic progression $P$ which is allegedly monochromatic
> under this coloring $C$. If $|P|< 10000=O(1)$, there is nothing to worry
> about. Otherwise, by our dichotomy we have that either (i) or (ii) holds. If
> (i) holds, $P$ cannot be monochromatic by design (we have that $I_{t_1} \cap
> P$ cannot be fully red (as it contains a $3$-AP), while $I_{t_2}\cap P$ cannot
> be fully blue). And if (ii) holds, then we can find $t$ so that $|P|
> <100|I_t\cap P|<100k_t$, and with $k_t = |I_t|^{o(1)} = O(\min(P))^{o(1)}$.
>
> QED!
>
> The best upper bound I know for $k_t$ is $\exp(\log^{1/2+o(1)}|I_t|)$ (from my
> own work), which one might expect to be close to tight when the other color
> avoids $3$-APs. If we instead forbid $2^{s-1}+1$-APs in the other color, one
> might expect to get a better off-diagonal bound of
> $\exp(\log^{1/s+o(1)}|I_t|)$. However it is open to push past the
> $1/2$-barrier (which I think is a lovely problem that maybe is not as
> well-known as it should be).
>
> (The site has been updated to address this comment.)

**Postings.** The thread post of 10 August 2025 is the only write-up by
Hunter. Boris Alexeev's lean-proofs repository holds a Lean 4 file
`src/latest/ErdosProblems/Erdos984.lean`, added 2026-08-18 and linked above
at the revision of 2026-08-24 that last changed it, whose header declares it
a formalization of a solution to Problem 984 with Zach Hunter as informal
author and the systems Codex and GPT-5.6 Sol as formal authors. Its theorem
`erdos_984` states that there is a coloring $\mathbb{N}\to\mathrm{Bool}$
such that for every $\epsilon>0$ some $A>0$ has $k\le A\,a^\epsilon$ for
every monochromatic $k$-term progression with $a\ge1$ and $d\ge1$; the
construction is assembled from a `HunterFamily` module of off-diagonal
colorings, and the file closes by printing the theorem's axioms. It is not
on the lists of Lean the corpus has built and audited, so it gives no
`formalized` evidence and is a `formalization` link on this page rather
than a claim of its own. No formal-conjectures statement exists, and the
site's formalization indicator reads No.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki: the inputs are the two published
van der Waerden constructions cited above, neither of which is carded.
