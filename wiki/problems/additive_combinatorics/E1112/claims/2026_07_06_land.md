**Claim.** For integers $k\ge3$ and $1\le d_1<d_2$, the question of
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E1112/_index|Problem 1112]] has the answer yes
exactly when $d_2\ge k+1$. The dichotomy answers the Statement (precise) of the
problem for every triple $(k,d_1,d_2)$. Above the threshold a ratio linear in
$d_2$ works: every sequence $B=\{b_1<b_2<\cdots\}$ of positive integers with
$b_{i+1}\ge rb_i$ admits a sequence $A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots\}$ with
$d_1\le a_{i+1}-a_i\le d_2$ for all $i$ and $kA\cap B=\emptyset$, for $r=192d_2$
in the original development and for $r=d_2+2$ in the September revision of the
paper. When $d_2\le k$ no ratio works, and more: for every sequence
$R_1,R_2,\ldots$ of positive integers there is one $B$ with $b_{i+1}\ge R_ib_i$
that meets $kA$ for every such $A$. In the notation of the site's commentary
(page last edited 28 December 2025), $r_k(d_1,d_2)$ exists if and only if
$d_2\ge k+1$, and then $r_k(d_1,d_2)\le192d_2$, independently of $d_1$, with the
revision claiming $r_k(d_1,d_2)\le d_2+2$; this agrees with the nonexistence of
$r_3(2,3)$ that Bollobás, Hegyvári and Jin proved ([BHJ97], on
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E1112/claims/1997_10_01_bollobas_hegyvari_jin|its claim page]]).
The write-up is J. Land, *A sharp dichotomy for bounded-gap sumsets*, Theorem 1
(eight pages, dated September 2026), at the pinned commit of 2026-09-13. Its
existence half places, by a nested-interval argument, a real $\theta$ with
$\{b_n\theta\}$ in a short fixed interval for all large $n$, and takes $A$ to be
the integer parts of $\alpha(m+i)$ with $\alpha=1/\theta$ in $(d_2-1,d_2]$: the
gaps are $d_2-1$ or $d_2$, and no $k$-fold sum of members of $A$ is a remaining
term of $B$. Its nonexistence half shows that, for $d_2\le k$, the $k$-fold
sumset of every admissible $A$ contains a full congruence class from some point
on, through a density consequence of Kneser's theorem and a finite subset-sum
lemma (a set $G$ of at least three positive integers with greatest common
divisor $1$ and maximum $M$ has a multiset of at most $M-1$ members of $G$ whose
subset sums contain $M$ consecutive integers), and then builds one $B$ meeting
every such tail. The $k$-fold sumset allows repeated summands, as the site's
$kA$ does. The result was first posted on the problem's discussion thread on
2026-07-06, with the original development (existence bound $192d_2$, archived on
Zenodo on 2026-07-25 as the record linked above), and submitted as a proof claim
on 2026-07-17; on 2026-09-13 the paper and the Lean proof were replaced by the
shortened argument described here, which the paper says is based on a manuscript
supplied by Stijn Cambie and which improves the bound to $d_2+2$. The revision's
proof is not checked in this corpus.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Johan Land
(account JohanLand) on 17 July 2026, giving "Fable 5, Opus 4.8, GPT 5.5 Pro" as
the AI used:

> Note: Proof previously discussed in the comment section before this "proof
> claim" functionality was added to the site. Briefly: For $k \ge 3$,
> $r_k(d_1,d_2)$ exists iff $d_2 \ge k+1$, with $r_k \le 192,d_2$ -
> independently of $d_1$. Existence: a Beatty sequence with slope $\gamma \in
> (k, d_2)$ confines $kA$ to width-$k$ windows, and a nested-interval argument
> steers $\gamma$ past every element of a lacunary $B$. Non-existence: a single
> diagonal $B$ works once every admissible $kA$ contains a full congruence class
> eventually; this reduces, via word combinatorics for two-letter gap alphabets
> (Sturmian case included) and a subset-sum lemma for larger ones - every finite
> $G$ with $\gcd(G)=1$, $|G|\ge 3$ admits a multiset of at most $\max G - 1$
> elements whose subset sums contain $\max G$ consecutive integers. Context:
> Everything is verified in Lean (see link below). More info: I've structured a
> repo to ease consumption of the proof:
> https://github.com/beetree/math_erdos_1112

**Formalization.** The repository's `lean/Erdos1112.lean` defines
`RatioWorks k d₁ d₂ r` (every $B$ with $b_1\ge1$, strictly increasing and
$r\,b_i\le b_{i+1}$ admits an $A$ with $a_1\ge1$, gaps in $[d_1,d_2]$ phrased
additively, and `kFoldSumset k a` disjoint from the range of $B$) and
`Question k d₁ d₂` as the existence of such a natural ratio; a bridge
`question_iff_questionInt` proves the equivalence with the problem's integer
ratio. `lean/Erdos1112Proof/Final.lean` proves `erdos_1112`:
`Question k d₁ d₂ ↔ k + 1 ≤ d₂` under `3 ≤ k`, `1 ≤ d₁` and `d₁ < d₂`, with
`erdos_1112_int` for the integer form, `erdos_1112_existence_bound` for the
ratio $d_2+2$ and `erdos_1112_strong_nonexistence` for the varying-ratio form.
The toolchain is Lean `v4.27.0`; the repository's readme says its audit file
`AxiomsCheck.lean` permits only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.
These files show no `sorry` at the pinned commit of 2026-09-13; this corpus has
not built, replayed or audited that revision. Boris Alexeev's lean-proofs
repository added on 2026-08-26 a port of the original development (existence
bound $192d_2$) to Lean 4.33, under Land's name as informal and formal author
and citing the thread post and the original repository
(`src/latest/ErdosProblems/Erdos1112.lean`, linked above at a pinned commit);
this corpus built that port, as the Acceptance paragraph records.

**Acceptance.** Formalized. This corpus's verification built Boris Alexeev's
lean-proofs repository at the pinned commit `8822f7dd` of 2026-09-15, linked
above (its `src/latest` project, Lean `v4.33.0`, Mathlib `v4.33.0`), whose
module `ErdosProblems/Erdos1112.lean` and development
`ErdosProblems/Erdos1112/` are the port of Land's original development of
July 2026, existence ratio $192d_2$, credited by the port's header to Land
as informal and formal author; the September revision in Land's repository
was not built, so the ratio $d_2+2$ is not formalized here. The verification
checked the axioms of the four final theorems `Erdos1112.erdos_1112`,
`Erdos1112.erdos_1112_int`, `Erdos1112.erdos_1112_strong_nonexistence` and
`Erdos1112.erdos_1112_existence_bound`, which are exactly `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. The comparator challenge
`ComparatorChallenges/ErdosProblems/Erdos1112.lean` of the same project pins
the four declarations with the definitions their types reach
(`IsLacunaryWith`, `IsLacunaryWithInt`, `IsVarLacunaryWith`, `HasGapsIn` and
`kFoldSumset`), and the fingerprint of each declaration was found identical
to the challenge's. The statements were audited clause by clause against the
problem's Statement: `erdos_1112` proves that for all $k\ge3$ and
$1\le d_1<d_2$ some natural ratio $r$ works if and only if $k+1\le d_2$, and
`erdos_1112_int` proves the same for an integer $r$, the site's wording; $B$
is infinite, strictly increasing and positive with $b_{i+1}\ge rb_i$, $A$ is
infinite and positive with gaps in $[d_1,d_2]$ stated additively, $kA$
allows repeated summands, and $kA\cap B=\emptyset$ is taken literally. A
finite $A$ would make the question trivial, and allowing a finite $B$
changes nothing. The build certifies the full answer this page claims, with
the varying-ratio nonexistence for $d_2\le k$
(`erdos_1112_strong_nonexistence`) and the ratio $192d_2$ above the
threshold (`erdos_1112_existence_bound`); it does not certify the ratio
$d_2+2$. Not reviewed: on the discussion thread the site's curator, Thomas
F. Bloom, wrote on 2026-07-13 that the main theorem `erdos_1112` of the
original development is a correct formalization of the claim and that the
code compiles with no `sorry`, and that the curator had not yet tried to
understand the proof; the site's label is OPEN (LEAN), its commentary (page
last edited 28 December 2025) does not mention the claim, and the community
database's formalization pointer names Land's repository as the solution; the
curator has not marked the problem settled, so no `reviewed` evidence is
listed. Not refereed: the write-up is posted in Land's repository and archived
on Zenodo, with no journal publication.
