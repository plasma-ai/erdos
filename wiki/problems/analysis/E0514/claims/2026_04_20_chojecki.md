***

**Claim.** The first two questions of
[[problems/analysis/E0514/_index|Problem 514]] have the answer yes.
P. Chojecki, *A note on an Erdős path problem for transcendental entire
functions*, a seven-page note dated 20 April 2026 and posted in the
problem's discussion thread the same day, proves in its Theorem 1 that
every transcendental entire function $f$ has a path to infinity $\gamma$
with

$$
\frac{\log\lvert f(\gamma(t))\rvert}{\log\lvert\gamma(t)\rvert}\to\infty
\quad(t\to\infty),
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\Bigl\lvert\frac{f(\gamma(t))}{\gamma(t)^n}\Bigr\rvert\to\infty
\text{ for every fixed }n,
$$

and that the same path satisfies $\ell_\gamma(R)=O(M(R,f)^\varepsilon)$
as $R\to\infty$ for every $\varepsilon>0$, where $\ell_\gamma(R)$ is the
length of the initial segment of $\gamma$ up to its first exit from the
disk $\lvert z\rvert<R$ and $M(R,f)$ the maximum modulus. The proof
applies Theorem B of Wu's 1985 paper (quoted as the note's Theorem 4) to
$u=\max\{\log\lvert f\rvert,-1\}$, whose hypothesis
$\max_{\lvert z\rvert=r}u/\log r\to\infty$ follows from Cauchy's estimate
for a transcendental $f$ (Lemma 3); the theorem gives the path and the
convergence of $\int_\gamma e^{-\delta u}\lvert\mathrm{d}z\rvert$ for
every $\delta>0$, and the length bound follows by writing
$1=e^{\varepsilon u}e^{-\varepsilon u}$ along the segment and bounding
$u$ by $\log M(R,f)$ there. The note's Theorem 2 constructs, from Theorem
1.4 of Langley's 2022 paper, one transcendental entire $f=e^G$ such that
for every unbounded connected set $E$ and every $\varepsilon>0$ there are
$w_k\in E$ with $\lvert w_k\rvert\to\infty$ and
$\lvert f(w_k)\rvert\le M(\lvert w_k\rvert,f)^\varepsilon$; so no fixed
power of $M(r,f)$ is a lower bound along a path for every $f$, which
answers the power example of the third question no. By the note's own
Remark 7 this does not settle the third question as the problem page
reads it, with an arbitrary fixed comparison function; that reading is
answered on
[[problems/analysis/E0514/claims/2026_04_28_oriike|Oriike's page]]. The
thread post announcing the note says that GPT-5.4 Pro produced the proof.
The statement follows the note's print.

**Submission note.** Posted to the site's forum by Przemek Chojecki on 20 April
2026:

> GPT-5.4 Pro claims to have a proof of the first question (+ second) and a
> negative answer to the third. Here are the notes. Mostly used is a pretty
> recent result of Langley, and also Wu.

**Covers.** The first question (the part `path`), answered yes, and the
second (the part `length`), answered yes with the bound
$\ell_\gamma(R)=O(M(R,f)^\varepsilon)$ for every $\varepsilon>0$. Not
covered: the third question (the part `growth`); Theorem 2 refutes only
its power example.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/analysis/E0514/claims/1984_12_01_lewis_rossi_weitsman|Lewis,
Rossi and Weitsman 1984]], the path theorem the note applies in Wu's
restatement, an accepted page; the length estimate and Theorem 2 are the
note's own, with Langley's theorem as outside input.
