***

**Claim.** Let $f(z)=\sum_{k\ge1}a_kz^{n_k}$ be an entire function with
$a_k\ne0$ for every $k$. G. Pólya, *Untersuchungen über Lücken und
Singularitäten von Potenzreihen*, Math. Z. 29 (1929), no. 1, 549--640,
doi:10.1007/BF01180553, proves that if $f$ has finite order and

$$
\limsup_{k\to\infty}(n_{k+1}-n_k)=\infty,
$$

then $f$ takes every complex value infinitely often. The statement follows
the site's account of the paper, which is cited there as [Po29]; the paper
is not held in this repository, so its own numbering of the result and its
proof are not reconstructed here.

**Covers.** Every instance of [[problems/analysis/E0517/_index|Problem 517]]
in which $f$ has finite order. The question assumes $n_k/k\to\infty$, and
this forces Pólya's gap condition: if the gaps were bounded, say
$n_{k+1}-n_k\le G$ for all $k\ge k_0$, then $n_k\le n_{k_0}+G(k-k_0)$ and
$n_k/k$ would stay bounded. So for an entire function of finite order the
answer is yes. The case of infinite order is not covered and remains open
beyond the subclass with $\sum1/n_k<\infty$, which
[[problems/analysis/E0517/claims/1927_01_01_biernacki|Biernacki 1927]] and
[[problems/analysis/E0517/claims/1983_01_01_murai|Murai 1983]] settle.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki: the theorem is the paper's own, and
the reduction of the question's hypothesis to the paper's is the elementary
bound above.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: a journal publication, Mathematische Zeitschrift
29 (1929), no. 1, the DOI linked above; the publisher's record dates the
issue December 1929, filled to the first of the month for this page's name.
Reviewed is not listed: the site credits the theorem in its commentary on a
problem it labels OPEN, which is commentary and not acceptance of a
solution. Formalized is not listed: no Lean statement or proof of the
theorem is recorded.
