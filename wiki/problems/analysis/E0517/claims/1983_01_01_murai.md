***

**Claim.** Let $f(z)=\sum_{k\ge1}a_kz^{n_k}$ be an entire function with
$a_k\ne0$ for every $k$ and $\sum_k1/n_k<\infty$ (Fejér gaps). Takafumi
Murai, *The deficiency of entire functions with Fejér gaps*, Ann. Inst.
Fourier 33 (1983), no. 3, 39--58, doi:10.5802/aif.930, proves that $f$ has
no finite deficient value in the sense of Nevanlinna theory: $\delta(a,f)=0$
for every $a\in\mathbb C$. The theorem is recorded on its
[[../library/analysis/murai_1983_deficiency_entire_functions_fejer_gaps/_index|library card]],
which also records the paper's engine, a proximity-function estimate
$m(r,f)>(1-\epsilon)\log M(r,f)$ outside a set of finite logarithmic
measure. The consequence for the question is immediate: $f$ is
transcendental, so $T(r,f)/\log r\to\infty$, while a value $a$ taken only
finitely often has $N(r,a)=O(\log r)$ and hence $\delta(a,f)=1$; so every
value is taken infinitely often. The paper presents its theorem as a
strengthening of this classical Fejér--Biernacki theorem, the result of
[[problems/analysis/E0517/claims/1927_01_01_biernacki|Biernacki 1927]].
Section 5 of the paper shows that the gap hypothesis cannot be weakened to
Fabry gaps, $k/n_k\to0$: it constructs an entire function with Fabry gaps
whose deficiency at $0$ equals $1$. That example does not bear on the
question, since deficiency $1$ at $0$ does not show that $0$ is taken
finitely often. The paper was cited on the site's discussion thread on
2026-03-26 as related to the problem.

**Covers.** Every instance of [[problems/analysis/E0517/_index|Problem 517]]
with $\sum_k1/n_k<\infty$, of any order; such exponents satisfy
$n_k/k\to\infty$, as the Biernacki page shows. Functions with
$n_k/k\to\infty$ and $\sum1/n_k=\infty$ are not covered; the finite-order
ones are settled by
[[problems/analysis/E0517/claims/1929_12_01_polya|Pólya 1929]], and the
infinite-order ones remain open.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki: the theorem is the paper's own, and
the deduction of infinitely many $a$-points from zero deficiency is the
elementary Nevanlinna-theory step above.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: a journal publication, Annales de l'Institut
Fourier 33 (1983), no. 3, the DOI linked above; the publisher's record
gives only the year, so the page is dated to its first day. Reviewed is not
listed: the site labels the problem OPEN, its commentary does not mention
the paper, and a thread comment is not acceptance. Formalized is not
listed: no Lean statement or proof of the theorem is recorded.
