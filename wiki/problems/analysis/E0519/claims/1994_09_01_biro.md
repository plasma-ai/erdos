***

**Claim.** The answer to [[problems/analysis/E0519/_index|Problem 519]] is
yes, with $c=1/2$. András Biró, *On a problem of Turán concerning sums of
powers of complex numbers*, Acta Math. Hungar. 65 (1994), no. 3, 209--216,
doi:10.1007/BF01875148, digested on its
[[../library/analysis/biro_1994_problem_turan_concerning_sums_powers_complex/_index|library card]],
proves in Theorem 1 that for arbitrary complex $z_1,\ldots,z_n$ with
$z_1=1$,

$$
\max_{1\le j\le n}\Bigl\lvert\sum_{t=1}^n z_t^j\Bigr\rvert>\frac12.
$$

This is an independent proof of the question's statement, with a constant
three times
[[problems/analysis/E0519/claims/1961_01_01_atkinson|Atkinson's]] bound of
1961; the paper's introduction records Atkinson's own later improvements,
to $1/3$ and then to $\pi/8$ for $n<1600$ and to a constant $s_0<\pi/8$
for all large $n$, and presents Theorem 1 as an improvement on them. The
proof forms the polynomial with roots $z_2,\ldots,z_n$, applies two
Newton--Girard identities to its coefficients, and uses a planar dichotomy
(Lemma 1) under which each new coefficient either makes a Newton--Girard
expression large or forces the consecutive coefficient partial sums to
grow. The complete published proof is reconstructed in this repository at
[[../library/analysis/biro_1994_problem_turan_concerning_sums_powers_complex/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
with its planar input at
[[../library/analysis/biro_1994_problem_turan_concerning_sums_powers_complex/lemma_1|Lemma 1]];
the reconstruction is compilation, not independent review, and the value
$1/2$ is not claimed to be sharp. Biró's later paper
[[problems/analysis/E0519/claims/2000_09_25_biro|Biró 2000]] improves the
constant.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the paper appeared in Acta Mathematica Hungarica,
a refereed journal; the publisher's record (Crossref) dates the issue
September 1994, and this page's date is the first day of that month.
Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas Bloom, labels the problem PROVED (LEAN)
and names this paper as the improvement of the constant to $1/2$ on
erdosproblems.com/519 (page last edited 1 February 2026). The proof has been
reconstructed here but not independently reviewed, and no review verdict is
claimed.
