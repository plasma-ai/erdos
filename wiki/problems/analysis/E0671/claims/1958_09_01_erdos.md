***

**Claim.** In P. Erdős, Problems and results on the theory of interpolation.
I, Acta Math. Acad. Sci. Hungar. 9 (1958), no. 3-4, 381-388, printed p. 384,
Erdős asserts that a node system in $[-1,1]$ can be built so that, for every
continuous $f$, there are continuum many points $x_0$ at which the Lebesgue
function is unbounded, his condition (4) on p. 382, that
$\limsup_n\sum_{k\le n}\lvert l_k(x_0)\rvert=\infty$, and nevertheless
$L_n(f,x_0)\to f(x_0)$. The paper gives no construction. This is a yes to
the first question of [[problems/analysis/E0671/_index|Problem 671]], in a
stronger form: the first question asks for one such point for every $f$, and
the assertion gives continuum many.

**Covers.** The first question only. On the same page Erdős writes that when
(4) holds at every point of $[-1,1]$ he cannot decide whether some continuous
$f$ has $L_n(f,x)$ divergent at every $x$, which is the setting of the second
question; the assertion says nothing about it.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki.

**Standing.** Withdrawn. P. Erdős and P. Vértesi, On the almost everywhere
divergence of Lagrange interpolatory polynomials for arbitrary system of
nodes, Acta Math. Acad. Sci. Hungar. 36 (1980), 71-89, section 1, p. 71,
recall the 1958 statement and write that it is perhaps true, that they
cannot prove it, and that the original proof was probably incomplete; the
corpus's card for the paper's 1981 correction,
[[../library/analysis/erdos_1981_correction_misprints_our_paper_almost_everywhere/_index|Erdős and Vértesi 1981]],
records that sentence. No proof of the 1958 assertion was published. The
page is dated by the issue's publication month, September 1958, as its first
day. The pending claims that answer both questions yes are on
[[problems/analysis/E0671/claims/2026_06_22_price|Price's page]] and
[[problems/analysis/E0671/claims/2026_07_24_quietmethod|QuietMethod's page]];
the problem's standing derives from those pages alone.
