**Claim.** Theorem 1.1 of the note *A Gaussian Construction for an Erdős Problem
on Zeros of Successive Derivatives*: fix $1/2<\beta<1$, let $\xi_k$ be
independent Gaussian coefficients and let

$$
f(z)=\sum_{k\ge0}\xi_k\frac{z^k}{(k!)^{1-\beta}}.
$$

Then almost every sample of $f$ is a transcendental entire function such that,
for every nonempty open $U\subseteq\mathbb{C}$, the derivative $f^{(n)}$ has a
zero in $U$ for all sufficiently large $n$. For every increasing sequence
$n_1<n_2<\cdots$ the zeros of the $f^{(n_k)}$ are then dense, which is the
affirmative answer to the corrected Statement of
[[problems/analysis/E0906/_index|Problem 906]], which asks for a transcendental
function. The proof combines a saddle-point estimate for the covariance kernels
of the derivatives with a self-contained Offord-type estimate for the
probability that a derivative has no zero in a fixed disk.

**Order.** The note (section 7) gives $f$ the order $1/(1-\beta)>2$, so the
function lies outside Theorem 1 of Boas and Reddy (1973), which as printed
concerns entire functions of order at most 2 and finite type and conflicts with
