**Claim.** There is a transcendental entire function $f$ such that for every
nonempty open $U\subseteq\mathbb{C}$ the derivative $f^{(n)}$ has a zero in $U$
for all sufficiently large $n$. For every increasing sequence $n_1<n_2<\cdots$
the zeros of the $f^{(n_k)}$ are then dense, which is the affirmative answer to
the corrected Statement of [[problems/analysis/E0906/_index|Problem 906]], which
asks for a transcendental $f$; the tab entry's notes call the polynomial
witnesses of the site's wording trivial. The function is a random Fock series

$$
f(z)=\sum_{k\ge0}\xi_k\frac{z^k}{\sqrt{k!}},
$$

with independent coefficients $\xi_k$ uniformly distributed on the closed unit
disk, and the claim is that a realization of it has the property. As the tab
entry describes the argument, a saddle-point estimate for the coefficients of
$f^{(n)}$ and a small-ball estimate in one coordinate give an exponential
lower-tail bound for $\log\lvert f^{(n)}(z)\rvert$; for a fixed disk, Jensen's
formula turns the event that $f^{(n)}$ has no zero in the disk into a large
logarithmic deficit on an interior circle, of order $\sqrt n$ because the
circular average of $\lvert z\rvert$ strictly exceeds its value at the center,
so the probability of such a hole is bounded by an exponentially small quantity.
The manuscript also proves the growth bound
$\lvert f(z)\rvert\le\sqrt2\exp(\lvert z\rvert^2)$; the entry's notes say this
conflicts with a 1973 theorem of Boas and Reddy as printed and that the
manuscript explains the quantifier conflict for a fixed disk. The claimant is
Eric Hou, who filed the claim and declares assistance from the AI system named
as ChatGPT 5.6 Sol. This page's account of the manuscript follows the tab
entry's summary and notes.
