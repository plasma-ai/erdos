# Problem 967

[[problems/analysis/_index|..]]

[[problems/analysis/E0967/claims/_index|claims/]]: The 1 claim page of Problem 967, one per claimant's result; the problem's standing derives from them.

***

**Statement.** Let $1<a_1<\cdots $ be a sequence of integers such that
$\sum\frac{1}{a_i}<\infty$. Is it true that, for every $t\in \mathbb{R}$,

$$
1+\sum_{k}\frac{1}{a_k^{1+it}}\neq 0?
$$

**Status.** DISPROVED (LEAN). The site credits Yip [Yi25], whose
representation theorem yields, for each real $t\neq 0$, an admissible infinite
sequence with $1+\sum_k a_k^{-(1+it)}=0$; its Lean marker refers to a
third-party formalization that this corpus has not built, and it records the
finite-sequence variant as open. The standing derives from
[[problems/analysis/E0967/claims/2025_12_18_yip|Yip's claim page]].

**Source.** [erdosproblems.com/967](https://www.erdosproblems.com/967), accessed
2026-09-04. Cite as: T. F. Bloom, Erdős Problem #967,
https://www.erdosproblems.com/967.

**References.**

- [ErIn64] Erdős, P. and Ingham, A. E., Arithmetical Tauberian theorems. Acta
  Arith. (1964), 341-356.
- [Yi25] F. Yip, On a problem of Erdős and Ingham. arXiv:2512.16528 (2025).

**Formalization.** The formal-conjectures statement file states the exact
infinite-sequence target with `sorry` bodies and points, through its

## Progress

Yip's *On a problem of Erdős and Ingham*, arXiv:2512.16528v1 (18 December
2025), Theorem 1.3 (printed/physical pp.1–2), states that for every real
$t\ne0$ and every $\lambda\in\mathbb C$ there is
$S\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge2}$ such that

$$
\sum_{n\in S}\frac1n<\infty,
\qquad
\sum_{n\in S}\frac1{n^{1+it}}=\lambda.
$$

The paragraph immediately after the theorem on p.2 explicitly allows $S$ to
be infinite, contained in $\mathbb Z_{\ge N}$ for any prescribed positive
integer $N$, and to satisfy $\sum_{n\in S}1/n\le |\lambda|+\delta$ for any
$\delta>0$. Choose one fixed nonzero $t$ and $\lambda=-1$, require $S$ to be
infinite, and enumerate it increasingly. Because
$|n^{-(1+it)}|=1/n$, the complex series is absolutely convergent, so the
increasing enumeration preserves its sum. This gives an admissible infinite
integer sequence with

$$
1+\sum_k\frac1{a_k^{1+it}}=0.
$$

This specialization of the preprint theorem refutes the universal
nonvanishing assertion. The statements of the underlying set representation
are recorded, with sketches of the printed proof, in
[[../library/analysis/yip_2025_problem_erdos_ingham/lemma_2_1|Lemma 2.1]] and
[[../library/analysis/yip_2025_problem_erdos_ingham/theorem_1_3|Theorem 1.3]]. The
p. 2 sentence allowing $S$ to be infinite is asserted without a separate
derivation in v1. Its omitted schedule is supplied in
[[../library/analysis/yip_2025_problem_erdos_ingham/infinite_refinement|the
infinite-tail refinement]], a separate project-authored completion from the same
lemma. An adjustable cap and half-residual steps keep each residual nonzero,
force infinitely many nonempty ordered blocks, and telescope to the claimed mass
bound. A remote-singleton reduction proves the zero-target case as well. The
supplement writes the exact increasing-enumeration transfer needed here. An
independent strong mathematical and source review, retained as the
[[../library/analysis/yip_2025_problem_erdos_ingham/evidence/verify/full_proof_review|full-proof
review]], confirmed these source-derived details and the exact
increasing-sequence transfer. Their separate authorship does not make them part
of the printed v1 proof and gives no formal or peer-review-acceptance credit.

Yip's Conjecture 3.1 and Question 3.2 (p.3) retain the finite-set question and
$S=\{2,3,5\}$ respectively. Their nonvanishing assertions are open in that
source. The general infinite construction does not resolve these finite
questions.

The historical locators are separate. Erdős and Ingham, *Arithmetical
Tauberian theorems*, Acta Arith. 9 (1964), printed p.341 / physical p.1, begin
with a finite or infinite nondecreasing sequence of real numbers
$1<a_1\le a_2\le\cdots$ with finite reciprocal sum. Theorem 4 is on printed
p.347 / physical p.7. It equates nonvanishing on $\operatorname{Re}s=1$ with
the Tauberian implication for their class $\mathcal I$ of nonnegative,
nondecreasing, locally bounded functions vanishing below $1$ (defined on
p.342). The distinct-integer question and $\{2,3,5\}$ discussion are on
printed p.355 / physical p.15; the authors call the latter a simple case,
without claiming it is the simplest. The modern strictly increasing integer
formulation is Yip's Question 1.1 on p.1.
