***

Fredy Yip, *On a problem of Erdős and Ingham*, arXiv:2512.16528v1 (18 December
2025), proves in Theorem 1.3 that for every real $t\neq 0$ and every
$\lambda\in\mathbb C$ there is a set $S\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge 2}$ with
$\sum_{n\in S}1/n<\infty$ and $\sum_{n\in S}n^{-(1+it)}=\lambda$, and states
after the theorem that $S$ may be taken infinite. Fix one nonzero $t$, take
$\lambda=-1$ and an infinite such $S$, and let $a_1<a_2<\cdots$ enumerate it.
This is a sequence of integers greater than $1$ with finite reciprocal sum, and
because $|n^{-(1+it)}|=1/n$ the series converges absolutely, so the enumeration
keeps its sum:

$$
1+\sum_k \frac{1}{a_k^{1+it}}=0.
$$

The question therefore fails for infinite sequences. The construction says
nothing about finite sequences, and the finite question, in particular for
$\{2,3,5\}$, stays open (Yip's Conjecture 3.1 and Question 3.2).
