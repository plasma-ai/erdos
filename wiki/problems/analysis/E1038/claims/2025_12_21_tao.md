***

Terence Tao, *Sublevel sets of logarithmic potentials*, a note dated 20
December 2025 and posted to the problem's thread on 21 December 2025, proves the
supremum half of the problem. For a probability measure $\mu$ on $\mathbb R$
with logarithmic potential $U_\mu(x)=\int\log|x-t|^{-1}\,d\mu(t)$, Theorem 1.1
states that if $\mu$ is supported in $[t_0-1,t_0+1]$ then
$|\{x:U_\mu(x)\ge0\}|\le2\sqrt2$, the bound being sharp for
$\mu=\frac12\delta_{-1}+\frac12\delta_1$; Corollary 1.1 applies it to the root
measure of a nonconstant monic polynomial $P$ with all roots real in
$[t_0-1,t_0+1]$ and gives $|\{x:|P(x)|\le1\}|\le2\sqrt2$, which the note says
resolves the question of Erdős, Herzog and Piranian. The proof rests on a
duality lemma, that a finite measure whose potential is negative on the support
interval cannot have its support inside the sublevel set, a monotone
measure-preserving rearrangement and three explicit trial measures, the
parameter values of the second and third of which the note says were supplied
by AlphaEvolve. An expanded version dated 22 December 2025 and posted to the
thread the same day first states the result as Theorem 2.1 for probability
measures, with the characterization of the equality case,
$\mu=\frac12\delta_{t_0-1}+\frac12\delta_{t_0+1}$; the note *Superlevel sets of
logarithmic potentials*, dated 27 December 2025 and posted to the thread on 28
December, restates that theorem and adds structural reductions and a one-cut
candidate for the infimum, which it does not determine. No independent check of
the proofs is recorded.

> Here is an expansion of the previous notes. It achieves step 1 (existence of

> could reduce to the case where the $\{U_\mu > 0 \}$ contains a large
> interval $I \supset (-\sqrt{2},0]$, in which $\mu$ consists of a Dirac mass
> at $-1$ of measure at least $1/2$; but I could not exclude the possibility
> that $\{U_\mu > 0 \}$ also contains other intervals (though in each such
> interval, $\mu$ can be assumed to be a Dirac mass).

**Covers.** The supremum only: $\sup|E_f|=2\sqrt2$ for
$E_f=\{x\in\mathbb R:|f(x)|<1\}$ over the class, with the equality case from the
version of 22 December onward. The claim says nothing about the infimum beyond
the reductions and the candidate value, which the three full claims of the
folder build on.

**Standing.** The note is a dated manuscript on the author's site, not a
refereed paper and not filed on the proof-claims tab. Darvas, Peng and Tao
describe it as the elementary proof Erdős asked for of the theorem on
[[problems/analysis/E1038/claims/1966_01_01_elbert|Elbert's page]], and Wang's
Theorem 10.1 imports Theorem 2.1 of the updated note for the supremum. The
formal-conjectures statement file, at its revision of 2026-09-18
([1038.lean](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/6fbb54f24ccc2e64dcfaffc28c58950e377110d2/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1038.lean)),
tags its supremum part research solved and cites the note for it, with a link
to the version of 22 December; a statement file is not a formalization link.
The site's label is OPEN and its commentary lists the supremum among the known
values, thanking Terence Tao among the contributors without crediting a proof,
so no reviewer is named and the claim stays claimed.
