***

Manjunath Krishnapur, Erik Lundberg and Koushik Ramachandran, *On the area of
polynomial lemniscates*, arXiv:2503.18270 (24 March 2025), prove a regular case
of the second question. For a compact $K$ let $\kappa_n(K,1)$ be the minimal
area of $\{z:|p(z)|<1\}$ over monic $p$ of degree $n$ with all zeros in $K$.
Theorem 6 states that if $K$ is the closure of a bounded open set with
$C^2$-smooth boundary and has capacity $1$, then $\inf_n\kappa_n(K,1)=0$, which
is $\mu(K)=0$ in the problem's notation; Theorem 7 adds that for any compact $K$
of capacity $1$, zeros of polynomials whose sublevel areas in $K$ tend to zero
equidistribute to the equilibrium measure of $K$. For the closed unit disc, the
case Erdős, Herzog and Piranian had settled qualitatively, the paper proves
$c/\log n\le\kappa_n(\overline{\mathbb D},1)\le C/\log\log n$, improving
Pommerenke's lower bound and Wagner's upper bound. Its introduction records that
the case of capacity above $1$ had been treated under a regularity condition in
the authors' earlier paper, *Inradius of random lemniscates*, J. Approx. Theory
299 (2024), Paper No. 106018, whose Corollary 1.6 gives $\mu(K)=0$ for compact
$K$ of capacity above $1$ under a Frostman-type bound on the equilibrium measure
(recorded on
[[problems/analysis/E1040/claims/2024_02_03_krishnapur_lundberg_ramachandran|its page]]),
and that the general unit-capacity case loses quantitative control. The paper's
[[../library/polynomials/krishnapur_2025_area_polynomial_lemniscates/_index|library card]]
records it; the statements above are those of the arXiv version, and the proofs
are not checked here.

**Covers.** The second question for one class of sets: a compact $K$ of capacity
$1$ that is the closure of a bounded open set with $C^2$ boundary has
$\mu(K)=0$. The claim says nothing about other compact sets of capacity $1$,
about unbounded closed sets, or about the first question; the case of capacity
above $1$ is recorded on
[[problems/analysis/E1040/claims/2026_04_03_ghosh_ramachandran|the page of Ghosh
and Ramachandran]], and the general case on the four later pages of the folder.

**Standing.** The arXiv record lists no journal reference (checked
2026-10-07), the paper is not named on the site's page or thread, and no
reviewer is named, so the claim stays claimed.
