***

**Claim.** Grunsky's question has a negative answer already in degree $4$, and
with simple roots. Goodman works with the open set $E(c)=\{z:|P(z)|<c\}$ and its
boundary lemniscate. First example (p. 359): for $P(z)=(z^2+1)(z-2)^2$ and
$c=|P((1\pm i)/2)|=5\sqrt5/4$, the lemniscate has double points at the two
critical points $(1\pm i)/2$, $E(c)$ has three components, and the component
containing $2$ has both double points on its boundary but omits their midpoint
$1/2$, where $|P(1/2)|=45/16>c$, so it is not convex. Theorem (p. 361): for
$Q(z)=3z^4-20z^3+6z^2-60z+303$ and $c=\sqrt{91{,}600}$, the roots of $Q$ are
simple and one of the four components of $E(c)$ fails to be convex; dividing $Q$
and $c$ by $3$ gives the monic form. The paper also records the referee's
example $P(z)=z(z^5-1)$ with $c=5/6^{6/5}$, whose lemniscate has five double
points on the boundary of the component of $0$, and raises the question of the
greatest number of nonconvex components as a function of the degree. The
statements are on the source card
[[../library/analysis/goodman_1966_convexity_level_curves_polynomial/_index|goodman_1966_convexity_level_curves_polynomial]].

**Closed set.** The problem is posed for the closed set $\{z:|f(z)|\le c\}$
with as many components as distinct roots. In both of Goodman's examples $c$
is a critical value at which the lemniscate has double points, so the closed
set at that same $c$ has fewer components than the open set, and the paper
does not treat the closed set. The disproof in the problem's exact terms is
[[problems/analysis/E1047/claims/1961_01_01_pommerenke|Pommerenke's Theorem 14]].

**Source.** A. W. Goodman, On the convexity of the level curves of a
polynomial, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 17 (1966), no. 2, 358--361, DOI
10.1090/S0002-9939-1966-0188408-3; received by the editors August 12, 1965,
presented to the Society January 24, 1966. The publisher's record dates the
issue to April 1966 without a day, and the page is named by the first of
that month.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the paper appeared in the Proceedings of the
American Mathematical Society. Reviewed: the site's curator, T. F. Bloom,
labels the problem disproved and credits Goodman's first example, the
simple-roots quartic and the referee's example in the problem's commentary.
Nothing here is independently reviewed by this project.

**Depends on.** Nothing on the wiki; the examples are verified by elementary
computation in the paper.
