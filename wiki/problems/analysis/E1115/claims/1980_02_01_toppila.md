***

**Claim.** The answer to the question of
[[problems/analysis/E1115/_index|Problem 1115]] is no: an entire function $f$ of
finite order, even of order zero, need not have a locally rectifiable path on
which $f\to\infty$ whose length $\ell(r)$ inside $|z|<r$ is $O(r)$. S. Toppila,
*On the length of asymptotic paths of entire functions of order zero*, Ann.
Acad. Sci. Fenn. Ser. A I Math. 5 (1980), 13--15, poses the question as Erdős's,
citing Problem 2.41 of Hayman's 1974 collection, and recalls that under the
growth condition $\log M(r,f)=O((\log r)^2)$ Hayman's 1960 theorem gives a ray
through the origin as such a path
([[problems/analysis/E1115/claims/1960_12_01_hayman|Hayman's claim page]]). Its
Theorem states that for every increasing function $\varphi(r)\to\infty$ there is
an entire function $f$ with

$$
\log M(r,f)=O\bigl(\varphi(r)(\log r)^2\bigr)
$$

such that every locally rectifiable curve $P$ on which $f(z)\to\infty$ has
$\limsup_{r\to\infty}\ell(r)/r=\infty$, where $\ell(r)$ is the length of $P$ in
$|z|<r$. With $\varphi(r)=\log r$ the function has order zero, so the definite
question has a negative answer already at order zero, and the note presents
Hayman's condition as the best possible growth condition for a linear-length
path. The two-page proof builds the function as an infinite product of
polynomials chosen so that $|f|\le1$ on a sequence of spiral paths circling the
origin, which every path on which $f\to\infty$ must eventually avoid, forcing
$\ell(e r_n)\ge2\pi nr_n$ for large $n$. The note's closing remark records that
the same result had been proved by Gol'dberg and Eremenko in their 1979 paper,
which has
[[problems/analysis/E1115/claims/1979_01_01_goldberg_eremenko|its own claim page]].

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the construction is self-contained.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Annales Academiae Scientiarum Fennicae, Series A I
Mathematica, volume 5 (1980), pp. 13--15; the journal's record dates the
article 1 February 1980, the date this page carries. Hayman and Lingham's
2018 survey of Hayman's problems (Update 2.7) records the note as an
independent proof of the Gol'dberg--Eremenko result. The site's curator
credits Gol'dberg and Eremenko alone and does not mention the note, so no
`reviewed` evidence is listed. No proof has been reproduced or reviewed in
this wiki. The problem also asks for a path of slowest growth and an estimate
of $\ell(r)$ in terms of $M(r)$; the note supplies no such estimate, and none
is claimed here.
