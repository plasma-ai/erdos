***

Theorem 1 of Garaev states that for every $A>0$ and every $x>x_0(A)$ at
least $\exp((\log\log x)^{A})$ integers $n\le x$ are values of both $\phi$
and $\sigma$. Since the count is unbounded in $x$, the equation
$\phi(n)=\sigma(m)$ has infinitely many solutions, which answers
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0048/_index|Problem 48]] in the
affirmative. The result sharpens Theorem 1 of Ford, Luca and Pomerance
([[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0048/claims/2009_06_18_ford_luca_pomerance|Ford–Luca–Pomerance 2010]]),
which gives the same count for one unspecified exponent $\alpha>0$. Garaev's
introduction says that the proof is based on a refined version of Konyagin's
approach, the alternative route described in a remark of the earlier paper
that avoids Heath-Brown's theorem linking Siegel zeros to twin primes, and on
the argument of that paper; Garaev's Lemma 4 follows from the proof of its Lemma
2.6. The common values are again built as $\sigma$ of a product of primes
$p\le x$ with $p+1$ smooth and certified as totient values through the
implication that $\phi(\operatorname{rad}(m))\mid m$ makes $m$ a value of
$\phi$; Lemma 1 uses Landau's theorem on the real zeros of two real primitive
$L$-functions, and Lemma 2 produces a scale at which $L(s,\chi)$ has no zeros
in a suitable region for all moduli up to a small power of $x$. The digest is
on the source card
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/garaev_2011_number_common_values_arithmetic_functions_below/_index|garaev_2011_number_common_values_arithmetic_functions_below]].

**Depends on.**
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0048/claims/2009_06_18_ford_luca_pomerance|Ford–Luca–Pomerance 2010]],

**Source.** M. Z. Garaev, On the number of common values of arithmetic
functions $\phi$ and $\sigma$ below $x$, Mosc. J. Comb. Number Theory 1
(2011), no. 3, 42–49, received 17 May 2011. The journal's record gives the
volume, the issue and the year and no month or day, so this page is dated by
