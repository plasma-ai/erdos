***

**Claim.** There is an absolute constant $C>0$ such that infinitely many
positive integers $n$ satisfy $\omega(n+k)\le\Omega(n+k)\le C\log k$ for
every integer $k\ge2$. This is Theorem 1.1 of Cheuk Fung (Joshua) Lau, *On
the number of prime factors of consecutive integers*, arXiv:2604.15042 (v1
2026-04-16, v2 2026-06-24), stated for $\Omega$, the number of prime factors
counted with multiplicity, and hence for $\omega\le\Omega$. It answers the
question of [[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0248/_index|Problem 248]] yes
after a shift of $n$: for such an $n$ put $n'=n+1$; then for every $j\ge1$,

$$
\omega(n'+j)=\omega(n+(j+1))\le C\log(j+1)\le Cj,
$$

since $\log(j+1)\le j$ for $j\ge1$, so infinitely many $n'$ have
$\omega(n'+j)\ll j$ for all $j\ge1$ with an absolute implied constant. The
paper's random models (Conjectures 5 and 6) say that the bound $C\log k$ is
sharp up to the constant. The proof refines the probabilistic sieve argument
of Tao and Teräväinen: $n$ is drawn from $[x,2x]$ weighted by a product of
Selberg sieve weights with polynomially decaying levels $R_k=x^{c/k^{50}}$,
and a sieve-weighted concentration estimate for $\Omega(n+k)$ (Proposition
5.5) makes the union bound over $k\ge2$ converge. Theorem 1.3 gives the same
bound for $n-k$. The source card is
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/lau_2026_number_prime_factors_consecutive_integers/_index|Lau 2026]].

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the argument is self-contained in the
preprint. It refines the method of
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0248/claims/2025_12_01_tao_teravainen|Tao and Teräväinen 2025]]
but does not rest on that result.

**Acceptance.** Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas F. Bloom, labels the
problem PROVED (LEAN) and credits Lau [La26] in the problem's commentary with
improving the bound to $\omega(n+k)\ll\log k$ for all $k\ge2$, noting that
$\omega$ may be replaced by $\Omega$ (page last edited 2026-04-17, after a
thread comment of the same day cited the preprint, which the site marks as
addressed); that documented acceptance is the `reviewed` evidence. Not
refereed: the preprint has no journal publication on record. Not formalized:
no Lean development of this result is known; the lean-proofs file linked from
the Tao–Teräväinen page formalizes their linear bound, not this one.
