***

**Claim.** For every $\epsilon>0$, every sufficiently large $n$ is a sum
$n=a+b$ in which neither $a$ nor $b$ has a prime factor exceeding
$n^{4/(9\sqrt e)+\epsilon}$; in the notation of
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0334/_index|Problem 334]],
$f(n)\ll_\epsilon n^{4/(9\sqrt e)+\epsilon}$, where
$4/(9\sqrt e)=0.2695\ldots$. The result is A. Balog, *On additive
representation of integers*, Acta Math. Hungar. 54 (1989), no. 3--4,
297--301, cited as [Ba89] on the problem page, whose commentary records it
as the best bound known. The statement is as the zbMATH review of the paper
gives it: every $N>N_0(\epsilon)$ is $a+b$ with $a,b>1$ and no prime factor
of $ab$ exceeding $N^{4/(9\sqrt e)+\epsilon}$; its proof is not checked
here.

**Covers.** The bound $f(n)\ll_\epsilon n^{4/(9\sqrt e)+\epsilon}$ for
every $\epsilon>0$. In particular $f(n)\le n^{1/3}$ for all large $n$, which
answers yes the question the site records as Erdős's original one. Not
covered: the best function $f$, which the problem asks for and no source
determines; the site expects $f(n)\le n^{o(1)}$.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki; the claim rests on the cited paper.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Acta Mathematica Hungarica 54 (1989), no. 3--4,
297--301, doi:10.1007/BF01952060, the DOI linked above. The site's curator,
T. F. Bloom, credits the bound to Balog in the problem page's commentary, but
the site labels the problem OPEN (page last edited 2026-04-03), so the credit
is not acceptance of the problem and no `reviewed` evidence is listed.

**Dating.** The page is dated by the issue month in the publisher's record,
September 1989; the day is a placeholder.
