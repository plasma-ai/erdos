***

**Claim.** Let $F(n)$ be the largest prime factor of $n(n+1)$, the quantity
of [[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0368/_index|Problem 368]]. For every
$\varepsilon>0$ and all sufficiently large $n$,

$$
F(n)>\frac{\log\log n}{1+\varepsilon},
$$

so $F(n)\gg\log\log n$. This follows from the theorem on p. 4 of K. Mahler,
Über den grössten Primteiler spezieller Polynome zweiten Grades, Archiv for
Mathematik og Naturvidenskab (1935), recorded on the library card
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/mahler_1935_uber_den_grossten_primteiler_spezieller/_index|Mahler 1935]]:
if $A_0$ is one of $\pm1,\pm2$, $D_1$ is squarefree and coprime to $A_0$,
and $x_0$ is a natural number coprime to $A_0$, then for every
$\varepsilon>0$ and all large $x_0$ the number $D_1x_0^2-A_0$ has a prime
factor $p>(\log\log x_0)/(1+\varepsilon)$. With $D_1=1$, $A_0=1$ and
$x_0=2n+1$ one has $x_0^2-1=4n(n+1)$; the prime factor the theorem gives
exceeds $2$ once $n$ is large, so it divides $n(n+1)$, and
$\log\log(2n+1)\ge\log\log n$. The method extends Størmer's theory of the
solutions of $x^2-Dy^2=1$ whose $y$ has all its prime factors dividing $D$,
as the card records.

**Covers.** The lower bound $F(n)\gg\log\log n$ only. It improves
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0368/claims/1918_06_01_polya|Pólya's]]
$F(n)\to\infty$ and is superseded by
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0368/claims/2023_12_06_pasten|Pasten's]]
$(\log\log n)^2/\log\log\log n$.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the claim rests on the cited paper.

Mathematik og Naturvidenskab in 1935; the record gives no month, so the
page is dated to the first day of the publication year. The site's
