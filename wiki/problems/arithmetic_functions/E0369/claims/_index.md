# problems/arithmetic_functions/E0369/claims

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0369/_index|..]]

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0369/claims/1976_08_01_eggleton_selfridge|1976_08_01_eggleton_selfridge]]: Eggleton and Selfridge give, for every epsilon, infinitely many runs of five
consecutive integers n to n plus 4 each n to the epsilon smooth, which
settles the question and the site's first reading for k at most 5.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0369/claims/1998_04_01_balog_wooley|1998_04_01_balog_wooley]]: Balog and Wooley prove that for every fixed u above 1 infinitely many n are
followed by about log log log log n consecutive n^(1/u)-smooth integers:
the wording and the first reading, the second for infinitely many n only.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0369/claims/2017_10_05_bober_fretwell_martin_wooley|2017_10_05_bober_fretwell_martin_wooley]]: Theorem 2.1 of Bober, Fretwell, Martin and Wooley gives, for every epsilon
and k and all large n, k consecutive n to the epsilon smooth integers in
the interval from n minus n to the theta to n for some theta below one.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0369/claims/2026_03_26_yang|2026_03_26_yang]]: Yang shows that for every epsilon and k and all large N the upper half of 1
to N holds k consecutive integers each smooth to its own epsilon power, the
site's second strengthening, accepted on the forum by the site's curator.
