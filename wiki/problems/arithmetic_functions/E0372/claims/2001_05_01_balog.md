***

**Claim.** With $P(n)$ the largest prime factor of $n$, there are infinitely
many $n$ with $P(n)>P(n+1)>P(n+2)$; for all large $x$ the number of such
$n\le x$ is $\gg\sqrt x$. This settles the statement of
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0372/_index|Problem 372]], a conjecture of
Erdős and Pomerance, who proved the ascending analog $P(n)<P(n+1)<P(n+2)$
for infinitely many $n$ and that $P(n)>P(n+1)$ holds on a set of positive
lower density
([[../library/arithmetic_functions/erdos_1978_largest_prime_factors/_index|Erdős and Pomerance 1978]]).
Balog also conjectures that the $n$ with descending triples have density
$1/6$; De Koninck and Doyon present a generalized form of that conjecture
([[../library/arithmetic_functions/dekoninck_2011_distance_between_smooth_numbers/_index|De Koninck and Doyon 2011]]).
The library holds no copy of Balog's paper; the statement above follows the
site's commentary and the formal-conjectures file.

**Acceptance.** Published in Studia Sci. Math. Hungar. 38 (2001), 45–50, a
refereed journal (`refereed`); the publisher's record dates the issue
2001-05-01, which dates this page. The site's curator, Thomas F. Bloom,
records the problem as solved by this paper and labels it proved
