***

**Claim.** In a blog post *Erdos problem #385, the parity problem, and
Siegel zeroes* (19 August 2024), Terence Tao observes that if, for some
$2<u<3$, the largest gap between consecutive semiprimes in $[x,2x]$ whose
prime factors lie in $[x^{1/u},x^{1-1/u}]$ is $o(x^{1/u})$, then both
questions of [[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0385/_index|Problem 385]] have
the answer yes. Indeed, such a semiprime $m<n$ within $o(n^{1/u})$ of $n$
has least prime factor at least $(n/2)^{1/u}$, so $m+p(m)-n\to\infty$, which
gives $F(n)>n$ for all large $n$ and $F(n)-n\to\infty$.

**Hypothesis.** The gap bound is unproved. Tao calls it very plausible, as a
consequence of a semiprime analog of Cramér's conjecture, but out of reach:
even on the Riemann hypothesis, gaps between primes in $[x,2x]$ are bounded
only by $O(\sqrt x\log x)$, and the bound for semiprimes is not much better,
falling short of $x^{1/u}$ for every $2<u<3$. Tao argues that the parity
problem blocks sieve methods here and expects no resolution without a
breakthrough strong enough to exclude Siegel zeros.

**Acceptance.** None. The post is not refereed, and the site's commentary,
on a problem it labels OPEN, only mentions it.
