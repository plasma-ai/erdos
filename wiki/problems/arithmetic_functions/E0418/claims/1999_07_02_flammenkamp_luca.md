***

**Claim.** A. Flammenkamp and F. Luca, *Infinite families of noncototients*,
Colloq. Math. 86 (2000), 37–41, received 2 July 1999, the date this page
carries. A noncototient is a positive integer $n$ for which $x-\phi(x)=n$ has
no solution. The paper's Proposition: if $k$ is an odd prime that is not a
Mersenne prime, $2^tk-1$ is composite for every $t\ge1$ (a Riesel number),
and $2k$ is a noncototient, then $2^mk$ is a noncototient for every $m\ge1$.
The paper's Theorem: for every $m\ge1$, each $2^mk$ with
$k\in\{509203, 2554843, 9203917, 9545351, 10645867, 11942443, 65484763\}$ is
a noncototient. The Riesel condition comes from covering systems of
congruences, and the condition on $2k$ is checked by computer: a solution $x$
of $x-\phi(x)=2k$ is even and squarefree and lies in $[2.39k,4k]$ in the
tested range. Either family answers yes to
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0418/_index|Problem 418]]: infinitely many
positive integers are not of the form $n-\phi(n)$. The value $k=509203$ is
Browkin and Schinzel's, so the theorem extends their result
([[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0418/claims/1994_04_11_browkin_schinzel|claim page]])
with six further values.
