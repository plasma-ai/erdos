***

**Claim.** Erdős proves that a real additive function $f$ equals $c\log n$
for a constant $c$ in two cases: when $f(n+1)\ge f(n)$ for every $n$
(Theorem XI), and when $f(n+1)-f(n)\to0$ (Theorem XIII). For Theorem XI the
monotonicity gives $f(m)\le f(n)\le f(2)+f(m)$ for odd $m<n<2m$, so $f$ is
finitely distributed in the paper's sense and Theorem V writes
$f(m)=c\log m+\varphi(m)$ with $\sum_p\varphi'(p)^2/p$ convergent; Theorem X,
on the distribution of $f(m+1)-f(m)$, then forces $\varphi$ to vanish. The
paper omits the proof of Theorem X as similar to that of an earlier paper.
Theorem XIII is proved directly, by comparing $f$ at integers built from the
prime powers on which $f(Q)/\log Q$ approaches its upper limit. The same
paper states the question of
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0491/_index|Problem 491]] itself, that
bounded consecutive differences give $f(n)=c\log n+\varphi(n)$ with $\varphi$
bounded, as a result that probably holds but that Erdős cannot prove.

**Covers.** The instances of
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0491/_index|Problem 491]] in which $f$ is
nondecreasing or $f(n+1)-f(n)\to0$. Both classes have bounded consecutive
differences, and for them the answer is yes with $f(n)=c\log n$ exactly.
General bounded differences are settled by
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0491/claims/1970_01_01_wirsing|Wirsing's theorem]].
