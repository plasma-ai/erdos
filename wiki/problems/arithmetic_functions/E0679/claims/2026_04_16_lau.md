***

**Claim.** Theorem 7.3 of Cheuk Fung Lau, On the number of prime factors of
consecutive integers, arXiv:2604.15042, carded at
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/lau_2026_number_prime_factors_consecutive_integers/_index|Lau 2026]],
in Section 7, titled by the paper as the conditional falsity of Erdős Problem
679. It assumes the paper's Conjecture 8: for some $C_0\ge1$ and some $d$
with $1\le d<C_0$, every interval $(x-(\log(x/2))^d,x]$ with $x$ large
contains an integer $m$ with $\omega(m)\ge C_0\log\log m/\log\log\log m$.
Under that assumption there is $\delta>0$ such that every sufficiently large
$n$ has some large $k<n$ with

$$
\omega(n-k)>(1+\delta)\frac{\log k}{\log\log k}.
$$

So, under the hypothesis only, the first question of
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0679/_index|Problem 679]] has the answer no
for every $\epsilon<\delta$: only finitely many $n$ have
$\omega(n-k)<(1+\epsilon)\log k/\log\log k$ for all large $k<n$. The proof
applies the conjecture at $x=n$ and compares $C_0\log\log(n-k)/\log\log\log
(n-k)$ with $\log k/\log\log k$ through $k\le(\log(n/2))^d$, which gives the
bound with any $\delta<C_0/d-1$. The claim is conditional: Conjecture 8 is
unproven, so this page derives nothing for the problem's standing. Section 7
is unchanged in the paper's second version (2026-06-24). The paper's
unconditional Theorem 1.3, that for some $C$ infinitely many $n$ have
$\omega(n-k)\le\Omega(n-k)\le C\log k$ for all $1<k<n$, settles no instance
of the problem and is progress, not a claim; its Conjecture 6, that the
$C\log k$ bound is sharp up to a constant, which would also give a negative
answer, is only a conjecture.
