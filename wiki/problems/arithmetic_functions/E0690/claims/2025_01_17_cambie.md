***

**Claim.** Let $d_k(p)$ be the density of the integers whose $k$th smallest
distinct prime factor is $p$. Theorem 5 of Cambie's paper: indexed by the
primes in increasing order, $d_k(p)$ is unimodal for $k=1,2,3$ and is not
unimodal for every $4\le k\le20$. The question of
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0690/_index|Problem 690]], whether $d_k(p)$
is unimodal for fixed $k\ge1$, is therefore answered no: the failure Erdős
expected occurs for every $k$ from $4$ to $20$. The proof writes $d_k(p_i)$
as $\delta_{k-1}(i-1)/p_i$, with $\delta_r(i)$ the density of integers
divisible by exactly $r$ of the first $i+1$ primes $p_0=2,\ldots,p_i$, proves
the three unimodal cases from the monotone tails of
$\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2$ and a finite check, and refutes unimodality for
$4\le k\le20$ by a computer check of that range (the notebook linked above),
printing the $k=4$ and $k=5$ sequences in its appendix, which show the strict
valleys $d_4(13)>d_4(17)<d_4(19)$ and $d_5(23)>d_5(29)<d_5(31)$; the library's
Theorem 5 page recomputes a strict valley $d_k(a)>d_k(b)<d_k(c)$ for every $k$
in the range with exact rational arithmetic.

**Reading of the question.** The thread records two readings: is the sequence
unimodal for every $k$ (answered no by this theorem), or, for each $k$, decide
whether it is (decided by this theorem for $k\le20$ only). On 2026-05-07 the
site's curator adopted the first reading and said they would mark the problem
solved by the small $k$ counterexamples unless someone defended the wider
version; the site marked it solved on 10 May 2026. Under the second reading
the remaining $k>20$ are the subject of the pending
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0690/claims/2026_05_08_wang_crapis|Wang–Crapis claim]].
The claim value follows the question's polarity: a yes-or-no question
answered no is `disproved`; the site's label, Solved, stays in the problem
page's Status sentence.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki.

The corpus's transcription of the proof is the library's

**Acceptance.** Refereed: *Journal of Number Theory* 280 (2026), 271--277,
the peer-reviewed version of arXiv:2501.10333 (v1, 17 January 2025).
Reviewed: the site's remarks cite the paper and thank the author, the site
labels the problem SOLVED, and the thread post of 2026-05-07 by the site's
curator, Thomas Bloom, records the decision to mark it solved by these
counterexamples; Bloom is independent of the author.
