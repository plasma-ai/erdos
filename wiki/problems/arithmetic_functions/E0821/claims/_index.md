# problems/arithmetic_functions/E0821/claims

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0821/claims/1998_01_01_baker_harman|1998_01_01_baker_harman]]: Corollary 1 of Baker and Harman's 1998 paper gives infinitely many n with
more than n to the power 0.7039 preimages under Euler's totient, which
settles the question for every epsilon at least 0.2961; refereed.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0821/claims/2022_11_14_lichtman|2022_11_14_lichtman]]: Corollary 1.3 of Lichtman's 2022 preprint gives infinitely many n with at
least n to the power 0.7156 preimages under Euler's totient, which settles
the question for every epsilon at least 0.2844; the site's best known bound.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0821/claims/2026_09_24_openai|2026_09_24_openai]]: The OpenAI release claims that for every positive epsilon infinitely many n
have more than n to the power one minus epsilon preimages under Euler's
totient, from a count of primes whose predecessor is x to the delta smooth.
