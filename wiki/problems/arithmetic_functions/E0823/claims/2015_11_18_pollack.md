***

Pollack's Theorem 1 states that for every real $\beta>0$ and every
$\varepsilon>0$ there are positive integers $m,n$ with $\sigma(m)=\sigma(n)$
and $|m/n-\beta|<\varepsilon$. Taking $\beta=\alpha\ge1$ and
$\varepsilon=1/k$ for $k=1,2,\dots$ gives integers $n_k,m_k$ with
$\sigma(n_k)=\sigma(m_k)$ and $n_k/m_k\to\alpha$, which is the sequence the
problem asks for. The proof shows that the closure of
$\{\log(m/n):\sigma(m)=\sigma(n)\}$ is the whole real line; it adapts the
Schinzel--Sierpiński construction of equal-$\sigma$ pairs with large ratio,
replacing the prime $k$-tuples conjecture with Zhang's bounded-gaps theorem in
the form, stated for general admissible linear forms, that Maynard proved. The
statement and the paper's other results are on the source card
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/pollack_2015_remarks_fibers_sum_divisors_function/_index|Pollack 2015]],
which follows the author's manuscript at the second link, from the author's
research page; the proof is not reconstructed in this repository.

**Acceptance.** Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas F. Bloom, records the answer
as yes, proved by Pollack, on the problem page (last edited 2025-09-28).
Published: P. Pollack,
*Remarks on fibers of the sum-of-divisors function*, in *Analytic Number
Theory: In Honor of Helmut Maier's 60th Birthday* (Springer, 2015), 305--320; a
chapter of an edited volume, so no journal refereeing is listed as evidence. The
volume gives the year 2015 and no day; the page's date is the day the chapter's
DOI record was created, 2015-11-18. The site's remarks and Erdős's 1974 paper
[Er74b], on the card
[[../library/number_theory/erdos_1974_remarks_problems_number_theory/_index|Erdős 1974]],
call the analogous statement for Euler's totient easy, and Pollack's Remark 3
says that the argument, with obvious modifications, proves Theorem 1 with
$\varphi$ in place of $\sigma$.
