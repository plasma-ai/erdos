***

**Claim.** Kenta Kitamura posted in the discussion thread of
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0878/_index|Problem 878]] on 2026-09-13, and
the same day on the formal-conjectures tracking issue (the two `discussion`
links), a Lean 4 development that answers the first two questions yes. The
theorem `erdos_878.parts.i` (the first `formalization` link) states that some
set of natural density one carries $f(n)=o(n\log\log n)$ and
$n\log\log n=O(F(n))$ along it; the theorem `erdos_878.parts.ii` (the second)
states that $\max_{n\le x}f(n)$ divided by $x\log x/\log\log x$ tends to $1$ as
$x\to\infty$. Both are closed with `answer(True)`. The post discloses
assistance from OpenAI Codex, Astra and ChatGPT; the repository's README names
OpenAI Codex and ChatGPT Astra. No informal author is named; Kitamura submitted
the development.

The development defines $F(n)$ as the largest sum of a set of distinct,
pairwise coprime integers from $2$ to $n$ whose prime factors divide $n$, with
no limit on their number, which is the problem page's Formulation. If the
number of summands were tied to $\omega(n)$, then $F=f$ and the first question
would fail. The repository also checks in Lean that the two maxima differ at
$x=210$, a fact it credits to earlier work, and it claims proofs of the site's
remark that $F(n)\sim\frac12 n\log\log n$ for almost all $n$ and of Erdős's
formula (17) in [Er84e].
