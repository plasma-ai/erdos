**Claim.** Write $P^+(n)$ for the largest prime factor of $n$ and $\rho$ for
Dickman's function. For every fixed $a,b\in(0,1)$,

$$
\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac1X\,\#\{2\le n\le X:\ P^+(n)\le n^{a},\
P^+(n+1)\le n^{b}\}=\rho(1/a)\,\rho(1/b),
$$

the limit over all real $X$ with ordinary counting (Theorem 1.1 of the
release's manuscript *The joint Dickman law for consecutive integers*,
2026-09-24, filed as the library's
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/openai_2026_joint_dickman_law_consecutive_integers/_index|intake card]]).
So the density asked for by
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0928/_index|Problem 928]] exists and equals
the product of the two Dickman densities: the events $P(n)<n^{\alpha}$ and
$P(n+1)<(n+1)^{\beta}$ are independent in the sense Erdős asked for, which
the manuscript calls the Erdős–Pomerance joint Dickman conjecture. Its Section
11 also gives the law with the fixed thresholds $X^{a}$ and $X^{b}$ and the
upper-tail independence that Erdős and Pomerance conjectured. Its Corollary
1.2, that $P^+(n)<P^+(n+1)$ has natural density $1/2$, follows because the
continuous limiting law gives the diagonal no mass; it is the question of
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0371/_index|Problem 371]], which carries its
own account. The method replaces the largest prime factor by finitely many
counts of prime factors in bins $(x^{k/J},x^{(k+1)/J}]$, so that the joint law
becomes a mixed decorrelation $\frac1x\sum_{n<x}g_x(n)F_x(n+1)\to0$ between
two bin characters with different phase vectors, one centered; the
decorrelation is proved through an amplification of the correlation over an
auxiliary logarithmic scale, an independent-site kernel, a second moment for
the latent rows, smoothing on logarithmic and residue space, and the vanishing
of the main energy.

**Bridge to the statement.** The problem's set uses strict inequalities and
the threshold $(n+1)^{\beta}$ for $n+1$; the manuscript and the Lean use
$\le$ and $n^{b}$. Up to $X$ the two sets differ by at most
$\pi(X^{\alpha})+\pi((X+1)^{\beta})$ integers: an $n$ in one set and not the
other either has $P(n)=n^{\alpha}$ exactly, so that $n=p^{1/\alpha}$ for a
prime $p\le X^{\alpha}$, or has $P(n+1)$ equal to a prime $q$ with
$n^{\beta}<q<(n+1)^{\beta}$, an interval of length below $1$ that determines
$n$ from $q\le(X+1)^{\beta}$. Both counts are $o(X)$, so the densities of the
two sets agree. The bridge is elementary and is stated here, not in Lean.

**Acceptance.** Formalized. The release's `lean/` folder states the theorem as
`OAI.JointDickmanPaper.joint_law` in the comparator challenge
`JointDickman.lean` (the third link), with the ordering corollaries
`increasing_order` and `decreasing_order`, proved in its solution module
`OAI.NumberTheory.JointDickman.PaperMain` (in the tree of the second link): the
predicate uses Mathlib's `Nat.maxPrimeFac`, the density counts
$2\le n\le\lfloor X\rfloor$ and divides by the real $X$, and the Dickman
function is defined through its delay equation; the challenge's configuration
permits only `propext`, `Quot.sound` and `Classical.choice`. This corpus's
verification built `OAI.JointDickmanPaper.joint_law` at the pinned revision of 6
October 2026 with the toolchain `leanprover/lean4:v4.34.1` and checked its
axioms, which are exactly those three, with no `sorry`; its fingerprint was
found identical to the challenge. The statement audit found the declaration
exactly Theorem 1.1 as displayed above, for every real $a,b\in(0,1)$, a
hypothesis that can be met: `Nat.maxPrimeFac` is the true largest prime factor
for every $n\ge2$, and the count uses only $n\ge2$; `realDensity` is the
ordinary two-sided natural density over real $X$, which leaving out $n=0,1$ does
not change; and the formal $\rho$, built level by level from the value $1$ on
$[0,1]$ and the equation $\rho(u)=1-\int_1^u\rho(t-1)\,dt/t$ through continuous
approximants, so that its integrals are genuine and not junk values, is the
Dickman function, evaluated at $1/a>1$ and $1/b>1$. The audit also checked the
bridge above, which is correct and is stated on this page, not in Lean; since
the Statement asks only whether the density exists, the theorem with the bridge
settles it in full. Not reviewed or refereed: no outside review, referee report
or acceptance by the site is known (the site labels the problem OPEN, its page
last edited 3 April 2026), and no review of the manuscript's proof is recorded.
The manuscript places the result against the earlier literature:
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/teravainen_2018_binary_correlations_multiplicative_functions/_index|Teräväinen 2018]]
proved the product law in logarithmic density (Theorem 1.14 there) and the
ordering in logarithmic density $1/2$; Wang [Wa21] proved the natural density
under the Elliott–Halberstam conjecture for friable integers, the accepted
conditional claim on
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0928/claims/2021_01_20_wang|Wang 2021]]; Tao
and Teräväinen obtained the ordinary law outside an exceptional set of scales;
and the lower natural density of each ordering rose from Erdős and Pomerance's
$0.0099$ through the $0.2017$ of
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/lu_2025_largest_prime_factors_consecutive_integers/_index|Lü and Wang]]
to $0.280$ in Yang's 2026 preprint (arXiv:2607.16032), none asserting that the
density exists. The release's README states that its manuscripts and supporting
proof artifacts were produced by an internal OpenAI model and that the
collection includes results at different stages of verification; the
manuscript's author line is OpenAI and names no person.
