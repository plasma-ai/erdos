***

**Claim.** L. Troupe, *Divisor sums representable as the sum of two
squares*, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 148 (2020), no. 10, 4189--4202, Theorem
1.2: the number $B_s(x)$ of $n\le x$ for which $s(n)$ is a sum of two
squares satisfies $B_s(x)\asymp x/(\log x)^{1/2}$
([[../library/arithmetic_functions/troupe_2020_divisor_sums_representable_as_sum_two/_index|card]]).
The paper presents this as a special case of the conjecture of
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0955/_index|Problem 955]]: the sums of two
squares have density zero, and so does their preimage.

**Covers.** The instance $A$ = the sums of two squares; the general
assertion stays open.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society,
a journal. The site's commentary credits the result, but the site labels the
problem OPEN, so no curator acceptance is listed.
