***

**Claim.** Theorem 1.1 of A. J. Irving, *The largest prime factor of
$X^3+2$*, states: let $\varpi=10^{-52}$ and let $X$ be sufficiently large;
then for at least a positive proportion of the integers $n\in(X,2X]$, $n^3+2$
has a prime factor exceeding $X^{1+\varpi}$. For $f(t)=t^3+2$ this gives
$F_f(n)\geq(n/2)^{1+\varpi}$ for all large $n$, in the notation of
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/_index|Problem 976]]. It improves the
exponent of
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2001_05_01_heath_brown|Heath-Brown's 2001 theorem]].

**Covers.** The first question for $f(t)=t^3+2$. It does not give
$F_f(n)\gg n^3$.
