***

**Claim.** Alexandru Pascadi, *Large sieve inequalities for exceptional Maass
forms and the greatest prime factor of $n^2+1$*, *Forum of Mathematics, Pi*
14 (2026), e8. Theorem 1.1 states that $P^+(m^2+1)>m^{1.3}$ for infinitely
many $m$. Its proof (Section 6.3, Notation 6.7 and (6.20), with the
concluding estimates on pp. 51--52) asserts the stronger dyadic bound

$$
P^+\!\left(\prod_{x\leq m\leq2x}(m^2+1)\right)\geq x^{1.30008}
$$

for every sufficiently large real $x$. With $x=n/2$ the dyadic product
divides $\prod_{m\le n}(m^2+1)$, so
$F_{t^2+1}(n)\geq2^{-1.30008}n^{1.30008}$ for all large $n$, in the notation
of [[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/_index|Problem 976]]. The assertion
and this consequence are recorded on
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/pascadi_2026_large_sieve_exceptional_maass_greatest_prime_factor/dyadic_product_bound|the dyadic bound card]].

**Covers.** The first question for $f(t)=t^2+1$. It does not give
$F_{t^2+1}(n)\gg n^2$ and covers no other polynomial.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: published in *Forum of Mathematics, Pi* 14 (2026),
e8, online 24 February 2026. The dyadic bound is an assertion inside the
published proof, not the wording of Theorem 1.1. The site labels the problem
OPEN.
