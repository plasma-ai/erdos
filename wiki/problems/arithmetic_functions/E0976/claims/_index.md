# problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/_index|..]]

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2001_05_01_heath_brown|2001_05_01_heath_brown]]: Heath-Brown proves that a positive proportion of n in (X, 2X] have n^3+2
with a prime factor above X^(1+10^-303), so the answer is yes for t^3+2.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2014_11_28_irving|2014_11_28_irving]]: Irving proves that a positive proportion of n in (X, 2X] have n^3+2 with
a prime factor above X^(1+10^-52), improving Heath-Brown's exponent for
t^3+2.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2015_06_22_de_la_breteche|2015_06_22_de_la_breteche]]: De la Bretèche proves that for every even monic irreducible quartic with
Galois group V4 a positive density of n <= X have f(n) with a prime
factor above X^(1+c), so the answer is yes for these quartics.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2022_12_07_dartyge_maynard|2022_12_07_dartyge_maynard]]: For a monic irreducible quartic with Galois group C4 or D4, a positive
proportion of m in (x, 2x] have f(m) with a prime factor at least
x^(1+c), so the answer is yes for these quartics.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2023_08_19_carella|2023_08_19_carella]]: An arXiv note claims that the greatest prime factor of the product of
n^2+1 over x <= n <= 2x is at least x^(3/2) as x tends to infinity.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2024_04_05_pascadi|2024_04_05_pascadi]]: The proof of Pascadi's Theorem 1.1 bounds the greatest prime factor of the
product of m^2+1 over x <= m <= 2x below by x^1.30008 for all large x,
which answers the first question for t^2+1.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2025_05_01_grimmelt_merikoski|2025_05_01_grimmelt_merikoski]]: An arXiv preprint gives, for fixed coprime a and squarefree h, an m in
[X, 2X] with a prime factor of am^2+h above X^1.312 for all large X, so
the answer would be yes for these quadratics.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2026_02_03_ermoshin|2026_02_03_ermoshin]]: An arXiv preprint proves that for every monic irreducible cubic a positive
proportion of m in [x, 2x] have f(m) with a prime factor above x^(1+c),
so the answer would be yes for these cubics.

[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0976/claims/2026_04_16_bhalla|2026_04_16_bhalla]]: An unpublished note derives the degree-scale bound, the greatest prime
factor of the running product at least a constant times n to the degree, for
every irreducible polynomial from an unproved prime-values hypothesis.
