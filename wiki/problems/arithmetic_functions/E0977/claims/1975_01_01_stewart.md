**Claim.** C. L. Stewart, *The greatest prime factor of $a^n-b^n$*, Acta
Arithmetica 26 (1974/75), no. 4, 427--433. Let $a>b>0$ be relatively prime
integers, let $P(m)$ be the greatest prime factor of $m$, and fix $\kappa$ with
$0<\kappa<1/\log2$.
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/stewart_nd_greatest_prime_factor/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
(printed p. 427) gives a strictly increasing unbounded function $f$, effectively
determined by $a$, $b$ and $\kappa$, with $P(\Phi_n(a,b))/n>f(n)$ for every
integer $n>2$ having at most $\kappa\log\log n$ distinct prime factors, where
$\Phi_n(a,b)$ is the homogeneous cyclotomic factor of $a^n-b^n$. Since
$\Phi_n(a,b)$ divides $a^n-b^n$, the transfer after equation (4) (printed
p. 428) gives $P(a^n-b^n)/n\to\infty$ as $n$ runs through the integers $n>2$
with at most $\kappa\log\log n$ distinct prime factors. With $a=2$ and $b=1$
this is the limit of [[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0977/_index|Problem 977]]
along those $n$.

**Covers.** The limit $P(2^n-1)/n\to\infty$ along the integers $n>2$ with at
most $\kappa\log\log n$ distinct prime factors, for each fixed
$0<\kappa<1/\log2$. For $1<\kappa<1/\log2$ this set has natural density one and
contains every sufficiently large prime, as Stewart notes on printed
pp. 427--428. It does not give the limit along all $n$, which Stewart's 2013
theorem proves
([[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0977/claims/2010_08_06_stewart|its claim page]]).
