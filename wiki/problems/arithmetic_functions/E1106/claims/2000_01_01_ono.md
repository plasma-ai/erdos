***

**Claim.** Corollary 2 of K. Ono, *Distribution of the partition function
modulo $m$*, Ann. of Math. (2) 151 (2000), no. 1, 293–307, digested on the card
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/ono_2000_distribution_partition_function_modulo_m/_index|Ono
2000]]: Erdős's conjecture that every prime $m$ divides $p(n)$ for some $n$ is
true. The corollary adds counts of the $n\le X$ with $p(n)\equiv0\pmod m$:
$\gg_m X$ for primes $m\ge5$, from Theorem 1, and $\gg_m\sqrt X$ for $m=2$;
for $m=3$ it rests on $p(3)=3$, and the paper notes that it is not known
whether $p(n)\equiv0\pmod3$ for infinitely many $n$.

The bridge to [[problems/arithmetic_functions/E1106/_index|Problem 1106]] is
not in the paper; it is stated here. Given $k$, each of the first $k$ primes
divides some $p(n_i)$, so for $n\ge\max_i n_i$ the product
$p(1)p(2)\cdots p(n)$ has at least $k$ distinct prime factors; hence
$F(n)\to\infty$. The corollary gives no rate.

**Covers.** The first question: $F(n)\to\infty$. The second question, whether
$F(n)>n$ for all large $n$, is not addressed.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki: the bridge is proved above.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the paper appeared in the Annals of Mathematics in
January 2000. The site's commentary credits this paper with the statement that
every prime divides some $p(n)$, but the site labels the problem OPEN, so the
commentary is not acceptance and no `reviewed` is listed. The page is dated by
the journal issue, which precedes the arXiv posting of 17 August 2000.
