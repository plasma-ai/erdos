***

**Claim.** Let $f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}$ be additive. If $f(n+1)\ge f(n)$
for every $n$, or if $f(n+1)-f(n)\to0$, then $f(n)=c\log n$ for some
constant $c\in\mathbb{R}$. Both statements are proved in P. Erdős, *On the
distribution function of additive functions*, Ann. of Math. (2) 47 (1946),
1--20, digested on the card
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/erdos_1946_distribution_function_additive_functions/_index|Erdős 1946]];
the paper also poses the question of
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E1122/_index|Problem 1122]] as a
conjecture, and Mangerel restates the two results in Section 1.2 of the
paper digested on
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/mangerel_2022_additive_functions_short_intervals_gaps_conjecture/_index|Mangerel 2022]].
The monotone case is the one the site's commentary describes as the case
of an empty $A$: the set $A=\{n: f(n+1)<f(n)\}$ of decreases is empty
exactly when $f$ is nondecreasing. The argument rests on the paper's structural theorem
that an additive function whose values on a positive proportion of the
integers up to $n$ lie within a bounded distance of each other is close to
$c\log$ at the primes, in the sense that $\sum_p((f(p)-c\log p)/p)^2$
converges for some $c$.

**Covers.** The instances of Problem 1122 in which $A$ is empty, that is,
$f$ is nondecreasing: an empty set has density zero, and for these the
answer is yes. Also the functions with $f(n+1)-f(n)\to0$, for which the
conclusion holds whether or not $A$ has density zero. It leaves open every
$f$ with a nonempty density-zero set of decreases whose differences do not
tend to zero, which is the problem's content; the refereed partial claim
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E1122/claims/2021_08_27_mangerel|Mangerel 2021]]
covers some of these, and the pending full claim
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E1122/claims/2026_09_08_gu|Gu 2026]]
asserts the rest.

second series, volume 47, number 1 (January 1946), pages 1--20; the page is
