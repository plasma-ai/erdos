***

**Claim.** The answer to
[[problems/covering_systems/E0277/_index|Problem 277]] is yes: for every
real $c$ there is a positive integer $n$ with $\sigma(n)>cn$ such that no
covering system has as its moduli distinct divisors of $n$ greater than $1$.
This is the theorem of J. A. Haight, *Covering systems of congruences, a
negative result*, which the site's commentary credits with the affirmative
answer; the theorem is stated here as the commentary states it, and the paper
is not held in the library. In the language of the follow-up question Erdős
asked in [Er80], with $f(x)$ the largest value of $\sigma(m)/m$ over $m<x$
whose divisors do not form a covering system, Haight's theorem says that
$f(x)\to\infty$. A second proof, of a quantitative strengthening, is
recorded on
[[problems/covering_systems/E0277/claims/2005_07_18_filaseta_ford_konyagin_pomerance_yu|the claim page of Filaseta, Ford, Konyagin, Pomerance and Yu]].

Erdős's follow-up question, as the site states it, asks whether
$f(x)=o(\log\log x)$. Filaseta, Ford, Konyagin, Pomerance and Yu proved
$f(x)\ge(1+o(1))\sqrt{\log\log x}$ in the paper held as
[[../library/integer_sequences/filaseta_2007_sieving_large_integers_covering_systems_congruences/_index|FFKPY 2007]].
A comment on the site's thread (2025-09-13) observes that Hough's theorem,
held as
[[../library/covering_systems/hough_2015_solution_minimum_modulus_problem_covering_systems/_index|Hough 2015]],
that every covering system with distinct moduli has a modulus below an
absolute constant $C$ answers the follow-up question negatively: let $n$ be
the least common multiple of the integers up to $y$ all of whose prime factors
exceed $C$; then every divisor of $n$ above $1$ exceeds $C$, so these divisors
cannot be the moduli of a covering system, while
$\sigma(n)/n\gg\log y\gg\log\log n$, so $f(x)\gg\log\log x$ and $f(x)$ is
not $o(\log\log x)$. The site's commentary records the consequence as
$f(x)=(1+o(1))\log\log x$, which is stronger than this argument supports:
by Mertens's theorem the construction gives
$\sigma(n)/n\sim e^{\gamma}\prod_{p\le C}(1-1/p)\log\log n$, a constant
of order $1/\log C$, while Gronwall's theorem bounds $f(x)$ above by
$(e^{\gamma}+o(1))\log\log x$; the constant $1$ is not established by it.

**Formalization.** The site's label carries a Lean qualification. The
formal-conjectures statement file for the problem carries the category
`research solved` and a `formal_proof` attribute pointing to
line 1294 of `src/latest/ErdosProblems/Erdos277.lean` in Boris Alexeev's
lean-proofs repository at the commit of 2026-09-07 linked above. That file
(first added 2026-08-15; 1,364 lines at the pin; Lean and Mathlib `v4.33.0`)
declares itself a formalization of a solution to the problem, names Haight
and Filaseta, Ford, Konyagin, Pomerance and Yu as informal authors and Codex
and GPT-5.6 Sol as formal authors, and proves `erdos_277`: for every real
$c$ there is $n$ with $\sigma(n)>cn$ such that every
`StrictCoveringSystem ℤ` has a modulus ideal not containing $n$, that is, a
modulus not dividing $n$. Its `CoveringSystem` structure is a finite family
of residue classes covering the ring with every modulus ideal nonzero and
proper, so the moduli exceed $1$, and the strict variant requires pairwise
distinct modulus ideals, matching the statement's distinct divisors. The
file's header says the proof uses the finite residual-density estimate of
Filaseta, Ford, Konyagin, Pomerance and Yu, and it ends with
`#print axioms` without the printed output. The formal-conjectures file
states the same proposition under `answer(True)`. The file is linked here
because it names Haight's theorem as the statement it proves and Haight as an
informal author; since its argument is that of Filaseta, Ford, Konyagin,
Pomerance and Yu, it is linked on their claim page as well. This corpus has
not built or audited the development, so `formalized` is not listed as
evidence.
