***

**Claim.** The statement of
[[problems/covering_systems/E0280/_index|Problem 280]] is false. Take
$n_k=2^k$ for $k\ge1$, $a_1=0$ and $a_k=2^{k-1}+1$ for $k\ge2$. The growth
condition holds with $\epsilon=1$, since $2^k>2k\log k$ for every $k\ge1$.
For every $k\ge1$ the only $m$ with $0\le m<2^k$ in none of the classes
$a_i\pmod{2^i}$, $1\le i\le k$, is $m=1$: an even $m$ lies in $0\pmod 2$,
and an odd $m$ with $1<m<2^k$ has $m-1=2^{i-1}t$ with $t$ odd and
$2\le i\le k$, so $m\equiv2^{i-1}+1\pmod{2^i}$. The count in the statement
is therefore the constant $1$, which is $o(k)$. The site's commentary writes
the system with $a_k=2^{k-1}+1$ for every $k\ge1$, the same classes since
$2\equiv0\pmod2$. Cambie's comment of 2025-08-10 also records a second
counterexample, which it attributes to Wouter van Doorn: a finite covering
system with moduli
$2,3,4,6,12$, which satisfy the growth condition for a small $\epsilon$,
continued by any larger moduli, leaves nothing uncovered from $k=5$ on. A
later comment of Cambie's (2025-08-11) notes what a nontrivial variant would
need: with every $a_i=0$ at least $(1-o(1))\epsilon k$ primes below $n_k$
stay uncovered, so a counterexample needs moduli sharing prime factors with
differing residues.

**Submission note.** Posted to the site's forum by Stijn Cambie on 10 August
2025:

> This question can be answered in the negative, by e.g. the following two
> simple examples (and there should be more of course).
>
> If one takes $n_i=2^i$ for every $i\ge 1$, $a_1=0,$ and $a_i=2^{i-1}+1$ for
> $i\ge 2$, then the desired number is actually $1$ (only the number $1$). The
> latter is $o(k)$.
>
> As an alternative, Wouter van Doorn observed that $\{2,3,4,6,12\}$ (which can
> be extended to an infinite family) gives a covering with $n_i> i \log(i)$,
> also resolving the question.

**Formalization.** The site's label carries a Lean qualification. Lorenzo
Luccioli posted on the thread (2026-04-18) a Lean formalization of the
counterexample produced with Aristotle, at the pinned gist linked above. Boris
Alexeev's lean-proofs repository holds a copy,
`src/latest/ErdosProblems/Erdos280.lean` (added 2026-04-28; 262 lines at
the pin of 2026-09-15), whose header names
Cambie as informal author and Aristotle and Luccioli as formal authors. It
proves `Erdos280.not_erdos_280`: there are sequences $n,a$ and
$\epsilon>0$ with $n$ strictly increasing, $a_i<n_i$ for $i\ge1$, the growth
condition for every $k\ge1$, exactly one uncovered $m<n_k$ for every
$k\ge1$, and the uncovered count divided by $k$ tending to $0$; a comment in
the file records the `#print axioms` output, `propext`, `Classical.choice`
and `Quot.sound`, under the name `Erdos280.erdos_280_counterexample`, which
the file's last line declares as an alias of `not_erdos_280`. The
formal-conjectures statement file, at the commit of 2026-09-18 linked from
the problem page, carries the category `research solved` and a
`formal_proof` attribute pointing to the repository's `v4.29.1` copy on
its main branch, unpinned; its `erdos_280` states the problem under
`answer(False)` with `uncoveredCount` over `Finset.range (n k)` and
indices $1\le i\le k$. This corpus has not built or audited either
development, so the page lists no `formalized` evidence.
