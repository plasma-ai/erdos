***

**Claim.** The answer to the last question of
[[problems/covering_systems/E1189/_index|Problem 1189]] is yes: for every odd
prime $p$ the divisors of $2^{p-1}p$ greater than one form an irreducible
covering set, so infinitely many $n$ have the property that their divisors above
one form an irreducible covering set. Sun calls $n$ a covering number when the
integers can be covered by classes with distinct moduli among the divisors of
$n$ greater than one, and primitive when no proper divisor of $n$ is a covering
number; Theorem 1.4 of the paper classifies the primitive covering numbers with
at most two prime divisors as exactly the $2^{p-1}p$, and Corollary 1.3 answers
Erdős's 1980 question by combining this primitivity with Simpson's theorem that
a minimal cover with least common multiple $N=\prod p^{\alpha_p}$ has more than
$\sum\alpha_p(p-1)$ classes, so that among the subsets of the divisor set only
the whole set can be the moduli of a cover. The divisor set of $12$ is the case
$p=3$, Erdős's own example. The source card is
[[../library/covering_systems/sun_2007_covering_numbers/_index|Sun 2007]], which
lists its theorems; the inequality used is on the card
[[../library/covering_systems/simpson_1985_regular_coverings_integers_arithmetic_progressions/_index|Simpson 1985]].

**Covers.** The fourth question alone. The count of irreducible covering
sets of size $k$, the extreme values of the largest modulus and the maximum
reciprocal sum are not addressed; the pending full claims of
[[problems/covering_systems/E1189/claims/2026_07_28_pickhardt|Pickhardt]]
and of
[[problems/covering_systems/E1189/claims/2026_07_13_snyder|Snyder]]
answer those, and both reuse this theorem for the fourth.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/covering_systems/E1189/claims/1985_01_01_simpson|Simpson's inequality]],
which the paper combines with its own classification of primitive covering
numbers.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Z.-W. Sun, *On covering numbers*, Integers 7
(2007), no. 2, A33, 11 pp., also printed in the proceedings volume
*Combinatorial Number Theory* (de Gruyter, 2007), 443--453, the DOI linked
above, which is the citation the problem page carries as [Su07]; the paper
was first posted to arXiv on 2006-01-01, which gives the page its date.
Integers 7 (2007), no. 2, is the journal's proceedings issue for the
Integers Conference 2005, whose preface describes the volume as refereed.
No `reviewed` evidence is listed: the site's curator states in the problem
page's commentary that Sun's theorem settles the final question, but the
site labels the problem as a whole OPEN (page last edited 8 April 2026), so
that remark is context and not acceptance, and the 2026 manuscript of
Pickhardt importing the theorem as established is not a review. Nothing
here rests on a review by this project.
