**Claim.** For an integer $m\ge1$ let $\epsilon_m$ be the supremum of
$\sum_i1/n_i$ over finite families of distinct moduli
$m<n_1<\cdots<n_k$ admitting pairwise disjoint residue classes
$a_i\pmod{n_i}$. With $S(m)=\sqrt{\log m\log\log m}$ in natural
logarithms,

$$
\epsilon_m=\exp\bigl(-(1+o(1))S(m)\bigr),
$$

that is, for every $\eta>0$ and all large $m$,
$e^{-(1+\eta)S(m)}\le\epsilon_m\le e^{-(1-\eta)S(m)}$. This is Corollary
1.2 of Ho's nine-page manuscript, the library's
[[../library/covering_systems/ho_2026_non_intersecting_arithmetic_progressions_spread_cores/_index|Ho 2026]],
with the statement and its deduction compiled on its
[[../library/covering_systems/ho_2026_non_intersecting_arithmetic_progressions_spread_cores/corollary_1_2|Corollary 1.2 page]].
The deduction is the transfer from the sharp asymptotic for the largest
number $f(N)$ of disjoint classes with distinct moduli at most $N$: the
upper bound by partial summation of $A(t)\le f(t)$ for a family's
counting function against $t^{-2}$, with a
[[../library/covering_systems/ho_2026_non_intersecting_arithmetic_progressions_spread_cores/lemma_5_1|tail-integral lemma]]
uniform over finite families; the lower bound by taking an extremal family
for $f(N)$ at $N=\lceil me^{2S(m)}\rceil$ and deleting its at most $m$
moduli not exceeding $m$. The manuscript's final page discloses
substantial mathematical and expository contributions from GPT-5.4 Pro
under Ho's guidance and revision, with Ho responsible for the text; the
site credits the resolution of Problem 202 to GPT-5.4 Pro and derives this
estimate from it. The first public posting and announcement are dated
2026-04-23, which names the page; the PDF cited is the 3 May 2026 commit
linked above. The estimate determines the leading exponent only: it does
not give $\epsilon_m\sim e^{-S(m)}$ or a value at any finite cutoff.

This is the corrected Statement of
[[problems/covering_systems/E1190/_index|Problem 1190]], whose supremum
replaces the site's maximum; Ho, the site's commentary and both Lean
developments use the same supremum, and the problem page's Notes record that
no finite family attains it.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/covering_systems/E0202/claims/2026_04_23_ho|Ho's sharp asymptotic for Problem 202]],
the theorem $f(N)=N\exp(-(1+o(1))S(N))$ whose upper bound is the new
content and whose lower bound is the construction of de la Bretèche, Ford
and Vandehey; the transfer itself uses only that asymptotic, partial
summation and the stability of $S$ under the change of scale.

**Acceptance.** Reviewed: Ho announced the manuscript, which states and
proves this corollary beside the Problem 202 theorem, on the site's
Problem 202 thread on 2026-04-23, where the site's curator (T. F. Bloom)
replied that they would update the site once a formalization was provided or
a human had vouched for the proof; on 2026-05-14 a Lean 4 development
formalizing Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2 was posted there. It reported
that its Problem 202 theorem depends only on `propext`, `Classical.choice`
and `Quot.sound` and had passed a SafeVerify check, and that the 1190
corollary uses no further axioms. Nat Sothanaphan confirmed it the same
day, adding that the Park–Pham input was formalized rather than assumed;
the site labels this problem SOLVED (LEAN) and states in its commentary
that the resolution of Problem 202 implies $\epsilon_m=L(m)^{-1+o(1)}$ by
the same reduction (page last edited 2026-05-28, as of 2026-10-07), and
the community ledger of AI contributions records the solution and the
formalization. Not refereed: the manuscript is an author PDF with no
journal publication or referee report located through 2026-09-05. Not
counted as formalized: the linked development defines $\epsilon_m$ as an
`sSup` of reciprocal sums of finite admissible families and its main
theorem states the eventual two-sided bound above, with no `sorry` or
`admit` token outside comments, but this corpus has run no Lean build,
kernel replay or axiom audit of it and records no CI result for the pinned
revision; Boris Alexeev's lean-proofs adaptation linked above ports the
same development. The problem page's section on formalization and
verification scope and the source card record the exact pins and limits.
