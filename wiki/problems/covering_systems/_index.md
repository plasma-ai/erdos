# Covering Systems

[[problems/_index|..]]

[[problems/covering_systems/E0002/_index|E0002/]]: Asks whether finite distinct
covering systems can have arbitrarily large minimum modulus; Hough proved an
absolute bound.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0007/_index|E0007/]]: Asks whether there is a
covering system of congruences whose moduli are distinct and all odd.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0008/_index|E0008/]]: Asks whether every finite
coloring of the integers admits a covering system whose moduli all receive the
same color.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0027/_index|E0027/]]: Asks whether one constant C
lets every tolerance and every N admit an almost covering system with distinct
moduli all between N and C times N.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0202/_index|E0202/]]: The largest number of
congruence classes with distinct moduli at most N that can be chosen so that no
integer lies in two of them.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0203/_index|E0203/]]: Asks whether some integer
coprime to 6 makes every number of the form two to the k times three to the l
times it, plus one, composite.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0204/_index|E0204/]]: Asks whether some integer has
a covering system using its divisors above one whose classes overlap only for
coprime pairs of moduli.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0273/_index|E0273/]]: Asks whether there is a
covering system of congruences whose moduli are all of the form p minus one for
primes p at least 5.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0274/_index|E0274/]]: Asks whether a group can be
exactly covered by more than one coset when the cosets have different sizes,
each element lying in exactly one.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0275/_index|E0275/]]: Asks whether a system of r
congruences that covers two to the r consecutive integers must cover every
integer.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0276/_index|E0276/]]: Asks whether some
Fibonacci-type sequence has all terms composite while no integer shares a factor
with every term.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0277/_index|E0277/]]: Asks whether, for every c,
some n has sum of divisors above c times n yet admits no covering system whose
moduli are distinct divisors of n above one.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0278/_index|E0278/]]: The maximum density of
integers covered by choosing one congruence class for each modulus in a finite
set, and whether equal classes minimize the density.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0279/_index|E0279/]]: Asks whether, for every k at
least 3, congruence classes can be chosen modulo each prime so that all large
integers lie in one of them with quotient at least k.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0280/_index|E0280/]]: Asks whether a sequence of
moduli growing faster than k log k always leaves a number of uncovered integers
below each modulus that is not small compared with k.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0281/_index|E0281/]]: Asks whether moduli whose
congruences always leave a density-zero set must have a finite initial segment
leaving density below any given epsilon.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0586/_index|E0586/]]: Asks whether there is a
system of congruences covering all integers in which no modulus divides another.

[[problems/covering_systems/E0947/_index|E0947/]]: Asks whether an exact
covering system exists, finitely many congruence classes with distinct moduli
such that every integer satisfies exactly one of them.

[[problems/covering_systems/E1113/_index|E1113/]]: Concerns Sierpinski numbers,
odd m for which two to the k times m plus one is never prime, and the sets of
primes that divide all those values.

[[problems/covering_systems/E1188/_index|E1188/]]: Concerns covering systems
with distinct moduli that are minimal, in that no proper subsystem still covers
every integer.

[[problems/covering_systems/E1189/_index|E1189/]]: Concerns sets of distinct
moduli that can cover the integers by some choice of residues but have no proper
subset that can.

[[problems/covering_systems/E1190/_index|E1190/]]: Asks for the size of the
supremum of reciprocal sums over finite disjoint congruence families with
distinct moduli greater than m; the supremum is known to logarithmic scale.

***

Systems of congruences covering the integers, exact and disjoint coverings, the
minimum modulus problem, and the analogous exact coverings of a group by cosets.
