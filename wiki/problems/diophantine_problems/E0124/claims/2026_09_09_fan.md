***

**Claim.** Steve Fan, *Strongly complete sets and a conjecture of Erdős*,
arXiv:2607.14071; Theorem 1.5 first appears in the fourth version, of 9
September 2026, and the fifth version is of 16 September 2026 (the
source card is
[[../library/additive_bases/fan_2026_strongly_complete_sets_conjecture_erdos/_index|fan_2026_strongly_complete_sets_conjecture_erdos]],
which records the theorem at statement level). Let $D$ be a finite nonempty
set of integers at least $2$ in which no two elements are powers of one
integer, partitioned as $D=D_1\cup D_2\cup C$ with $\gcd(C)=1$ and
$\sum_{d\in D_i}1/(d-1)\ge1$ for $i=1,2$. Then the set of all powers $d^n$
with $d\in D$ and $n\ge0$ is strongly complete: it stays complete, every
sufficiently large integer being a sum of distinct elements, after any finite
subset is removed. The theorem refines Theorem 1.23 of Bergelson and Simmons
([[problems/diophantine_problems/E0124/claims/2015_07_08_bergelson_simmons|claim page]]),
which needs three parts with reciprocal sums at least $1$ beside the gcd
part. The paper's AI disclosure, as the source card records it, says that
ChatGPT 5.6 was used for proofreading and suggested a core idea of the
shorter proof of its Lemma 3.2. The argument was not reconstructed in this
corpus.

**Covers.** The second question of
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0124/_index|Problem 124]], for every $k\ge1$,
at every tuple $d_1<\cdots<d_r$ that admits such a partition: yes, since the
powers with exponent below $k$ form a finite set and a sum of distinct powers
$d^n$ with $n\ge k$ is $\sum_ic_ia_i$ with $a_i\in P(d_i,k)$. Not covered:
tuples without such a partition, in particular every tuple whose reciprocal
sum is at most $2$, and the first question.

**Standing.** Claimed. The paper is an arXiv preprint with no journal record
and no published independent review. The site's commentary (page last edited
1 December 2025) predates the theorem and labels the problem OPEN; a post on
the site's thread of 23 September 2026 cites the theorem as settling the
second question when the reciprocal sum exceeds $2$. No Lean formalization
of the theorem is known, and this corpus has built nothing, so the page
lists no evidence.
