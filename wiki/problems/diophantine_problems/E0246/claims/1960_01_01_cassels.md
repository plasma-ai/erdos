***

**Claim.** Let $a,b>1$ be coprime integers. Every sufficiently large integer
is a sum of distinct integers of the form $a^kb^l$ with $k,l\geq 0$, the
statement of [[problems/diophantine_problems/E0246/_index|Problem 246]], in
its corrected Statement, which takes $a,b\geq2$. Cassels obtains it from his
Theorem I: if a set $A$ of positive integers satisfies
$(A(2n)-A(n))/\log\log n\to\infty$, where $A(n)$ counts the elements of $A$ up
to $n$, and $\sum_{\alpha\in A}\lVert\alpha\theta\rVert^2$ diverges for every
real $\theta\in(0,1)$, then every sufficiently large integer is a sum of
distinct elements of $A$. The paper's introduction (p. 111) says that the
investigation was touched off by Birch's paper on the sums of the numbers
$p^\alpha q^\beta$ for coprime $p,q$, and that Birch's results are an
immediate consequence of Theorem I; the verification of the two hypotheses for
the set $\{a^kb^l\}$ is Cassels's and was not reconstructed here. The paper's
digest is the
[[../library/integer_sequences/cassels_1960_representation_integers_as_sums_distinct_summands/_index|library card]],
which records
[[../library/integer_sequences/cassels_1960_representation_integers_as_sums_distinct_summands/theorem_i|Theorem
I]] and the passage on Birch as read clause by clause on the printed pages,
and the proof of Theorem I (Section 2, the Hardy--Littlewood circle method
applied to a thinned subset of $A$) as read for pointers but not checked step
by step.

**Source.** J. W. S. Cassels, On the representation of integers as the sums
of distinct summands taken from a fixed set, Acta Sci. Math. (Szeged) 21
(1960), 111--124, received 3 September 1959. The volume carries the year and
no day, so this page is dated by the first day of 1960; the result follows
Birch's, whose page is
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0246/claims/1959_10_01_birch|Birch's theorem]],
and the two are recorded apart because Cassels proves the statement by his
own, more general criterion rather than by Birch's argument.
