# problems/diophantine_problems/E0261/claims

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0261/_index|..]]

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0261/claims/1990_01_01_borwein_loring|1990_01_01_borwein_loring]]: Borwein and Loring's 1990 identity writes n over two to the n as a sum of m
consecutive terms k over two to the k for n = 2^(m+1) - m - 2, so infinitely
many n have the property; refereed, it answers the first question yes.

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0261/claims/1990_01_01_borwein_loring_conditional|1990_01_01_borwein_loring_conditional]]: Borwein and Loring's Corollary 1 shows that if the iteration a to 2(a mod n)
always reaches zero (their Conjecture 1), then every n has the property of
the second question; refereed and conditional, so it settles no standing.

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0261/claims/2020_08_04_tengely_ulas_zygadlo|2020_08_04_tengely_ulas_zygadlo]]: Tengely, Ulas and Zygadło verify that for every n at most ten thousand, n
over two to the n is a sum of at least two distinct terms k over two to the
k; refereed, it covers the second question up to ten thousand.
