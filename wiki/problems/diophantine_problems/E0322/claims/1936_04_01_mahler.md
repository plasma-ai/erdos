***

**Claim.** Kurt Mahler, *Note on Hypothesis K of Hardy and Littlewood*,
starts from the identity
$$
(9\xi^4)^3+(3\xi-9\xi^4)^3+(1-9\xi^3)^3=1
$$
and replaces $\xi$ by $\xi/\eta$, which gives
$(9\xi^4)^3+(3\xi\eta^3-9\xi^4)^3+(\eta^4-9\xi^3\eta)^3=\eta^{12}$. All three
cubes are positive when $\eta>0$ and $0<\xi<9^{-1/3}\eta$, so each such
integer $\xi$ writes $\eta^{12}$ as a sum of three positive cubes, and
distinct $\xi$ give distinct triples. Mahler concludes that for all large $N$
which are twelfth powers, $x^3+y^3+z^3=N$ has at least $9^{-1/3}N^{1/12}$
solutions in non-negative integers, which refutes Hardy and Littlewood's
Hypothesis K for $n=3$. In the notation of
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0322/_index|Problem 322]], with $A$ the
cubes, $1_A^{(3)}(n)\ge9^{-1/3}n^{1/12}$ for infinitely many $n$, so for every
$c<1/12$ there are infinitely many $n$ with $1_A^{(3)}(n)>n^c$.

**Covers.** The second question for $k=3$: yes. The order of growth of
$1_A^{(3)}(n)$, and every $k\ge4$, are not addressed; Mahler's identities in
$n$ variables have terms of both signs and give no lower bound for sums of
$n$ non-negative $n$th powers.
