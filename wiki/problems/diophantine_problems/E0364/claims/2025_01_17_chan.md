**Claim.** Theorem 1 of T. H. Chan, A note on three consecutive powerful
numbers, Integers 25 (2025), Paper No. A7: there are no three consecutive
powerful numbers $x^3-1=p^3y^2$, $x^3$, $x^3+1=q^3z^2$ with $p,q$ prime and
$x,y,z$ positive integers. Corollary 1 of the paper deduces that
$64x^6-1=p^3q^3y^2$ has no solution in integers $x,y$ with $p,q$ prime. The
proof combines factorization and $3$-adic valuation lemmas for
$x^2\pm x+1$, a reduction of quartic equations to elliptic curves, and the
Pell equation $x^2-3y^2=1$ with second-order recurrences
([[../library/diophantine_problems/chan_2025_note_three_consecutive_powerful_numbers/_index|card]]).
The journal published the paper on 2025-01-17, before its arXiv posting of
2025-03-27, so the page is dated by the journal.

**Covers.** Triples centered at a cube whose outer members are both a prime
cubed times a square, a partial no to the question of
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0364/_index|Problem 364]]. Triples of other
shapes, and those whose middle member is not a cube, are untouched.

**Acceptance.** Refereed in Integers (received 2024-10-17, accepted
2025-01-07): the `refereed` evidence. The site labels the problem
VERIFIABLE, an open label, so its commentary's mention of the paper is not
`reviewed` evidence.
