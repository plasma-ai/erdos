***

**Claim.** R. Obláth, *Über das Produkt fünf aufeinander folgender Zahlen in
einer arithmetischen Reihe*, Publ. Math. Debrecen 1 (1950), 222--226. The
paper proves that the product $n(n+d)(n+2d)(n+3d)(n+4d)$ of five
consecutive terms of an arithmetic progression of positive integers with
$\gcd(n,d)=1$ is never a perfect square, extending Euler's theorem for four
terms. The statement is given as Győry, Hajdu and Saradha record it (Canad.
Math. Bull. 47 (2004), printed p. 373, their reference [11], which "extended
this result to the case $k=5$"; their proof of Theorem 1 takes the case
$\ell=2$, $k=5$ from it) and as Bennett, Bruin, Győry and Hajdu record it
(Proc. London Math. Soc. (3) 92 (2006), Section 1, their references [26]
and [27]). The library holds no copy of the paper. This is the case
$(k,\ell)=(5,2)$ of
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0672/_index|Problem 672]]. The site credits
the case to Obláth under its key [Ob51], which names his 1951 note in J.
Indian Math. Soc.; the 2004 paper places the case $k=5$ in this 1950 paper.
The other cases the site credits to Obláth have $k=3$ and lie outside the
question.

**Covers.** Length $k=5$ with exponent $\ell=2$ (and so every even exponent).
Not covered: $k=5$ with odd exponents, and every other length.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the result rests on the cited paper.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Publicationes Mathematicae Debrecen 1 (1950). The
site's commentary credits the case, but the site labels the problem
VERIFIABLE, an open label, so the commentary is not `reviewed` evidence. The
case is also part of the later
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0672/claims/2004_09_01_gyory_hajdu_saradha|Győry–Hajdu–Saradha theorem]],
whose proof for $\ell=2$ cites this paper.
