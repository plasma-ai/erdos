***

**Claim.** P. Erdős and J. L. Selfridge, *The product of consecutive integers
is never a power*, Illinois J. Math. 19 (1975), no. 2, 292--301. Theorem 1
(printed p. 292) reads: "The product of two or more consecutive positive
integers is never a power." Equivalently, $(n+1)(n+2)\cdots(n+k)=x^\ell$ has
no solution with $k\ge2$, $\ell\ge2$ and $n\ge0$. A progression with $d=1$
is coprime for every initial term, so for $d=1$ the product in
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0672/_index|Problem 672]] is never a perfect
power, for every length $k\ge4$. Library home:
[[../library/diophantine_problems/erdos_1975_product_consecutive_integers_is_never_power/_index|erdos_1975_product_consecutive_integers_is_never_power]].
The paper's Section 4 (printed p. 300) adds, without proof, that for each
fixed $d$ there must be a threshold $t_d$ beyond which the product of $t$
terms is never a power; that remark is not part of this claim.

**Covers.** Common difference $d=1$, every length $k\ge4$ and every exponent
$\ell\ge2$. Not covered: every $d\ge2$.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the result rests on the cited paper.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Illinois Journal of Mathematics 19 (1975), no. 2.
The site's commentary credits the theorem, but the site labels the problem
VERIFIABLE, an open label, so the commentary is not `reviewed` evidence.
