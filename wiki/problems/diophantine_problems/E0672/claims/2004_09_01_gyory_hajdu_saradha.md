**Claim.** K. Győry, L. Hajdu and N. Saradha, *On the Diophantine equation
$n(n+d)\cdots(n+(k-1)d)=by^l$*, Canad. Math. Bull. 47 (2004), no. 3,
373--388. For positive integers $n,d,y,b$, integers $k,\ell\ge2$,
$\gcd(n,d)=1$, $P(b)\le k$ and $b$ free of $\ell$th powers (printed
p. 373), Theorem 1 (printed p. 374) states that the equation has no solution
when $k=4$ or $5$ and $b=1$: a product of four or five consecutive positive
terms of a coprime arithmetic progression is never a perfect power. The
case $\ell=2$ is taken from Euler ($k=4$) and from
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0672/claims/1950_01_01_oblath|Obláth]]
($k=5$). Bennett, Bruin, Győry and Hajdu (2006, printed pp. 273--274) found
the paper's argument for $\ell=3$ invalid and corrected it in their
Section 5, so the theorem holds with that corrected proof. Library home:
[[../library/diophantine_problems/gyory_2004_diophantine_equation/_index|gyory_2004_diophantine_equation]].
These are the lengths $k=4$ and $k=5$ of
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0672/_index|Problem 672]].

**Covers.** Lengths $k=4$ and $k=5$, every $d$ and every exponent
$\ell\ge2$. Not covered: every length $k\ge6$.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0672/claims/2006_03_01_bennett_bruin_gyory_hajdu|Bennett–Bruin–Győry–Hajdu]]
(the corrected proof for $\ell=3$) and
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0672/claims/1950_01_01_oblath|Obláth]]
(the case $k=5$, $\ell=2$).

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Canadian Mathematical Bulletin 47 (2004), no. 3.
The site's commentary credits the theorem, but the site labels the problem
VERIFIABLE, an open label, so the commentary is not `reviewed` evidence.
