# Problem 686

[[problems/diophantine_problems/_index|..]]

[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0686/claims/_index|claims/]]: The 2 claim pages of Problem 686, one per claimant's result; the problem's standing derives from them.

***

**Statement.** Can every integer $N\geq 2$ be written as

$$
N=\frac{\prod_{1\leq i\leq k}(m+i)}{\prod_{1\leq i\leq k}(n+i)}
$$

for some $k\geq 2$ and $m\geq n+k$?

**Status.** Open. The site's label is OPEN.

**Source.** [erdosproblems.com/686](https://www.erdosproblems.com/686), accessed
2026-09-04. Cite as: T. F. Bloom, Erdős Problem #686,
https://www.erdosproblems.com/686.

## Current assessment

The question is open on the site. The non-square case is settled with $k=2$ by
a Lean proof found by AlphaProof and merged into formal-conjectures, recorded
on
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0686/claims/2026_02_25_deepmind|its claim page]];
this corpus has not built it. For squares, a note by Nat Sothanaphan,
generated with GPT-5.4 Thinking and recorded on
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0686/claims/2026_03_08_sothanaphan|its claim page]],
claims that the squares representable with $k=2$ are exactly those of the form
$A_r(y)^2$, a Pell family that includes $36$ and $1225$. Whether every square
$N\ge4$ is representable for some $k$ remains open.

The formal-conjectures file also tags three other variants solved, and none
needs its own page. The variant `nine` is a Lean witness ($k=3$, $n=11$,
$m=25$) for $N=9$, added by pull request 4321 of 2026-06-25, whose description
says it was drafted with Claude; a thread search of 2025-08-09 had already
represented $N=9$. The variant `four_two` shows that $4$ is not representable
with $k=2$, which settles no instance. The variant `four_three` is tagged
solved but has no proof in the file, and it settles no instance.

Nat Sothanaphan linked three further notes in the thread, and none needs its
own page. The note of 2026-03-04, generated by GPT-5.2 Thinking, is subsumed by
the note of 2026-03-08: its representable squares, with roots congruent to $2$
mod $4$ or from the Pell families of its divisibility theorem, all have the
form $A_r(y)^2$. The note of 2026-03-12, generated by GPT-5.4 Thinking, treats
$k=3$; it represents $N=9$ and $N=16$, which a thread search had already
represented on 2025-08-09, and its non-representation results for $k=3$ settle
no instance. The note of 2026-03-14, generated by GPT-5.4 Thinking, excludes
only $k$ in a finite set, so it settles no instance.
