***

**Claim.** For every integer $\ell\geq 2$,

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{Q_2(n(n+1)\cdots(n+\ell))}{n^2}=\infty,
$$

where $Q_2(m)$ is the powerful part of $m$; this answers the second question
of [[problems/diophantine_problems/E0935/_index|Problem 935]] yes. Tony Feng
and twenty-three coauthors, *Semi-Autonomous Mathematics Discovery with
Gemini: A Case Study on the Erdős Problems*, arXiv:2601.22401, carded as
[[../library/distance_problems/feng_2026_semi_autonomous_mathematics_discovery_gemini_case/_index|Feng et al. 2026]],
give the proof in Section 3.2 of version 1 (29 January 2026) and in Section
4.2 of version 3 (5 February 2026).

**Covers.** The second question: for every $\ell\geq 2$ the ratio of
$Q_2(n(n+1)\cdots(n+\ell))$ to $n^2$ is unbounded. The first and third
questions are not addressed.

**The argument.** Since $n(n+1)(n+2)$ divides $n(n+1)\cdots(n+\ell)$, it is
enough to treat $\ell=2$. Let $x_k+y_k\sqrt8=(3+\sqrt8)^k$, so that
$x_k^2-8y_k^2=1$, and put $n_k=8y_k^2$. Then $n_k=2^3y_k^2$ and $n_k+1=x_k^2$
are both powerful. The paper's Lemma 5 shows that for every prime
$p\equiv 5\pmod 8$ some $k$ has $p^2\mid n_k+2$. No odd prime divides two of
$n_k$, $n_k+1$, $n_k+2$, so for that $k$ the powerful part of the product is at
least $n_k(n_k+1)Q_2(n_k+2)$, and the ratio to $n_k^2$ exceeds $p^2$. As $p$
runs over the infinitely many primes $p\equiv 5\pmod 8$, the ratio is
unbounded.

**Claimant.** The paper attributes the solution to Aletheia, a research agent
built on Gemini Deep Think. Version 1 lists the result as a partial AI
solution. Version 3 lists it as an independent rediscovery: its Addendum 4.1
records that Wouter van Doorn gave the same construction in a comment of
20 November 2025 on the site's thread for
[[problems/arithmetic_functions/E0367/_index|Problem 367]], an almost
identical question, and the authors report that the agent did not access that
page.

**Standing.** The claim is `claimed`. The site labels the problem OPEN, so its
commentary crediting van Doorn's construction, also given by Aletheia, with
the second part is not acceptance; the preprint has no journal publication.
