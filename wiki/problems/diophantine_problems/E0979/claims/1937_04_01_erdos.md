***

**Claim.** The case $k=2$ of
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0979/_index|Problem 979]]:
$\limsup_n f_2(n)=\infty$, where $f_2(n)$ counts the solutions of
$n=p_1^2+p_2^2$ in primes. P. Erdős, *On the sum and difference of squares of
primes*, J. London Math. Soc. 12 (1937), 133-136, cited as [Er37b] on the
problem page, proves that for infinitely many $n$ the equation $n=p^2+q^2$ has
more than $n^{c/(\log\log n)^2}$ solutions in primes, for an absolute $c>0$.
The sequel, *On the sum and difference of squares of primes, II*, J. London
Math. Soc. 12 (1937), 168-171 (library home
[[../library/diophantine_problems/erdos_1937_sum_difference_squares_primes/_index|erdos_1937_sum_difference_squares_primes]]),
sharpens the count to more than $n^{c/\log\log n}$ solutions for infinitely
many $n$, by Brun's method, used through the Brun--Titchmarsh bound for primes
in arithmetic progressions.

**Covers.** The case $k=2$, with a lower bound on the size of $f_2(n)$ along
a sequence of $n$. The cases $k\ge3$ are not touched.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: both parts appeared in the Journal of the London
Mathematical Society, volume 12 (1937), Part I in issue 46 (April 1937) and
Part II in issue 47 (July 1937). Not reviewed under the corpus's rule: the
site credits [Er37b] with the case $k=2$, but it labels the problem OPEN, so
that remark is commentary on an open problem and not an acceptance that
settles it.
