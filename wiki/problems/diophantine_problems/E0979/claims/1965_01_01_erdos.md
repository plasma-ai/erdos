***

**Claim.** The case $k=3$ of
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0979/_index|Problem 979]]:
$\limsup_n f_3(n)=\infty$, where $f_3(n)$ counts the solutions of
$n=p_1^3+p_2^3+p_3^3$ in primes. In his 1965 survey, Erdős, Paul, *Some recent
advances and current problems in number theory*, Lectures on Modern
Mathematics, Vol. III, Wiley (1965), 196--244 (cited as [Er65b] on the problem
page; library home
[[../library/number_theory/erdos_1965_recent_advances_current_problems_number_theory/_index|erdos_1965_recent_advances_current_problems_number_theory]]),
Erdős states on p. 224, after the case $k=2$, that he can also prove the case
$k=3$, that the proof is unpublished, and that it seems to need special
properties of the primes. The site's curator quoted the sentence in the
problem's thread (second link above). Wang and Zhang report that Erdős and
Graham, *Old and New Problems and Results in Combinatorial Number Theory*
(1980), p. 47, repeat the assertion without a proof.

**Covers.** The case $k=3$. The cases $k\ge4$ are not touched.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Standing.** Claimed, not accepted: no proof by Erdős has appeared, so there
is nothing to review or referee, and the site describes the proof as
unpublished. The page is dated to the first day of 1965, the survey's year.
Later proofs of the case $k=3$ are
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0979/claims/2026_08_17_kitamura|Kitamura's Lean proof]]
and
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E0979/claims/2026_08_18_wang_zhang|Wang and Zhang's preprint]];
neither reconstructs Erdős's argument.
