***

**Claim.** Let $p_1<p_2<\cdots$ be the primes, with the convention $p_0=1$,
under which $n=1$ lies in $[p_0,p_1)$ and counts; the formal-conjectures
statement adopts the same convention. If $p_{k-1}\le n<p_k$ and every prime
dividing $n!+1$ is $p_k$ or $p_{k+1}$, then $n\le5$; so $n=1,2,3,4,5$ are the
only such $n$, and
[[problems/diophantine_problems/E1058/_index|Problem 1058]] is answered
affirmatively: there are only finitely many. This is the Theorem of F. Luca,
*On a conjecture of Erdős and Stewart*, Math. Comp. 70 (2001), no. 234,
893–896, received 1999-01-04 and published electronically 2000-03-08, the date
this page carries; the source card is
[[../library/diophantine_problems/luca_2001_conjecture_erdos_stewart/_index|luca_2001_conjecture_erdos_stewart]].
The conjecture it proves is the one Guy reports as Erdős and Stewart's in
Problem A2 of *Unsolved problems in number theory*.

**Method.** A $2$-adic lower bound for linear forms in two logarithms
(Bugeaud–Laurent), applied to
$\operatorname{ord}_2(n!)= \operatorname{ord}_2(p_k^ap_{k+1}^b-1)$, gives
$n<7\,242\,116$ (so $p_{k+1}<7.5\cdot10^6$); a computer search over cubic
residues modulo the primes $q\le193$ with $q\equiv1\pmod 3$ forces $3\mid a$ and
$3\mid b$ in any remaining solution, which the Erdős–Obláth theorem on
$x^p\pm y^p=n!$ excludes; a second computation disposes of $n\le193$. No
independent check of the proof is recorded.

**Acceptance.** The paper is refereed: Math. Comp. 70 (2001), no. 234. The
site's curator, Thomas Bloom, marks Problem 1058 proved and credits Luca's
paper. The formal-conjectures statement for Problem 1058 is closed by `sorry`
but carries a formal-proof attribute pointing at the Lean 4 file linked above,
which declares itself a formalization of Luca's solution (informal author
Florian Luca; formal authors recorded as the AI systems Codex and GPT-5.6 Sol)
and proves that $n$ is a solution exactly when $n\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}$. That file
is linked as the claimant's formalization; this corpus has not built or audited
it, so it is not listed as `formalized` evidence.
