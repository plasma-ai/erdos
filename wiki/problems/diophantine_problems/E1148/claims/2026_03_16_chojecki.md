***

**Claim.** There is an $N$ such that every integer $n\ge N$ has integers
$x,y,z$ with $n=x^2+y^2-z^2$ and $\max(x^2,y^2,z^2)\le n$ (Theorem 1.1 of the
note). This answers [[problems/diophantine_problems/E1148/_index|Problem 1148]]
affirmatively. The threshold $N$ is not explicit (Remark 5.1).

**Source.** P. Chojecki, Bounded representations by $x^2+y^2-z^2$, note dated
2026-03-16, posted to the site's forum the same day. The note names Chojecki as
its author; in the thread Chojecki wrote that the proof came out of an exchange
with GPT-5.4 Pro and was checked with Gemini 3.1, and the site credits Chojecki
and GPT-5.4 Pro. An earlier note by the same author, [Sieve methods and a
problem of Erdős](https://www.ulam.ai/research/erdos1148.pdf) (2026-01-26),
proved the representation for a density-one set of $n$ with $O(N^{3/4})$
exceptions up to $N$; the full note supersedes it.

**The argument.** Squares are trivial ($n=m^2+0^2-0^2$). For non-square $n$,
the substitution $a=x+z$, $b=2y$, $c=z-x$ turns a representation into a
binary quadratic form $[a,b,c]$ of discriminant $b^2-4ac=4n$ with $b$ even and
$a\equiv c\pmod 2$, and the bound $\max(x^2,y^2,z^2)\le n$ into the condition
that the scaled point $(a,b,c)/(2\sqrt n)$ lies in a fixed relatively compact
open patch of the hyperboloid $B^2-4AC=1$. Duke's theorem, in the
point-counting form the note deduces from Theorem 2.3 of
[[../library/diophantine_problems/einsiedler_2012_distribution_closed_geodesics_modular_surface_duke/_index|Einsiedler, Lindenstrauss, Michel and Venkatesh]],
supplies a primitive form of discriminant $4n$ in a smaller patch for every
large $n$; two explicit matrices preserving the discriminant then fix the
parity of the coefficients while keeping the point in the patch, and the
substitution is reversed.

**Lean.** The claimant's Lean 4 file (the first formalization link, posted
2026-03-17 as produced with Gemini 3.1, Opus 4.6, GPT-5.4 and the UlamAI
prover) proves the theorem from a hypothesis `DukeTheoremStatement`, which
asserts that for every large $n$ there is a primitive triple $(a,b,c)$ of
discriminant $4n$ whose scaled point lies in the patch and whose first and
third coefficients have equal parity. The file therefore checks the
dictionary between such triples and representations and the bound on the
three squares, while both the Duke input and the note's parity step (the two
matrices) are assumed in the hypothesis; the file declares no axiom and has
no `sorry`, but the hypothesis is not proved in Lean, and a thread comment of
2026-03-18 noted that the homogeneous dynamics it would need was not well
represented in Mathlib. The site's label PROVED (LEAN) refers to
this conditional development. The second formalization link is the file
`src/latest/ErdosProblems/Erdos1148.lean` of Boris Alexeev's repository
`lean-proofs` (Lean and Mathlib v4.33.0; 248 lines at the pinned commit),
which declares itself a formalization of a solution to Problem 1148,
unconditional, with the Duke hypothesis removed; it names GPT-5.4 Pro and
Chojecki as informal authors, lists the tools of the original conditional
formalization, keeps the conditional proof as `erdos_1148_of_duke`, derives
the theorem `erdos_1148` from a separately imported local-existence module,
and records Alexeev as the author of the completing commit. The
formal-conjectures statement `erdos_1148` is tagged `research solved` and
carries a `formal_proof` link to that pinned file, noting that it removes
the Duke hypothesis. The unconditional claim is the file's own: this corpus
has not built or audited either file, so the page lists no `formalized`
evidence.
