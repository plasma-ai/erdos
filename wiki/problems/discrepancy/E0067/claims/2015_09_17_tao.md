**Claim.** For every $f:\mathbb{N}\to\{-1,+1\}$ and every $C>0$ there are
$d,m\ge1$ with

$$
\left\lvert\sum_{1\le k\le m} f(kd)\right\rvert > C,
$$

so the answer to [[problems/discrepancy/E0067/_index|Problem 67]] is yes. This
is Corollary 1.2 of the paper, the sign-valued case of its Theorem 1.1, which
gives the same conclusion for every $f$ whose values have norm one in a real
or complex Hilbert space. The paper is held as
[[../library/discrepancy/tao_2016_erdos_discrepancy_problem/_index|Tao 2016]].

**Argument.** The proof has three steps. A Fourier-analytic reduction from the
Polymath5 project replaces $f$ by a random completely multiplicative function
$g$ with values in the unit circle whose discrepancy controls that of $f$. The
author's logarithmically averaged form of the Elliott conjecture then forces
such a $g$ to behave like a modulated Dirichlet character. An extension of a
further Polymath5 argument shows that character-like functions still have
unbounded discrepancy. The unit-norm hypothesis at every $n$ is necessary: a
non-principal Dirichlet character of period $q$ has discrepancy at most $q$
but vanishes on the multiples of $q$.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Discrete Analysis 2016, Paper No. 1, published
2016-02-28, following the preprint arXiv:1509.05363 of 2015-09-17 (six
versions, the last of 2017-01-13). Reviewed: the site's curator, T. F.
Bloom, records the question as true and proved by Tao in the problem's
commentary (page last edited 2026-04-17, read 2026-10-07); the curator is
independent of the author. The community database, which the claimant
maintains, also lists the problem as proved; that listing is not independent
review. The only formalization recorded is the statement in
formal-conjectures, linked from the problem page; no kernel-checked proof is
recorded, and this corpus has audited none.

**Not covered.** Erdős's further conjecture, stated in several of the problem's
sources, that the maximum over $md\le x$ grows at least like $\log x$ is a
separate question the paper leaves open. Its Example 1.4, the
Borwein--Choi--Coons function, is a completely multiplicative
$f:\mathbb{N}\to\{-1,+1\}$ whose discrepancy up to $N$ is comparable to
$\log N$, so the conjectured order would be sharp, and its Example 1.5 is a
unit-vector-valued $f$ whose discrepancy grows only like $\sqrt{\log N}$; the
paper conjectures that $\sqrt{\log N}$ is best possible for Theorem 1.1, says it
is unclear whether a $\pm1$ sequence can grow that slowly, and notes that its
argument gives an explicit lower bound in principle, one likely far too weak to
reach $\sqrt{\log N}$. The site records McNamara's lower bound of order
$(\log\log x)^{1/484-o(1)}$ for the variant in which $m\le x$ and $d\le e^x$
(McNamara's 2021 UCLA dissertation, Chapter 4, entry [Mc21] on the problem
page), and Erdős's question about multiplicative $f$. Neither bears on the
standing of the question above.
