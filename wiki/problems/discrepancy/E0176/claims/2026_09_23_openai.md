***

**Claim.** The preprint *Quantitative superexponential bounds for van der
Waerden numbers* of the OpenAI mathematics release (dated 23 September 2026,
authored by OpenAI) states as its main theorem that there is an absolute
integer $K_0$ such that for every $k\ge K_0$ and every $r\ge2$

$$
W_r(k)>k^{ck\lfloor\log_2r\rfloor},\qquad c=10^{-5},
$$

where $W_r(k)$ is the least $N$ such that every map $[N]\to[r]$ is
constant on some $k$-term arithmetic progression inside $[N]$ (fewer than
$r$ colors may be used). For two colors this is $W(k)>k^{k/100000}$ for all
large $k$, so $W(k)^{1/k}\to\infty$, the question of
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0138/_index|Problem 138]], which the
manuscript names as its target; its introduction credits the earlier bounds
of Berlekamp, Szabó, Kozik and Shabanov, Hunter, Fox and Hunter, and Campos,
Fox and Schildkraut, and says its bound gives the full superexponential
limit and holds uniformly down to two colors, where Fox and Hunter's needs
three colors and Campos, Fox and Schildkraut's settles only $W_2(k)/2^k$.
The manuscript has a
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/openai_2026_quantitative_superexponential_bounds_van_der_waerden_numbers/_index|library card]],
with the main theorem on its
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/openai_2026_quantitative_superexponential_bounds_van_der_waerden_numbers/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]]
page. The release's README says its manuscripts were produced by an
internal OpenAI model and that the collection includes results at different
stages of verification.

The bearing on [[problems/discrepancy/E0176/_index|Problem 176]] is through
the identity $N(k,k)=W(k)$, which the site's commentary on the problem
states and the manuscript does not. A $k$-term progression carries $k$
signs, so its sum has absolute value at most $k$ and the parity of $k$; a
sum of absolute value at least $k-1$ is therefore a sum of absolute value
exactly $k$, a monochromatic progression, so $N(k,k-1)=W(k)$ as well (the
case $\ell=k-1$ of a parity remark in the site's thread, recorded on the
problem page). Hence the manuscript's theorem gives
$N(k,k)>k^{k/100000}$ for all large $k$, which no exponential $C^k$
bounds. The first displayed question of the Statement therefore fails at
$c=1$, provided the theorem holds.

**Covers.** The case $c=1$ of the first displayed question, whether for
every $c>0$ some $C>1$ bounds $N(k,ck)$ by $C^k$, which fails because
$N(k,k)=W(k)$ grows faster than any exponential. Nothing is settled for
$0<c<1$, the substantive range of the question, nor for the two displayed
special cases $N(k,2)$ and $N(k,\sqrt k)$, nor for the request for good
upper bounds. For $c>1$ no $N(k,ck)$ exists at all, since no $k$-term
sum exceeds $k$ in absolute value; that is a formulation note on the
problem page, not part of this claim.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/additive_combinatorics/E0138/claims/2026_09_23_openai|OpenAI's accepted claim on Problem 138]]
supplies Theorem 1.1, the bound $W(k)>k^{k/100000}$ for all large $k$; the
reduction to $N(k,k)$ is the elementary identity stated above.

**Formalization.** The release's Lean tree at the pinned revision states the
theorem in `ComparatorChallenges/QuantitativeVanDerWaerden.lean`
(`OAI.QuantitativeVanDerWaerden.uniform_lower_bound`: for some $K$, every
$k\ge K$ and $r\ge2$ have $k^{k\lfloor\log_2r\rfloor/100000}<W(r,k)$ over
the reals, with `W r k` the least positive $N$ such that every coloring
$\mathbb N\to\mathrm{Fin}\,r$ has a one-color $k$-term progression with
positive step inside $[0,N)$), with `sorry` as the challenge form, and
proves it in `OAI/Combinatorics/ProgressionColoring/Main.lean`, which also
derives `kthRoot_tendsto` (the divergence of $W(r,k)^{1/k}$ for each
$r\ge2$). The comparator record permits only `propext`, `Quot.sound` and
`Classical.choice`; the release's catalog `formalization.yaml` lists the
declaration; toolchain `leanprover/lean4:v4.34.1`. For Problem 138 this
corpus's verification built `uniform_lower_bound` and `kthRoot_tendsto`
at the pinned revision and checked that their axioms are exactly
`propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, with the comparator
fingerprint identical; that record is Problem 138's. Nothing about
$N(k,\ell)$ or the identity above was formalized or reviewed, so
`formalized` is not listed as evidence here.

**Standing.** Claimed, partial. The manuscript is a release preprint with no
journal record and no independent review known to this corpus; the site's page
for Problem 176 was OPEN last edited 4 April 2026, with no proof claim and
thirteen comments, all earlier than the release; they include the Lean bounds
for $N(k,2)$ and $N(k,\sqrt k)$ recorded on their own pages, and none settles
the case $c=1$. A refereed version, a documented independent acceptance, or a
kernel-checked formalization of the reduction to this problem whose statement
this corpus audits would be needed before anything here moves to accepted.
