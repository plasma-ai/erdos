**Claim.** K. F. Roth, *Remark concerning integer sequences*, Acta Arith. 9
(1964), no. 3, 257--260, cited as [Ro64] on the problem page. For a set
$\mathcal N$ of distinct positive integers not exceeding $N$ with density
$\eta=\lvert\mathcal N\rvert/N$, let $V_q(m)$ be the sum over the residue
classes $h$ modulo $q$ of the squared difference between the number of
elements of $\mathcal N$ up to $m$ in the class $h$ and its expected value
$\eta m/q$. The Theorem (p. 257) states that for every $Q$

$$
\sum_{q\le Q}\frac1q\sum_{m\le N}V_q(m)+Q\sum_{q\le Q}V_q(N)\gg\eta(1-\eta)Q^2N
$$

with an absolute implied constant, and its corollary (4), with
$Q=\lfloor N^{1/2}\rfloor$, gives $m_0\le N$ and $q_0\le N^{1/2}$ with
$q_0^{-1}V_{q_0}(m_0)\gg\eta(1-\eta)N^{1/2}$: some arithmetic progression of
common difference at most $N^{1/2}$ inside $[1,N]$ has discrepancy
$\gg(\eta(1-\eta))^{1/2}N^{1/4}$. The proof compares upper and lower
estimates for the integral of $\sum_{q\le Q}\lvert F(q\alpha)S(\alpha)\rvert^2$,
with $S$ the exponential sum of the indicator minus its mean and $F$ a
partial geometric sum. The source is carded at
[[../library/discrepancy/roth_1964_remark_concerning_integer_sequences/_index|roth_1964_remark_concerning_integer_sequences]].

**Covers.** A lower bound on $h(d)$ in
[[problems/discrepancy/E0177/_index|Problem 177]]: no coloring
$f:\mathbb N\to\{-1,1\}$ has $\max_{P_d}\lvert\sum_{n\in P_d}f(n)\rvert=O(d^p)$
for any $p<1/2$. Applied to the set of $n\le N$ with $f(n)=1$, whose density
is bounded away from $0$ and $1$ when the discrepancy on the progressions of
difference $1$ is $o(N)$, the corollary gives a progression with difference
$d\le N^{1/2}$ and discrepancy $\gg N^{1/4}\ge d^{1/2}$ up to constants; the
site's commentary records the consequence as $h(d)\gg d^{1/2}$, and that
inference from Roth's theorem is the site's, not the paper's. The result
settles nothing about the order of $h(d)$ beyond this bound.
