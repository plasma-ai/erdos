***

**Claim.** J. Beck, *A discrepancy problem: balancing infinite dimensional
vectors*, in *Number Theory -- Diophantine Problems, Uniform Distribution and
Applications* (Springer, 2017), 61--82, cited as [Be17] on the problem page. The
chapter's abstract states the result as a corollary of a general theorem on
balancing infinite-dimensional vectors in the maximum norm: for every
$\epsilon>0$ there is a function $g:\mathbb N\to\{-1,1\}$ such that, with
$D_g(d)=\max_{a\ge1,m\ge1}\lvert\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}g(a+id)\rvert$ the largest sum
along a finite arithmetic progression of common difference $d$,
$D_g(d)\le d^{8+\epsilon}$ for all sufficiently large $d\ge d_0(\epsilon)$,
independently of the starting point and the length of the progression; in the
notation of [[problems/discrepancy/E0177/_index|Problem 177]],
$h(d)\le d^{8+\epsilon}$ is attainable for every $\epsilon>0$. The abstract adds
that Roth's theorem gives $D_g(d)\ge\sqrt d/20$ for every $g$, so a polynomial
bound is the right order. The argument enumerates the residue classes modulo
each $d$ by increasing modulus, writes a progression as the difference of two
prefixes of one class, and balances the membership vectors of the classes, a
method Korsky's 2026 manuscript on
[[problems/discrepancy/E0177/claims/2026_09_19_korsky|its claim page]] describes
as the one it refines. The chapter has no library card.

**Covers.** The upper bound $h(d)\le d^{8+\epsilon}$, for every $\epsilon>0$ and
all $d\ge d_0(\epsilon)$, alone. The problem asks for the smallest $h(d)$, and
the chapter determines neither its order nor a lower bound; Roth's bound on
[[problems/discrepancy/E0177/claims/1964_01_01_roth|its claim page]] leaves the
exponent between $1/2$ and $8$, and Korsky's claimed bound lowers the upper
exponent to $5/2+2\sqrt2$.
