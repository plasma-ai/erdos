**Claim.** For every sequence $z_1,z_2,\ldots$ on the unit circle, with
$s(k,n)=\sum_{j\le n}z_j^k$ and $A_k=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\lvert s(k,n)\rvert$,
there are infinitely many $k$ with $A_k>c_1\log k$, for an absolute constant
$c_1>0$; in particular $\limsup_kA_k=\infty$. This is the Theorem of Section
1 of P. Erdős, Some remarks on number theory, Israel J. Math. 3 (1965), 6-12
([Er65c] on the problem page), pp. 6-7. With $z_j=e(x_j)$ it answers the
first question of [[problems/discrepancy/E0987/_index|Problem 987]] yes for
every sequence in $(0,1)$: the paper's $A_k$ is the problem's $A_k$, a limit
superior over $n$, which the paper distinguishes from $B_k$, the supremum
over $n$ of the same sums. Erdős writes that in his 1964 paper ([Er64b] on
the problem page) he had observed $\limsup_kB_k=\infty$ but stated that he
could not prove the same for $A_k$, and that he had "overlooked the fact that
it is very easy to show" the theorem.

**Covers.** The first question: $\limsup_{k\to\infty}A_k=\infty$ for every
sequence, with the rate $\log k$. The rate was raised to $k^{1/2}$ by
[[problems/discrepancy/E0987/claims/1967_01_01_clunie|Clunie's theorem]],
which the paper's footnote added in proof already reports; the second
question, whether $A_k=o(k)$ is possible, is answered by
[[problems/discrepancy/E0987/claims/2026_04_08_alexeev_putterman_sawhney_sellke_valiant|the 2026 construction]].

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the result rests on the cited paper
alone.

**Proof (pp. 6-7, read in full).** Dirichlet's theorem on simultaneous
approximation gives, for any $n$ complex numbers $y_1,\dots,y_n$ of modulus one,
an integer $1\le k\le10^n$ with $\operatorname{Re}(y_i^k)>1/2$ for every $i$.
Applied to the blocks $z_{rn+1},\dots,z_{(r+1)n}$ for $r=0,1,2,\dots$, this
yields one $k\le10^n$ that serves infinitely many blocks, each of whose sums
then exceeds $n/2$ in modulus, so $A_k\ge n/4$; since $k\le10^n$, this is
$A_k>c_1\log k$, and letting $n$ grow gives infinitely many such $k$. The paper
adds that $A_k\ge ck$ for infinitely many $k$ may hold, with $c\le1$ by a remark
of Clunie that a footnote records, asks for the least $f(n,c)$ such that any $n$
complex numbers with $\lvert z_i\rvert\ge1$ have some $1\le k\le f(n,c)$ with
$\lvert\sum_{i\le n}z_i^k\rvert\ge c$, notes $f(n,1)=n$ from Turán's results,
and records in a footnote added in proof that Clunie proved
$f(n,c)<g(c)\,n\log n$ and $A_k>ck^{1/2}$.

**Source and dating.** The page's basis is the scan in the Rényi Institute's
Erdős archive, linked above. It was received on 10 February 1965 and appeared in
volume 3, issue 1, which the publisher's record dates March 1965; the day in the
page name is a placeholder. The site's commentary credits the proof to Erdős
under its key [Er65b], which the site's reference record resolves to his 1965
Wiley lectures, which do not contain the passage; the Israel J. Math. note is
the paper that holds it, as the problem page's reference [Er65c] records.

**Formalization.** The contributor's fork of formal-conjectures linked above
states the theorem as `erdos_987.variants.log_lower_bound`, for sequences in
$(0,1)$: there is $c>0$ with $c\log k\le A_k$ for infinitely many $k$. Its
proof outline follows Erdős's argument, with simultaneous Dirichlet
approximation to denominator $7^n$, the same block device and a pigeonhole on
the blocks; the repository's main branch points the declaration's
`formal_proof` attribute at that file. Not built or audited here, so it
adds no evidence kind.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Israel J. Math. 3 (1965), 6-12, a journal paper.
