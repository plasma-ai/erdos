***

**Claim.** For every sequence $x_1,x_2,\ldots\in(0,1)$, with
$A_k=\limsup_n\lvert\sum_{j\le n}e(kx_j)\rvert$, there are infinitely many
$k$ with $A_k\gg k^{1/2}$; in particular $\limsup_k A_k=\infty$. The same
paper shows that some sequences have $A_k\le k$ for every $k$, a bound that is
not $o(k)$ and so leaves the second question of
[[problems/discrepancy/E0987/_index|Problem 987]] open.

**Covers.** The first question: $\limsup_{k\to\infty}A_k=\infty$ for every
sequence, with the rate $k^{1/2}$ in place of Erdős's $\log k$. The second
question, whether $A_k=o(k)$ is possible, is answered by
[[problems/discrepancy/E0987/claims/2026_04_08_alexeev_putterman_sawhney_sellke_valiant|the 2026 construction]].

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the result rests on the cited paper
alone.

**Earlier and later bounds.** The site records that Erdős remarked in 1964 that
the variant with $\sup_n$ in place of $\limsup_n$ is easily seen to be unbounded
in $k$, and that in 1965 he gave a short proof of $A_k\gg\log k$ for infinitely
many $k$, which already answers the first question
([[problems/discrepancy/E0987/claims/1965_03_01_erdos|Erdős's claim page]]);
Clunie's bound is the first of power type. For sequences that take only finitely
many distinct values, Liu's 1969 bound $A_k>k^{1-\delta}/5$ for infinitely many
$k$ is on [[problems/discrepancy/E0987/claims/1969_06_01_liu|Liu's claim page]].
The 2025 forum proof and Lean formalization of the first question are on
[[problems/discrepancy/E0987/claims/2025_08_30_tao|Tao's claim page]].

**Dating.** The page is dated by the publication year. The journal record
(J. London Math. Soc. 42 (1967), 133–136) gives no day, and the day in the
page name is a placeholder.

**Formalization.** The contributor's fork of formal-conjectures linked
above states Clunie's bound as `erdos_987.variants.sqrt_lower_bound` and
his linear upper bound as `erdos_987.variants.linear_upper_bound`, with
proofs its comments attribute to the lemmas of the 1967 paper; the
repository's main branch points its `formal_proof` attributes at that file.
Not built or audited here, so it adds no evidence kind.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: J. London Math. Soc. 42 (1967), 133–136. Reviewed: the
site's curator, Thomas Bloom, labels the problem PROVED (LEAN) and credits
Clunie in the problem's commentary with $A_k\gg k^{1/2}$ for infinitely many $k$
and with sequences having $A_k\le k$ for all $k$ (page last edited 2026-04-09).
The curator is independent of the author. The statements above follow the site's
record of the paper.
