# Problem 101

[[problems/discrete_geometry/_index|..]]

***

**Statement.** Given $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$, no five of which are on a
line, the number of lines containing four points is $o(n^2)$.

**Status.** Open.

**Source.** [erdosproblems.com/101](https://www.erdosproblems.com/101), accessed
2026-09-04. Cite as: T. F. Bloom, Erdős Problem #101,
https://www.erdosproblems.com/101.

**References.**

- [BGS74] Burr, Stefan A. and Grünbaum, Branko and Sloane, N. J. A., The orchard
  problem. Geometriae Dedicata (1974), 397-424.
- [FuPa84] Füredi, Z. and Palásti, I., Arrangements of lines with a large number
  of triangles. Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. (1984), 561-566.
- [Gr76] Grünbaum, Branko, New views on some old questions of combinatorial
  geometry. Colloquio Internazionale sulle Teorie Combinatorie (Roma, 1973),
  Tomo I (1976), 451-468.
- [SoSt13] Solymosi, József and Stojaković, Miloš, Many collinear $k$-tuples
  with no $k+1$ collinear points. Discrete Comput. Geom. (2013), 811-820.
