***

**Claim.** The answer to [[problems/discrete_geometry/E0106/_index|Problem 106]]
is no: $f(17)>4$, so $f(k^2+1)=k$ fails at $k=4$. The witness is a packing of
seventeen squares in the unit square, sixteen of them parallel to its sides and
one rotated, described first in a square of side $4$ and then scaled by one
quarter, with side lengths summing to

$$
4+\frac{78}{4\cdot 1048735}=4.0000185\ldots
$$

The packing differs from the one on
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0106/claims/2026_07_29_silverstein|Silverstein's claim page]],
whose sum is $4.000124\ldots$, so it is a separate counterexample rather than a
formalization of that one. The Lean file proves that the seventeen squares
lie in the unit square with pairwise disjoint interiors and that their side
lengths sum to the value above; its theorem `Erdos106.four_lt_f_seventeen`,
$4<f(17)$, carries the claim.

**Claimant.** The Lean proof was added to Boris Alexeev's repository of
formalized Erdős problems on 2026-07-29, about fifteen hours before
Silverstein's claim was posted, with OpenAI Codex as its only named author and
no informal author; on 2026-08-23 its header was extended to name A. Raj Singh
as informal author and Codex and GPT-5.6 Sol as formal authors, which is how
the file linked above reads at its pinned commit. No informal manuscript of
this packing is recorded, and Singh's paper on the problem, listed among the
problem page's references, reformulates the conjecture without a
counterexample.

**Acceptance.** Formalized. This corpus's verification built the module
`ErdosProblems.Erdos106` of the repository's `src/latest` folder at the pinned
commit of 2026-09-15 (Lean `v4.33.0`, Mathlib `v4.33.0`), together with the
repository's comparator challenge for the problem, and checked the axioms of
`Erdos106.four_lt_f_seventeen` and `Erdos106.not_erdos_106`, which are exactly
`propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. The built file is the
version at the pinned commit, a revision of the 2026-07-29 posting moved to
Lean `v4.33.0` and given the extended header; the original posting was not
built. The challenge pins only `Erdos106.not_erdos_106`,
$\neg\,\forall k\in\mathbb N,\ f(k^2+1)=k$, with the definitions its type
reaches (a square given by its center, a unit side direction and its side
length; its closed and open point sets; the box $[0,L]^2$; a packing; the total
side length; the set of attainable totals; and $f$ as its supremum), and the
fingerprint of that declaration and of each of those definitions was found
identical to the challenge. That theorem alone does not carry the claim: it
already holds at $k=0$, where the identity asks $f(1)=0$ while one unit square
gives $f(1)=1$, and it says nothing about $f(17)$. The claim rests on
`Erdos106.four_lt_f_seventeen`, which the challenge does not pin but which is
stated over the same compared $f$. The statement audit found that $f$ is
faithful to the problem: the squares may be rotated, each is a closed square
of positive side inside the closed unit square, their interiors are pairwise
disjoint, and $f(n)$ is the supremum of a nonempty set bounded above, so
$4<f(17)$ is exactly the disproof at $k=4$. Not reviewed: the site's curator
credits Silverstein's packing
([[problems/discrete_geometry/E0106/claims/2026_07_29_silverstein|its claim page]]),
not this one, and the site's label notes a Lean verification without linking
one; no outside reviewer has published an examination of this development. Not
refereed: there is no journal publication of this packing.
