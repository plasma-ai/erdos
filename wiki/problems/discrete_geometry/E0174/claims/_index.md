# problems/discrete_geometry/E0174/claims

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0174/_index|..]]

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0174/claims/1973_05_01_erdos_graham_montgomery_rothschild_spencer_straus|1973_05_01_erdos_graham_montgomery_rothschild_spencer_straus]]: Theorems 13 and 20 of Euclidean Ramsey Theorems I: every finite Ramsey set
lies on a sphere, and the orthogonal product of two Ramsey sets is Ramsey,
so every rectangular box and its subsets are Ramsey; refereed, JCTA 1973.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0174/claims/1990_01_01_frankl_rodl|1990_01_01_frankl_rodl]]: Theorem 5.1 of the 1990 paper: every finite affinely independent set is
super-Ramsey, with exponentially dense finite witnesses, so every
nondegenerate simplex is Ramsey; refereed, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 3 (1990).

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0174/claims/1991_07_01_kriz|1991_07_01_kriz]]: Theorem 4.3 of the 1991 paper: a finite set with a transitive soluble group
of isometries is Ramsey, with a two-orbit extension; so every regular
polygon and each regular polyhedron is Ramsey; refereed, PAMS 112 (1991).

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0174/claims/1992_10_01_kriz|1992_10_01_kriz]]: The 1992 Discrete Mathematics note proves that every trapezoid in its
sense, a quadrilateral inscribed in a circle with two parallel sides, is
Ramsey; refereed, Discrete Math. 108 (1992).

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0174/claims/2026_09_20_palvolgyi|2026_09_20_palvolgyi]]: Seven points on a circle, with a transcendental radius, claimed not to be
Ramsey; the first claimed disproof of Graham's conjecture that every
spherical set is Ramsey, with the proof attributed by the author to ChatGPT.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0174/claims/2026_09_23_openai|2026_09_23_openai]]: A finite set is Ramsey exactly when an explicit linear system over the
tensor square of its coordinate field has a solution; kernel-checked in
Lean, built and axiom-audited here, with no decision procedure proved.
