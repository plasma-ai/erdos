***

Green and Tao determine $f(n)$ exactly for all sufficiently large $n$: $n$
points in the real plane, not all on one line, determine at least $n/2$
ordinary lines, lines through exactly two of the points (Theorem 1.2, the
Dirac-Motzkin conjecture), and more precisely at least $f(n)$ of them, where
$f(2m)=m$, $f(4m+1)=3m$ and $f(4m-1)=3m-3$, so $f(n)=3\lfloor n/4\rfloor$ for
odd $n$ (Theorem 2.2). Every one of these values is attained: $n/2$ equally
spaced points on a circle together with the $n/2$ points at infinity in the
directions of the lines joining them (the sides and diagonals of the regular
polygon) span exactly $n/2$ ordinary lines. When $n/2$ is even, adding the
center gives $n+1$ points with $3n/4$ ordinary lines, and removing a point at
infinity that lies on the tangents at two opposite circle points gives $n-1$
points with $3n/4-3$; when $n/2$ is odd, removing any one point at infinity
gives $n-1$ points with $3(n-2)/4$ (Proposition 2.1, the Böröczky examples);
Theorem 2.2 adds that, up to a projective transformation, these are the only
sets attaining $f(n)$. Both theorems follow from a structure theorem
(Theorems 1.4 and 1.5): a set with at most $Kn$ ordinary lines has all but
$O(K)$ of its points on a cubic curve, for $n$ large in terms of $K$. The
source card
[[../library/discrete_geometry/green_2013_sets_defining_few_ordinary_lines/_index|records
Theorems 1.2 and 2.2, Proposition 2.1 and the structure theorems]].

This settles the second question of
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0210/_index|Problem 210]], how fast $f(n)$ grows:
linearly, with $f(n)=n/2$ for even $n$ and $f(n)=3\lfloor n/4\rfloor$ for odd
$n$ once $n$ is large, after the bounds $3n/7$ of
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0210/claims/1958_01_01_kelly_moser|Kelly and Moser]]
and $6n/13$ of
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0210/claims/1993_02_01_csima_sawyer|Csima and Sawyer]],
and
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0210/claims/1951_05_01_motzkin|Motzkin's proof]]
that $f(n)\to\infty$. The threshold $n_0$ is not made explicit, and the exact
value of $f(n)$ for small $n$ is not what the question asks.

The result is refereed: Ben Green and Terence Tao, On sets defining few
ordinary lines, Discrete Comput. Geom. 50 (2013), no. 2, 409-468; the preprint
is arXiv:1208.4714, first posted 2012-08-23. The site's curator, T. F. Bloom,
marks the problem proved and credits this paper with the bound $n/2$ for large
$n$ and with the odd case.
