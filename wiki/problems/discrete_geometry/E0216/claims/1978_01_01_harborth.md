***

**Claim.** Every set of 10 points in the plane in general position contains
five points in convex position with no point of the set inside their convex
hull, and there is a set of 9 points in general position with no such
pentagon. In the notation of
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0216/_index|Problem 216]], $g(5)=10$.

**Covers.** The value $g(5)=10$, the instance $k=5$ of the estimation half of
the question. The value $g(4)=5$ is Erdős's observation; the instance $k=6$ is
settled on the
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0216/claims/2007_09_01_nicolas|Nicolás]],
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0216/claims/2007_09_11_gerken|Gerken]] and
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0216/claims/2024_03_01_heule_scheucher|Heule–Scheucher]]
claim pages, and $g(k)$ does not exist for $k\ge7$ by
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0216/claims/1983_12_01_horton|Horton's result]].

**Acceptance.** The paper is refereed: H. Harborth, Konvexe Fünfecke in
ebenen Punktmengen, Elemente der Mathematik 33 (1978), 116–118, in the
journal's Kleine Mitteilungen; the link above is the e-periodica record of the
volume. The site's remarks credit the value to this paper; the site's label,
disproved, rests on Horton's result, so that remark is not acceptance of this
partial claim.
