***

**Claim.** For every $k$ there is a set of $2^k$ points in the plane that
contains no empty convex heptagon: no seven of its points are the vertices of
a convex heptagon whose interior is free of the set. Hence $g(7)$ does not
exist, and neither does $g(k)$ for any $k\ge7$, since an empty convex $k$-gon
contains an empty convex heptagon among its vertices. The problem asks whether
$g(k)$ exists for every $k$; Horton's construction answers no. The problem's
wording and the site's page omit the general-position convention; Horton
defines $g(n)$ over sets with no three collinear, as the literature on $g(k)$
does, and $S_k$ is such a set.

**The construction.** Horton's set $S_k$ consists of the points $(i,d(i))$
for $0\le i<2^k$, where $d(i)$ is read off the binary digits of $i$ with a
base $c=2^k+1$. Splitting on the lowest binary digit cuts $S_k$ into a bottom
half $B$ and a top half $T$ that are scaled translates of each other, and
every point of $T$ lies above every line through two points of $B$. An empty
convex polygon lying in one half maps affinely onto an empty convex polygon of
the smaller set, so one may assume it meets both halves. Such a polygon has at
most three vertices in $B$ and three in $T$, hence at most six. The
[[../library/discrete_geometry/horton_1983_sets_no_empty_convex_7_gons/_index|source card]]
records the paper's observations and the counting step.

**What remains of the question.** The function exists for small $k$:
$g(4)=5$ (Erdős), $g(5)=10$
([[problems/discrete_geometry/E0216/claims/1978_01_01_harborth|Harborth]]),
and $g(6)$ exists by the independent proofs of
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0216/claims/2007_09_01_nicolas|Nicolás]] and
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0216/claims/2007_09_11_gerken|Gerken]], with
$g(6)=30$ established by
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0216/claims/2024_03_01_heule_scheucher|Heule and Scheucher]].
Horton's note already records $g(5)=10$ and leaves the hexagon case open.

**Acceptance.** The paper is refereed: J. D. Horton, Sets with no empty
convex 7-gons, Canad. Math. Bull. 26 (1983), no. 4, 482–484. The curator of
erdosproblems.com, Thomas Bloom, labels the problem disproved and credits
Horton's paper for the nonexistence of $g(k)$ for $k\ge7$.
