# problems/discrete_geometry/E0352/claims

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0352/_index|..]]

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0352/claims/1978_01_01_erdos|1978_01_01_erdos]]: Erdős's statement, with the proof left to the reader, that a planar set of
infinite measure contains a triangle of every prescribed area; later sources
add unbounded sets of positive measure, so these sets answer the question.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0352/claims/2002_01_01_freiling_mauldin|2002_01_01_freiling_mauldin]]: Freiling and Mauldin's theorem, reported by Mauldin in 2002, that a planar
set with no triangle of area greater than one has outer measure at most
$4\pi/\sqrt{27}$; for convex sets it answers the question yes.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0352/claims/2013_01_01_freiling_mauldin|2013_01_01_freiling_mauldin]]: The result reported in Mauldin's 2013 survey that Erdős's constant
$4\pi/\sqrt{27}$ is the sharp threshold when the set is the union of the
interiors of at most three compact convex sets; the general case is open.
