# problems/discrete_geometry/E0506/claims

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0506/_index|..]]

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0506/claims/1967_03_01_elliott|1967_03_01_elliott]]: Elliott's 1967 theorem asserts that n > 393 points of the plane, not all on
one circle or one line, determine at least C(n-1,2) circles; the bound is
false, as a circle with n-1 points and one point off it shows.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0506/claims/1994_09_01_balintova_balint|1994_09_01_balintova_balint]]: Bálintová and Bálint prove that n points, not all on one circle or one line,
determine at least 5n(n-1)/133 circles through exactly three of them, and
print the corrected Elliott bound without proof; refereed.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0506/claims/2009_07_03_purdy_smith|2009_07_03_purdy_smith]]: Purdy and Smith correct Elliott's 1967 theorem: n points, not all on one
circle or line, determine at least 1 + C(n-1,2) - floor((n-1)/2) circles
once n > 393, and the bound is attained; only finitely many n remain.

[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0506/claims/2026_08_20_wrona|2026_08_20_wrona]]: A claimed formula for the least number of circles determined by n points
that are neither collinear nor concyclic, for every n at least 4, with a
Lean development by the claimant; AI-assisted and unreviewed.
