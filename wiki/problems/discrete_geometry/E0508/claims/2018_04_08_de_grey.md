***

**Claim.** Aubrey D. N. J. de Grey, *The chromatic number of the plane is at
least 5*, Geombinatorics 28 (2018), no. 1, 18–31; posted as arXiv:1804.02385
on 8 April 2018; carded at
[[../library/discrete_geometry/grey_2018_chromatic_number_plane_is_at_least/_index|grey_2018_chromatic_number_plane_is_at_least]].
The paper presents a family of finite unit-distance graphs in the plane that
admit no proper $4$-coloring; the smallest it reports has $1581$ vertices.
Since a proper coloring of the plane restricts to a proper coloring of every
unit-distance graph drawn in it, the chromatic number asked for by
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0508/_index|Problem 508]] satisfies
$\chi(\mathbb R^2)\ge5$, the first improvement of either Hadwiger–Nelson
bound since the bounds $4\le\chi\le7$ of 1950. The construction starts from
the seven-vertex hexagonal unit-distance graph $H$, whose $4$-colorings fall
into four types, two with a monochromatic triple of vertices and two
without; a graph $L$ of $52$ copies of $H$ forces some copy to carry a
monochromatic triple in every $4$-coloring, and a graph $M$, checked by a
custom backtracking search, admits no $4$-coloring in which its central copy
of $H$ carries one. Assembling copies of $M$ along $L$ gives a
non-$4$-colorable graph on $20425$ vertices, which deleting and adding
vertices reduces to $1581$ vertices; the paper reports that others confirmed
with SAT solvers that this graph has no $4$-coloring, as recorded on the
[[../library/discrete_geometry/grey_2018_chromatic_number_plane_is_at_least/section_5_1|Section 5.1 page]].

**Covers.** The exclusion of four colors, $\chi(\mathbb R^2)\ge5$, and no
more: the paper gives no upper bound and does not determine $\chi$. The
bound is superseded by the accepted exclusion of five colors on
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0508/claims/2026_09_23_openai|OpenAI's claim page]],
which gives $6\le\chi(\mathbb R^2)\le7$.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Geombinatorics published the paper. Later papers
found smaller non-$4$-colorable unit-distance graphs and other proofs of the
bound, recorded on the problem page. The site's curator credits the lower
bound five to this paper in the problem's commentary, but the site labels
the problem OPEN, so that credit is context and not `reviewed` evidence.
