***

**Claim.** J.-P. Kahane, *Sur le recouvrement d'un cercle par des arcs
disposés au hasard*, C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 248 (1959), 184–186, is a note
of the séance of 5 January 1959. It places arcs of given lengths
$1>l_1\ge l_2\ge\cdots$ independently and uniformly on the circle of unit
length. Its Theorem 1 proves that the arcs cover the whole circle almost
surely as soon as

$$
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{\log t}\sum_{l_n>1/t}l_n>1.
$$

Theorem 2 shows that the constant $1$ cannot be lowered: for every
$\varepsilon>0$, some sequence whose lim sup exceeds $1-\varepsilon$ does
not cover almost surely. Theorem 3 proves that covering almost surely
requires $\limsup_{n\to\infty}l_n\,n\log\log n>0$. The note says that its
results do not decide $l_n=\alpha/n$ with $\alpha\le1$. The site credits
Kahane with $a_n=(1+c)/n$, $c>0$. As written, $a_1=1+c$ exceeds the
circumference, so the first arc alone covers the circle. The case has
content for lengths equal to $(1+c)/n$ from some index on. There the sum in
Theorem 1 is $(1+c)\log t+O(1)$, so the lim sup is $1+c>1$ (a remark of this
page). The paper has no library card.

**Covers.** The nonincreasing sequences of lengths below $1$ that satisfy
Theorem 1's condition, which cover almost surely; they include lengths
$(1+c)/n$ from some index on. Also the sequences with
$l_n\,n\log\log n\to0$, which do not cover almost surely (Theorem 3). Not
covered: $l_n=\alpha/n$ with $\alpha\le1$, which the note leaves open and
the site credits to Erdős (unpublished), and the criterion itself, which is
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0526/claims/1972_09_01_shepp|Shepp's]].

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the note appeared in C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 248
(1959). The site's curator, T. F. Bloom, labels the problem SOLVED and credits
Kahane with the case $a_n=(1+c)/n$ (problem page accessed); the label settles
the problem through Shepp's criterion, not this note, so the page lists no
`reviewed` evidence.
