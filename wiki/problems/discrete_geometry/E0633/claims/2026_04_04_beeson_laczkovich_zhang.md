***

**Claim.** Beeson, Laczkovich and Zhang [BLZ26] prove that a triangle can
be cut into a positive nonsquare number of congruent triangles if and only
if its angles can be labeled $(A,B,C)$ so that one of eight conditions
holds: $A=B$ (the isosceles triangles); $C=\pi/2$ with legs in ratio $M/K$
for positive integers $M,K$ with $M^2+K^2$ not a square; the angles are
$(\pi/6,\pi/2,\pi/3)$; $C=\pi/3$ and $\sqrt3\tan(A/2)$ is rational; $B=2A$
and $\sqrt3\tan(A/2)$ is rational; $B=2A$ and $\sin(A/2)$ is rational;
$C=A/2+B$ and $2\sin(A/4)=M/K$ for positive integers $M,K$ with $2K^2-M^2$
not a square; or $C=2A+B/2$ and $\sqrt3\tan(A/2)$ is rational. The
triangles [[problems/discrete_geometry/E0633/_index|Problem 633]] asks
for, those that can only be cut into a square number of congruent
triangles, are exactly the triangles outside these eight families; by
the paper's Corollary 2, outside the isosceles triangles they miss only
countably many similarity classes. The statement, the paper's dependency
boundary and a rewritten deduction are on
[[../library/discrete_geometry/beeson_2026_solution_erdos_problem_633/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
of the source card, whose qualifications (a misidentified reference on
p. 9, an unproved remark on p. 16, and the secondary Theorem 32) do not
touch the classification.
