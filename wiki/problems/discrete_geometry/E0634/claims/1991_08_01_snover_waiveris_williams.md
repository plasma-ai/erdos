***

**Claim.** S. L. Snover, C. Waiveris and J. K. Williams, Rep-tiling for
triangles, Discrete Math. 91 (1991), no. 2, 193–200, classify the cuttings of
a triangle into $N$ congruent triangles similar to itself. $N$ is $n^2$ (any
triangle), $n^2+m^2$ (for non-square $N$, only the right triangle with legs in
ratio $n:m$) or $3n^2$ (only the triangle with angles $\pi/6$, $\pi/3$ and
$\pi/2$), and every value in these families occurs. The altitude to the
hypotenuse splits the right triangle with legs in ratio $n:m$ into two copies
similar to it, which are cut into $n^2$ and $m^2$ congruent pieces; the
triangle with angles $\pi/6$, $\pi/3$ and $\pi/2$ is cut into three copies
similar to it. Harries's manuscripts
([[problems/discrete_geometry/E0634/claims/2026_07_24_harries|first claim
page]], [[problems/discrete_geometry/E0634/claims/2026_07_27_harries|second
claim page]]) cite the paper for these counts and this construction. Such a
cutting is a cutting into congruent triangles, so these values occur for
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0634/_index|Problem 634]].

**Covers.** The values $n^2+m^2$ and $3n^2$ ($n,m\ge1$) occur. Nothing is
claimed about cuttings whose pieces are not similar to the whole triangle.
