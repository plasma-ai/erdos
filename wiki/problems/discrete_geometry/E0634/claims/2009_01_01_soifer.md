***

**Claim.** Alexander Soifer, Is there anything beyond the solution?, chapter
6 of How Does One Cut a Triangle?, 2nd ed., Springer, New York, 2009, pp.
47–50, DOI 10.1007/978-0-387-74652-4_6, reports Erdős's question and shows
that the numbers $2n^2$, $3n^2$, $6n^2$ and $n^2+m^2$ occur as the number of
congruent triangles into which some triangle can be cut, beside the squares
$n^2$, which any triangle realizes. This is the source the site's remarks on
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0634/_index|Problem 634]] credit for these
families, and the 2026 manuscripts on the prime values cite the same pages
for the classical constructions; the page is dated to the year of the cited
edition.

**Covers.** The values $2n^2$, $3n^2$, $6n^2$ and $n^2+m^2$ occur for all
positive integers $n$ and $m$. Nothing is claimed about other values of $n$.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Standing.** Claimed. The chapter is part of a monograph, not a refereed
journal publication, and the site labels the problem OPEN, so its credit is
commentary, not acceptance. The constructions are used as known by George,
Harries, Beeson and Bonfioli in their manuscripts recorded on this problem's
other claim pages.
