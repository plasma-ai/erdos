***

**Claim.** Yan X Zhang, Tiling triangles with $2\pi/3$ angles,
arXiv:2512.22696 (version 1 posted 27 December 2025, version 4 of 4 April
2026), proves (Theorem 4) that for a tile with sides $a\ge b$ and
$c=\sqrt{a^2+ab+b^2}$, all three integers, and so with an angle of $2\pi/3$,
the length $mab$ is equiconstructible for every integer
$m\ge3\lceil(c^2-a-b)/(ab)\rceil$, and hence an equilateral triangle can be
cut into $m^2ab$ congruent copies of the tile. So every such $m^2ab$ occurs
as a value of $n$ for [[problems/discrete_geometry/E0634/_index|Problem
634]]. The integrality of all three sides is the standing assumption of the
paper's Section 2.1, forced for the tiles in question by the rationality
theorem of Beeson and Zhang; the construction cuts an equilateral triangle
into three ideal trapezoids. Lemma 3 shows the family sharp for squarefree
$a$ and $b$, and Conjecture 1 asserts that every equiconstructible length is
a multiple of $ab$. The digest is on the
[[../library/discrete_geometry/zhang_2025_tiling_triangles_angles/_index|source
card]].

**Covers.** The values $m^2ab$ for every integer-sided tile $(a,b,c)$ with
$c^2=a^2+ab+b^2$ and every $m\ge3\lceil(c^2-a-b)/(ab)\rceil$ occur. Nothing
is claimed about other values of $n$; the paper's conjecture on which counts
the $2\pi/3$ family admits is not a claim.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Standing.** Claimed. The preprint has no journal record; the site's
remarks credit the result, but the site labels the problem OPEN, so the
credit is commentary, not acceptance. The site's remark states the result
for arbitrary integers $a\ge b$, without the integrality of $c$, and
misprints the third side as $\sqrt{a^2+b^2+2+ab}$. Harries's second
manuscript
([[problems/discrete_geometry/E0634/claims/2026_07_27_harries|claim page]])
imports Theorem 4 and the row transfers of Propositions 8 to 11 and reports
sharper thresholds for the same rows.
