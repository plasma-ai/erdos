**Claim.** B. Green and T. Tao, On sets defining few ordinary lines,
*Discrete & Computational Geometry* 50 (2013), no. 2, 409--468
([[../library/discrete_geometry/green_2013_sets_defining_few_ordinary_lines/_index|source card]]),
prove in Theorem 1.3 that there is an $n_0$ such that every set of $n\ge n_0$
points in the plane has at most $\lfloor n(n-3)/6\rfloor+1$ lines containing
exactly three of its points. The cubic-curve construction recorded on the
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0669/claims/1974_02_01_burr_grunbaum_sloane|Burr--Grünbaum--Sloane claim page]]
attains this number, so in the notation of
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0669/_index|Problem 669]]
$f_3(n)=\lfloor n(n-3)/6\rfloor+1$ for all large $n$. The proof rests on a
structure theorem: a set with few ordinary lines lies, up to a bounded number
of points, on a cubic curve.

**Covers.** The exact value of $f_3(n)$ for all large $n$. It gives no exact
value of $F_3(n)$ and nothing for $k\ge4$.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0669/claims/1974_02_01_burr_grunbaum_sloane|Burr, Grünbaum and Sloane's claim]],
for the matching construction.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the paper appeared in *Discrete & Computational
Geometry*. The site's page does not cite it, and the site labels the problem
OPEN, so no curator review is listed.
