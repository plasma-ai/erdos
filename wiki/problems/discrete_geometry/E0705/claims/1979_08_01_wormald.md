***

**Claim.** N. Wormald, *A 4-chromatic graph with a special plane drawing*, J.
Austral. Math. Soc. Ser. A 28 (1979), no. 1, 1–8
([[../library/discrete_geometry/wormald_1979_chromatic_graph_special_plane_drawing/_index|source card]]),
exhibits a finite set of $6448$ points in the plane whose unit distance graph,
with an edge exactly when two points are at distance $1$, has girth $5$ and
chromatic number $4$. The abstract graph attaches a $5$-cycle to each
$5$-subset of $13$ ordered points; a $3$-coloring would make some $5$-subset
monochromatic and leave its attached $5$-cycle two colors, which is
impossible. The plane realization is shown to exist by continuity arguments
checked by computer. In the terms of
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0705/_index|Problem 705]], no $k\le5$ makes every
finite unit distance graph of girth at least $k$ $3$-colorable.

**Covers.** No $k\le5$ works. Nothing is settled for $k\ge6$; that case is
settled by
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0705/claims/1999_10_01_odonnell|O'Donnell's dissertation]].
