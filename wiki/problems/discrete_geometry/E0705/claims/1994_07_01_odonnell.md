***

**Claim.** P. O'Donnell, *A triangle-free 4-chromatic graph in the plane*,
Geombinatorics 4 (1994), no. 1, 23–29, constructs a unit distance graph in
the plane on $56$ vertices with girth $4$ and chromatic number $4$, as the
site's commentary on
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0705/_index|Problem 705]] reports. If its point
set has no unit distance besides the graph's edges, no $k\le4$ makes every
finite unit distance graph of girth at least $k$ $3$-colorable.

**Covers.** No $k\le4$ works. Nothing is settled for $k\ge5$; Wormald's graph
of girth $5$ settles $k=5$
([[problems/discrete_geometry/E0705/claims/1979_08_01_wormald|Wormald 1979]]),
and O'Donnell's dissertation every $k$
([[problems/discrete_geometry/E0705/claims/1999_10_01_odonnell|O'Donnell 1999]]).

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Standing.** Claimed. The paper appeared in Geombinatorics, whose archive
lists it in the issue of July 1994 and holds no copy online. The problem asks
about the faithful graph of a point set, with an edge exactly when two points
are at distance $1$, while O'Donnell's own definition of a unit distance graph
in his 1999 dissertation allows further unit distances between non-adjacent
vertices. Whether this paper's point set has none is not established from the
paper, so `refereed` is not listed. The curator's label credits the
dissertation, not this paper.

**Dating.** The journal's archive dates the issue July 1994; the day is a
placeholder.
