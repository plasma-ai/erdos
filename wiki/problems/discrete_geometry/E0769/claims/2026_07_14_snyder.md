***

**Claim.** The particular question of
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0769/_index|Problem 769]], whether $c(n)\gg
n^n$, is answered no. The Lean 4 theorem
`Erdos769.erdos769_lower_bound_false`, in `Research/Solution.lean` of the
`starfleet/erdos-769` folder of the `williamjblair/lean-proofs` repository,
proves `¬ Erdos769LowerBound`, where the development's `Erdos769LowerBound`
says that there are positive integers $A$, $B$ and $N$ with $A\,n^n\le B\,c$
whenever $n\ge N$ and $c$ is the cutoff of dimension $n$: the least $c$ such
that every $k\ge c$ is the tile count of an exact decomposition of the unit
$n$-cube into axis-parallel homothetic cubes, in the development's half-open
model. The route, as the development's files and the Star Fleet Math report
describe it: for odd $n\ge201$ every $k\ge n\,2^n\lceil 49n/100\rceil^n+2$ is
such a tile count, by regular grid tilings, the substitution that replaces one
cube by $m^n$ cubes, and a Bézout conductor bound for the resulting increments
modulo $2^n-1$; this threshold is $o(n^n)$, so $c(n)/n^n\to0$ along the odd
dimensions and no absolute constant bounds $c(n)/n^n$ below. The hosting
repository's index credits the proof to Colin Snyder of Star Fleet Math
(starfleetmath.com), whose site describes Star Fleet as an AI system of agent
harnesses each running a GPT-5.6 instance; that is the AI system named here,
and the development names no informal author, so it is an independent proof
rather than a formalization of either forum claim. The Star Fleet Math site
distributes the development as the archive linked above and carries its report
(the `record` link), dated 2026-07-14, when the site's own automated referee
accepted it, the date this page carries; the Internet Archive's first capture
of starfleetmath.com, at 2026-07-15 00:25 UTC, already lists the result, and
the `lean-proofs` repository hosted a copy on 2026-07-23. The result is not on
the site's proof-claims tab.

**Covers.** The bound $c(n)=o(n^n)$ along the odd integers, hence a negative
answer to the question whether $c(n)\gg n^n$ holds uniformly in $n$. It does
not give the order of $c(n)$, says nothing about $c(n)$ for even $n$, in
particular nothing about the case $n+1$ prime in which Erdős expected
$c(n)>n^n$, and the problem's request for good bounds is not settled by it;
the catalog's own variant `erdos_769.variants.growth_rate`, asking whether
$\log c(n)/(n\log n)$ has a limit, stays tagged open. The same negative
answer, by written arguments with sharper thresholds, is claimed on
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0769/claims/2026_07_24_zeng|Zeng's page]]
(listed ten days after the date of the Star Fleet Math entry) and
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0769/claims/2026_08_05_korsky|Korsky's page]];
the three are independent, and none is known to cite another.
