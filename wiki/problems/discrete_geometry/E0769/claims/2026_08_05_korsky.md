***

**Claim.** Samuel Korsky, *Improved Bounds for Cubical Decompositions*,
manuscript dated 5 August 2026, posted the same day as a partial proof claim
on the site's proof-claims tab of
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0769/_index|Problem 769]]; the tab records the
result as obtained using GPT-5.6 Pro, and the manuscript's acknowledgments say
that the author contributed the initial discussion of quadratic nonresidues
in the upper bound and that almost all of the remaining work, including the
proofs and the writing, was carried out by GPT-5.6 Pro in an extended
interaction, the author reviewing the manuscript and accepting responsibility
for it. Let $P(n)$ be the largest prime $p$ with $p-1\mid n$ and let
$\theta=1/(4\sqrt e)$. Theorem 1.1 states that for every $\varepsilon>0$
there is $C_\varepsilon$ with

$$
c(n)\le\bigl(C_\varepsilon\max\{P(n),\,n^{\theta+\varepsilon}\}\bigr)^n
$$

for all sufficiently large $n$, so that $c(n)\le n^{(\theta+\varepsilon)n}$
for large odd $n$, where $P(n)=2$; and that under the generalized Riemann
hypothesis for Dirichlet $L$-functions there is an absolute $C_0$ with
$c(n)\le(C_0\max\{P(n),(\log n)^2\})^n$ for large $n$, so
$c(n)\le(C_0\log^2n)^n$ for large odd $n$. Theorem 1.2 states that there is
an absolute $C_1$ such that for all large $n$ and every odd prime $p$ with
$p-1\mid n$, a tiling of the unit $n$-cube by $N\not\equiv1\pmod p$ cubes has

$$
N\ge\Bigl(\Bigl(1-\frac1{\log_23}\Bigr)n-\log_2n-C_1\Bigr)2^n,
$$

and hence that this lower bound holds for $c(n)$ when $n$ is even (take
$p=3$). The upper bound reduces, through a numerical-semigroup lemma, to
finding a short range $2\le m\le M$ of grid sizes for which the increments
$m^n-1$ have no common prime factor; a common prime $q$ puts $1,\ldots,M$
into the subgroup of $n$-th roots of unity of $\mathbf F_q^\times$, a
nonresidue estimate of Pollack at the Burgess scale (or the conditional
estimate of Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan) supplies a prime outside a proper
such subgroup, and products of a fixed number of small primes then give more
than $n$ distinct elements of it. The lower bound doubles a tiling and deletes
the unit cubes: when every side length is a $p$-adic unit the tile count is
$1$ modulo $p$, so some tile has a side of nonzero $p$-adic valuation, and an
entropy estimate against a lower-dimensional reduction gives the constant
$1-1/\log_23$. The manuscript's notes on the tab record the consequence for
the threshold $h(n)$ of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0770/_index|Problem 770]]: for large $n$,
$P(n)\le h(n)\le\max\{P(n),n^{\theta+\varepsilon}\}$, and under GRH
$h(n)\le\max\{P(n),C(\log n)^2\}$.

**Covers.** The upper bound on $c(n)$ for all large $n$ in terms of $P(n)$
and the Burgess exponent, which gives $c(n)=o(n^n)$ along the odd integers
and so a negative answer to the uniform question $c(n)\gg n^n$; the lower
bound of order $n2^n$ for even $n$, above the bound $2^{n+1}-1$ of Connor and
Marmorino; and, conditionally on the generalized Riemann hypothesis, the
polylogarithmic form of the upper bound. The order of $c(n)$ is not
determined, and the case $n+1$ prime, in which $P(n)=n+1$ and the stated upper
bound has the shape $(Cn)^n$, is not improved.
