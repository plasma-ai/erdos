***

**Claim.** Theorem 7.2.5 of A. Blokhuis, *Few-distance sets*, CWI Tract 7,
Mathematisch Centrum, Amsterdam, 1984
([[../library/distance_problems/blokhuis_1984_few_distance_sets/_index|card]]),
states that an isosceles set $X$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$, one in which every three
points form an isosceles triangle, satisfies $|X|\le(d+1)(d+2)/2$, with
equality only for a two-distance set or a spherical two-distance set together
with its center. A set with no three points at pairwise distinct distances is
exactly an isosceles set, so in the notation of
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E1088/_index|Problem 1088]]

$$
f_d(3)\le\frac{(d+1)(d+2)}{2}+1.
$$

The two-distance sets of size $\binom{d+1}{2}$ give a matching lower bound of
the same order, so $f_d(3)=d^2/2+O(d)$, as the site's remarks record through
[[problems/distance_problems/E0503/_index|Problem 503]].

**Covers.** The case $n=3$ of the problem's second question: $f_d(3)$ grows
polynomially in $d$, so $f_d(3)=2^{o(d)}$. Erdős [Er75f, p. 104] wrote that
he and Straus could not prove this even for $n=3$. Nothing is claimed for
$n\ge4$.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Acceptance.** None. The CWI Tract, Blokhuis's thesis, is not a journal
publication, so `refereed` is not listed; and the site labels the problem
OPEN, so its remarks are not `reviewed` evidence.
