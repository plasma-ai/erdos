**Claim.** Let $P$ be a set of $n$ points in the plane, let $u_1,\ldots,u_t$
be the distances it determines, and let $f(u_i)$ be the number of unordered
pairs of points of $P$ at distance $u_i$. Proposition 2.2 of Guth and Katz
bounds the number of ordered quadruples $(p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4)$ of points of $P$
with $|p_1-p_2|=|p_3-p_4|>0$ by $O(n^3\log n)$. Grouping the quadruples by
the common distance, that number is $\sum_i (2f(u_i))^2$, so

$$
\sum_i f(u_i)^2 \ll n^3\log n \ll_\epsilon n^{3+\epsilon}
$$

for every $\epsilon>0$, which answers
[[problems/distance_problems/E0095/_index|Problem 95]] in the affirmative
with a stronger bound than the one asked. The quadruple count is the
intermediate result from which the paper's main theorem, that $n$ points
determine $\gg n/\log n$ distinct distances, follows by the Cauchy–Schwarz
inequality. The paper is carded at
[[../library/distance_problems/guth_2015_erdos_distinct_distance_problem_plane/_index|guth_2015_erdos_distinct_distance_problem_plane]],
whose annotation records the main theorem and the incidence bound behind it.

**Acceptance.** The paper is refereed: Larry Guth and Nets Hawk Katz, On the
Erdős distinct distance problem in the plane, Ann. of Math. (2) 181 (2015),
no. 1, 155–190; the preprint arXiv:1011.4105 was first posted on
2010-11-17. The site's curator, Thomas Bloom, marks the problem proved and
credits Guth and Katz with the bound $\sum_i f(u_i)^2 \ll n^3\log n$ on the
problem page; the site's export of 2026-09-04 records the label
"PROVED (LEAN)".

**Formalization.** Boris Alexeev's repository of formalized Erdős problems
holds a Lean development, added on 2026-08-17 and linked above at a pinned
commit through its landing note, whose Lean file's header names Guth and
Katz as informal authors and Codex and GPT-5.6 Sol as formal authors. Its
theorem `erdos_95` proves the $n^{3+\epsilon}$ bound the problem asks for
through the Elekes–Sharir reduction and an $\epsilon$-loss bound on rich
points obtained by polynomial partitioning, not from the $n^3\log n$ bound;
the file also carries a separate $n^3\log n$ route that this theorem does
not use. The statement `erdos_95` in formal-conjectures, at the catalog's
commit of 2026-09-20, is tagged research solved and points to that Lean
file. This corpus has neither built nor audited the development, so no
`formalized` evidence is listed.

**The convex case.** The site credits the case of a convex polygon to Altman
(1963); the paper
[[../library/distance_problems/altman_1963_problem_p_erdos/_index|carded in
the library]] proves that a convex $n$-gon determines at least $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$
distinct distances and prints no statement about $\sum_i f(u_i)^2$, so that
attribution has no claim page. The convex case is
[[problems/distance_problems/E0094/_index|Problem 94]], settled by
[[problems/distance_problems/E0094/claims/1995_09_01_lefmann_thiele|Lefmann
and Thiele]] with the bound $O(n^3)$.
