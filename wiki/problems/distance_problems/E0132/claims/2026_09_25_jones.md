**Claim.** Two results on the multiplicities of the extreme distances of a
finite planar set, from Wingate Jones's manuscript *Second-largest and minimum
distance multiplicities in planar point sets: an upper bound of 4/3, and
related results on Erdős Problem #132* (2026). Write $\Delta$, $\Delta_2$ and
$\delta$ for the largest, second-largest and smallest distances determined by
an $n$-point set $A\subset\mathbb R^2$, $\mu(d)$ for the number of unordered
pairs at distance $d$, $L_1$ and $L_2$ for the first two convex layers of $A$
and $m_3$ for the number of points of depth at least $3$.

1. *Layer condition.* Suppose every vertex of the convex hull of $A$ has at
   most three points of $A$ at distance $\Delta_2$ from it, which holds in
   particular when no four points of $A$ lie on a circle. Then
   $\mu(\Delta_2)\le\tfrac32|L_1|+|L_2|$, and the diameter and $\Delta_2$
   each have multiplicity at most $n$ as soon as $|L_1|\le2m_3$, so
   the first question of
   [[problems/distance_problems/E0132/_index|Problem 132]] has the answer yes
   for such sets. Theorem 1.3 of Clemen, Dumitrescu and Liu [CDL25] needs no
   hypothesis on the hull vertices and assumes
   $\min\{\tfrac32(|L_1|+|L_2|),\tfrac43|L_1|+2|L_2|,2|L_1|+|L_2|\}\le n$.
   Under the hypothesis above, the condition $|L_1|\le2m_3$, equivalently
   $\tfrac32|L_1|+|L_2|\le n$, follows from the first and third alternatives
   (the third is $|L_1|\le m_3$) but not from the second, so the result covers
   sets that theorem does not, as the manuscript's own remark says, without
   containing it.
2. *The $4/3$ bound.* For every $n$-point planar set,
   $\min\{\mu(\Delta_2),\mu(\delta)\}\le\tfrac43n+C_0$ for an absolute
   constant $C_0$. This bears on Problem 1.6 of [CDL25], which asks for the
   limit superior $L$ of $\max_{|A|=n}\min\{\mu(\Delta_2),\mu(\delta)\}/n$;
   the claimant gives the previous bounds as $9/7\le L\le3/2$, which the
   $4/3$ bound narrows to $9/7\le L\le4/3$: the lower bound is the
   construction posted in the problem's discussion thread on 25 July 2026 by
   ienjoymath
   ([[problems/distance_problems/E0132/claims/2026_07_25_ienjoymath|claim page]]),
   improving the published $9/8$ of Proposition 1.5 of [CDL25], and the upper
   one is Vesztergombi's inequality $\mu(\Delta_2)\le3n/2$
   ([[../library/distance_problems/vesztergombi_1987_large_distances_planar_sets/_index|vesztergombi_1987_large_distances_planar_sets]]).

The manuscript also reports, without formal verification, sharper bounds in
most parameter ranges, at least three distances of multiplicity at most $n$
for convex sets with $6\le n\le18$ (the cases $n=7$, $11$ and $13$ by a SAT
check inside Lean), and a linear number of such distances for almost
cocircular convex sets.

**Covers.** The first question for sets satisfying the layer condition of
result 1, that is $|L_1|\le2m_3$ with no hull vertex seeing four points at
distance $\Delta_2$; and the $4/3$ upper bound of result 2, which concerns a
related quantity and is not one of the problem's questions. The manuscript
also claims, without formal verification, a positive answer to the second
question for sets in convex position with all but $w=o(n)$ of their points on
one circle: such a set has at least $(n-3w+1)/4$ distances each occurring
between one and $n$ times when $n\ge3w$ and $n+w\ge91$. Neither question of
the problem is settled in general, as the claimant's own notes say.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki. The Hopf–Pannwitz bound [HoPa34] and
Vesztergombi's inequality are, by the claimant's account, proved inside the
Lean development rather than assumed.
