***

**Claim.** Among the lattice points of the tiling of the plane by equilateral
triangles there are, for every $n$ from $3$ to $8$, $n$ points, no three on a
line and no four on a circle, that determine $n-1$ distinct distances with the
$i$-th occurring $i$ times. This is the result of I. Palásti, *Lattice-point
examples for a question of Erdős*, Period. Math. Hungar. 20 (1989), no. 3,
231–235, as the zbMATH review (Zbl 0687.52004) states it: examples were known
for $n=3,\ldots,7$, and the paper finds examples for $n=3,\ldots,8$ on the
triangular lattice, so the eight-point example is new. Burt, Goldstein,
Manski, Miller, Palsson and Suh give its coordinates in Figure 1 of
*Crescent configurations*
([[../library/distance_problems/burt_2015_crescent_configurations/_index|card]]).
In the notation of [[problems/distance_problems/E0217/_index|Problem 217]],
the answer is yes for $n=8$.

**Covers.** The instance $n=8$, with lattice examples for every $n\le7$. The
property of the problem does not pass to subsets, so each example settles only
its own $n$; the instances $n=5$, $6$ and $7$ are also settled on
[[problems/distance_problems/E0217/claims/1983_01_01_pomerance|Pomerance's]],
[[problems/distance_problems/E0217/claims/1989_01_01_palasti|Palásti's
six-point]] and
[[problems/distance_problems/E0217/claims/1987_01_01_palasti|seven-point]]
claim pages. Not covered: every $n\ge9$, for which no example is known.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Periodica Mathematica Hungarica 20 (1989), no. 3,
231–235, September 1989; the Crossref record of the DOI gives these data. The
site's remarks credit the eight-point construction to this paper, but the site
labels the problem OPEN, so that credit is commentary on an open problem and
no `reviewed` evidence is listed.
