***

**Claim.** Let $M_2(n)$ be the largest size of a set in $\mathbb R^n$ whose
points determine exactly two distinct distances, the quantity whose exact
value the site's wording of
[[problems/distance_problems/E0502/_index|Problem 502]] asks for. Lisoněk
determines it for every $n\leq8$:

$$
M_2(1),\ldots,M_2(8)=3,\ 5,\ 6,\ 10,\ 16,\ 27,\ 29,\ 45.
$$

The paper is P. Lisoněk, *New maximal two-distance sets*, J. Combin. Theory
Ser. A 77 (1997), no. 2, 318–338, DOI 10.1006/jcta.1997.2749. Its new sets
are, in $\mathbb R^7$, a set of $29=\binom82+1$ points, one more than the
$\binom{n+1}{2}=28$ midpoints of the edges of a regular $7$-simplex, and, in
$\mathbb R^8$, a set of $45=\binom{10}{2}$ points containing it, which
attains the upper bound $\binom{n+2}{2}$ of Bannai, Bannai and Stanton. Ge,
Koolen and Munemasa's introduction
([[../library/distance_problems/ge_2026_two_distance_set_277_points_23_dimensions/_index|card]])
credits Lisoněk with determining the sizes of the largest two-distance sets
in dimensions $d\leq8$ and cites Lisoněk's Theorem 4.4 for the uniqueness,
up to isometry and scaling, of the $29$-point set in $\mathbb R^7$; the
sequence of maxima is recorded as A027627 in the OEIS with the paper as its
reference. The paper is not held in the library.

**Covers.** The exact value of $M_2(n)$ for $n\leq8$. The claim's value is
`answered` because the result determines, for these dimensions, what the
site's wording asks to determine. Not covered: the asymptotic behavior of
$M_2(n)$, which the corrected Statement asks for, and any $n\geq9$, where
the bounds $\binom{n+1}{2}\leq M_2(n)\leq\binom{n+2}{2}$ on the problem
page leave a gap, improved at one dimension by Ge, Koolen and Munemasa's
$277$-point set in $\mathbb R^{23}$.

**Standing.** Rejected: the result determines the exact maximum for
$n\leq8$, the site's wording's question; the corrected Statement asks for
the asymptotic behavior, which finitely many values do not reach. The
problem page records the exact maximum as a variant under its Formulation
and credits the values there.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Acceptance.** The `refereed` evidence is the journal publication cited
above, in the Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series A. The site's remarks
do not mention the result, so no `reviewed` evidence is listed. The record
dates the issue to February 1997 with no finer date, so the page carries the
first day of that month.
