**Claim.** Let $f(d)$ be the largest size of an isosceles set in
$\mathbb{R}^d$, a set in which every three points determine an isosceles
triangle. Y. J. Ionin, Isosceles sets, Electron. J. Combin. 16 (2009), no. 1,
#R141, determines in Section 5 and in the table of Section 1

$$
f(1),\dots,f(8)=3,\ 6,\ 8,\ 11,\ 17,\ 28,\ 30,\ 45,
$$

and describes every isosceles set of maximum size for $d\le7$: one set up to
similarity in each dimension except $d=4$, where there are two (a regular
pentagon and a congruent one in an orthogonal plane with their common
center, and the ten edge midpoints of a regular simplex with its center).
The abstract states the exact answer for $d\le7$ and that Blokhuis's bound
$\binom{d+2}{2}$ is attained for $d=1,2,6$ and $8$. Section 5 combines
Blokhuis's decomposition theorem for isosceles sets with more than two
distances (the paper's Theorem 2.15, from the Blokhuis thesis carded as
[[../library/distance_problems/blokhuis_1984_few_distance_sets/_index|Blokhuis 1984]]),
the paper's Lemma 5.1 on strongly regular two-distance sets, and Lisoněk's
maximum two-distance sets in dimensions up to eight; it cites Kelly for
$d=2$, gives its own short argument for $d=3$ to $7$, and for $d=8$ takes
Lisoněk's 45-point two-distance set, which meets Blokhuis's bound. The
paper's main subject is the binary Hamming space, where it bounds isosceles
subsets; those results are outside
[[problems/distance_problems/E0503/_index|Problem 503]].

**Covers.** The instances $d\le8$ of the problem, with the values above.
The values for $d=2$ and $d=3$ are Kelly's and Croft's, recorded on
[[problems/distance_problems/E0503/claims/1947_04_01_kelly|Kelly's claim page]]
and
[[problems/distance_problems/E0503/claims/1962_01_01_croft|Croft's claim page]];
the values for $d=4$ to $8$ are first determined here, the value $11$ for
$d=4$ independently on
[[problems/distance_problems/E0503/claims/2010_01_01_kido|Kido's claim page]].
Nothing is claimed about $d\ge9$.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/distance_problems/E0503/claims/1947_04_01_kelly|Kelly's claim page]],
cited for the plane. The two-distance values are Lisoněk's (J. Combin.
Theory Ser. A 77 (1997), 318–338) and the decomposition theorem is
Blokhuis's, both literature the paper cites.

**Acceptance.** The paper is refereed: The Electronic Journal of
Combinatorics 16 (2009), no. 1, Research Paper 141, published 24 November
2009, as its record gives it; the paper thanks the referee. The site labels
the problem OPEN and does not cite the paper, so no `reviewed` evidence is
listed.
