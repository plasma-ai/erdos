***

**Claim.** H. Kido, On isosceles sets in the 4-dimensional Euclidean space,
Int. J. Combin. 2010, Article ID 803210, proves that the maximum cardinality
of an isosceles set in $\mathbb{R}^4$, a set in which every three points
determine an isosceles triangle, is $11$, and that there are exactly two
11-point isosceles sets in $\mathbb{R}^4$ up to isomorphism, as the abstract
states. This answers the instance $d=4$ of
[[problems/distance_problems/E0503/_index|Problem 503]] with the value $11$,
which Ionin's paper of 2009 also derives, with the same two extremal sets,
on [[problems/distance_problems/E0503/claims/2009_11_24_ionin|Ionin's claim page]].
Kido's earlier paper, Classification of isosceles eight-point sets in
three-dimensional Euclidean space, European J. Combin. 27 (2006), 329–341,
proved the uniqueness of the eight-point set in $\mathbb{R}^3$ and settles
no instance by itself.

**Covers.** The instance $d=4$ of the problem, answered $11$, with the
classification of the extremal sets. Nothing is claimed about other
dimensions.

**Acceptance.** The paper is refereed: International Journal of
Combinatorics, volume 2010, Article ID 803210, as its record gives it. The
site labels the problem OPEN and does not cite the paper, so no `reviewed`
evidence is listed. The page is dated to the publication year, the record
giving no day.
