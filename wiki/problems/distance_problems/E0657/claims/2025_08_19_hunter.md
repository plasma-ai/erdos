***

**Claim.** Let $f(n)$ be, as on
[[problems/distance_problems/E0657/claims/2008_12_01_dumitrescu|Dumitrescu's
claim page]], the least number of distinct distances of an $n$-point
collinear set with no isosceles triple, divided by $n$; such sets are exactly
the sets of $n$ reals without a nontrivial three-term arithmetic progression,
and they are admissible sets for
[[problems/distance_problems/E0657/_index|Problem 657]]. The site's
commentary credits Zach Hunter with the observation, made in the problem's
discussion thread on 19 August 2025, that a result of Ruzsa combined with
standard tools of additive combinatorics turns the recent bounds on the size
of progression-free subsets of $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ into the lower bound
$$
f(n)\ge2^{c(\log n)^{1/9}}
$$
for an absolute constant $c>0$, a quasipolynomial improvement of
Dumitrescu's $(\log n)^c$. The details were written out in the thread by
Alfaiz on 1 October 2025, using the Kelley–Meka bound
([[../library/additive_combinatorics/kelley_2023_strong_bounds_3_progressions/_index|card]])
as sharpened by Bloom and Sisask
([[../library/additive_combinatorics/bloom_2023_improvement_kelley_meka_bounds_three_term/_index|card]]):
a progression-free set with few distinct differences would, by Ruzsa's
argument and the Plünnecke–Ruzsa inequalities, embed densely into a short
interval, where those bounds forbid it. Quanyu Tang's reply of the same day
noted, and Alfaiz agreed on 2 October 2025, that the deduction holds for
subsets of the line or of a torsion-free abelian group and not for planar
sets, since a projection does not preserve the absence of isosceles
triangles. A later thread comment by Alfaiz, of 6 August 2026, states that
Raghavan's 2026 progression-free bound raises the exponent $1/9$ to $1/6$;
that comment is disclosed here and gets no page of its own.

**Covers.** The problem's assertion for sets on a line, with the bound
$f(n)\ge2^{c(\log n)^{1/9}}$. Not covered: planar sets not on a line; the
problem's question remains open.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Standing.** Claimed. The result exists as a thread observation and the
site's commentary: no written source by its authors states it, and it has no
arXiv version, no journal record, no formalization and no independent review.
The commentary credits it to Hunter, with details by Alfaiz and Tang, but the
site labels the problem OPEN (page last edited 15 October 2025), so the credit
is not an acceptance and no `reviewed` evidence is listed. The claim is dated
by Hunter's comment of 19 August 2025.
