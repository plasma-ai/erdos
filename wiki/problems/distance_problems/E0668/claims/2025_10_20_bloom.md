***

**Claim.** The second question of
[[problems/distance_problems/E0668/_index|Problem 668]] is answered no. Four
points in the plane cannot have all six distances equal to $1$, so at most
five unit distances occur among four points. Five unit distances force two
unit equilateral triangles on a common edge, a rhombus with angles $60^\circ$
and $120^\circ$ that is unique up to congruence. So $n=4$ has exactly one
incongruent maximizer.

**Covers.** The second question only; the first, whether the count tends to
infinity, is untouched.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Claimant and date.** The remark is the site's unsigned commentary, whose
recommended citation names the curator, T. F. Bloom, as the page's author;
the page is filed under that name and dated by the earliest dated revision in
the site's history, of 20 October 2025, which already carries it. Anay
Aggarwal first posted the observation on the discussion thread on 13 August
2025, and the site's page thanks Aggarwal.

**Acceptance.** None documented: the site labels the problem OPEN.
