***

**Claim.** The answer to [[problems/distance_problems/E0756/_index|Problem 756]]
is yes. Krishnendu Bhowmick, *A note on a problem of Erdős about rich
distances*, Studia Sci. Math. Hungar. 62 (2025), no. 1, 89--94; posted as
arXiv:2407.01174, *A problem of Erdős about rich distances*, on 1 July 2024. For
every $n$ the paper constructs a set of $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$ in which
$\lfloor n/4\rfloor$ distinct distances each occur for at least $n+1$ pairs of
points, so $\gg n$ distances occur more than $n$ times, as the question asks
(Theorem 1.1). More generally, for every $m\geq1$ and every $n$ it gives $n$
points with $\lfloor n/(2(m+1))\rfloor$ distances each occurring at least $n+m$
times (Theorem 1.2, vacuous for $n<m+3$). The witnesses are built from regular
polygons. A regular $(m+1)$-gon has $\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ distances each
occurring $m+1$ times (Claim 2.1). For odd $n=2m+1$ the polygon is rotated about
one of its vertices, and the two copies share only that vertex, so they have
$2m+1=n$ points and each of the $\lfloor m/2\rfloor=\lfloor n/4\rfloor$
distances occurs $2(m+1)=n+1$ times; for even $n=2m$ the polygon is reflected in
one of its edges, the two copies share only that edge's two endpoints, so they
have $2m=n$ points and each of those distances occurs at least $2(m+1)-1=n+1$
times, the shared edge being counted once. Theorem 1.2 iterates the same two
moves on a regular $(k+2)$-gon, $r-2$ rotations about a fixed vertex and $m+2-r$
reflections in edges, where $n=(m+1)k+r$ with $2\leq r\leq m+2$. The source card
is
[[../library/distance_problems/bhowmick_2024_problem_erdos_about_rich_distances/_index|Bhowmick 2024]].
The question comes from Erdős and Pach, who asked it in the stronger form of
whether every distance other than the largest can occur more than $n$ times; by
Hopf and Pannwitz the largest distance occurs at most $n$ times, and that
stronger form is a separate problem.

**Acceptance.** The result is refereed: it appeared in Studia Scientiarum
Mathematicarum Hungarica. The site's curator, Thomas Bloom, marks the problem
proved and credits Bhowmick's construction; the site labels the problem PROVED
(LEAN) (export of 2026-09-04).

**Formalization.** The site's label carries a Lean marker. Wouter van Doorn
announced on the site's discussion thread on 15 March 2026 that Bhowmick's proof
had been formalized by Aristotle (Harmonic), and posted the file in van Doorn's
own repository, linked above at its commit of that day. Boris Alexeev's
repository of formalized Erdős problems holds the same development, linked above
at a pinned commit; its header declares it a Lean formalization of a solution to
Problem 756 with Bhowmick as informal author and Aristotle and van Doorn as
formal authors. Its theorem `Erdos756.erdos_756` states that for every $n$ there
is an $n$-point set of complex numbers and a set of $\lfloor n/4\rfloor$ of its
distances each realized by at least $n+1$ unordered pairs, and the file prints
its axioms as `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. The
[formal-conjectures statement
file](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/6fbb54f24ccc2e64dcfaffc28c58950e377110d2/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/756.lean)
for the problem (last changed 2026-09-18) records this proof, through a
`formal_proof` attribute pointing at the unpinned `src/v4.29.1` copy in
Alexeev's repository, against its variant `erdos_756.variants.bhowmick`, which
states the $\lfloor n/4\rfloor$ construction; its main statement and its general
variant carry no proof. This corpus has not built the file or audited its
statement, so it is a link here and not `formalized` evidence.
