**Claim.** Let $M(R)$ be the supremum of the measures of measurable sets
$A$ in the disc of radius $R$ about the origin such that $|a-b|$ is never a
positive integer for distinct $a,b\in A$, the quantity
[[problems/distance_problems/E0953/_index|Problem 953]] asks about. Theorem
1.1 of Przemek Chojecki's note *A Poisson–Bessel kernel bound for planar sets
avoiding integer distances* states that $M(R)\ll R^{1/2}$ for all $R\geq1$,
and consequently $M(R)=R^{1/2+o(1)}$ as $R\to\infty$; Theorem 1 of the
shorter note *The order of growth of planar sets avoiding integer distances*
states the same. The proof reduces the measurable problem to the uniform
estimate $N(X,\delta)\leq C\delta^{-2}X^{1/2}$ for $X\geq1$ and
$0<\delta<1/10$ (Theorem 1.2), where $N(X,\delta)$ counts points in a disc of
radius $X$ whose pairwise distances stay at least $\delta$ from the
integers, and proves that estimate with the positive-definite kernel
$K_s(t)=\sum_{k\geq1}(k+2sk^2)e^{-sk}J_0(2\pi kt)$, whose Poisson expansion
has non-positive terms away from $s$-neighborhoods of the integers. The
matching lower bound $M(R)\gg_\varepsilon R^{1/2-\varepsilon}$ comes from
Sárközy's theorem on robust point sets. Both notes read the question as
asking for the order of growth of $M(R)$ and take $M(R)=R^{1/2+o(1)}$ as its
answer; the problem page's Formulation also asks whether the factor
$R^{o(1)}$ can be removed. The notes are carded at
[[../library/distance_problems/chojecki_2026_poisson_bessel_kernel_bound_planar_sets/_index|chojecki_2026_poisson_bessel_kernel_bound_planar_sets]]
and
[[../library/distance_problems/chojecki_2026_order_growth_planar_sets_avoiding_integer/_index|chojecki_2026_order_growth_planar_sets_avoiding_integer]].

**Covers.** The exponent of growth of $M(R)$: jointly with the lower bound
$M(R)\gg_\varepsilon R^{1/2-\varepsilon}$ that the site credits, Sárközy's
theorem as Koizumi and Kovač adapted it on the site's thread, the theorem
settles the exponent $1/2$ in $M(R)=R^{1/2+o(1)}$. The claim's value is
`answered` because the result determines that exponent. Not covered: the
constant-factor question, whether $M(R)$ has order exactly $R^{1/2}$, that
is, whether the factor $R^{o(1)}$ between the two bounds can be removed; the
problem stays open on it.

**Claimant.** Chojecki posted the bound on the site's discussion thread on
27 April 2026, crediting GPT-5.5 Pro, with the long note; the short note,
which Chojecki attributed to GPT-5.5, followed on 28 April 2026. Nat
Sothanaphan posted a streamlined write-up of the same argument on 28 April
2026, prepared with GPT-5.5 Thinking (Theorem 2.1 of that write-up is the
same bound); it is linked above and carded at
[[../library/distance_problems/sothanaphan_2026_compact_poissonbessel_proof_integer_distance_free/_index|sothanaphan_2026_compact_poissonbessel_proof_integer_distance_free]].

**Depends on.**
[[problems/number_theory/E0466/claims/1976_01_01_sarkozy|Sárközy's power lower bound]],
which supplies the lower half of $M(R)=R^{1/2+o(1)}$.

**Acceptance.** Reviewed: on 8 May 2026 Vjekoslav Kovač, who is independent
of the claimant, wrote on the site's thread that the proof Chojecki obtained
is correct and that Sothanaphan's simplification presents it well. Not
refereed. The site's curator has not credited the result: the site labels
the problem OPEN. Allen Hart's Lean development, linked above at a pinned
commit, declares Chojecki's long note its primary informal source and
proves the upper bound alone (`erdos953_upper`, stated for closed discs);
this corpus has not built or audited it, so `formalized` is not listed.
