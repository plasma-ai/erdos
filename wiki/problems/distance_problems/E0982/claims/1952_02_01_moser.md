***

**Claim.** Every set of $n$ points in convex position in the plane has a
point from which there are at least $\lceil n/3\rceil$ distinct distances to
the other points. This is the theorem as Dumitrescu restates it, "Theorem 1
(Moser)", citing L. Moser, On different distances determined by $n$ points,
Amer. Math. Monthly 59 (1952), 85--91
([[problems/distance_problems/E0982/claims/2006_09_29_dumitrescu|Dumitrescu 2006]],
p. 503). Erdős and Fishburn give the same bound as
$f(n)\ge\lfloor(n+2)/3\rfloor$ and note that Moser applied his bound to the
total number of distances among the vertices, while others, Altman among
them, observed that his proof gives the bound at a single vertex
([[problems/distance_problems/E0982/claims/1994_01_01_erdos_fishburn|Erdős and Fishburn 1994]],
pp. 111--112). The argument takes the smallest disk enclosing the polygon and
finds a vertex on its boundary whose distances to the vertices of one cap
cut off by a chord are all distinct.

**Covers.** The statement of
[[problems/distance_problems/E0982/_index|Problem 982]] for $n=3,4,5,7$,
where $\lceil n/3\rceil=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. For every other $n\ge6$ the
bound is smaller than $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the result rests on the cited paper.
The paper is not filed in the library, and the statement above is taken
from the two restatements cited.

**Dating.** The paper appeared in volume 59, number 2, the February 1952
issue (Crossref record); the day in the page name is a placeholder.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: The American Mathematical Monthly 59 (1952),
no. 2, 85--91. The site's commentary lists the bound; its label,
FALSIFIABLE, settles nothing, so the curator's listing is not counted as
review.
