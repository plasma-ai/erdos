***

**Claim.** The answer to [[problems/divisors/E0164/_index|Problem 164]] is
yes. For a primitive set $A$ (no member divides another, and $A\neq\{1\}$)
write $f(A)=\sum_{a\in A}1/(a\log a)$, which converges by Erdős's 1935
theorem
([[../library/divisors/erdos_1935_note_sequences_integers_no_one_which/_index|Erdős 1935]]).
Lichtman proves that $f(A)\leq f(\mathbb{P})=1.6366\ldots$ for every primitive
$A$, where $\mathbb{P}$ is the set of primes (Theorem 1.2 of the preprint; the
repository's reading is on the card
[[../library/divisors/lichtman_2022_proof_erdos_primitive_set_conjecture/_index|Lichtman 2022]]).
The earlier bounds were $f(A)<1.84$ by Erdős and Zhang and
$f(A)<e^\gamma=1.781\ldots$ by Lichtman and Pomerance. The argument refines
the Lichtman--Pomerance method through Mertens' product theorem; its new input
is that a primitive set cannot contain many elements whose largest prime
factor is only slightly below the element, which gains a factor $\pi/4$ on
each composite element, and $e^\gamma\pi/4<f(\mathbb{P})$. The same paper
proves that every odd prime $p$ is Erdős strong, meaning $f(A)\leq f(\{p\})$
whenever every element of the primitive set $A$ has least prime factor $p$,
and leaves $p=2$ open; that case was later settled by the authors of the
second proof on the claim page
[[problems/divisors/E0164/claims/2026_05_01_alexeev_barreto_li_lichtman_price_shah_tang_tao|Alexeev and coauthors]].
