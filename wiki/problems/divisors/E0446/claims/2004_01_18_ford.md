***

**Claim.** Write $H(x,y,z)$ for the number of $n\le x$ with a divisor in
$(y,z]$, $H_r(x,y,z)$ for those with exactly $r$ such divisors, and
$\varepsilon(y,z)$, $\varepsilon_r(y,z)$ for the limits of $H/x$ and $H_r/x$.
Ford's Corollary 2, at $c=2$, gives the first answer asked by
[[problems/divisors/E0446/_index|Problem 446]]:

$$
\delta(n)=\varepsilon(n,2n)\asymp\frac{1}{(\log n)^{\alpha}(\log\log n)^{3/2}},
\qquad
\alpha=1-\frac{1+\log\log 2}{\log 2}=0.08607\ldots,
$$

the order of magnitude of the density of integers with a divisor in $(n,2n)$,
with matching upper and lower bounds; this sharpens Erdős's 1960 estimate
$\delta(n)=(\log n)^{-\alpha+o(1)}$ and Tenenbaum's 1984 bounds, which match
only up to slowly varying factors. For the second question, Theorem 4 gives
$H_1(x,y,z)/H(x,y,z)\asymp\log\log(z/y+10)/\log(z/y+10)$ in a wide range, and
Corollary 7 states the consequence for the densities: for every $r\ge1$ and
$\lambda>1$,

$$
\frac{\varepsilon_r(y,\lambda y)}{\varepsilon(y,\lambda y)}\gg_{r,\lambda}1 .
$$

With $r=1$ and $\lambda=2$ this is $\delta_1(n)\gg\delta(n)$, so
$\delta_1(n)=o(\delta(n))$ is false; Ford states this as the refutation of
his Conjecture 1, which he attributes to Erdős. The intervals are half-open,
$(y,2y]$, where the problem writes $(n,2n)$; the integers divisible by $2n$
itself have density $1/(2n)$, far below $\delta(n)$, so the orders are the
same. The paper is filed as
[[../library/divisors/ford_2008_distribution_integers_divisor_given_interval/_index|Ford 2008]];
the method combines sieve-style reductions of $H$ and $H_r$ to averages of
divisor-distribution functions with new bounds for uniform order statistics.

Both parts of the problem are settled by this one result: the growth rate is
determined, and the second question is answered no. Ford proves more than
the problem asks, $\varepsilon_r(y,z)/\varepsilon(y,z)\to0$ whenever
$z/y\to\infty$ and the order of $H_r$ for $r\ge2$ in most ranges.
