***

**Claim.** The answer to [[problems/divisors/E0449/_index|Problem 449]] is
no. For every constant $K>0$ the integers $n$ with $r(n)>K\tau(n)$ contain a
set of positive density, so already for $\epsilon=1$ the inequality
$r(n)<\epsilon\tau(n)$ fails on a set of positive density and cannot hold for
almost all $n$. The argument, which the site credits to Kevin Ford, compares
$r(n)$ with the dyadic divisor count $\tau^+(n)$ of
[[problems/divisors/E0448/_index|Problem 448]], the number of $k$ with a
divisor of $n$ in $[2^k,2^{k+1})$. Write $c_k$ for the number of divisors of
$n$ in that block, so that $\sum_k c_k=\tau(n)$ with $\tau^+(n)$ nonzero
terms. Two distinct divisors in one block satisfy $d_1<d_2<2d_1$, so

$$
\sum_k c_k^2=\tau(n)+2\,\#\{d_1<d_2 \text{ in one block}\}\le\tau(n)+2r(n),
$$

and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality $\tau(n)^2\le\tau^+(n)\sum_k c_k^2$ gives

$$
\frac{\tau(n)^2}{\tau^+(n)}\le\tau(n)+2r(n).
$$

The site displays this bound without the factor $2$, a form that fails at
$n=6$; the missing factor was pointed out in the problem's discussion thread
on 2026-08-28 and is corrected in the Lean development linked above, and it
does not affect the conclusion. The other input is the positive-density
statement the site draws from the negative solution of Problem 448: for every
$\alpha>0$ the integers with $\tau^+(n)\le\alpha\tau(n)$ contain a set of
positive density. It is elementary: on the progression $n\equiv M\pmod{M^2}$
with $M=6^a$, the divisor count is $(a+1)^2$ times that of the cofactor while
the dyadic count is at most $3a+2$ times it, so $\tau^+(n)\le\alpha\tau(n)$
on the whole progression once $a$ is large. The statement sits inside the
study of the ratio $\tau^+(n)/\tau(n)$ by Erdős and Tenenbaum (1981)
([[../library/divisors/erdos_1981_sur_la_structure_de_la_suite/_index|card]])
and by Hall and Tenenbaum (1988), who prove that the ratio has a distribution
function. On that set with $\alpha=1/(2K+2)$ the left side is at least
$(2K+2)\tau(n)$, so $r(n)\ge(K+\tfrac12)\tau(n)>K\tau(n)$. The Lean
development linked above works on the same progression with $M=6^a$ but in
the other order: it proves $r(M)>K\tau(M)$ for $M$ itself from the
Cauchy-Schwarz bound, using $\tau(6^a)=(a+1)^2$ and $\tau^+(6^a)\le3a+1$,
and transfers the inequality to $n=Mq$ through $r(Mq)\ge r(M)\tau(q)$ for
$q$ coprime to $M$, without bounding $\tau^+(n)$. The site adds that
Hall and Tenenbaum, *Divisors* (Cambridge Tracts in Mathematics 90, 1988),
Section 4.6, give the argument for an essentially identical problem; the book
is not held here.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Claimant and date.** The site's page credits the observation to Kevin Ford
and thanks Ford for it, without a date. The remark is already present in an
archived copy of the page from 2024-07-13, linked above, which is the earliest
dated record of it known here and names this page.

**Acceptance.** Thomas Bloom, the site's curator, marks the problem disproved
and credits Ford's observation on the problem page. No separate publication
of the deduction is known here; the related result in Hall and Tenenbaum's book is
cited above as the site cites it. Boris Alexeev's repository of formalized
Erdős problems holds a Lean development, added on 2026-08-17, whose index
page of 2026-08-22 is linked above at a pinned commit; its header names Ford
as informal author and Codex and GPT-5.6 Sol as formal authors; it proves that for every $K>0$ the set of $n$
with $K\tau(n)<r(n)$ contains a set of positive density and hence the
negation of the problem's statement. This corpus has not built or audited it,
so no `formalized` evidence is listed. The claim is accepted on the curator's
documented acceptance.
