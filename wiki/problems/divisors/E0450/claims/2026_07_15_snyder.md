**Claim.** Read [[problems/divisors/E0450/_index|Problem 450]] with the
bound required for every translate $x$: call a length $y$ sufficient for
$\epsilon$ and $n$ when every interval $(x,x+y)$ of integers, for every
$x\ge0$, contains at most $\epsilon y$ integers with a divisor in $(n,2n)$,
and let $y(\epsilon,n)$ be the least $y_0$ such that every $y\ge y_0$ is
sufficient. The claim is that for every fixed $0<\epsilon<1$ the order of
$y(\epsilon,n)$ is linear in $n$: $y(\epsilon,n)=\Theta_\epsilon(n)$. For
$\epsilon\ge1$ the question is trivial, since an open window of length $y$
holds at most $y-1<\epsilon y$ integers, so every length is sufficient. The
upper bound is explicit. Choose a finite set $S_\epsilon$ of primes, all at
least $5$, with
$\sum_{p\in S_\epsilon}1/p>152/\epsilon$, and put
$Q_\epsilon=\prod_{p\in S_\epsilon}p^2$; then $y=n(Q_\epsilon+2)$ is
sufficient for all large $n$. For the lower bound, for every fixed
$0<\epsilon<1$ and all large $n$ the length $y=n$ is not sufficient, so any
sufficient threshold exceeds $n$ eventually. The write-up is the solution
page on Star Fleet Math linked above.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Colin
Snyder (account coffeewithcolin) on 15 July 2026, giving "GPT 5.6 (custom
harness)" as the AI used:

> We claim the optimal order is linear: an explicit $y=n(Q_\epsilon+2)$ works
> for every translate $x$, and no $o(n)$ length works for any fixed
> $0<\epsilon<1$, so $y(\epsilon,n)=\Theta_\epsilon(n)$. Proved in Lean 4 /
> Mathlib (theorem turanLinearAnswer_isSufficientScale plus a matching
> lower-bound theorem), standard axioms only, no sorry. Idea: the bound must
> hold for every $x$, which defeats global density arguments (just past a
> factorial translate, a length-$n$ interval has $n-1$ integers with such a
> divisor; that is also the lower obstruction). Fix a finite set $S$ of primes
> and let $U(m)=|{p\in S: p\mid m}|$. The inequality $U(d)+U(k)\le W(dk)$, where
> $W$ also counts square factors, forces every bad $m=dk$ into one of three rare
> classes, each periodic mod $\prod_{p\in S}p^2$ and controlled on every
> interval by Chebyshev and Markov bounds. Notes: Verify: run the included
> checker (rejects sorry/admit/local axioms/unsafe, ~8,600 build jobs); "#print
> axioms" on both final theorems gives exactly [propext, Classical.choice,
> Quot.sound]. This bundle was also rebuilt from the exact public zip on
> separate hardware with the same result.

**Argument.** Immediately after a multiple of the least common multiple of
$n+1,\ldots,2n-1$, each of the next $n-1$ integers past the first $n$ has a
divisor in $(n,2n)$, so a window of length $n$ fails for every
$\epsilon<1$; this also shows that no length that is $o(n)$ can work.
Quantitatively, a sufficient window of length $y$ placed over those $n-1$
integers must satisfy $n-1\le\epsilon y$, so $y(\epsilon,n)\ge(n-1)/\epsilon$
and the constant in the linear order is at least about $1/\epsilon$. For
the upper bound, the write-up counts, for $m$ in the window with a divisor
$d\in(n,2n)$ and $m=dk$, the primes of $S_\epsilon$ dividing $d$, $k$ and
$m$: writing $U(m)$ for the number of primes of $S_\epsilon$ dividing $m$
and $W(m)$ for $U(m)$ plus the number whose square divides $m$, the
inequality $U(d)+U(k)\le W(dk)$ puts every such $m$ into one of three
classes, a low count for $d$, a low count for $k$, or a high two-level count
for $m$. Each class is periodic modulo $Q_\epsilon$, and second-moment
(Chebyshev) and first-moment (Markov) estimates over a period bound it on
every window of length at least $n(Q_\epsilon+2)$, with one period of
boundary loss; the three bounds add up to at most $152y/\mu$ with
$\mu=\sum_{p\in S_\epsilon}1/p$, which is below $\epsilon y$ by the choice of
$S_\epsilon$. The write-up says that global density estimates, such as
Ford's theorem on integers with a divisor in a given interval, do not
control exceptional translates, which is why a local, periodic statistic is
used instead.

**Reading of the question.** The site's remarks say that the quantifier on $x$
is not clear. The claim fixes the reading in which the bound holds for every $x$
and every length at least $y$, and treats $\epsilon$ as fixed while
$n\to\infty$; the formal-conjectures statement file of the problem
([`450.lean`](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/6fbb54f24ccc2e64dcfaffc28c58950e377110d2/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/450.lean),
at its commit of 2026-09-18) adopts the same reading of $x$ and of the window
lengths, defines the threshold accordingly, leaves its exact value open, and
records the upper bound as a solved auxiliary statement with this claim's Lean
file as its formal proof. The pull request of 2026-08-07 that added that record
([formal-conjectures
#4588](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/4588)) kept
the headline question open on the ground that the problem asks for the best
possible bound and the proof gives the order rather than the optimal constant,
an outside judgment that the result is partial; the fix of 2026-09-12
([#5786](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/5786))
restated the headline as the threshold $y(\epsilon,n)$ itself. This page
records the claim with scope `partial`: it determines the order of
$y(\epsilon,n)$ in $n$ under one reading of the quantifier on $x$, which
neither the source nor the site's curator fixes, and it leaves the exact
threshold open. The dependence of the constant on $\epsilon$ is left open by
the write-up itself: the lcm construction above puts it at least about
$1/\epsilon$, and the explicit length gives at most $Q_\epsilon+2$. The regime
in which $\epsilon$ shrinks with $n$ is outside the claim.

**Covers.** For fixed $0<\epsilon<1$ as $n\to\infty$, under the reading that
the bound holds for every translate $x$ and every length at least $y$, the
order $y(\epsilon,n)=\Theta_\epsilon(n)$. Not covered: the reading over
typical $x$, the exact threshold and its dependence on $\epsilon$, and
$\epsilon$ shrinking with $n$.

**Formal verification by the author.** The downloadable bundle linked
above holds a Lean 4 project against a pinned Mathlib with the theorems
`turanLinearAnswer_isSufficientScale` (the upper bound, for the explicit
length $n\cdot(\prod_{p\in S}p^2+2)$) and
`sufficientScale_eventually_gt_n` (every sufficient scale exceeds $n$
eventually for $0<\epsilon<1$). The solution page reports a build of about
8,600 jobs with a checker that rejects `sorry`, `admit`, local axioms and
unsafe declarations, and an axiom report of `propext`, `Classical.choice`
and `Quot.sound` for both theorems; the forum entry adds that the bundle was
rebuilt from the public archive on separate hardware with the same result.
A copy of the project's main file is the fourth link, at the commit of
2026-07-30 that the formal-conjectures file pins; that repository's index
lists it under Colin Snyder's name as faithful to the statement file's
target, with the repository's continuous-integration build and axiom audit
as its verification, and the formal-conjectures pull request of 2026-08-07
reports that the linked theorem was read against the statement file's
definitions and matches them. Neither check examines the mathematics, and
none of this has been built or audited here, so no `formalized` evidence is
listed.
