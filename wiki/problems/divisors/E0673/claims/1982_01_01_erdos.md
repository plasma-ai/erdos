***

**Claim.** The answer to the first question of
[[problems/divisors/E0673/_index|Problem 673]] is yes: $G(n)\to\infty$ for
almost all $n$, that is, for every $C$ the integers with $G(n)>C$ have density
$1$. Erdős posed the question at the 1978 Luminy Journées Arithmétiques
([Er79e], Astérisque 61, 1979, pp. 73--74, where the sum is written $g(n)$;
carded at
[[../library/divisors/erdos_1979_unconventional_problems_number_theory_asterisque/_index|erdos_1979_unconventional_problems_number_theory_asterisque]])
and recalled it in the 1982 survey carded at
[[../library/discrete_geometry/erdos_1982_my_favourite_problems_which_recently_have/_index|erdos_1982_my_favourite_problems_which_recently_have]],
Chapter II, section 6, printed p. 66, where he writes the sum as $Q(n)$ and
says: "It is trivial that $Q(n)\to\infty$ for almost all $n$." The same
passage withdraws his earlier belief that the divergence would imply his
conjecture on consecutive divisors $d_i<d_{i+1}<2d_i$, says he hopes to prove
that $Q(n)/\tau(n)$ has a distribution function, which "should follow from our
work with Tenenbaum", and judges the stronger statement
$Q(n)>\tau(n)/2$ for almost all $n$ almost certainly false; a note on printed
p. 67, added after the paper was completed, says that he and Tenenbaum proved
in July 1981, at the number theory meeting in Budapest, that $Q(n)/\tau(n)$
has a continuous distribution function. The survey gives no proof of the
divergence. The site records the argument, observed by Terence Tao: if $m>1$
divides $n$, each divisor $d$ of $n/m$ is followed in the ordered list of
divisors of $n$ by a divisor at most $dm$, so the term $d_i/d_{i+1}$ at $d$ is
at least $1/m$ and

$$
\frac{\tau(n/m)}{m}\le G(n)\le\tau(n);
$$

for even $n$ this gives $\tau(n)/4\le G(n)\le\tau(n)$. The divergence
follows: given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $B$ so that the integers with no prime
factor $p\le B$, whose density is $\prod_{p\le B}(1-1/p)$, have density at
most $\varepsilon$; an $n$ with a prime factor $p\le B$ has
$G(n)\ge\tau(n/p)/p\ge\tau(n)/(2B)$, since $\tau(n/p)\ge\tau(n)/2$ when
$p\mid n$, and $\tau(n)\to\infty$ for almost all $n$, so for every $C$ the
integers with $G(n)\le C$ have upper density at most $\varepsilon$, for every
$\varepsilon$. The lower bound needs $m>1$, since at $m=1$ it would read
$\tau(n)\le G(n)$, which is false; the site's display allows any divisor $m$.
The site records Tao's suggestion that the conjecture was a slip that Erdős
corrected a year later into [[problems/divisors/E0448/_index|Problem 448]].

**Covers.** The first question only: $G(n)\to\infty$ on a set of density $1$.
It gives no asymptotic formula for $\sum_{n\le X}G(n)$, the second question,
which Erdős and Tenenbaum answered in 1983
([[problems/divisors/E0673/claims/1983_01_01_erdos_tenenbaum|their claim page]]);
the growth of the average, which Erdős's original list called easy, follows
from Tao's lower bound or from their formula.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Acceptance.** Thomas Bloom, the site's curator, marks the problem proved,
records Tao's bounds and cites the 1982 remark on the problem page. Erdős's
survey asserts the divergence without argument and Tao's observation is a site
remark; a refereed argument is Erdős and Tenenbaum's bound
$G(n)\ge\tau(n)/(2P^-(n))$, $P^-(n)$ the least prime factor of $n$, on p. 127
of their 1983 paper
([[problems/divisors/E0673/claims/1983_01_01_erdos_tenenbaum|their claim page]]).
The Lean development in Boris Alexeev's repository whose theorem
`G_tendsToInfinityAlmostAll` proves the divergence, through `tao_lower_bound`
with the hypothesis $m>1$, names Erdős and Tenenbaum as informal authors and is
linked from
[[problems/divisors/E0673/claims/1983_01_01_erdos_tenenbaum|their claim page]],
which records this corpus's build of it as formalized evidence for their
result. The claim is accepted on the curator's documented acceptance of an
elementary argument stated in full on the site.
