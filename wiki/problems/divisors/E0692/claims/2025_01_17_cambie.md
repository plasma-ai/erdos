**Claim.** For the natural density $\delta_1(n,m)$ of the integers with
exactly one divisor in the open interval $(n,m)$, the sequence
$(\delta_1(n,m))_{m\ge n+2}$ need not be unimodular. Cambie computes the dip
$\delta_1(3,6)=7/20>\delta_1(3,7)=1/3<\delta_1(3,8)=38/105$, reports a
computer check that unimodularity fails for every $2\le n\le20$ (the
notebooks are in Cambie's repository, linked above at its revision of 27 May
2025), and proves (Theorem 3) that for some $c>0$ the sequence has
$\omega(\exp(n^c))$ local maxima for all sufficiently large $n$. Theorem 1 of
the same paper proves the positive case $n=1$: $\delta_1(1,m)$ is
non-increasing in $m$. The problem's precise Statement is therefore answered
in the negative. On the variant recorded under the problem page's
Formulation, where $\delta_1(n,m)$ attains its maximum for fixed $n$,
Theorem 1 answers only $n=1$: it puts the maximum $1/2$ at $m=3$, tied with
$m=4$, since $\delta_1(1,3)=\delta_1(1,4)=1/2$ and $\delta_1(1,5)=1/3$.
Every $n\ge2$ is left open.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the paper appeared in the *Journal of Number
Theory*. Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas Bloom, marks Problem 692
disproved and credits Cambie's computation and many-local-maxima theorem
for it. This corpus has not independently reviewed the paper, and
its own reading awards no standing.

**Formalization.** Pietro Monticone posted on the site's thread on 2 April
2026 a Lean 4 file autoformalized by Aristotle (Harmonic), authored as
Monticone and Aristotle, which proves `delta1 3 7 < delta1 3 6` and
`delta1 3 7 < delta1 3 8` for its own rational residue-proportion
definition of $\delta_1$. It is a formalization of Cambie's finite example
and is linked above at the pinned revision. This corpus has not built it; it
does not bridge its definition to natural density or state a general negation
of unimodularity, and it is no `formalized` evidence of this corpus. Boris
Alexeev's lean-proofs repository re-hosts the file, added on 6 May 2026 and
linked above at a pinned revision, naming Cambie as informal author and
Aristotle and Monticone as formal authors; this corpus has not built it
