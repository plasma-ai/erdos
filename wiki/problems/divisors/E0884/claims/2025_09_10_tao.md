***

**Claim.** Let $1=d_1<\cdots<d_{\tau(n)}=n$ be the divisors of $n$. Terence
Tao's note "On the sum of reciprocals of gaps between divisors" (dated 9
September 2025) proves that, assuming the qualitative Hardy--Littlewood prime
tuples conjecture (every admissible tuple $h_1,\ldots,h_k$ has infinitely many
$n$ with $n+h_1,\ldots,n+h_k$ all prime), the ratio

$$
\frac{\sum_{1\le i<j\le\tau(n)}\frac{1}{d_j-d_i}}{1+\sum_{1\le i<\tau(n)}\frac{1}{d_{i+1}-d_i}}
$$

is unbounded as $n$ ranges over the natural numbers, so the inequality the
problem asks about holds with no absolute constant. The construction takes $n$
to be the product of the primes $t-h_1,\ldots,t-h_K$ for an admissible tuple
of diameter of order $K^2$ with consecutive elements at least of order $K$
apart, and $t$ chosen so that all $K$ shifts are prime, which is where the
hypothesis enters. The pairwise sum over these $K$ prime divisors is then of
order $\log K$ times the consecutive sum (the one-dimensional Coulomb-energy
lower bound against roughly equal spacing), and a count of near-collisions
among products of the primes keeps the other divisors from spoiling the
ratio. The note remarks that the statement's upper summation limit should be
$j\le\tau(n)$ rather than $j<\tau(n)$ as printed in [Er98], which does not
affect the question, and that the numerology nearly allows the hypothesis to
be removed.

**Hypothesis.** The qualitative prime tuples conjecture above is unproved; the
claim is therefore conditional and derives no standing by itself.

**Standing.** The site's curator, Thomas Bloom, records in the problem's remarks
that Tao disproved the statement assuming the prime tuples conjecture; the
site's label rests on Larsen's unconditional disproof, so the remark
acknowledges this result without settling the problem through it. The note is
not refereed and no Lean proof of it is recorded, so the claim is `claimed`. The
construction was made unconditional by
[[problems/divisors/E0884/claims/2026_03_27_larsen|Larsen's disproof]], which
repeats it over many scales.
