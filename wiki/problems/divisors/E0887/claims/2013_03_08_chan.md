***

**Claim.** T. H. Chan, *Factors of a perfect square*, Acta Arith. 163 (2014),
no. 2, 141--143 (posted to arXiv as *Perfect squares have at most five divisors
close to its square root*;
[[../library/divisors/chan_2014_factors_perfect_square/_index|card]]), Theorem
1.3: there is an absolute constant $C_0$ such that, for every $c\ge3$, every
perfect square $n>e^{C_0c^6(\log c)^5}$ has at most five divisors in
$[\sqrt n-cn^{1/4},\sqrt n+cn^{1/4}]$. Given $C>0$, take $c=\max(C,3)$: every
large square has at most five divisors in $(n^{1/2},n^{1/2}+Cn^{1/4})$. So the
answer to [[problems/divisors/E0887/_index|Problem 887]] is yes for squares,
with $K=5$, as the paper says of the Erdős-Rosenfeld question. The squares
$(X_k-2)^2(X_k+2)^2$ built from $X^2-2Y^2=2$ have five divisors within
$5n^{1/4}$ of $\sqrt n$, so five is optimal for the two-sided window.

**Covers.** The question with $n$ restricted to perfect squares.
