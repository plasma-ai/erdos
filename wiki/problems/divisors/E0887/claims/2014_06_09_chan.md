***

**Claim.** T. H. Chan, *Factors of almost squares and lattice points on
circles*, Int. J. Number Theory 11 (2015), no. 5, 1701--1708
([[../library/divisors/chan_2015_factors_almost_squares_lattice_points_circles/_index|card]]),
Theorem 2 (arXiv numbering): every sufficiently large $n=(N-a)(N+b)$ with
integers $0\le a\le b\le e^{(\log n)^{2/7}}$ has at most eighteen divisors in
$[\sqrt n-n^{1/4}(\log n)^{1/14},\sqrt n+n^{1/4}(\log n)^{1/14}]$. For each
$C>0$, $Cn^{1/4}<n^{1/4}(\log n)^{1/14}$ once $n$ is large, so the answer to
[[problems/divisors/E0887/_index|Problem 887]] is yes for these $n$, with
$K=18$. The site prints the factorization as $(N-a)(N-b)$; the paper's is
$(N-a)(N+b)$.

**Covers.** The question with $n$ restricted to these numbers, a class that
contains $N^2-1$, $N^2-4$ and the perfect squares.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: International Journal of Number Theory. The site
labels the problem OPEN, so its commentary is not acceptance.
