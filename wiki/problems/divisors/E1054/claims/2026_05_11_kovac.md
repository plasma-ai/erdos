**Claim.** Vjekoslav Kovač, *An improved bound in Erdős problem #1054*, a
note dated 17 May 2026 in its current version, proves (Theorem 1) that there
is a $c>0$ such that for all sufficiently small $\delta>0$ and all $X\ge1$

$$
\#\{N\le X: f(N)\le\delta N\}\ll\exp\bigl(-e^{(1/\delta)^c}\bigr)\,X .
$$

In particular the proportion of $N\le X$ with $f(N)\le\delta N$ is
$O_M(\delta^M)$ for every $M>0$, and the upper density of
$\{N: f(N)\le\delta N\}$ is $O(\exp(-e^{(1/\delta)^c}))$. The first version,
posted on 11 May 2026, proved the $O_M(\delta^M)$ bound; the revision of
17 May 2026 gives the doubly exponential bound, after a suggestion of the
site's curator in the thread. The note builds on the argument Terence Tao
posted in the site's comments, which gives upper density $\ll\delta^2$; its
abstract also acknowledges Liam Price's AI-generated proof. The posting
states that no AI was used.

Since the set where $f(N)\le\delta N$ has upper density below $1$ for small
$\delta$, $f(N)=o(N)$ fails, and it fails along every set of density one.

**Submission note.** Posted to the site's forum by Vjekoslav Kovač on 11 May
2026:

> Since there have been many comments scattered around (thanks Thomas for moving
> them all here), I wrote up a short self-contained proof that the fraction of
> the numbers $n\in[1,x]$ satisfying $f(n)\leq \delta n$ is $\ll_M \delta^M$ for
> every $M>0$ (uniformly in $x>0$). [EDIT: After Thomas's comment, this is now
> improved to quite rapid decay $\ll \exp(-e^{(1/\delta)^c})$ for some small
> $c>0$.] It is based on Terry's proof, so all credit goes to him. This is just
> a minor modification and even a simplification - no decay in $k$ estimate is
> shown first. (No AI was used. Note that Terry's comment is from Nov 2025.)

Posted to the site's forum by Vjekoslav Kovač on 17 May 2026:

> I updated the short note to give the quantitatively stronger result (motivated
> by Thomas's comment and promised above): The fraction of the numbers
> $n\in[1,x]$ satisfying $f(n)\leq \delta n$ is $\ll \exp(-e^{(1/\delta)^c})$
> for some small constant $c>0$ and for all sufficiently small $\delta>0$,
> uniformly in $x>0$.
>
> I also now rather prefer to talk about the uniform fraction bound\[
> \sup_{x>0}\frac{\#\{n\in[1,x] \,:\, f(n)\leq \delta n\}}{x} \ll
> \exp(-e^{(1/\delta)^c}) \]rather than the asymptotic upper density\[
> \limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{\#\{n\in[1,x] \,:\, f(n)\leq \delta n\}}{x} \ll
> \exp(-e^{(1/\delta)^c}). \]Before I thought that the upper density is the
> right way of quantifying the failure of Erdos's conjecture, but I'm no longer
> sure. The former is a stronger property (and it is safer to formulate the
> result that way), as the density could theoretically be $0$. However, I don't
> know how to prove/disprove this for every small $\delta>0$, and I don't even
> have an opinion on whether the density should really be $0$ or not for every
> sufficiently small $\delta>0$.
>
> Experiments based on the table of values of f are also a bit indecisive: For
> $\delta=1/2$ and for $x=1000, 5000, 10000, 20000$ the fractions are: $0.0830,
> 0.0670, 0.0602, 0.05625$ and it is unclear if this goes to $0$ or stabilizes
> at a positive number. For $\delta=1/10$ and for $x=1000, 5000, 10000, 20000$
> the decay seems more convincing, as the fractions are now $0.0020, 0.0004,
> 0.0002, 0.0001$, but it might only be the case that these numbers stabilize
> much later (necessarily to a much smaller quantity).
>
> For large $\delta$ (probably already $\delta>1$) it should be easy to see that
> the upper density is in fact strictly positive. Perhaps this is to be added if
> the whole thing starts converging to some paper. Speaking of large ratios
> $f(n)/n$, the $\limsup$ part of the original question is also something to
> think about.
>
> EDIT: A brave conjecture would be that the distributions of the ratios
> $f(n)/n$ for $n\in[1,x]$ converge to some limit distribution as $x\to\infty$.
> An even braver conjecture is that the support of this limit distribution is
> the whole half-line $[0,\infty)$. Its approximation with $x=20000$ then looks
> like this (drawn in Wolfram Mathematica 13). Both of the above claims
> (positive density of $f(n)\leq\delta n$ for every $\delta>0$ and $\limsup
> f(n)/n=\infty$) would be consequences of this conjecture.

**Covers.** Parts (i) and (ii) of
[[problems/divisors/E1054/_index|Problem 1054]] as its Formulation reads
them: $f(n)=o(n)$ is false, and it is false for almost all $n$. Part (iii),
the limsup, is not addressed.
