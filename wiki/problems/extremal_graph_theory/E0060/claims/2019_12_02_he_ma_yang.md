***

**Claim.** For every sufficiently large even $q$, every graph on $q^2+q+1$
vertices with $\frac12q(q+1)^2+1$ edges contains at least $q-1$ copies of
$C_4$, and the paper characterizes the graphs with the fewest copies (He, Ma
and Yang, Some extremal results on 4-cycles, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 149
(2021), 92--108). The result first appeared as Theorem 1.5 of
arXiv:1912.00986v1, Stability and supersaturation of $4$-cycles, for even
$q\geq 10^{12}$: such a graph either contains at least $2q-3$ copies of $C_4$
or is an orthogonal polarity graph of order $q$ with one edge added, in which
case it contains $q-1$, $q$ or $q+1$ copies. Later versions of that arXiv
record became the authors' separate paper in CSIAM Trans. Appl. Math. 4
(2023), 74--128. When $q$ is a power of $2$, Füredi's bound and the polarity
graph give $\mathrm{ex}(q^2+q+1;C_4)=\frac12q(q+1)^2$. Deleting edges only
removes four-cycles, so every graph on $n=q^2+q+1$ vertices with more than
$\mathrm{ex}(n;C_4)$ edges has at least $q-1=(1-o(1))\sqrt n$ copies of
$C_4$, which is what
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0060/_index|Problem 60]] asks at these
orders. The v1 manuscript states this as Corollary 1.6: for $q=2^k$ with
$k\geq 40$, the least number of copies of $C_4$ in a graph on $q^2+q+1$
vertices with $\mathrm{ex}(q^2+q+1;C_4)+1$ edges is exactly $q-1$, attained
exactly by an orthogonal polarity graph of order $q$ with an edge added
between two vertices of degree $q$.

**Covers.** The orders $n=q^2+q+1$ with $q$ a sufficiently large power of
$2$. Nothing for other $n$; the problem stays open. The site's commentary
credits the paper with the conjecture for every even $q$, but for even $q$
that is not a power of $2$ the value of $\mathrm{ex}(q^2+q+1;C_4)$ is not
known to equal $\frac12q(q+1)^2$, so a graph with $\mathrm{ex}(n;C_4)+1$
edges need not have the theorem's edge count and the theorem does not reach
the problem there; the formal-conjectures variant
`erdos_60.variants.he_ma_yang`, linked from the problem page, states the
result for powers of $2$ only.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 149 (2021), 92--108. The
site's commentary credits the paper, but the site labels the problem OPEN, so
that credit is not `reviewed` evidence. No Lean proof of the result is
recorded; the formal-conjectures variant carries `sorry`.
