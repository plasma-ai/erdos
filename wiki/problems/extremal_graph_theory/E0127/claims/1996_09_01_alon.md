***

**Claim.** There are constants $c>0$ and $n_0$ such that for every even
$n>n_0$ and $e=n^2/2$, every graph with $e$ edges contains a bipartite
subgraph with at least

$$
\frac e2+\sqrt{\frac e8}+ce^{1/4}
$$

edges. This is Theorem 1.1 of N. Alon, *Bipartite subgraphs*, Combinatorica
**16** (1996), no. 3, 301--311; the corpus states it and reconstructs its
proof on its
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/alon_1996_bipartite_subgraphs/theorem_1_1|result page]].
The Edwards baseline of [[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0127/_index|Problem
127]] is $e/2+(\sqrt{8e+1}-1)/8=e/2+\sqrt{e/8}+O(1)$, so the correction
satisfies $f(n^2/2)\ge ce^{1/4}-O(1)\gg n^{1/2}$ along this sequence, in the
integer convention of the page as well as the real one, and $f(m_i)\to\infty$
for $m_i=i^2/2$ with $i$ even. The answer to the question is yes. The same
paper's
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/alon_1996_bipartite_subgraphs/inequality_2|inequality (2)]]
gives $f(m)\ll m^{1/4}$ for every $m$, so the exponent $1/4$ is best possible.

**Formalization.** The file `src/latest/ErdosProblems/Erdos127.lean` of
Boris Alexeev's `plby/lean-proofs` repository, at the commit linked above,
declares itself a formalization of this theorem: it names Alon as the
informal author, names Codex and GPT-5.6 Sol as its formal authors, and

Edwards baseline $m/2+(\sqrt{8m+1}-1)/8$ as a real number, defines

graph with $m$ edges has a bipartite subgraph with at least baseline plus $k$
edges, and declares, in the namespace `Erdos127`,

```lean
theorem erdos_127 :
    ∃ mseq : ℕ → ℕ, Tendsto mseq atTop atTop ∧
      Tendsto (fun i ↦ correction (mseq i)) atTop atTop
```

the edge counts $(2^{20}t^2)^2/2$: the question in the integer convention the
