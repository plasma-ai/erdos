***

**Claim.** Results of A. A. Razborov, *More about sparse halves in
triangle-free graphs*, Mat. Sb. 213 (2022), no. 1, 119--140 (English
translation Sb. Math. 213 (2022), no. 1), first posted as arXiv:2104.09406 on
2021-04-19 and cited as [Ra22] on the problem page. The paper writes
$\beta(G)$ for the least number of edges spanned by half the vertices of $G$
divided by $n^2$, $\rho(G)=2e(G)/n^2$ for the edge density and $\alpha(G)$
for the independence number divided by $n$, and calls Erdős's conjecture
$\beta(G)\le1/50$ for every triangle-free $G$ Conjecture 1. Theorem 3.2:
$\beta(G)\le27/1024$ for every triangle-free $G$. Conjecture 1 holds for
every triangle-free graph without an induced matching of size $2$
(Theorem 3.3); with $\rho(G)\le(33-\sqrt{161})/116\approx0.1751$, that is,
with at most $(33-\sqrt{161})n^2/232$ edges (Theorem 3.4); that is strongly
regular (Theorem 3.5, first stated in the arXiv version of 2021-07-28); with
$\alpha(G)\ge2/5$ (Corollary 3.7, from Theorem 3.6's bound
$\beta(G)\le\frac12\alpha(G)(\frac12-\alpha(G))$ for $\alpha(G)\ge3/8$),
hence also with maximum degree at least $2n/5$, a vertex's neighborhood being
independent; and of girth at least $5$ (Theorem 3.8). Each is the
contrapositive of
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0128/_index|Problem 128]] on its class, and
the low-density case extends Keevash and Sudakov's range of at most $n^2/12$
edges. The $27/1024$ bound is the limit of the method that weights the three
parts cut out by one edge uniformly, with the Clebsch graph extremal, and it
alone settles no instance of the problem. Library home
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/razborov_2022_more_about_sparse_halves_triangle_free/_index|razborov_2022_more_about_sparse_halves_triangle_free]].

**Covers.** Triangle-free graphs without an induced matching of size $2$; of
girth at least $5$; with independence number at least $2n/5$ (so with maximum
degree at least $2n/5$); with edge density $\rho(G)\le(33-\sqrt{161})/116$;
and strongly regular. Not covered: the general question, for which the paper
gives the constant $27/1024$ in place of $1/50$, which settles no instance;
the question as posed stays open.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; Proposition 1.1 reproves the part of
Krivelevich's argument it uses, and the flag-algebra inequality of
Theorem 3.1 is proved in the paper with a Maple worksheet the author posts.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the paper appeared in Matematicheskii Sbornik with
its English translation in Sbornik: Mathematics. The site's commentary
credits the paper with the constant $27/1024$ only and labels the problem
FALSIFIABLE, which settles nothing, so that credit is not listed as
`reviewed`.
