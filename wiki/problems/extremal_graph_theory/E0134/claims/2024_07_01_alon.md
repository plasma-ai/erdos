*Sum(m)it280*, Bolyai Society Mathematical Studies 32, Springer (2026), 13--29,

note are posted, whose embedded dates are 1 and 2 July 2024, and the second adds

**The result.** Theorem 3.2: let $G$ be a triangle-free graph on $n$
vertices with maximum degree at most $c(n)\sqrt n$, where

$$
2\frac{(\log n)^{1/3}}{n^{1/6}}\le c(n)\le\frac1{10}
$$

and $n$ is large. Then at most $2.5c(n)n^2$ edges can be added to $G$ to
obtain a triangle-free graph of diameter two. The proof runs a bounded-degree
triangle-free process whose outcome has independence number below $5c(n)n$
with high probability, completes it to a maximal triangle-free graph, and
bounds the degrees by the independence number. For the problem's fixed
$\epsilon,\delta>0$, choose $0<\epsilon_0<\min\{\epsilon,1/6\}$ and
$c(n)=n^{-\epsilon_0}$: the degree hypothesis $\Delta(G)<n^{1/2-\epsilon}$
implies $\Delta(G)\le c(n)\sqrt n$, the theorem's range holds for large
$n$, and fewer than $2.5n^{2-\epsilon_0}<\delta n^2$ edges are added. So the
answer to the question is yes, and the theorem page records the deduction step
by step. The same theorem resolves
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0618/_index|Problem 618]], the $o(\sqrt n)$
form of the question.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the argument is self-contained and the
problem page's account rests on this claim.
