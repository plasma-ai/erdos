**Claim.** $\mathrm{ex}(n;C_6)\gg n^{4/3}$ and $\mathrm{ex}(n;C_{10})\gg n^{6/5}$,
the statement of Problem 572 for $k=3$ and $k=5$.
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/benson_1966_minimal_regular_graphs_girths_eight_twelve/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
of the paper on the library's
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/benson_1966_minimal_regular_graphs_girths_eight_twelve/_index|source card]]
(p. 1091): the point-line incidence graph $G_8$ of a non-degenerate quadric
in $P(4,q)$ is a regular bipartite graph of degree $q+1$ and girth $8$ on
$1+q+q^2+q^3$ points and as many lines (p. 1092).
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/benson_1966_minimal_regular_graphs_girths_eight_twelve/theorem_2|Theorem 2]]:
the graph $G_{12}$ on the points of a quadric in $P(6,q)$ and its
distinguished lines is a regular bipartite graph of degree $q+1$ and girth
$12$ on $(q+1)(1+q^2+q^4)$ points and as many lines (p. 1093). The paper
states no extremal number; the step to the bounds is the elementary count
recorded on the problem page. $G_8$ has $n=2(1+q+q^2+q^3)$ vertices,
$\frac n2(q+1)$ edges and no cycle shorter than $8$, and $\frac n2\le(q+1)^3$,
so $\mathrm{ex}(n;C_6)\ge2^{-4/3}n^{4/3}$ at these orders; $G_{12}$ has
$n=2(q+1)(1+q^2+q^4)$ vertices, $\frac n2(q+1)$ edges and no cycle shorter
than $12$, and $\frac n2\le(q+1)^5$, so $\mathrm{ex}(n;C_{10})\ge2^{-6/5}n^{6/5}$.
Since $\mathrm{ex}(n;C_{2k})$ is nondecreasing in $n$ and consecutive prime
powers differ by a factor at most $2$, both bounds hold for every $n$ with
smaller constants. Bondy and Simonovits record the conjecture as known for
$k=2,3,5$ in
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/bondy_1974_cycles_even_length_graphs/remark_1|Remark 1]]
of [BoSi74] (p. 98), citing Benson among others; the site's commentary
credits Benson with the cases $k=3$ and $k=5$.

**Covers.** The instances $k=3$ (the cycle $C_6$) and $k=5$ (the cycle
$C_{10}$) of the statement for every $k\ge3$. Nothing for $k=4$ or any
$k\ge6$, which remain open; the problem has no full claim.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki: the constructions are the paper's, and
the counting step is elementary and recorded on the problem page.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Clark T. Benson, *Minimal regular graphs of girths
eight and twelve*, Canad. J. Math. 18 (1966), 1091--1094,
doi:10.4153/CJM-1966-109-8, a refereed journal; the publisher's record gives
the year only, and this page's date is the first day of that year by the
corpus's convention. No `reviewed` evidence is listed: the site labels the
problem OPEN, and commentary on an open problem is not an acceptance.

**Read depth.** Theorems 1 and 2 and the counts on pp. 1092--1093 were read
clause by clause; the proofs were not read, and nothing is independently
reviewed in this corpus.
