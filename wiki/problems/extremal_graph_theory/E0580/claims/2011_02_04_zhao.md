***

**Claim.** Yi Zhao, *Proof of the $(n/2-n/2-n/2)$ Conjecture for large
$n$*, Electron. J. Combin. 18 (2011), no. 1, Paper 27, 61 pp.,
doi:10.37236/514 (submitted 6 June 2008, accepted 22 January 2011, published
4 February 2011, this page's date). Theorem 1.6, the paper's main theorem
(p. 2;
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/zhao_2011_proof_n_2_n_2_n_2_conjecture_large_n/theorem_1_6|result page]]):
there is a threshold $n_0$ such that for every $n\ge n_0$, a graph of
order $n$ in which at least $\lceil n/2\rceil$ vertices have degree at
least $\lceil n/2\rceil$ contains every tree with at most
$\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ edges. The site asks about trees on at most $n/2$
vertices under the hypothesis that at least $n/2$ vertices have degree at
least $n/2$; such a tree has at most $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ edges, and the
site's hypothesis implies the rounded one because degrees and counts are
integers, so the theorem answers the site's question affirmatively for every
$n\ge n_0$. The threshold is existential: neither the statement nor the
introduction gives a value, and the proof goes through the Regularity Lemma,
so none is to be expected from it. The theorem therefore reduces the problem
to a finite check, which is what the site's label DECIDABLE records; the
check's extent is unknown. Construction 1.7 (p. 3;
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/zhao_2011_proof_n_2_n_2_n_2_conjecture_large_n/construction_1_7|result page]])
shows that in Zhao's edge form the count $n/2$ of large-degree vertices
cannot be lowered to $n/2-\sqrt n-2$; its tree has $n/2+1$ vertices, so it
does not bear on the site's vertex form directly.

**Covers.** Every order $n\ge n_0$, for a threshold $n_0$ the paper does
not state. What remains is the finite check of every $n<n_0$: once those
orders are verified, the problem of
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0580/_index|Problem 580]] is settled in the
affirmative. No source on record closes any part of that remainder, and its
extent is unknown because $n_0$ is not explicit. A computer-assisted
verification for $n\le19$, submitted to the site's proof-claim tab in July
2026 and unreviewed, has
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0580/claims/2026_07_14_zeraoulia|its own claim page]].
The approximate theorem of Ajtai, Komlós and Szemerédi (1995), with
$(1+\rho)n/2$ in both places for large $n$, is the earlier result the
paper supersedes and is known here only through Zhao's Theorem 1.5.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki: the proof is the paper's. The
statements of Conjecture 1.3, Theorems 1.5 and 1.6 and Construction 1.7
(pp. 2--3) are this page's basis; the proof (Sections 3--7 and the appendix,
pp. 5--61) was not read.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the Electronic Journal of Combinatorics is a refereed
journal (submitted 6 June 2008, accepted 22 January 2011, by its Crossref
record). As context, not evidence: after a comment of 23 October 2025 on the
site's discussion thread pointed to the paper, the site's curator, Thomas Bloom,
relabeled the problem DECIDABLE and credits Zhao in the problem's commentary
with the proof for all sufficiently large $n$ (page last edited 24 October
2025); the community database lists the problem as decidable, its record last
updated 23 October 2025. The label records the reduction to a finite check
without settling the problem, so the credit is not `reviewed` evidence. The
acceptance rests on the refereed venue alone; this repository's own review is
not claimed as evidence.
