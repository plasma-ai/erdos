***

**Claim.** The account sallerk, in a comment of 31 August 2026 (the claim's
date) on the site's discussion thread, reports that every connected graph on
at most $11$ vertices can be partitioned into at most $\lceil n/2\rceil$
edge-disjoint paths: $11{,}716{,}571$ graphs at $n=10$ and
$1{,}006{,}700{,}565$ at $n=11$ were decided, with no counterexample and no
graph left undecided. The check is over simple paths, not trails, as the
statement of [[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0583/_index|Problem 583]]
requires. Each sweep is split into shards and accepted only when the shard
counts sum to the number of connected graphs on $n$ vertices (OEIS A001349),
which the comment presents as its exhaustiveness certificate. A second
decider written from the definition agrees with the fast one on all $992$
connected graphs on $4$ to $7$ vertices; beyond $n=7$ the result rests on the
fast decider and the count alone. The comment argues the range is not
vacuous, since from $n=7$ upward a growing share of the connected graphs
($36$ of $853$ at $n=7$, $6{,}666{,}730$ of $11{,}716{,}571$ at $n=10$) is
reached by none of the six partial results the site lists. The code, run
records and a reproduction note are in a public repository, and the comment
discloses that the searches and computations were done with AI assistance.
The repository link is pinned to the revision of 31 August 2026 that the
comment posted; the folder was pruned on 7 September 2026.

**Independent reproduction.** A comment of 17 September 2026 by the account
herong reproduces the $n\le11$ result with an independent decider; it has its
own page,
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0583/claims/2026_09_17_herong|herong's check]].

**Covers.** Connected graphs on at most $11$ vertices. Nothing for $n\ge12$:
the comment says the $n=12$ sweep leaves $35{,}633{,}639$ graphs its
heuristic cannot decide, and the problem asks about every $n$, so neither a
proof nor a counterexample follows.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki.

**Standing.** Claimed: a forum computation with provenance, which the site
does not verify; reproduced by a second forum computation (herong's, above),
not reviewed and given no credit by this corpus, which has not run either
check. The claim is partial, so the problem's standing is unchanged by it.
