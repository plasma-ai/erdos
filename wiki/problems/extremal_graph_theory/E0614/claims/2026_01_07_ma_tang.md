***

**Claim.** Write $H$ for the complement of an $n$-vertex graph $G$. Every
$k+2$ vertices of $G$ induce a subgraph of maximum degree at least $k$ exactly
when every $k+2$ vertices of $H$ induce a subgraph of minimum degree at most
$1$, so $f(n,k)$ of [[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0614/_index|Problem 614]]
is $\binom n2$ minus the largest number of edges of an $n$-vertex graph with
that property. The note draws three consequences. For $k=1$ the property says
that $H$ is triangle-free, so by Mantel's theorem

$$
f(n,1)=\binom n2-\left\lfloor\frac{n^2}4\right\rfloor\qquad(n\ge2)
$$

(its Theorem 2.1). For $k=2$ the admissible $H$ are exactly the $C_4$-free
graphs, since a $4$-vertex graph of minimum degree at least $2$ is Hamiltonian,
so $f(n,2)=\binom n2-\mathrm{ex}(n,C_4)$ for $n\ge4$ (Theorem 3.1). For each
fixed $k\ge2$, with $\mathcal F_k^{\min}$ the finite family of graphs on $k+2$
vertices that have minimum degree at least $2$ and lose that property when any
edge is deleted, the admissible $H$ are exactly the $\mathcal F_k^{\min}$-free
graphs, so $f(n,k)=\binom n2-\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal F_k^{\min})$ (Theorem 4.5).
An exhaustive computer search gives the families for $3\le k\le6$; for $k=3$
it is $\{C_5,K_{2,3},K_1\vee2K_2\}$, where $K_1\vee2K_2$ is a vertex joined to
two disjoint edges, so $f(n,3)=\binom n2-\mathrm{ex}(n,\{C_5,K_{2,3},K_1\vee
2K_2\})$ for $n\ge5$ (Corollary 4.6).

**Covers.** The instance $k=1$, where $f(n,1)$ is determined for every
$n\ge2$. For $k\ge2$ the result is a reduction: $f(n,k)$ equals $\binom n2$
minus a Turán number of a finite family, and the note asserts that identity
as its answer, but the Turán numbers $\mathrm{ex}(n,C_4)$ and
$\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal F_k^{\min})$ for $k\ge3$ are not determined there or
elsewhere, so no value of $f(n,k)$ with $k\ge2$ is settled. The thread post
says that the authors do not know whether this Turán-type reformulation is
the kind of answer Erdős had in mind.

**The posting.** The note was posted on the site's discussion thread on 7
January 2026 by Quanyu Tang, who describes it as a short note written with a
coauthor named only as Ma; it is hosted in a GitHub repository under Tang's
name, linked above at the commit of that day, and carries no title, author
list or date on its face. The determination of $f(n,1)$ is Mantel's theorem
applied to the complement; the general reduction is a few lines of
elementary graph theory, and the families for $3\le k\le6$ come from a
computer search whose code the repository's description names but whose
output is given only as figures.
