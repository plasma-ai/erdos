> The key step is a reformulation: define the deficiency
> $D(F)=|E_{0}|+|E_{w}|-|A|$, where $E_{0}$ counts edges whose endpoints have no
> common neighbor, $E_{w}$ counts edges having a witness (a vertex whose only
> common neighbor with one endpoint is the other), and $A$ counts non-adjacent

> always works; otherwise a local supply/demand ledger of witness configurations
> reduces everything to one global counting inequality, proved by an explicit
> injection of demand units into pairwise-disjoint pools of supply slots. Notes:

**The claim.** The answer to
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0742/_index|Problem 742]] is yes for every
$n$: a graph on $n$ vertices of diameter $2$ in which deleting any edge
increases the diameter has at most $\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$ edges. The route, in
this page's words from the write-up (sections 1.2 to 1.4). For a graph $F$ on
$n$ vertices let $D(F)$ be the number of edges $uv$ with
$N(u)\cap N(v)=\emptyset$, plus the number of edges $uv$ with
$N(u)\cap N(v)\ne\emptyset$ that have a witness, a vertex $y\ne u$ not
adjacent to $u$ with $N(u)\cap N(y)=\{v\}$ (or the same with $u$ and $v$
exchanged), minus the number of non-edges $\{x,y\}$ with
$N(x)\cap N(y)\ne\emptyset$. For a critical graph $G$ of diameter $2$ with $e$
edges, $D(G)=2e-\binom n2$, so the bound is equivalent to
$D(G)\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. The claim is that $D(F)\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ for
every graph $F$ on $n$ vertices (attained by balanced complete bipartite
graphs and by perfect matchings; the write-up characterizes no equality case),
by induction on $n$: both ends of an edge are removed, the edge chosen so that
$D$ grows by at most $1$. Such an edge is at hand whenever two adjacent
vertices share no neighbor; failing that, the witness configurations near a
candidate edge are counted and the step reduces to one inequality between two
of these counts, established by an injection from one side into the other. The
write-up claims the bound for every $n$, says that no step closes by
exhaustion, and says that it does not prove the equality clause; the write-up
is linked at the commit of 5 August 2026 that added it, the commit after the
one the submission pins for the Lean file; its text is unchanged at the
repository's head commit of 10 August 2026, accessed 2026-10-07. A comment on
the tab of 2026-08-05 stated that the problem was already solved and asked the
submitter to summarize what was new; the comment refers, in this page's
reading, to the large-$n$ theorem. The submitter answered on 2026-08-10 that
the equality clause also follows and linked a second write-up and a second
Lean file for it on the repository's main branch.
