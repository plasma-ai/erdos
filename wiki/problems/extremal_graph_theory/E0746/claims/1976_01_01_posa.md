***

**Claim.** Theorem 3 of L. Pósa, *Hamiltonian circuits in random graphs*,
Discrete Math. **14** (1976), no. 4, 359--364 (received 26 October 1974),
p. 364, paged at
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/posa_1976_hamiltonian_circuits_random_graphs/theorem_3|Theorem 3]]:
"Let us consider $n$ vertices and place $[c_1n\log n]$ edges between them at
random. The graph $G$ so arising contains a Hamiltonian circuit with
probability tending to 1. ($c_1$ is a number for which Theorem 2 holds.)"
Theorem 2 (p. 363) is the same statement in the binomial model with edge
probability $(c_1\log n)/n$, for a sufficiently large $c_1$. The graph of
Theorem 3 is chosen uniformly among the graphs with exactly $[c_1n\log n]$
edges on $n$ labeled vertices, the model of
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0746/_index|Problem 746]]. Hamiltonicity is
preserved by adding edges, so for every fixed $\epsilon\ge c_1-\tfrac12$ the
random graph with at least $(\tfrac12+\epsilon)n\log n$ edges is almost
surely Hamiltonian. The issue carries the year only, so this page's name
uses the first day of it.

**Covers.** Every fixed $\epsilon\ge c_1-\tfrac12$, where $c_1$ is a
constant for which Pósa's Theorem 2 holds (the paper names none; Komlós and
Szemerédi's abstract reports that $c>3$ suffices); smaller $\epsilon$ are
not covered. The full statement is proved on the claim pages
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0746/claims/1976_01_22_korshunov|Korshunov]]
and
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0746/claims/1983_01_01_komlos_szemeredi|Komlós and Szemerédi]].

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Discrete Mathematics 14 (1976), no. 4. The site's
curator, Thomas Bloom, credits the theorem in the problem's commentary, and
Erdős's 1982 Singapore paper (§ 1, p. 69) says the conjecture "was proved by
Pósa in a very ingenious way with $cn\log n$ instead of
$(\tfrac12+\varepsilon)n\log n$". Both are context, not evidence: the label
PROVED rests on Korshunov and on Komlós and Szemerédi, and the problem lists
no parts.
