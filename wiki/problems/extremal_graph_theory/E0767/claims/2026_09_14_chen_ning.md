**Claim.** For integers $k\ge1$ and $n\ge k+2$ let $g_k(n)$ be the maximum
number of edges of an $n$-vertex graph with no cycle having a vertex incident
with at least $k$ chords. Theorem 1.3 of Xiaozheng Chen and Bo Ning, *On
Erdős Problem 767: Cycles with Chords*, arXiv:2609.15330v1 (14 September
2026; 22 pages), states that for all $k\ge1$ and $n\ge k+2$

$$
g_k(n)=\max\Bigl\{\Bigl\lfloor\tfrac{(k+1)n}2\Bigr\rfloor,\
\max\Bigl\{a(n-a)+\Bigl\lfloor\tfrac{a(k+1-a)}2\Bigr\rfloor:
a\in\mathbb Z,\ \Bigl\lfloor\tfrac{k+1}2\Bigr\rfloor+1\le a\le k+1\Bigr\}\Bigr\}
$$

(the abstract's display omits the outer "max"; the paper's Theorem 1.3
prints it). For $k\ge2$ the theorem gives $g_k(n)=(k+1)(n-k-1)$ for all
$n\ge\lceil(5k+1)/2\rceil$, and the threshold is sharp: Construction 3.3
(pp. 4--5), a set $X$ of $a$ vertices identified with $\mathbb Z_a$ and
carrying the differences $1,\ldots,\lfloor(k+1-a)/2\rfloor$ (plus a
half-shift matching when $k+1-a$ is odd), joined completely to an
independent set $Y$ of $n-a$ vertices, has $a(n-a)+\lfloor a(k+1-a)/2\rfloor$
edges and no cycle with $k$ chords at one vertex, and Remark 3.4 takes $a=k$
at $n=\lceil(5k+1)/2\rceil-1$ to get $(k+1)(n-k-1)+1$ edges. The lower bound
for small $n$ is a second, nearly regular construction (Section 3); the upper
bound (Section 5) builds on the stability theorem of Ma and Ning
(Combinatorica 40 (2020), 105--147) for Bondy's theorem on long cycles. The
paper restates Jiang's theorem as its Theorem 1.2 with the hypotheses
$k\ge1$ and $n\ge3k+3$, credits Erdős's conjecture of the formula for
$n\ge2k+2$ to his 1969 paper, says that Lewin disproved that conjecture,
citing B. Bollobás, *Extremal Graph Theory* (1978), p. 398, Problem 12,
and takes Bollobás's question with an unspecified threshold $n(k)$ from
Problem 13 of the same page; its reference [19] is Pósa's Problem 127, from
which the case $k=1$ of the formula follows. The preprint was named in the
site's discussion thread on 15 September 2026, and the site's reference list
did not carry it as of 2026-09-18.

For [[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0767/_index|Problem 767]], the
formula gives $g_k(n)=(k+1)n-(k+1)^2$ for all large $n$, for every
$k\ge1$ (the case $k=1$ is Pósa's $2n-4$ for $n\ge4$, on
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0767/claims/1961_01_01_posa|its claim page]]),
so it settles the question a second time, after
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0767/claims/2004_04_07_jiang|Jiang]],
and names the exact threshold at which the equality begins, which Jiang's
$3k+3$ bounds from above. Since $\lceil(5k+1)/2\rceil$ equals $2k+2$ for
$k=2,3$ and exceeds it from $k=4$ on, the sharpness places the failure of
Erdős's range $n\ge2k+2$ at $k\ge4$, in agreement with Erdős's 1975 report
that Lewin's examples exist for large $k$.

**AI assistance and formalization.** The paper's declaration of AI usage
(p. 21) says that the work began while the authors were using GPT-5.5 Pro,
that the authors proved the cases $k\le7$ and a stability version of Jiang's
theorem themselves, and that, given those manuscripts, the system's second
proposed general conjecture became the main theorem; the authors wrote and
checked the final text. The same declaration says that every original result
of the paper, Theorem 1.3 included, has been formalized and machine-checked
in Lean 4, with the source at the repository linked above (its one commit,
of 10 September 2026, is the pin; the repository describes itself as a Lean 4
formalization for Erdős Problem 767). The repository's README says that its
development targets the authors' final manuscript, that its public claim is
the verification of the paper's internal conclusions conditional on nine
cited literature theorems packaged as hypotheses, which it does not prove,
and that `theorem_1_3` is its entry point. Because the development declares
itself a formalization of this paper's result, it is a link on this page and
not its own claim. This corpus has not built, audited or kernel-checked it,
so it gives no `formalized` evidence.
