***

**Claim.** The statement of
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0807/_index|Problem 807]] is false: for
$G=G(n,1/2)$ the equality $\tau(G)=n-\alpha(G)$ does not hold with probability
tending to $1$. Let $\beta(G)$ be the largest number of vertices of an induced
complete bipartite subgraph of $G$, and let $k_0=k_0(n)$ be the largest $k$
for which the expected number $f(k)=\binom nk2^{-\binom k2}$ of independent
$k$-sets is at least $1$. Every graph satisfies $\tau(G)\le n-\beta(G)+1$,
since the edges outside a largest induced complete bipartite subgraph $H$ can
be covered by stars centered at the vertices outside $H$, and $H$ is one more
piece. Theorem 1.1 of N. Alon, *Bipartite decomposition of random graphs*,
J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 113 (2015), 220--235, first posted as arXiv:1402.6466
on 2014-02-26, states: (i) if $1=o(f(k_0))$ and $f(k_0+1)=o(1)$, then whp
$\alpha(G)=k_0$ and $\beta(G)=k_0+2$, so $\tau(G)\le n-\alpha(G)-1$ whp; (ii)
if $f(k_0)=\Theta(1)$, then whp one of the four combinations of
$\alpha(G)\in\{k_0-1,k_0\}$ with $\beta(G)\in\{k_0+1,k_0+2\}$ holds, each with
probability bounded away from $0$ and $1$; (iii) the same with $k_0+1$ in place
of $k_0$ when $f(k_0+1)=\Theta(1)$. The corpus states the theorem on its
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/alon_2015_bipartite_decomposition_random_graphs/theorem_1_1|result page]].

The hypothesis of (i) holds for most $n$, in the sense that a uniform random
integer in $[1,M]$ satisfies it with probability tending to $1$ as
$M\to\infty$ (the paper, p. 3). Along those $n$ the probability of the
equality tends to $0$, so it cannot tend to $1$ along all $n$, and the
statement, which asks for exactly that, is false. The problem page adds a
remark on the exceptional $n$: in three of the four cases of (ii) the bound
$\tau(G)\le n-\beta(G)+1$ already gives $\tau(G)<n-\alpha(G)$, so the
equality fails with probability bounded away from $0$ for every large $n$;
that remark is the problem page's and is not part of the claim. The paper
leaves open whether for the exceptional $n$ the equality holds with
probability bounded away from $0$, and conjectures (Conjecture 4.1) that
$\tau(G)=n-\beta(G)+1$ whp.

[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/alon_2015_bipartite_decomposition_random_graphs/_index|source card]];

disproves the statement a second time, for every $n$ with high probability.
