***

**Claim.** J. L. Leonard, *On a conjecture of Bollobás and Erdős*, Period.
Math. Hungar. **3** (1973), no. 3--4, 281--284, exhibits a graph $G$ with
$57$ points and $141$ edges in which no two points are joined by five
internally disjoint paths
([[../library/extremal_graph_theory/leonard_1973_conjecture_bollobas_erdos/counterexample_p281|pp. 281--282]]).
Since $57=1+14\cdot4$ and $141=1+14\binom52$, this is the question of
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0915/_index|Problem 915]] at $m=5$, $n=14$
under the vertex-disjoint reading, and the answer there is no. The paper
goes on
([[../library/extremal_graph_theory/leonard_1973_conjecture_bollobas_erdos/bound_p282|pp. 282--283]])
to build, for every integer $s$, graphs with $n$ points and more than
$\lfloor5n/2\rfloor+s$ edges and no such pair, so the threshold $k_5(n)$ is
not $\frac52n$ plus a constant. Leonard writes that he suspects the
edge-disjoint form of the conjecture to be true, and his earlier paper of
1972 had proved it at $m=5$.

The page targets the vertex-disjoint reading of the question, under which
the statement is asserted for every $m\ge2$ and $n\ge1$; the counterexample
refutes it at $m=5$, $n=14$ and hence as a whole, so the claim is a full
disproof. The edge-disjoint reading, under which the conjecture is true for
every $m$ by Mader's theorem, is recorded as a variant on
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0915/claims/1973_09_01_mader|Mader's claim page]].
The disproof for every $m\ge5$, with the exact value of $k_5(n)$, is
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E0915/claims/1974_10_01_sorensen_thomassen|Sørensen and Thomassen's]].
