***

**Claim.** P. Turán, *Eine Extremalaufgabe aus der Graphentheorie*, Mat.
Fiz. Lapok 48 (1941), 436--452 (in Hungarian; Zbl 0026.26903), the paper
that footnote 1 on p. 122 of Erdős's *On a theorem of Rademacher-Turán*
(Illinois J. Math. 6 (1962); filed as
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1962_theorem_rademacher_turan/_index|erdos_1962_theorem_rademacher_turan]])
cites for Turán's theorem. As the zbMATH review of Zbl 0026.26903 states
it, the paper proves that among graphs on $n$ vertices containing no
complete graph on $k$ vertices, the graph $D(n,k)$ has the maximum number
of edges, and is the only such graph; here $D(n,k)$ joins two vertices
exactly when their labels in $1,\dots,n$ are incongruent modulo $k-1$, and
it has $\frac12(k-2)(k-1)^{-1}(n^2-r^2)+\binom r2$ edges, where $r$ is the
remainder of $n$ on division by $k-1$. The case $k=3$, which Mantel proved
in 1907, is the statement that p. 122 of Erdős's paper records as "a
special case of Turán's theorem": a graph on $n$ vertices with more than
$\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$ edges contains a triangle. The extremal graph
$D(n,3)$ is the complete bipartite graph
$K_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\lceil n/2\rceil}$, which has $\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor$
edges, no triangle and, for $n\ge2$, chromatic number $2$. So, in the
conventions of
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1011/_index|Problem 1011]],
$f_2(n)=\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor+1$ for every $n\ge2$; Erdős's 1971 list gives
the same value as $u_2=[\tfrac14n^2]+1$, "the well known theorem of Turán".
The record gives the year only, so the page's month and day are
placeholders.

**Covers.** The case $r=2$: $f_2(n)=\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor+1$ for every
$n\ge2$, the condition being vacuous for $n=1$. Nothing about $r\ge3$.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; Turán's theorem is the whole
argument.

**Acceptance.** Refereed journal publication in Matematikai és Fizikai Lapok,
which is the `refereed` evidence. The site credits Turán's theorem on a problem
it labels OPEN, which is not acceptance, so `reviewed` is not listed. The paper
is in Hungarian and is not examined in this corpus; the statement is taken from
the zbMATH review of Zbl 0026.26903, and Erdős's 1962 and 1971 papers, which
quote the theorem, agree with it.
