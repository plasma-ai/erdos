***

**Claim.** In the conventions of
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1011/_index|Problem 1011]],
$f_3(n)=\lfloor(n-1)^2/4\rfloor+2$ for every $n\ge5$: every graph on $n$
vertices with at least $\lfloor(n-1)^2/4\rfloor+2$ edges and chromatic
number at least $3$ contains a triangle, and some triangle-free graph on $n$
vertices with chromatic number $3$ has $\lfloor(n-1)^2/4\rfloor+1$ edges.
The claimed result is Lemma 1 of P. Erdős, *On a theorem of
Rademacher-Turán*, Illinois J. Math. 6 (1962), no. 1, 122--127, p. 123:
every graph on $n$ vertices with $\lfloor(n-1)^2/4\rfloor+2$ edges that is
not "even" (not bipartite) contains a triangle, which Erdős says "was found
jointly by Gallai and myself" and "was also found by Mr. Andrásfai
independently"; and the example on p. 124, a triangle-free graph with a
five-cycle whose edge count attains the proof's bound
$\lfloor(n-1)^2/4\rfloor+1$ for every $n\ge5$ (the problem page states the
example with its parameters, corrects a misprint in them and checks the
count). For a triangle-free graph, chromatic number at least $3$ is the same
as not bipartite, which is how the lemma answers the problem's $r=3$. The
corpus states the lemma, the proof's bound and the example on its result
page
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1962_theorem_rademacher_turan/lemma_1|Lemma 1]];
Ren, Wang, Wang and Yang restate the bound as Theorem 1.2 of their preprint
with the graph $H_0$
([[../library/extremal_graph_theory/ren_2024_extremal_triangle_free_graphs_chromatic_number/theorem_1_2|Theorem
1.2]]). The claimant is Erdős, the paper's only author; he credits the lemma
to joint work with Gallai and to Andrásfai independently, and the site's
commentary credits Erdős and Gallai. The page's date is the issue's, 1 March
1962 (Crossref).

**Covers.** The value of $f_3(n)$ for every $n\ge5$; for $n\le4$ no
triangle-free graph has chromatic number $3$ and the condition is vacuous.
Nothing about $f_r(n)$ for any $r\ge4$, which the problem asks for as
well; the problem stays open.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki; the paper's lemma and example are the
whole argument.

**Acceptance.** The paper is refereed: Illinois J. Math. 6 (1962), no. 1,
122--127, a journal publication, which is the `refereed` evidence. The
site's commentary credits Erdős and Gallai with $f_3(n)$, but the site
labels the problem OPEN, so the commentary is not acceptance of the problem
and is not listed as `reviewed`. Read depth: the lemma, the attribution and
the example were read clause by clause; the proof was read for structure,
and the $k=2$ arithmetic of the example is an authored check on the problem
page; nothing is independently reviewed by this project.
