***

**Claim.**
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/ore_1961_arc_coverings_graphs/theorem_4_3|Theorem 4.3]]
(p. 320) of O. Ore, *Arc coverings of graphs*, Ann. Mat. Pura Appl. (4) 55
(1961), no. 1, 315--321, in the corpus's words: a graph on $n$ vertices with
at least $\frac12(n-1)(n-2)+2=\binom{n-1}2+2$ edges has a Hamilton circuit;
with exactly $\binom{n-1}2+1$ edges the only graphs without one are
$K_{n-1}$ with a pendant edge and, for $n=5$, one further graph (the
paper's Fig. 3). The count $\binom{n-1}2+2$ is the edge count of
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1012/_index|Problem 1012]] at $k=0$,
$\binom{n-1}2+\binom22+1$, and a cycle on $n-0$ vertices is a Hamilton
circuit. No graph on $n\le2$ vertices meets the count, so the implication
holds for every $n\ge1$ and $f(0)=1$. The page's date is the first day of
the issue's month, December 1961 (Crossref).

**Covers.** The case $k=0$ only, with its sharpness at $\binom{n-1}2+1$
edges. Nothing about $k\ge1$.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the paper's theorem, with its
Theorems 3.2 and 4.2, is the whole argument.

**Acceptance.** Refereed journal publication in the Annali di Matematica
Pura ed Applicata (Crossref: issue of December 1961), which is the
`refereed` evidence; Woodall's 1972 paper (p. 749) credits the case $r=0$
to Ore. The site's label SOLVED rests on
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1012/claims/1972_05_01_woodall|Woodall]],
so its credit of $f(0)=1$ to Ore is not listed as `reviewed`. The proof
(pp. 320--321) is followed on the library's result page; nothing here is
independently reviewed.
