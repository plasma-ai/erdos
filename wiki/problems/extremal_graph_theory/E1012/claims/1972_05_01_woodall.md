***

**Claim.** [[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1012/_index|Problem 1012]]
asks how large $n$ must be, in terms of $k\ge0$, for
$\binom{n-k-1}2+\binom{k+2}2+1$ edges on $n$ vertices to force a cycle on
$n-k$ vertices; the answer is that $n\ge2k+3$ suffices, so $f(k)=2k+3$ is
admissible for every $k$, and the edge count cannot be lowered there. The
claimed result is D. R. Woodall, *Sufficient conditions for circuits in
graphs*, Proc. London Math. Soc. (3) 24 (1972), no. 4, 739--755 (received
20 August 1970, revised 19 January 1971; issued May 1972, whose nominal
first day is this page's date), filed on its
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/woodall_1972_sufficient_conditions_circuits_graphs/_index|card]].
Its
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/woodall_1972_sufficient_conditions_circuits_graphs/corollary_11_1|Corollary 11.1]]
(p. 749), in the corpus's words: a graph on $n\ge r+3$ vertices contains a
circuit of every length $d$ with $3\le d\le n-r$ provided it has at least
$\binom{n-r-1}2+\binom{r+2}2+1$ edges when $n\ge2r+3$, or at least
$[\frac14n^2]+1$ edges when $n<2r+3$. The paper's $r$ is this problem's
$k$; the corollary is the case of minimum valency $0$ of Theorem 11
(pp. 747--748), the paper's main theorem. With $r=k$ the first bound is the
problem's count and $d=n-k$ lies in the range, which gives the claim. The
paper states (p. 749) that the first bound is the least possible because
of its graph $G_4(n,r)$, a complete graph on $n-r-1$ vertices and one on
$r+2$ vertices sharing a vertex, which has one edge fewer and no circuit of
length $n-r$ or more (p. 741); p. 741 records that Erdős asked the question
at Oxford in 1969, citing Problem 4 of his 1971 list, and p. 749 presents
the corollary as its answer, crediting the cases $r=0$ and $r=1$ to Ore and
to Bondy.

**How the printed corollary reaches the site's question.** The site's
$f(k)$ is a threshold, any value from which the implication holds for all
larger $n$, and the question asks how small it can be. The corollary gives
$f(k)\le2k+3$ directly. For $k+3\le n\le2k+2$ its second bound applies, and
the problem's count is at least $[\frac14n^2]+1$ there (with $a=n-k-1$ and
$b=k+2$, $a+b=n+1$ and $\binom a2+\binom b2\ge[\frac14n^2]$, an elementary
comparison made in this corpus and recorded on the result page as a filing
observation, stated by no source), while for $n\le k+2$ the count exceeds
$\binom n2$ and the implication is vacuous; so the implication holds for
every $n\ge1$, as a thread comment of 29 October 2025 argued from Li and
Ning's restatement. Only the first half, $f(k)\le2k+3$ with the sharp
count, is Woodall's printed claim; the small range rests on the second
bound plus the comparison, and nothing is independently reviewed.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the paper's own statements and the
elementary comparison above are the whole argument.

**Acceptance.** Refereed publication in the Proceedings of the London
Mathematical Society (Crossref record: volume s3-24, issue 4, pp. 739--755,
issued May 1972), which is the `refereed` evidence. The `reviewed` evidence
is documented acceptance by named experts and by the site's curator, Thomas
Bloom: Li and Ning, *Stability of Woodall's theorem and spectral conditions
for large cycles*, Electron. J. Combin. 30 (2023), P1.39 (refereed, open
access; filed on its
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/li_2023_stability_woodall_theorem_spectral_conditions_large_cycles/_index|card]]),
restate the $n\ge2k+3$ half as their Theorem 8 with the same count and range
and record that the sharing-vertex graph makes the count sharp, so that the
extremal number of $C_{n-k}$ is $\binom{n-k-1}2+\binom{k+2}2$ for
$n\ge2k+3$; and the curator labels the problem SOLVED, records Woodall's
theorem as settling the question completely, and the community database
agrees (it lists the problem as solved as of its last update, 31 October
2025). Read depth: Corollary 11.1 and Theorem 11 are checked clause by
clause; the proof of Theorem 11 (pp. 748--749) and the proofs of Lemmas 11.1
and 11.2 and Sublemma 11.2.1 (pp. 745--747) are followed at the level of
their case structure, the inequalities not checked, and the cited results of
Bondy, Dirac, Pósa and Erdős taken as statements. The earlier cases the site
credits, Ore's $f(0)=1$ (Ann. Mat. Pura Appl. 1961, Theorem 4.3) and Bondy's
$f(1)=1$ (Discrete Math. 1971, Theorem 2), have their own accepted partial
claim pages,
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1012/claims/1961_12_01_ore|Ore 1961]] and
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1012/claims/1971_09_01_bondy|Bondy 1971]],
and are special cases of this claim.
