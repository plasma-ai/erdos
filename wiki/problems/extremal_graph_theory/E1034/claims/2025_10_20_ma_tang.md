***

**Claim.** The answer to
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1034/_index|Problem 1034]] is no: for every
$\varepsilon>0$ and all sufficiently large $n$ there is a graph on $n$ vertices
with more than $n^2/4$ edges in which no triangle has more than
$(2-\sqrt{5/2}+\varepsilon)n$ vertices with two or more neighbors on it, and
$2-\sqrt{5/2}=0.418861\ldots$ is below $\frac12$. The claimed result is Theorem
2.1 of J. Ma and Q. Tang, *On Erdős problem \#1034*, a three-page note hosted on
the first author's page (the file the site links; no arXiv identifier or
journal; PDF metadata dated 21 October 2025); the corpus states it on the result
page
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/ma_2025_erdos_problem_1034/theorem_2_1|Theorem 2.1]]
([[../library/extremal_graph_theory/ma_2025_erdos_problem_1034/_index|card]]).
The construction is explicit: a side $B$ of $\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor$ vertices
partitioned into cliques of about $c_1(\alpha)n$ vertices, an independent side
$S$, every edge between $B$ and $S$, and $\alpha^*=1-1/\sqrt{10}$; every
triangle has two or three vertices in one clique of $B$, so the vertices joined
to two of its vertices are $S$ and that clique, and the edge count exceeds
$n^2/4$ by the choice of the clique size. The proof is a two-page computation
written out on the problem page. The note reads the site's "every vertex is
joined to at least two vertices of $T$" as every $y_i$, the reading the problem
page adopts; the count of such vertices is at most
$(2-\sqrt{5/2}+\varepsilon)n<(\frac12-o(1))n$ for large $n$, which is the
negation of the question under either reading. The thread's first post of 20
October 2025, the page's date, announced the note; an edit to the post corrects
its constant: the construction gives $2-\sqrt{5/2}\approx0.4189$, not the
smaller constant $\sqrt2-1\approx0.4142$ (a stronger bound) that the earlier
version of the note stated, and the authors add that a finer computation might
improve the constant slightly; the file the site links is the corrected version.
The authors' thread post of 27 October 2025 sketches the further statement that
the conjecture fails for $K_4$-free graphs too, with the constant
$2\sqrt3-3\approx0.464$; that sketch is not in the note, is unreviewed, and is
not part of this claim. The note also records the bounds
$(\frac16-o(1))n\le h(n)\le(2-\sqrt{5/2}+o(1))n$ for Erdős's general question,
which stays open.

**Submission note.** Posted to the site's forum by Quanyu Tang on 20 October
2025:

> Jie Ma and I have given a negative answer to this problem; see our note
> (here).
>
> We construct graphs with more than $n^{2}/4$ edges in which every triangle has
> at most $(2-\sqrt{5/2}+o(1))n$ vertices adjacent to at least two of its
> vertices.
>
> Hence this disproves the problem. A closer look at the original source of this
> problem (see also Section 3 of our note) shows that Erdős and Faudree also
> wrote:
>
> ''Perhaps this conjecture is a bit too optimistic, but if it is not true one
> should try to determine the largest $h(n)$ for which, in every $G(n;\lfloor
> n^2/4\rfloor+1)$, there is a triangle $(x_1,x_2,x_3)$ and $h(n)$ other
> vertices which are joined to at least two of the $x$'s.''
>
> Combining our construction with the classical result on the existence of a
> book of size $n/6$ in every graph with $\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor+1$ edges, we
> obtain\[ \bigl(\tfrac{1}{6}-o(1)\bigr)n \le h(n) \le (2-\sqrt{5/2}+o(1))n.
> \]EDIT: The note has been updated. We realized that our construction gives
> $2-\sqrt{5/2}\approx0.4189$ instead of $\sqrt{2}-1$, though a more careful
> calculation could show a slightly better constant.
>
> (The site has been updated to address this comment.)

Posted to the site's forum by Quanyu Tang on 27 October 2025:

> As the page notes, Erdős also wrote: "Perhaps if our $G$ has no $K_4$, i.e. no
> vertex is joined to all three of the $x$'s, the answer will be different."
>
> We now point out that even under the $K_4$-free assumption, the statement of
> this problem is still false. In fact we have the following result:
>
> **Theorem.** For all sufficiently large $n$ there exists a $K_4$-free graph
> $G$ on $n$ vertices with\[ e(G)>\frac{n^2}{4} \qquad\text{and}\qquad
> \max_{T} |Y(T)| \le (2\sqrt{3}-3+o(1))\,n, \]where the maximum is over all
> triangles $T$ in $G$ and\[ Y(T):=\{\,v\in V(G)\setminus V(T):\, |\{u\in
> V(T):\, uv\in E(G)\}|\ge 2\,\}. \]Proof (sketch). Let $a:=\lfloor
> n/\sqrt{3}\rfloor+2$ and $b:=n-a$. Partition $V(G)=A\sqcup B$ with $|A|=a$,
> $|B|=b$. Make all edges between $A$ and $B$ present, no edges inside $B$,
> and inside $A$ place a bipartite graph $H$ with a prescribed number $E$ of
> edges, where\[ E:=\min\{m\in\mathbb{N}:~ab+m>n^2/4\}\qquad(\text{hence }E\le
> n^2/4-ab+2). \]Write $s:=\lfloor a/2\rfloor$ and express $E=rs+t$ with $r\ge
> 0$ and $0\le t<s$. Split $A=A_1\sqcup A_2$ with $|A_1|=s$, $|A_2|=a-s$, and
> decompose $K_{A_1,A_2}$ into $s'$ disjoint matchings $M_0,\dots,M_{s'-1}$ (a
> standard 1-factorization), where $s'\in\{s,s+1\}$. Let $H$ be the union of
> $r$ full matchings plus $t$ edges of the next one. Then\[ e(H)=E
> \quad\text{and}\quad \Delta(H)\le r+1\le \frac{E}{s}+1. \]By construction
> $G[B]$ is independent and $G[A]=H$ is bipartite, so $G$ is $K_4$-free, while
> $e(G)=ab+e(H)>n^2/4$. Every triangle in $G$ is of the form $\{u,v,w\}$ with
> $uv\in E(H)$ and $w\in B$. For such $T$,\[ |Y(T)|=
> (b-1)+\big(d_H(u)-1\big)+\big(d_H(v)-1\big) = b+d_H(u)+d_H(v)-3 \le
> b+2\Delta(H)-3. \]Hence\[ \max_T |Y(T)| \le b + \frac{2E}{s}+O(1) \le b +
> \frac{n^2}{a-2} - \frac{4ab}{a-2}+O(1) = \frac{n^2}{a-2} - 3(n-a) + O(1),
> \]where we used $s\ge (a-2)/2$ and $E\le n^2/4-ab+2$. The right-hand side,
> viewed as a function of $c:=a-2$, is minimized at $c=n/\sqrt{3}$ and equals
> $(2\sqrt{3}-3)n+o(n)$. Since $a=\lfloor n/\sqrt{3}\rfloor+2$, this yields
> the claimed bound.
>
> (The site has been updated to address this comment.)

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the construction and its count are
self-contained, and the problem page's deduction of the lower bound on
$h(n)$ from Khadzhiivanov's book theorem is not consumed by this claim.

**Formalization.** The file `src/v4.29.1/ErdosProblems/Erdos1034.lean` of
Boris Alexeev's repository plby/lean-proofs (Lean v4.29.1 with Mathlib
v4.29.1, importing `Mathlib`; 1,575 lines at the pinned commit of
2026-09-15, linked above), announced in the site's forum on 4 December
2025, declares itself a formalization of this solution: its header names
Jie Ma, Quanyu Tang and ChatGPT as informal authors and the automated
prover Aristotle, Namrata Anand and Alexeev as formal authors. It defines
the graph `MaTangGraph n α s` of the note with `alpha_star = 1 - 1/√10`,
proves `MaTang_main`, that for every $\varepsilon>0$ and all large $n$ the
graph has more than $n^2/4$ edges and every triangle has at most
$(2-\sqrt{5/2}+\varepsilon)n$ vertices with two neighbors in it, and then
proves `not_erdos_1034`, the negation of its own `erdos_1034`: for every
$\varepsilon>0$ and all large $n$, every graph on $n$ vertices with more
than $n^2/4$ edges has a triangle with more than $(\frac12-\varepsilon)n$
vertices joined to two of its vertices. That `erdos_1034` is the
collection's statement of the problem with its $Y$ taken as the full set of
vertices with two neighbors in $T$, an equivalent form (the problem page
transcribes both). Comments after `#print axioms MaTang_main` and
`#print axioms not_erdos_1034` report the axioms `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, and the file contains no `sorry`,
`axiom`, `native_decide` or `unsafe`. The repository's note
`ErdosProblems/Erdos1034.md` (the `record` link) lists copies for five
toolchains (Lean v4.24.0 to v4.33.0). Nothing was built, replayed or
audited here, and the fidelity of its statement to
the question was not independently reviewed by this project, so the page
lists no `formalized` evidence.
