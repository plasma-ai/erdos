***

**Claim.** The answer to
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1036/_index|Problem 1036]] is yes: for every
$c>0$ there is $\delta>0$ such that, for all sufficiently large $n$, every graph
$G$ on $n$ vertices with no complete and no empty induced subgraph on more than
$c\log n$ vertices has at least $2^{\delta n}$ pairwise non-isomorphic induced
subgraphs. The claimed result is Theorem 1.3 of S. Shelah, *Erdős and Rényi
conjecture*, J. Combin. Theory Ser. A 82 (1998), no. 2, 179--185, DOI
10.1006/jcta.1997.2845 (Crossref record accessed; issued May 1998,
whose nominal first day the paper link carries): for every $c_1>0$ there is
$c_2>0$ such that, for $n$ large enough, a graph $G$ on $n$ vertices with
neither a complete subgraph nor an edgeless subgraph on at least $c_1\log n$
vertices has $I(G)\ge2^{c_2n}$, where $I(G)$ is the number of induced subgraphs
of $G$ up to isomorphism (Definition 1.2) and $\log$ is the base-two logarithm
(Notation 1.1), a convention that changes $c_1$ by a constant factor only. The
theorem's printed hypothesis reads "a graph with $n$ edges [sic]" in the arXiv
version
([[../library/extremal_graph_theory/shelah_1998_erdos_renyi_conjecture/_index|card]]);
the abstract, the conjecture as stated in the introduction and the proof concern
a graph with $n$ vertices, and the claim recorded here is the vertex form. The
site's "more than $c\log n$" and the theorem's "at least $c_1\log n$" differ by
the choice of constant. The paper names the statement as a conjecture of Erdős
and Rényi and records the earlier bounds: Alon and Hajnal's
$I(G)\ge2^{n/2t^{20\log(2t)}}$ with $t$ the largest trivial subgraph, which for
$t\le c\log n$ gives $\exp(n(\log n)^{-O(\log\log n)})$ (the introduction prints
$t\ge c\log n$, a slip: the bound weakens as $t$ grows)
([[../library/extremal_graph_theory/alon_1991_ramsey_graphs_contain_many_distinct_induced/_index|card]]),
and the parallel theorem with the bipartite Ramsey function in place of the
trivial-subgraph size, which the introduction credits to Erdős and Rényi; it is
Theorem 2 of Erdős and Hajnal [ErHa89b]
([[../library/extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1989_number_distinct_induced_subgraphs_graph/_index|card]]),
as the site and Erdős's 1993 survey
([[../library/extremal_graph_theory/erdos_1993_my_favorite_solved_unsolved_problems_graph_theory/_index|card]],
Chapter V, problem 14) credit it: for $c>0$ and $k>2c\log2$, if neither $G$ nor
its complement contains $K_{c\log n,c\log n}$, then $G$ has at least $2^{n/4k}$
pairwise non-isomorphic induced subgraphs for all large $n$; it has its own
partial claim page,
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1036/claims/1989_05_01_erdos_hajnal|Erdős and Hajnal]].
Remark 1.4 blows up each vertex of a Ramsey graph into $m$ vertices, giving
$I(G)\le2^{n\log_2(m+1)}$, and conjectures that this is the worst case.

**Formalization.** The file `src/v4.29.1/ErdosProblems/Erdos1036.lean` of
Boris Alexeev's repository plby/lean-proofs (Lean v4.29.1 with Mathlib
v4.29.1; 3,916 lines at the pinned commit of 2026-09-15, linked above),
announced in the site's forum on 21 January 2026, declares itself a
formalization of this result: its header names Shelah as the informal
author and the automated prover Aristotle and Alexeev as formal authors,
and its opening comment says that it formalizes the main theorem of the
paper. Its final theorem `erdos_1036` states that for every real $c>0$
there are $\varepsilon>0$ and $n_0$ such that every finite simple graph on
$n\ge n_0$ vertices whose clique number and independence number are both at
most $c\log_2 n$ (`hom_num`) has at least $2^{\varepsilon n}$ vertex subsets
up to isomorphism of the induced graphs (`I_num`), the statement of the
problem with the base of the logarithm rescaling $c$; a comment after
`#print axioms erdos_1036` reports the axioms `propext`, `choice` and
`Quot.sound`, and the file contains no `sorry`, `axiom`, `native_decide` or
`unsafe`. The repository's note `ErdosProblems/Erdos1036.md` (the
`record` link) lists copies for five toolchains (Lean v4.24.0 to v4.33.0).
Nothing was built, replayed or audited here, and the fidelity of its
statement to the question was not independently
reviewed by this project, so the page lists no `formalized` evidence.
