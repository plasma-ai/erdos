***

**Claim.** S. Wagon, *A bound on the chromatic number of graphs without certain
induced subgraphs*, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 29 (1980), no. 3, 345--346, proves
the
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/wagon_1980_bound_chromatic_number_graphs_without_certain_induced_subgraphs/theorem_p345|Theorem]]
(p. 345): "If the graph $G$ does not contain the complement of a chordless
4-cycle as an induced subgraph, then $\chi(G)\le\binom{\omega(G)+1}2$." The
excluded graph is $K_2\cup K_2$. Two anticomplete sets of chromatic number at
least $2$ each contain an edge, and the two edges induce $K_2\cup K_2$; so a
graph with $\omega(G)<t$ and no such sets has
$\chi(G)\le\binom{\omega(G)+1}2\le\binom t2$. Hence $d(t,2)\le\binom t2+1$ for
[[problems/extremal_graph_theory/E1111/_index|Problem 1111]], as El-Zahar and
Erdős note (Combinatorica 5 (1985), p. 296) and the site's commentary credits.

**Covers.** The case $c=2$ of the statement, for every $t$.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki: the one-line deduction of
$d(t,2)\le\binom t2+1$ from the Theorem is written above, as on the problem
page.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 29 (1980), no. 3, 345--346
(Crossref: December 1980; the day is the issue's nominal first day, used for
this page's date). The site labels the problem OPEN, so its commentary crediting
the result is not review, and no `reviewed` evidence is listed.
