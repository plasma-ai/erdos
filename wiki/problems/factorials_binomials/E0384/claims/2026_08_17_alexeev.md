***

The statement as the site displays it asks for a prime divisor $p<n/2$ of
$\binom nk$ whenever $1<k<n-1$, apart from $\binom73$. It is false: $\binom42=6$
has the prime divisors $2$ and $3$, and no prime is below $4/2=2$. The Lean
file `Erdos384.lean` in Boris Alexeev's repository of Lean proofs (first
committed 2026-08-17; the link pins the 2026-09-15 revision) states this strict
formulation as `Erdos384StrictStatement`, with $2p<n$ for $p<n/2$ and the
exception at both $(7,3)$ and $(7,4)$, and proves its negation `not_erdos_384`
from that witness. The file's header names Ecklund as the informal author and
the AI systems Codex and GPT-5.6 Sol as its formal authors, and says that the
non-strict bound $p\leq n/2$ of Ecklund's theorem is the one that holds; the
submitter of the repository is the claimant here. The same observation appears
on this corpus's problem page with the witness $\binom62=15$, whose prime
divisors are $3=n/2$ and $5$, and a comment of 2026-02-04 in the site's
discussion thread had already pointed out that Ecklund's bound is $p\leq n/2$
while the page prints $p<n/2$.

The refutation rests on nothing but the arithmetic of the witness. Ecklund's
theorem, on the page
[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0384/claims/1969_05_01_ecklund|Ecklund 1969]],
proves the corrected Statement and plays no part in it.

**Why it is rejected.** It answers the site's wording, not the corrected
statement. [[problems/factorials_binomials/E0384/_index|Problem 384]] judges
the corrected Statement, with the bound $p\leq n/2$ that Erdős and Graham's
own report of the problem requires and that Ecklund's theorem proves; the
problem page's Notes give the evidence. The witness $\binom42=6$ has the prime
divisor $2=n/2$, so it is no counterexample to the corrected Statement, whose
only exception is $\binom73=\binom74=35$, and the refutation settles no
instance of it. The record is kept because the formal-conjectures record names
the file as the formal proof of its strict variant, and the site's Lean
qualification dates from the file's last change; the problem page's Notes
credit the result.

**Standing.** The site's curator labels the problem proved and credits
Ecklund's theorem, so no outside reviewer has accepted a refutation of the
problem; and the Lean file is third-party work that this corpus has not built
or audited, so it gives a formalization link and no `formalized` evidence. The
formal-conjectures record at the linked commit tags the variant
`erdos_384.variants.strict` as `answer(False)` and names this file as its
formal proof; the file's last change, on 24 August 2026, is the date from
which the community database records the formal status Lean shown in the
site's label.
