***

**Claim.** The answer to
[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0398/_index|Problem 398]] is yes: $n=4,5,7$ are
the only solutions of $n!=x^2-1$. Ahmad Sabihi's manuscript *A short solution
for Brocard-Ramanujans' problem (Erdős' conjecture) and its generalization*,
posted on ResearchGate as publication 313677604 and linked above, claims an
elementary proof of this statement and of a generalization. The manuscript was
posted on ResearchGate on 14 February 2017, the upload date its file identifier
encodes, and the page is dated by that posting; it was brought to the site's
discussion thread on 19 October 2025 with a request for a professional reading.
It was not submitted to the site's proof-claims tab, and the site's problem page
does not mention it.

**Standing.** Rejected. Thomas Bloom, the site's curator, replied in the
thread the same day that the manuscript has not appeared in a peer-reviewed
journal, that its methods are elementary case analysis, and that a proof of
this problem without deeper machinery is unlikely, without locating an error.
On 17 November 2025 Stijn Cambie wrote of having asked the author, who had
not submitted the manuscript anywhere and knew of no mistake, and then
reported two errors: equation (4.28) has many solutions for the quantity $c$
where the argument needs one, so the appendix does not do what the argument
asks of it; and at the top of p. 14 the step fails when the quantity $A$ is
itself prime, with $n=32$, $A=17$ as an explicit example, because the
inequality $p^2\le n!$ invoked there has no bearing on the divisibility in
question. No reply by the author is in the thread, no refereed publication or
independent review exists, and the site's label is unchanged. The claim is
recorded as rejected on the error exhibited in the thread; the manuscript is
not held in the library, and this record rests on the thread.
