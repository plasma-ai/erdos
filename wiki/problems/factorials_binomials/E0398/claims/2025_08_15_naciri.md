***

**Claim.** A. M. Naciri, *On the Brocard–Ramanujan equation with 7-free
integers and prime powers*, Integers 25 (2025), #A71, Theorem 1: (i) for every
$k\geq2$ the equation $n!+1=x^2$ has only finitely many solutions $(n,x)$ with
$x\pm1$ a $k$-free number, and the only possible solutions with $x\pm1$
7-free are $(4,5)$, $(5,11)$ and $(7,71)$; (ii) for every $l\geq2$ it has only
finitely many solutions with $x\pm1$ having fewer than $l$ prime divisors, and
the only possible solution with $x\pm1$ a prime power is $(4,5)$. The proof
treats $x-1$ and $x+1$ alike, so in each case one of the two cofactors carries
the condition. It bounds a divisor of $n!$ through Chebyshev's bounds for
$\pi(n)$ and Legendre's formula, compares with $n!\geq(n/e)^n$, and finishes
with Berndt and Galway's search, which found no further solution with
$n\leq10^9$.

**Covers.** The problem's assertion for two families of solutions of
$n!+1=x^2$: the solutions with $x+1$ or $x-1$ 7-free are exactly $(4,5)$,
$(5,11)$ and $(7,71)$, and the only solution with $x+1$ or $x-1$ a prime power
is $(4,5)$. Not covered: every other solution. The finiteness statements for
$k$-free cofactors and for cofactors with fewer than $l$ prime divisors settle
no instance. Both cases rest on Berndt and Galway's search to $n\leq10^9$. For
prime powers the paper's bound $n\leq4e^2\cdot9^8$ is about $1.27\times10^9$,
not below $10^9$ as printed; the same argument with $\omega(x\pm1)=1$ in place
of $l=2$ gives $n\leq4e^2\cdot5^4<2\cdot10^4$, so the statement stands.
