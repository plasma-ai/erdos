***

**Claim.** Somnath Maiti, *Results of Brocard-Ramanujan problem on diophantine
equation $n!+1=m^2$* (arXiv:2004.09256), states as Theorem 2.15 of its second
version (5 February 2026) that the equation has no solution beyond $n=4,5,7$,
the answer yes to
[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0398/_index|Problem 398]]. Its introduction
and Conclusion 2.16 say that the study claims to establish this. It also
states as Theorem 2.5 that the equation has only finitely many solutions,
unconditionally. The first version (9 April 2020) claimed only the finiteness
and said that the stronger statement had not been established, so the page is
dated by the second version, the first posting of the full claim. The
manuscript writes $\sqrt{n!}=k+\epsilon$ with $0<\epsilon<1$, notes that a
solution needs $n!=k(k+2)$, and bounds from below the value of $\epsilon$ a new
solution with $n\geq10^5$ would need.

**Standing.** Claimed. The manuscript is unrefereed, was not posted to the
site, and no review of it is known. The proof of Theorem 2.15 compares the
growth of $k=\lfloor\sqrt{n!}\rfloor$ with that of $\epsilon=\sqrt{n!}-k$ and
asserts, without an estimate, that $\epsilon$ cannot reach the value a
solution needs.
