# problems/factorials_binomials/E0399/claims

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0399/_index|..]]

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0399/claims/1937_01_01_erdos_oblath|1937_01_01_erdos_oblath]]: Erdős and Obláth (Acta Szeged 8 (1937)) prove that n! = x^k ± y^k has no
solution with x, y coprime and xy > 1 when k > 2 is not 4, and none with
k = 4 for sufficiently large n; refereed.

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0399/claims/1973_05_01_pollack_shapiro|1973_05_01_pollack_shapiro]]: Pollack and Shapiro (Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 26 (1973)) are credited with
showing that n! = x^4 - 1 has no solution; the monograph's wider report,
the whole coprime case k = 4, is unconfirmed; refereed.

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0399/claims/2025_04_07_barfield|2025_04_07_barfield]]: 10! equals 48^4 minus 36^4, a solution of n! = x^k - y^k with xy > 1 and
k = 4, so the equation does have solutions; the bases share the factor 12,
outside the coprime case Erdős and Obláth had settled.

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0399/claims/2025_09_09_cambie|2025_09_09_cambie]]: Cambie's remark, credited in the site's commentary, that n! = x^4 + y^4 has
no solution with x, y coprime and xy > 1, by a congruence modulo 8.
