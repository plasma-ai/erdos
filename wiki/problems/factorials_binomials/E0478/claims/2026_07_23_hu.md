**Claim.** Xiyu Hu's manuscript "A Super-Square-Root Lower Bound for
Factorial Residues Modulo a Prime" (in the author's GitHub repository,
published 2026-07-23; the link pins the revision of the same day) proves
that, for $A_p=\{k!\bmod p:1\le k<p\}$ as in
[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0478/_index|Problem 478]],

$$
|A_p|\gg p^{8/15}.
$$

The elementary bound is $|A_p|\gg p^{1/2}$, since every nonzero residue is a
quotient of two factorials, and the best published constant is
$(\sqrt2-o(1))p^{1/2}$ ([GSSV24], carded at
[[../library/factorials_binomials/grebennikov_2024_sequence/_index|Grebennikov, Sagdeev, Semchankau and Vasilevskii 2024]]).
The argument uses three consecutive
factorials: in $\mathbb F_p$ one has $(n+2)!=(n+1)!+((n+1)!)^2/n!$, so with
$T_a(x)=a+a^2/x$ the factorial sequence supplies at least $p-2$ transitions
$T_a(x)=y$ with $a$, $x$ and $y$ in $A_p$. After Cauchy–Schwarz the
compositions $T_b\circ T_a^{-1}$ are affine maps; a multiplicity-two count
bounds the repeated lines, and the point-line incidence bound of Stevens and
de Zeeuw for Cartesian products (Bull. Lond. Math. Soc. 49 (2017);
arXiv:1609.06284) gives the exponent $8/15$.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Xiyu Hu
(account hxypqr) on 23 July 2026, giving "GPT-5.6 Sol" as the AI used:

> For a prime \(p\), let\[ A_p=\{k!\pmod p:1\leq k<p\}. \]I prove the lower
> bound\[ |A_p|\gg p^{8/15}. \]Thus the exponent goes strictly beyond the
> elementary square-root bound. This does not resolve the original Erdős
> problem: in particular, it does not prove positive density or the conjectured
> asymptotic\[ |A_p|\sim(1-1/e)p. \]The main idea is to use three consecutive
> factorials and the identity\[ (n+2)!=(n+1)!+\frac{((n+1)!)^2}{n!} \]in
> \(\mathbb F_p\). Defining\[ T_a(x)=a+\frac{a^2}{x}, \]the factorial sequence
> supplies at least \(p-2\) transitions inside \(A_p\). After applying
> Cauchy--Schwarz, the compositions \(T_b\circ T_a^{-1}\) become affine lines.
> Then a multiplicity-two calculation, together with a incidence geometry
> result, the Cartesian-product point-line incidence theorem of Stevens and de
> Zeeuw, then gives the exponent \(8/15\).

**Covers.** The lower bound $|A_p|\gg p^{8/15}$. It does not prove that
$|A_p|\gg p$, nor the asymptotic $|A_p|\sim(1-1/e)p$ the problem asks for; the
manuscript, its repository and the claim's summary all say so.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Formalization.** The repository's `lean/` folder is a Lean 4 development
(Lean 4.29.0 with the matching Mathlib) whose theorem
`factorialResidues_cleared_eight_fifteenths` proves
$(p-2)^8\le(K+D^4)\,|A_p|^{15}$ from an explicit hypothesis named
`StevensDeZeeuwWeightedCorollary`, the published incidence theorem left
unformalized; the repository calls this a partial formalization, reports no
`sorry` and no project axiom, and documents under `docs/` which steps are
checked and how the incidence theorem's hypotheses are met in the
application. This corpus has not built the development, so the link is a
`formalization` link and gives no `formalized` evidence.

**Authorship and tools.** Xiyu Hu is the manuscript's sole author and posted
the claim. The repository's disclosure says that OpenAI's ChatGPT and Codex
assisted with proof exploration, algebraic and literature checks, exposition,
LaTeX preparation and the Lean development and are not authors; the forum
entry names the system as GPT-5.6 Sol.
