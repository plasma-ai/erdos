***

**Claim.** The Sylvester-Schur theorem in the binomial form Erdős proves in
[Er34], printed p. 283: "If $n\ge2k$, then $\binom nk$ contains a prime
divisor greater than $k$." In the notation of
[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0683/_index|Problem 683]] this is
$P(\binom nk)>k$ for $k\le n/2$. The paper opens with the theorem in its
interval form, that for $n>k$ the block $n,n+1,\ldots,n+k-1$ contains an
integer with a prime divisor greater than $k$, and says that Sylvester first
proved it and that Schur rediscovered and reproved it; Erdős's own proof is
elementary and avoids Chebyshev's theorem. The statement is the library
result page
[[../library/factorials_binomials/erdos_1934_theorem_sylvester_schur/theorem|erdos_1934_theorem_sylvester_schur / theorem]].

**Covers.** Since $\binom nk=\binom n{n-k}$, the theorem applied to $n-k$ gives
$P(\binom nk)>n-k$, that is $P(\binom nk)\ge n-k+1$, whenever $n-k\le n/2$. So
for $n/2\le k\le n-1$ the inequality of the problem, read as its Formulation
states, holds for every $c>0$. For $k<n/2$ the theorem gives only
$P(\binom nk)>k$, which is weaker than the bound asked, and it says nothing
about a power of $k$; those instances stay open.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki; the result rests on the cited paper,
whose statement the library result page above records.
