***

**Claim.** The answer to [[problems/factorials_binomials/E0698/_index|Problem 698]]
is yes. Bergman proves (Theorem 2 of the paper and its equations (7) and (8);
the library card is
[[../library/factorials_binomials/bergman_2011_common_divisors_multinomial_coefficients/_index|Bergman 2011]])
that for $2\leq i\leq j\leq n/2$

$$
\gcd\left(\binom{n}{i},\binom{n}{j}\right)
\geq\frac{(n-i+2)(n-i+1)}{i(i-1)}
\left(\frac{2^{2i-3}(i-1)}{n^3}\right)^{1/2}
\geq\frac{n^{1/2}\,2^{i-7/2}}{i\,(i-1)^{1/2}} .
$$

Bergman notes that (8) weakens to a bound tending to infinity with $n$ that
is independent of $i$, which answers the question; the explicit constant is
this page's own elementary consequence of (8), not a statement of the paper:
the factor $2^{i-7/2}/(i(i-1)^{1/2})$ is at least $1/6$, its value at $i=3$,
for every $i\geq 2$, so $\gcd(\binom ni,\binom nj)\geq n^{1/2}/6$ whenever
$2\leq i<j\leq n/2$, and $h(n)=n^{1/2}/6$ works. The proof lets the symmetric
group act on pairs of two-block decompositions of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ with block
sizes $i$ and $j$; the orbit sizes $Q_h$ are all divisible by the least common
multiple $L$ of the two coefficients, the combination $(i-1)Q_1^2-2iQ_0Q_2$ is
divisible by $L^2$ and small after its leading terms cancel, and the resulting
bound on $L$ becomes a bound on the gcd through
$\gcd\cdot L=\binom ni\binom nj$. The
question comes from Erdős and Szekeres
([[../library/factorials_binomials/erdos_1978_number_theoretic_problems_binomial_coefficients/_index|Erdős and Szekeres 1978]]),
whose identity $\binom nj\binom ji=\binom ni\binom{n-i}{j-i}$ gives
$\gcd(\binom ni,\binom nj)\geq\binom ni/\binom ji\geq 2^i$, a bound that grows
with $i$ but not with $n$ and is attained for $i=1$, $j=p$ and $n=2p$ with $p$
prime; they asked for growth in $n$ uniform over $2\leq i<j\leq n/2$, which
Bergman's bound supplies.

**A sharper constant.** In the site's discussion thread on 2026-01-16, Wouter
van Doorn posted a rewritten proof along Bergman's lines giving
$\gcd(\binom ni,\binom nj)>2^i\sqrt n/(4i\sqrt{i-1})$ for $2\leq i<j\leq n/2$,
which improves Bergman's constant, together with the Lean formalization below.
The post is a thread comment on Bergman's result, so it is disclosed here and
has no page of its own.

**Formalization.** The Lean file `ErdosProblem698.lean` in van Doorn's
repository names Bergman as the author of the original proof, van Doorn as the
author of the rewritten proof and the AI system Aristotle (Harmonic) as the
formalizer; its theorem `binomial_gcd_lower_bound` states van Doorn's
inequality, which implies the problem's statement with $h(n)=\sqrt n/4$. The
thread post of 2026-01-16 links the file, which the repository's history dates
