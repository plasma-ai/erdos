**Claim.** For the triples $1\le i<j\le n/2$ of
[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0699/_index|Problem 699]], some prime
$p\ge i$ divides both $\binom ni$ and $\binom nj$ whenever $j\le 3i/2$, and
whenever $n=2j$. The write-up is the Overleaf document linked above,
submitted to the site's proof-claims tab on 2026-07-18; it comes with
exact-integer code for the finite checks the argument needs.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Liam Price
(account Leeham) on 18 July 2026, giving "GPT 5.6 Sol Pro" as the AI used, which
the site marks as accepted as correct:

> GPT-5.6 Sol Pro proves the conjecture when $j\le 3i/2$ or $n=2j$, including
> reproducible exact-integer code for the finite verification.

**Argument.** The curator's reply on the claim lays out the first case. Let
$v$ be the part of $\binom ni$ supported on the primes $p\ge i$. A
counterexample makes $v$ coprime to $\binom nj$. From the identity

$$
\binom ni\binom{n-i}{j-i}=\binom nj\binom ji,
$$

a divisor of $\binom ni$ coprime to $\binom nj$ divides $\binom ji$, so
$v\mid\binom ji$, and Vandermonde's identity gives
$\binom ji^2<\binom{2j}{2(j-i)}$. The theorem of Ecklund, Eggleton, Erdős and
Selfridge that $v>\binom ni^{1/2}$ outside finitely many explicit exceptions
then forces $\binom ni<\binom{2j}{2(j-i)}$ at any counterexample. When
$j\le3i/2$, $2(j-i)\le i<j\le n/2$ gives
$\binom{2j}{2(j-i)}\le\binom{2j}i\le\binom ni$, a contradiction. The central
case $n=2j$ needs a longer elementary argument. The same inequality shows that
for fixed $i<j$ only finitely many $n$ can be counterexamples, which the site
states as an easy fact. Proposition 2.4 of van Doorn and Rocca's manuscript
on the
[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0699/claims/2026_07_25_van_doorn_rocca|van Doorn–Rocca claim page]]
proves the case $j\le3i/2$ from the same two inputs.

**Covers.** Every triple with $j\le 3i/2$ and every triple with $n=2j$. The
triples with $3i/2<j<n/2$ remain, and the problem's label is unchanged.
