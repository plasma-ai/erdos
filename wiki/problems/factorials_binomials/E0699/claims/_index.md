# problems/factorials_binomials/E0699/claims

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0699/_index|..]]

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0699/claims/2026_07_18_price|2026_07_18_price]]: A GPT 5.6 Sol Pro argument submitted by Liam Price proves that a common
prime at least i divides both binomial coefficients whenever j is at most
3i/2 or n = 2j, the two cases the site's curator credits on the problem page.

[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0699/claims/2026_07_25_van_doorn_rocca|2026_07_25_van_doorn_rocca]]: Two unpublished 2026 manuscripts by Wouter van Doorn and Stefano Rocca prove
that no counterexample has i = 1, 2 or i at least 1476, that each fixed i at
least 4 allows only finitely many, and leave i = 3 open.
