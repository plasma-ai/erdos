**Claim.** For $f(n)=\min_{1<k\le n/2}\gcd\!\left(n,\binom nk\right)$ as in
[[problems/factorials_binomials/E0700/_index|Problem 700]], there are
infinitely many composite $n$ with $f(n)>n^{1/2}$. The write-up, the Overleaf
document linked above, proves more: there are fixed even integers $0<a<c$
and infinitely many primes $p$ for which $p+a$ and $p+a+c$ are also prime,
and for $n=p(p+a)(p+a+c)$ with $p$ sufficiently large one has

$$
f(n)=p(p+a)=n^{2/3}(1+o(1)),
$$

so every sufficiently large member of this family is squarefree with three
prime factors and satisfies $f(n)>\sqrt n$. The identity is asserted only for
large $p$, and the write-up needs the order $a<c$ of the two shifts. The
write-up's Lemma 4 gets the prime triples from Maynard's theorem on primes in
admissible tuples, applied to the admissible set $\{2^i:1\le i\le K\}$ with
$K$ so large that infinitely many translates contain three primes; some triple
of positions $i<j<u$ then recurs infinitely often, and its gaps satisfy
$c=2^u-2^j\ge2^j>2^j-2^i=a$. Its Proposition 3 gives $f(pqr)=pq$ for primes
$p<q<r$ with $c>a$ and $p>2(a+c)^2$, by an elementary argument from Lucas's
theorem. For the large members with $f(n)=p(p+a)$, since $p+a+c$ is the
largest prime factor of $n$, the equality $f(n)=n/P(n)$ holds, the equality
the first question asks to characterize.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Liam Price
(account Leeham) on 27 July 2026, giving "GPT 5.6 Sol Pro" as the AI used, which
the site marks as accepted as correct:

> GPT 5.6 Sol Pro proves the stronger result that there exist fixed positive
> even integers $a<c$ and infinitely many primes $p$ such that $p+a$ and $p+a+c$
> are also prime. Writing\[ n=p(p+a)(p+a+c), \]one has\[
> f(n)=p(p+a)=n^{2/3}(1+o(1)). \]Thus $n$ is squarefree with exactly three
> distinct prime factors, and $f(n)>\sqrt{n}$ for all sufficiently large members
> of this family. This answers the second question in the affirmative.

**Covers.** The second question, answered yes: infinitely many composite $n$
have $f(n)>n^{1/2}$, with $f(n)\sim n^{2/3}$ along the family. The first
question (which composite $n$ have $f(n)=n/P(n)$) and the third (whether
$f(n)\ll_A n/(\log n)^A$ for every $A$) remain open, and the problem's label
is unchanged.
