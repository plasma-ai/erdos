***

**Claim.** Neil Hindman, in *On a conjecture of Erdős, Faber, and Lovász
about $n$-colorings*
([[../library/graph_coloring/hindman_1981_conjecture_erdos_faber_lovasz_n_colorings/_index|card]]),
works with the dual form of [[problems/graph_coloring/E0019/_index|Problem
19]]: a family of finite sets, any two meeting in at most one point, with $n$
points in all, should have a coloring by $n$ colors in which sets of the same
color are disjoint. Take the $n$ cliques of $G$ as the points and, for each
vertex lying in two or more cliques, the set of cliques containing it. A
coloring of this family colors those vertices properly, and each vertex lying
in a single clique then takes a color its clique leaves free. Call the sets of
size at least $3$ the large sets, and complete them by every pair of their
points that no large set contains. Corollary 3.3 shows that the family can be
colored when some isomorphic copy of this completed family has a split
coloring, the notion Section 3 introduces. A computer check found such a copy
for every family on at most $10$ points whose sets have at least two points
each and meet pairwise in at most one point. Hence the conjecture holds
whenever the large sets span at most $10$ points, and in particular for every
$n\le10$.

**Covers.** Every $n\le10$ and, for every $n$, each configuration in which at
most ten of the cliques contain a vertex lying in three or more cliques. The
site's remark and the variant `lt_ten` of the
[formal-conjectures statement file](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/fb211502aef38b4cd64666cd0379b8912f39358e/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/19.lean)
give the range as $n<10$; the paper's computation also covers $n=10$.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Canad. J. Math. **33** (1981), no. 3, 563–570
(received 25 September 1979, revised 14 November 1979). The site's label
decidable does not settle the problem, so the curator's mention is context and
not `reviewed` evidence.
