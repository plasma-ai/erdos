***

Jun Gao, Qingyi Huo and Jie Ma prove, in *A strengthening on odd cycles in
graphs of given chromatic number*
([[../library/graph_coloring/gao_2021_strengthening_odd_cycles_graphs_given_chromatic/_index|card]]),
that every graph of chromatic number $m+1\geq3$ contains cycles of
$\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ consecutive odd lengths. With $m+1=2k+3$ this says that a
graph of chromatic number at least $2k+3$ has cycles of $k+1$ consecutive odd
lengths, so a graph whose odd cycles have at most $k$ distinct lengths has
$\chi(G)\leq2k+2$: the inequality of
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0058/_index|Problem 58]], which Gyárfás had proved
with distinct rather than consecutive lengths. The paper also proves that for
$m\geq6$ a graph of chromatic number $m+1$ has $m$ cycles of consecutive
lengths unless some block is $K_{m+1}$ (Theorem 1.2), confirms a conjecture of
Verstraëte on $m-1$ consecutive cycle lengths (Theorem 1.3), and shows that
every $(m+1)$-critical non-complete graph with $m\geq6$ has cycles of all
lengths modulo $m$ (Theorem 1.4). The proofs split on whether the graph
contains a triangle.

**Covers.** The inequality $\chi(G)\leq2k+2$ for a graph whose odd cycles have
at most $k$ distinct lengths, in the stronger form that chromatic number at
least $2k+3$ forces $k+1$ consecutive odd cycle lengths. It does not cover the
equality case of Problem 58, that $\chi(G)=2k+2$ only when $G$ contains
$K_{2k+2}$, which rests on Gyárfás's theorem. The consecutive-odd-lengths
statement comes from Theorem 1.3, which the arXiv version proves in the
paper for every $m\geq5$ while placing the cases $m=3$ and $m=4$ in a
separate note posted as an arXiv ancillary file; the published abstract
limits the Verstraëte and Moore–West corollaries to $m\geq6$. The case of
chromatic number $5$, which is $k=1$ here and $m=4$ in the paper, therefore
rests on that appendix and not on the published paper; Gyárfás's theorem
covers $k=1$ in any case.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: SIAM J. Discrete Math. **35** (2021), no. 4,
2317–2327, after its first posting as arXiv:2012.10624 on 2020-12-19. The
site's curator, Thomas Bloom, mentions this stronger form in the problem's
commentary, but the label proved credits Gyárfás, so the mention is context
and not `reviewed` evidence. The library card summarizes the paper's
statements and does not verify its proofs; it is not acceptance evidence.
