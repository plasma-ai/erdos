***

Rödl's paper *Nearly bipartite graphs with large chromatic number*
(Combinatorica **2** (1982), no. 4, 377–383) is credited by the site's
curator with a positive answer to
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0074/_index|Problem 74]] for the linear budgets:
for every fixed $\epsilon>0$ there is a graph of chromatic number $\aleph_0$
every finite subgraph of which on $n$ vertices can be made bipartite by
deleting at most $\epsilon n$ edges. The site also credits the paper with the
analogous assertion for hypergraphs, which is a variant of the question and
not part of this claim.

**Covers.** The instances $f(n)=\epsilon n$ for each fixed $\epsilon>0$: for
every such $f$ there is a graph of infinite chromatic number with the
required property. The budgets of slower growth, including $f(n)=\sqrt n$,
are not covered.

**Standing.** The statement recorded here is the site's attribution, which
the paper's title, its publisher record (Crossref gives the venue, volume,
issue 4, pages and the issue month December 1982; zbMATH gives the venue,
volume, pages and year; neither has an abstract) and its subject classes
(graph coloring and hypergraphs) are consistent with but do not state; the
paper is not held, and the statement is recorded from the site's attribution
rather than from the paper's text, so the page lists no `refereed` evidence.
The site labels the problem disproved and does not label this part, so the
curator's credit is not `reviewed` evidence. The page is dated to the first
day of the issue month.
