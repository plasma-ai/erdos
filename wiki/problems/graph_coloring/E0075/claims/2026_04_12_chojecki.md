Theorem 1 of the note *A note on the $n^{1-\epsilon}$ version of an Erdős
problem* (ulam.ai, 3 pages, dated 12 April 2026) states that the Specker graph
$S=G_1(\omega_1,3)$ has $|V(S)|=\chi(S)=\aleph_1$ and that there is a constant
$C>0$ such that every finite subgraph $H\subseteq S$ on $n\ge2$ vertices has
$\alpha(H)\ge n/(C\log_2 n)$; consequently, for every $\epsilon>0$ and all
sufficiently large $n$, every subgraph of $S$ on $n$ vertices has an
independent set of size greater than $n^{1-\epsilon}$. This answers the first
question of [[problems/graph_coloring/E0075/_index|Problem 75]] yes. The route
is short: Lemma 1 identifies the finite Specker graph $G_1(r,3)$ with the
type-graph $G(r,112122)$; Lemma 2 checks that the type $112122$ is irreducible
with block decomposition $11\,|\,212\,|\,2$, so it has three blocks; Theorem
1.7 of Avart, Kay, Reiher and Rödl, *The chromatic number of finite
type-graphs*, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B **122** (2017), 877–896, then gives
$\chi(G(r,112122))=\Theta(\log_2 r)$, so a finite subgraph on $n$ vertices,
which lives inside $G_1(r,3)$ with $r\le3n$, has chromatic number at most
$C\log_2 n$ and a color class of the required size; and Lemma 1.1(b) of
[EHS82] gives $\chi(G_1(\omega_1,3))=\omega_1$. The source card is
[[../library/graph_coloring/chojecki_2026_note_n_1_epsilon_version_erdos/_index|chojecki_2026_note_n_1_epsilon_version_erdos]].

**Claimant and postings.** The claimant is Przemek Chojecki, who posted the
note in the site's discussion thread on 12 April 2026, saying that they obtained
the proof with GPT-5.4 Pro and that the classical Specker graph already gives
the positive answer; the note prints no author line.

**Covers.** The first question: a graph of chromatic number $\aleph_1$ on
$\aleph_1$ vertices whose $n$-vertex subgraphs have independent sets larger
than $n^{1-\epsilon}$, for each $\epsilon>0$ and all large $n$. The second
question, an independent set of size $\gg n$, is not covered; the note's
Remark 1 recalls Theorem 2 of [EHS82], that the least independence number over
$m$-vertex subsets of the countable Specker graph $G_1(\omega,3)$ is
$O(m\log\log m/\log m)$, so no Specker graph gives a linear bound.

**Acceptance.** None: the note is unrefereed. In the same thread a commenter
reported a check run with GPT-5.4 Thinking that found one minor issue and
noted that the bound proved is the stronger $\alpha(H)\gg n/\log n$. Thomas
Bloom, the site's curator, replied that the construction was already studied
in [EHS82], perhaps without the lower bound being published, and that Erdős
may have forgotten this when posing the 1995 version of the question. That
reply is commentary on a problem the site labels OPEN (page last edited 29
January 2026), so it is not acceptance, and the problem's standing stays open.
