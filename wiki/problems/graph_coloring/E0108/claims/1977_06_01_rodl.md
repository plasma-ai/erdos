***

Rödl [Ro77] proves that for every $k$ there is $f(k)$ such that every graph
of chromatic number at least $f(k)$ contains a triangle-free subgraph of
chromatic number at least $k$. A triangle-free graph has girth at least $4$,
so $f(k,4)=f(k)$ is finite for every $k\ge2$: the case $r=4$ of
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0108/_index|Problem 108]] holds. The statement is
the whole of [[problems/graph_coloring/E0923/_index|Problem 923]], whose
accepted claim page records the theorem, its printed stronger form and a
public Lean formalization of it; the site's commentary on Problem 108 credits
Rödl with the $r=4$ case and points to Problem 923. Steiner's preprint of
August 2026 proves the same case with a single-exponential bound in place of
Rödl's tower
([[problems/graph_coloring/E0108/claims/2026_08_03_steiner|its claim page]]).

**Covers.** The case $r=4$ for every $k\ge2$. The cases $r\ge5$ are not
covered: for $k\ge7$ they fail by the accepted counterexample claim, for
$4\le k\le6$ by the pending Nguyen–Walczak claim, and for $k\in\{2,3\}$
they hold for the elementary reasons recorded on the problem page.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0923/claims/1977_06_01_rodl|Rödl's triangle-free subgraphs of large chromatic number]].

**Acceptance.** Refereed: V. Rödl, On the chromatic number of subgraphs of a
given graph, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 64 (1977), no. 2, 370--371, DOI
10.1090/S0002-9939-1977-0469806-4; the Crossref record gives the publication
month June 1977 and no day, so the page is dated to the first of that month.
The site labels Problem 108 OPEN and lists no parts, so the curator's credit
for the $r=4$ case is not `reviewed` evidence here; the curator's acceptance
of the same theorem as the solution of Problem 923 is recorded on that
problem's claim page.
