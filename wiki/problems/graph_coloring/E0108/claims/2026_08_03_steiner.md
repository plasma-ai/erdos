***

**Claim.** Raphael Steiner's preprint *Locally bipartite subgraphs via
multicolor Ramsey numbers* (arXiv:2608.02522) proves, as its Theorem 1.3, that
for every fixed odd $g\ge5$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, for all sufficiently
large $k$, every graph $G$ with
$\chi(G)\ge\exp^{((g-3)/2)}(k^{3+\varepsilon})$, where $\exp^{(i)}$ is the
$i$-fold iterated exponential, contains a subgraph of chromatic number at least
$k$ and odd girth at least $g$; in particular every graph with chromatic number
at least $e^{k^{3+\varepsilon}}$ contains a triangle-free subgraph of chromatic
number at least $k$. A triangle-free graph has girth at least $4$, so in the
indexing of [[problems/graph_coloring/E0108/_index|Problem 108]] the function
$f(k,4)$ is at most $e^{k^{3+o(1)}}$ for all large $k$, where Rödl's proof gives
a tower of height $\Theta(k^2\log k)$. The paper writes $f_g(k)$ for the
problem's $f(k,g)$ and $h_g(k)$ for the odd-girth analogue, so that
$f_4=h_5$. The proof passes from a proper $\ell$-coloring of $G$ and a
$K$-coloring of the edges of $K_\ell$ without short monochromatic odd cycles to
an edge-disjoint decomposition of $G$ into $K$ subgraphs of odd girth at least
$g$, one of which has chromatic number at least $k$ because the chromatic
number of a union is at most the product of the chromatic numbers; the
multicolor Ramsey lower bound (the paper's Theorem 1.7) makes $K$ small enough.
The same theorem proves a conjecture of Mohar and Wu (2018) and, through
Corollary 1.4, the odd-girth variant of Erdős and Hajnal's infinite question for
chromatic number $\aleph_0$. Theorems 1.5 and 1.6 extend the statement to the
fractional chromatic number, the Hall ratio and the strict vector chromatic
number.

**Versions.** The first version, of 3 August 2026 and titled *The Erdős-Hajnal
conjecture for odd-girth*, deduces the bound $f_4(k)\le e^{k^{3+o(1)}}$ from
OpenAI's lower bound on the multicolor Ramsey numbers of triangles and the
odd-girth statement from a companion paper's bound for odd cycles. The second
version, of 7 September 2026 and retitled as above, supersedes the first and the
companion paper (arXiv:2608.02537) and proves its own bound, Theorem 1.7: for
every fixed $p$ and all large $k$ the $k$-color Ramsey number of the family
$\{C_3,C_5,\dots,C_{2p+1}\}$ is at least $(\log^{(p-1)}k)^{k/3-o(k)}$, the case
$p=1$ being OpenAI's theorem and the construction a modification of OpenAI's.
The author's disclosure says that the connection to Ramsey numbers, the bounds
for $f_4$ and the proofs of Theorems 1.3 and 1.5 are the author's own, that the
idea and a first draft of the proof of Theorem 1.7 are due to ChatGPT 5.6 Pro
and a first proof of Lemma 4.5 to ChatGPT 5.6 Sol Ultra, both rewritten by the
author, and that ChatGPT 5.6 Pro was used for proofreading. The author linked
the paper in the problem's discussion thread on 4 August 2026, noting the
single-exponential upper bound for $f(k,4)$ beside Pettie, Tardos and Walczak's
tower-type lower bound for $f(k,5)$.

**Covers.** The case $r=4$: $f(k,4)\le e^{k^{3+o(1)}}$ for all large $k$, so
$f(k,4)$ is finite for every $k\ge2$, since $f(k,4)$ is nondecreasing in $k$;
this is a second, quantitative proof of the instance Rödl settled
([[problems/graph_coloring/E0108/claims/1977_06_01_rodl|Rödl's claim page]]) and
improves Rödl's bound. The odd-girth results for $g\ge7$ relax girth to odd
girth and settle no further instance of the problem; the cases $r\ge5$ are not
covered.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0183/claims/2026_08_01_openai|OpenAI's lower bound on the multicolor Ramsey numbers of triangles]],
which the first version cites for the bound on $f_4$ and which is the case
$p=1$ of the second version's Theorem 1.7.

**Acceptance.** None: the paper is a preprint without a refereed publication,
the site labels the problem OPEN and its commentary does not credit the result,
and the thread post is the author's own. Nguyen and Walczak's note on the
problem cites the paper as a significant improvement of Rödl's upper bound.
