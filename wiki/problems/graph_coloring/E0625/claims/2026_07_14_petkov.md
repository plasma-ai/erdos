**Claim.** The answer is yes, quantitatively. With $G_n\sim G(n,1/2)$ and
natural logarithms, the manuscript's main theorem states

$$
\mathbb P\!\left(
\chi(G_n)-\zeta(G_n)\geq
\frac{(\log 2)^2}{4}\log\!\left(\frac{200}{153}\right)
\frac{n}{(\log n)^3}
\right)\longrightarrow1
$$

through all integers $n\to\infty$, so the gap tends to infinity with high
probability along the full sequence, at the scale Heckel conjectured; a
phase-resolved refinement with a larger coefficient is stated as well. The
argument signs the color classes of a four-size cocoloring profile, so that
classes may be independent sets or cliques, lowers the first-moment threshold
by order $n/(\log n)^3$ uniformly in the rounding phase, reduces the signed
second moment through an exact overlap identity to a bipartite configuration
model, bounds the overlap terms, obtains from Paley–Zygmund a cocoloring that
may exist only with small probability, and amplifies it to a high-probability
bound on $\zeta(G_n)$ by a bounded-differences argument; the lower bound on
$\chi(G_n)$ is the usual first-moment count. The
[[../library/graph_coloring/petkov_2026_full_sequence_chromatic_cochromatic_gap/_index|source card]]
holds the digest, the exact statement and the corpus's reading notes.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Samuil
Petkov (account Sam_Petkov) on 14 July 2026, giving "ChatGPT-5.6 Ultra and SOl"
as the AI used:

> We build on Heckel's $2^k$ signing idea, but replace the phase-restricted
> tame-colouring argument with a four-size profile and a direct signed second
> moment. This covers the exceptional phases and gives, with high probability,
> $$
> \chi(G)-\zeta(G)\ge c\frac{n}{(\ln n)^3}.
> $$
> Proof idea First-moment counting gives a lower bound for $\chi(G)$; choose
> parts of sizes $\alpha-2,\alpha-3,\alpha-4,\alpha-5$; signing each part as
> independent or complete shifts the cocolouring threshold downward by order
> $n/(\ln n)^3$; an exact overlap identity reduces the second moment to a
> bipartite configuration model; the overlap contributions are bounded;
> Paley--Zygmund gives a possibly rare cocolouring; a leftover concentration
> argument upgrades this to a high-probability upper bound for $\zeta(G)$;
> combining the two bounds proves the claimed gap. The new steps are the
> phase-uniform four-size profile and the direct signed-overlap analysis.These
> remove Heckel's restriction. Notes: The solution was obtained with immense
> help from Sol Ultra and the independent verifications were performed with Sol
> Pro. There have been 7 so far but no review from a human expert. I am waiting
> for arxiv Combinators referral approval and will post it there asap.
> Additionally I have included the lean formalization but I am currently working
> on formalizing sections 6-9 all the others pass with flying colors. The
> initial proof was completed on July 12th. EDIT: The formalisation link now
> goes to the Palomar registry.

The Palomar registry's description of entry PALOMAR-2026-09-02-000006:

> A Lean 4 formalization of the full-sequence quantitative theorem for G(n,1/2):
> with probability tending to one, the chromatic number minus the cochromatic
> number is at least ((log 2)^2/4) log(200/153) times n/(log n)^3. The formal
> statement uses the finite binomial random-graph measure, natural-valued
> chromatic and cochromatic numbers, real subtraction, and convergence along all
> natural n.

**Acceptance.** Reviewed: on 5 September 2026 the site's curator, Thomas Bloom,
who is independent of the claimant, changed the problem's label from OPEN to
SOLVED and credited Petkov and GPT-5.6 in the commentary with improving the gap
to $\gg n/(\log n)^3$ almost surely. A Palomar registry entry of 2 September
2026 reports that the Lean kernel and a second checker accepted the declaration
`Erdos625.erdos625` at the pinned commit of the author's formalization
repository with only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, together
with an automated statement review by a language model the record names as
`codex:gpt-5.6-sol`; the entry's own metadata describes the work as
self-assessed with independent kernel replay and not peer-reviewed. The corpus
did not build this Lean, so no `formalized` evidence is listed, and an automated
review is not an outside reviewer. The manuscript is not refereed (the arXiv
record lists no journal reference). On the site, the curator Thomas Bloom asked
in the forum for the formalization to be registered on Palomar and edited that
link into the claim; a commenter flagged the dead manuscript link, and a
moderator replaced it with the arXiv link at the claimant's request, since the
claimant could not edit the claim. The corpus's own partial reviews of the
amplification and overlap lemmas, recorded on the source card, are the project's
review and give no acceptance evidence.
