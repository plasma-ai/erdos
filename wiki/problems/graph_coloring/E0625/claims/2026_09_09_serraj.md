**Claim.** The answer is yes: $\chi(G_n)-\zeta(G_n)\to\infty$ with high
probability along the full sequence of integers. The manuscript presents its
route as different from Petkov's four-size signed-profile argument (the
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0625/claims/2026_07_14_petkov|accepted claim]]). By
the forum entry's account, it builds on the Heckel–Panagiotou coloring framework
and adds Proposition 6.1 (SM-CO-TAIL), which adapts their second-moment
estimates to cocolorings; in the similar and middle-3 regimes of overlapping
partition pairs it replaces the tame$(b)$ first-moment requirement with a
cochromatic first-moment bound. The remaining parameter ranges are handled by
Gibbs-profile and Kullback–Leibler threshold comparisons and by two explicit
profile constructions, H5 and H2. The proof covers four phase bands: on three of
them the manuscript's abstract states the gap $(0.07-o(1))\,n/(\ln n)^3$, and on
the central band it uses unchanged Theorem 1 of
[[../library/graph_coloring/heckel_2024_difference_between_chromatic_cochromatic_number_random/_index|Heckel's later paper]].
The final section assembles the four bands into divergence of the gap with high
probability along every $n$.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Fayçal
Serraj (account Veno) on 9 September 2026, giving "GPT-5.6 Sol (OpenAI Codex)"
as the AI used:

> I claim a complete proof of Erdős Problem 625 along the full sequence of
> integers. The proof follows a different route from the currently listed
> four-size signed-profile argument. It builds on the Heckel–Panagiotou
> colouring framework and introduces Proposition 6.1 (SM-CO-TAIL), which adapts
> the second-moment analysis to the cochromatic setting. The main technical
> point is the treatment of the similar and middle-3 regimes in Sections
> 6.2–6.3, replacing the ordinary tame(b) first-moment requirement by a
> cochromatic lower bound. The remaining parameter ranges are handled through
> Gibbs-profile/KL threshold comparisons and the H5 and H2 profile
> constructions. Sections 7, 8 and 10 treat the boundary regimes, while Heckel’s
> published result is used unchanged for the central range. Section 11 assembles
> the four ranges to obtain divergence of the chromatic–cochromatic gap with
> high probability along the full sequence. Notes: I became aware of Samuil
> Petkov’s existing proof claim only after completing this manuscript, and no
> argument from that claim was used in its development. The two approaches
> appear structurally different: Petkov’s claim uses a phase-uniform signed
> profile supported on four consecutive class sizes and a direct signed-overlap
> second-moment analysis, whereas this manuscript adapts the Heckel–Panagiotou
> second-moment framework through Proposition 6.1 and uses Gibbs/KL profile
> comparisons to cover the remaining phases. The manuscript was developed
> through a multi-cycle AI-assisted research workflow involving iterative
> derivation, source verification and fresh-context adversarial model audits. It
> has not yet received independent human expert verification or formal Lean
> verification.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki; the outside input is Heckel's theorem
