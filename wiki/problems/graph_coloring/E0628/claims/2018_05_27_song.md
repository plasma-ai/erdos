***

**Claim.** Zi-Xia Song, *Erdős–Lovász Tihany Conjecture for graphs with
forbidden holes*, proves as its Theorem 1.5: let $G$ be a graph with
$\alpha(G)\ge3$ and $\omega(G)<\chi(G)=s+t-1$, where $s,t\ge2$ are integers;
if $G$ has no induced cycle of length between $4$ and $2\alpha(G)-1$, then $G$
is $(s,t)$-splittable, that is, its vertex set splits into two parts inducing
subgraphs of chromatic numbers at least $s$ and at least $t$. In the terms of
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0628/_index|Problem 628]], with $k=\chi(G)$ and
$(a,b)=(s,t)$, the question has the answer yes for these graphs and every
admissible pair. The proof shows that a minimal counterexample would be a
quasi-line graph, for which the conjecture is
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0628/claims/2008_12_28_balogh_kostochka_prince_stiebitz|Balogh, Kostochka, Prince and Stiebitz's theorem]],
using the strong perfect graph theorem; no independent check of the proof is
recorded.

**Covers.** Graphs with $\alpha(G)\ge3$ and no hole of length between $4$
and $2\alpha(G)-1$, for every $k$ and every admissible pair $(a,b)$. Graphs
with $\alpha(G)=2$ are the case Balogh, Kostochka, Prince and Stiebitz
settled; graphs with a short hole are not covered.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0628/claims/2008_12_28_balogh_kostochka_prince_stiebitz|Balogh, Kostochka, Prince and Stiebitz's quasi-line case]],
to which the proof reduces a minimal counterexample.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Discrete Math. 342 (2019), no. 9, 2632–2635,
doi:10.1016/j.disc.2019.05.034. The preprint is arXiv:1805.11437, first
posted 27 May 2018, the date of this page. The site's curator does not credit
the paper; the problem's commentary cites only Song's survey of the
conjecture for the further partial results. A forum member linked the paper
in the problem's discussion thread on 25 October 2025.
