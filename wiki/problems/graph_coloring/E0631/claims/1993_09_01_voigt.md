***

**Claim.** The second question of
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0631/_index|Problem 631]] has the answer yes: the
bound $5$ is best possible, because there is a planar graph $G$ with
$\chi_L(G)>4$. The claimed result is the construction of Voigt, *List
colourings of planar graphs*, a planar graph on $238$ vertices with a list
assignment of four colors per vertex admitting no proper coloring from the
lists. Together with
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0631/claims/1994_09_01_thomassen|Thomassen's bound]]
this graph has $\chi_L(G)=5$ exactly. Its statement and vertex count are as
Theorem 1.3 of
[[../library/graph_coloring/gutner_1996_complexity_planar_graph_choosability/_index|Gutner 1996]]
quotes them.

**Covers.** The second question only: a planar graph that is not
$4$-choosable, so no bound below $5$ holds for all planar graphs. The upper
bound itself is Thomassen's claim above.

**Acceptance.** Refereed publication: Discrete Math. 120 (1993), no. 1--3,
215--219, doi:10.1016/0012-365X(93)90579-I. The Crossref record dates the
issue to September 1993 without a day, so the page takes the first of that
month as its date. The site's curator, T. F. Bloom, labels the problem proved
and credits [Vo93] for the planar graph with $\chi_L(G)=5$, which the page
lists as `reviewed`. A smaller construction is
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0631/claims/1996_11_01_gutner|Gutner's]].

**Formalization.** The file in Boris Alexeev's lean-proofs collection, linked
above, declares itself a formalization of a solution to the problem and names
Voigt and Thomassen as the informal authors and Codex and GPT-5.6 Sol as the
formal authors. Its sharpness witness is not Voigt's graph: it builds a
twelve-block graph on $86$ vertices after
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0631/claims/1996_11_01_gutner|Gutner's construction]],
seven new vertices per block on a shared root edge and without the
identifications that bring Gutner's count to $75$, and proves that its list
chromatic number is $5$ under the development's surrogate planarity hypothesis,
a recursive certificate of triangulated discs that stands in for planarity
because Mathlib has none. This corpus has not built or audited it, so the page
lists no `formalized` evidence.
