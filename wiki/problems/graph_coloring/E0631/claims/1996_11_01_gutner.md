***

**Claim.** The second question of
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0631/_index|Problem 631]] has the answer yes by an
independent and smaller construction: Theorem 1.7 of Gutner, *The complexity
of planar graph choosability*, gives a planar graph with $75$ vertices that is
not $4$-choosable. The graph is built from twelve copies of a gadget with
seven vertices besides the two shared vertices $u$ and $v$, with the lists of
the gadget chosen
so that each of the twelve admissible color pairs on $u$ and $v$ blocks one
copy; identifying one vertex between consecutive copies brings the count to
$2+12\cdot7-11=75$. The proof is in Section 2 of the paper; the
[[../library/graph_coloring/gutner_1996_complexity_planar_graph_choosability/_index|source card]]
gives only a digest of the paper, without the statement or the proof, and no
independent check of the proof's case analysis is recorded.

**Covers.** The second question only, by a smaller witness than
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0631/claims/1993_09_01_voigt|Voigt's]]: a planar
graph that is not $4$-choosable. A still smaller witness is
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0631/claims/1996_01_01_mirzakhani|Mirzakhani's]].
The upper bound is
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0631/claims/1994_09_01_thomassen|Thomassen's claim]].

**Acceptance.** Refereed publication: Discrete Math. 159 (1996), no. 1--3,
119--130, doi:10.1016/0012-365X(95)00104-5; the author later posted the text
as arXiv:0802.2668. The Crossref record dates the issue to November 1996
without a day, so the page takes the first of that month as its date. The
site's curator, T. F. Bloom, credits [Gu96] with the simpler construction on
the problem page, which the page lists as `reviewed`. The same paper proves
that deciding whether a planar graph is $4$-choosable is NP-hard, which is
not part of the question.

**Formalization.** The Lean 4 development in Boris Alexeev's lean-proofs
collection linked from
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0631/claims/1993_09_01_voigt|Voigt's]] and
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0631/claims/1994_09_01_thomassen|Thomassen's]]
pages, whose header names those two as its informal authors, takes its
sharpness witness from this construction: a twelve-block graph on $86$
vertices, seven new vertices per block on a shared root edge, without the
identifications that bring the count here to $75$. The file does not name
Gutner as an author, so it is not linked here; this corpus has not built or
audited it.
