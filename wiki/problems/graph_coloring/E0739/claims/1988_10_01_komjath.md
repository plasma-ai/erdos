***

**Claim.** The statement of [[problems/graph_coloring/E0739/_index|Problem 739]]
is not a theorem of ZFC. Komjáth proves that it is consistent with ZFC that
$2^{\aleph_0}=2^{\aleph_1}=2^{\aleph_2}=\aleph_3$ and that there is a graph of
chromatic number $\aleph_2$ none of whose subgraphs has chromatic number
$\aleph_1$. In that model the statement fails for $\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_2$ and
$\mathfrak{n}=\aleph_1$, so, provided ZFC is consistent, ZFC does not prove it.
The page is dated by the paper's journal issue, October 1988; the day is not
recorded.

**Covers.** One side of an independence result: the statement fails at
$\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_2$ and $\mathfrak{n}=\aleph_1$ in a model of ZFC, so ZFC
does not prove it, but nothing here shows that ZFC does not disprove it. One
side alone leaves the question open, so the result leaves the problem open. It
would be settled as independent by a model in which the statement holds for
every pair of infinite cardinals, and as disproved by a refutation in ZFC
alone; neither is recorded.

**What it leaves open.** The result says nothing about disprovability. No model
is recorded in which the statement holds for every pair of infinite cardinals,
and whether it follows from the Generalized Continuum Hypothesis is open.
Shelah shows that under the axiom of constructibility there is no
counterexample of size $\aleph_2$ to the case $\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_2$,
$\mathfrak{n}=\aleph_1$
([[../library/graph_coloring/shelah_1990_incompactness_chromatic_numbers_graphs/_index|card]]);
the paper's introduction credits Komjáth with the independence of that case
from ZFC, and the site records the case as holding under constructibility
without the size restriction. The claim value is `not_provable`, not
`independent`.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Komjáth, P., Consistency results on infinite graphs,
Israel J. Math. 61 (1988), no. 3, 285–294. Reviewed: the site's curator,
Thomas Bloom, labels the problem not provable and credits Komjáth's consistency
result, independently of its author. The paper is not held in the library; the
account above follows the site's record and the introduction of Shelah's paper,
which records Komjáth's result before proving the constructible-universe case.
