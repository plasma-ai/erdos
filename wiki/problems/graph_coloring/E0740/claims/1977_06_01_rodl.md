***

**Claim.** The answer to [[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/_index|Problem 740]] is
yes when $\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_0$ and $r\le4$: a graph of chromatic number
$\aleph_0$ has a triangle-free subgraph of chromatic number $\aleph_0$. Rödl,
*On the chromatic number of subgraphs of a given graph*, proves the finite
theorem: for all positive integers $m$ and $n$ there is $\varphi(m,n)$ such that
a graph $G$ with $\chi(G)\ge\varphi(m,n)$ contains a complete subgraph $K_m$ or
a triangle-free subgraph $H$ with $\chi(H)\ge n$. The proof is an induction on
$m$: the vertices are linearly ordered, the left neighborhood of each vertex,
which contains no $K_{m-1}$, is colored with few colors by the induction
hypothesis, the edges are split by the color of their lower endpoint, and
Zykov's observation that, when the edges of a graph of chromatic number above
$(p-1)(q-1)$ are split in two, one part has chromatic number at least $p$ or the
other at least $q$ gives a triangle-free part of large chromatic number. As the
paper notes, the conjecture of Erdős and Hajnal follows: since $K_m$ contains,
for $m$ large in terms of $n$, a triangle-free graph of chromatic number at
least $n$ (Tutte's construction), every finite graph of chromatic number at
least $\varphi(m,n)$ contains a triangle-free subgraph of chromatic number at
least $n$. The infinite case is the standard derivation: if $\chi(G)=\aleph_0$,
the de Bruijn–Erdős theorem gives finite subgraphs of $G$ of arbitrarily large
chromatic number, and deleting finitely many vertices leaves a graph of
chromatic number $\aleph_0$; choosing in turn pairwise vertex-disjoint finite
triangle-free subgraphs $H_1,H_2,\ldots$ with $\chi(H_i)>i$ and keeping only
their internal edges gives a triangle-free subgraph of $G$ with countably many
vertices and chromatic number $\aleph_0$.

**Covers.** The case $\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_0$ with $r=3$ or $r=4$, since a graph
with no odd cycle of length at most $4$ is a triangle-free graph; for $r=1$ and
$r=2$ the graph itself qualifies. It says nothing about $r\ge5$, which the
pending
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims/2026_08_16_dottedcalculator|DottedCalculator]]
and [[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims/2026_09_07_steiner|Steiner]] claims
address, nor about uncountable $\mathfrak{m}$, where
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims/1988_09_01_komjath_shelah|Komjáth and Shelah]]
show that the statement consistently fails. Its finitary form is the case $r=4$
of [[problems/graph_coloring/E0108/_index|Problem 108]].

**Acceptance.** Refereed: V. Rödl, On the chromatic number of subgraphs of a
given graph, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 64 (1977), no. 2, 370–371,
doi:10.1090/S0002-9939-1977-0469806-4; the issue is dated June 1977 without a
day, so the page carries the first of that month. The site's notes credit
Rödl with the case $\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_0$, $r=3$, but label the problem
OPEN, so no `reviewed` evidence is listed. Erdős's 1981 problem paper
([[../library/set_systems/erdos_1981_combinatorial_problems_which_i_would_most/_index|card]])
records the same credit, for $\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_0$ and a shortest odd
circuit of length at least $5$, and the introduction of Komjáth and Shelah's
1988 paper records that Rödl showed the $\kappa=\aleph_0$ case. The paper is
not held in the library; the statement and proof above follow the published
paper.
