**Claim.** The answer is yes when $\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_0$: a graph $G$ of
countably infinite chromatic number contains, for every $r\ge1$, a subgraph of
chromatic number $\aleph_0$ with no odd cycle of length at most $r$. The
write-up, *The Countable Case of Erdős Problem 740*, dated 16 August 2026,
proves this as its Theorem 3.1 in the form: every graph that is not finitely
colorable contains, for every $r\ge1$, a countable subgraph $H$ with
$\chi(H)=\aleph_0$ whose shortest odd cycle is longer than $r$. It combines
two finite theorems. Steiner's theorem (Theorem 1.3 of arXiv:2608.02522,
stated as the write-up's Theorem 2.1): for an odd integer $g\ge5$ and
$\varepsilon>0$, for every sufficiently large $k$, every finite graph $F$ with
$\chi(F)\ge\exp^{((g-3)/2)}(k^{3+\varepsilon})$ contains a subgraph $J$ with
$\chi(J)\ge k$ and no odd cycle shorter than $g$, where $\exp^{(t)}$ is the
$t$-fold iterated exponential. The de Bruijn–Erdős theorem: a graph whose
finite subgraphs are all $q$-colorable is $q$-colorable. Fixing an odd
$g>\max\{r,3\}$ and a strictly increasing sequence $k_1<k_2<\cdots$ of large
integers, the write-up takes in turn a finite subgraph of $G$, disjoint from
the vertices already used, of chromatic number large enough for Steiner's
theorem, inside it a finite subgraph $H_i$ with $\chi(H_i)\ge k_i$ and no odd
cycle shorter than $g$, and lets $H$ be the union of the $H_i$ with only their
internal edges; every cycle of $H$ lies in one block, so $H$ has no odd cycle
of length at most $r$, and $H$ is not finitely colorable but has countably
many vertices, so $\chi(H)=\aleph_0$.

**Covers.** The case $\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_0$ for every $r\ge1$; the claimant's
theorem concerns no uncountable cardinal. Rödl's refereed theorem already gives
$\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_0$ for $r\le4$
([[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims/1977_06_01_rodl|claim page]]), so the
new content is the extension to every $r$. The write-up's section 4 reports,
from Theorem 2 of Komjáth and Shelah, that in a model of CH the statement fails
at $\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_1$ and so, if ZFC is consistent, the all-cardinals
statement is not provable in ZFC; that result is Komjáth and Shelah's
([[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims/1988_09_01_komjath_shelah|claim page]]),
not the claimant's. The
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims/2026_09_06_land|pending negative claim]]
asserts a counterexample at $\aleph_1$ in ZFC alone. Steiner's revised preprint
states and proves the same case independently
([[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims/2026_09_07_steiner|claim page]]).
