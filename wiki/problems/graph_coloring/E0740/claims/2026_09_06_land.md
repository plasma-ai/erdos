***

**Claim.** The answer is no. In ZFC alone, with no use of the continuum
hypothesis, there is a graph $G$ with $\chi(G)=\aleph_1$ such that every triangle-free
subgraph of $G$, induced or not, has a proper coloring with countably many
colors; so for $\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_1$ and $r=3$ no subgraph of chromatic
number $\mathfrak{m}$ avoids the odd cycles of length at most $r$, and the
question fails. As the forum entry describes it, $G$ is the comparability
graph of the tree of injective sequences of positive integers, indexed by
countable ordinals, whose range is coinfinite, a tree the entry attributes to
Bowler and Pitz; uncountable chromatic number follows by a diagonalization,
and the key step is a countable coloring of any triangle-free subgraph in
which two adjacent vertices of the same color would force a triangle inside
that subgraph. The graph has $2^{\aleph_0}$ vertices, so the claim addresses
the formulation with no bound on the vertex set.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Johan Land
(account JohanLand) on 6 September 2026, giving "GPT-6-Astra, Fable 5.1,
Gemini-3.8-Flash" as the AI used:

> I'm proposing a negative solution to Problem 740 in ZFC. The manuscript
> constructs a graph \(G\) with \(\chi(G)=\aleph_1\) such that every
> triangle-free subgraph of \(G\), not necessarily induced, is countably
> colorable. This would answer the question negatively already for the exclusion
> of triangles. The construction is the comparability graph of the tree of
> injective countable ordinal sequences of positive integers with coinfinite
> range. This tree already appears in Bowler–Pitz. Uncountable chromatic number
> follows by diagonalization; the proposed key observation is a countable
> coloring of any triangle-free subgraph, where equal colors on an edge would
> force a triangle within that subgraph. The host has \(2^{\aleph_0}\) vertices,
> so the claim concerns the unrestricted-vertex-set formulation. No CH
> assumption is used. Formalization coming...

**Manuscript and formalization.** The manuscript, *A sequence-tree
obstruction to preserving uncountable chromatic number*, dated 6 September
2026, is a PDF in the author's GitHub repository, linked at the repository's
last commit of that day. The same repository holds a Lean 4 development
(toolchain v4.34.0-rc2) whose final theorem, as its README states, is the
negation of the formal-conjectures statement of the problem at a pinned
revision of that catalog, with the chromatic number taken as a cardinal; the
README reports that `lake build` checks the main results against an axiom
whitelist of `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, and the author's
comment on the forum entry, posted the same day, says the same. The README
states that the Lean implementation was produced through the `agy` tool using
gemini-3.8-flash-high, with Codex coordinating the work, reviewing the
statement correspondence and running the local verification commands. The
corpus did not build this Lean or audit its statement, so no `formalized`
evidence is listed.

**Claimant.** Johan Land, who posted the claim on the erdosproblems.com forum
on 6 September 2026, naming GPT-6-Astra, Fable 5.1 and Gemini-3.8-Flash as
tools.

**Context.** The partial claim for $\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_0$
([[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims/2026_08_16_dottedcalculator|claim
page]]) concerns a different cardinal and is compatible with this one. Komjáth
and Shelah
([[../library/set_theory/komjath_1988_forcing_constructions_uncountably_chromatic_graphs/_index|card]];
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims/1988_09_01_komjath_shelah|claim page]])
proved in 1988 that such a graph exists consistently: their Theorem 2
(Komjáth's) gives, in a model of CH, an $\aleph_1$-chromatic graph on
$\omega_1$ all of whose triangle-free subgraphs are countably chromatic, and
their Theorem 1 (Shelah's) gives one in a model of $2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_2$;
they wrote that the conjecture is probably false in ZFC already but that they
could not show it. The present claim is a counterexample in ZFC where Komjáth
and Shelah give a consistent one; the site's
[[problems/set_theory/E1175/_index|Problem 1175]] asks the weaker existential
form.

**Acceptance.** None: the manuscript is not refereed, no outside reviewer has
endorsed it, and the site labels the problem OPEN. The
manuscript is not held in the library; the account above follows the forum
entry and the repository's README.
