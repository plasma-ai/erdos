***

**Claim.** The answer to [[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/_index|Problem 740]] is
yes when $\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_0$. Corollary 1.4 of Raphael Steiner, *Locally
bipartite subgraphs via multicolor Ramsey numbers* (arXiv:2608.02522v2, 7
September 2026; version 1, of 3 August 2026, states nothing about infinite
graphs), reads: every infinite graph of infinite chromatic number contains, for
every odd $g\ge5$, a subgraph of infinite chromatic number and odd girth at
least $g$. Appendix A derives it from Theorem 1.3 (for odd $g\ge5$ and
$\varepsilon>0$, for all large $k$, every finite graph with
$\chi(G)\ge\exp^{((g-3)/2)}(k^{3+\varepsilon})$ contains a subgraph of chromatic
number at least $k$ and odd girth at least $g$, where $\exp^{(t)}$ is the
$t$-fold iterated exponential): the finite subgraphs $G_1,\ldots,G_{i-1}$
already chosen span a finite graph, so deleting their vertices leaves a graph of
infinite chromatic number, the de Bruijn–Erdős theorem gives finite subgraphs of
it of arbitrarily large chromatic number, Theorem 1.3 gives inside one of them a
subgraph $G_i$ with $\chi(G_i)\ge i$ and odd girth at least $g$, and the union
of the pairwise vertex-disjoint $G_i$ has infinite chromatic number and odd
girth at least $g$. The introduction states the 1975 question of Erdős and
Hajnal, cited to Erdős's paper in the Proceedings of the Fifth British
Combinatorial Conference, as whether for every infinite cardinal $\mathfrak{m}$
and every odd $g\ge5$ every graph of chromatic number at least $\mathfrak{m}$
has a subgraph of chromatic number at least $\mathfrak{m}$ and odd girth at
least $g$, and says that the corollary resolves it for $\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_0$.

**Covers.** The case $\mathfrak{m}=\aleph_0$ for every $r\ge1$: a graph with no
odd cycle of length at most $r$ is one of odd girth above $r$, so for $r\ge3$ an
odd $g>r$, with $g\ge5$, serves, and for $r\le2$ the graph itself qualifies.
Rödl's refereed theorem already gives $r\le4$
([[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims/1977_06_01_rodl|claim page]]). The
corollary says nothing about uncountable $\mathfrak{m}$, where
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims/1988_09_01_komjath_shelah|Komjáth and Shelah]]
show that the statement consistently fails at $\aleph_1$.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0108/claims/2026_08_03_steiner|Steiner's odd-girth theorem]],
Theorem 1.3 of the same preprint, from which Appendix A derives the corollary.

**Context.** The forum claim of 16 August 2026
([[problems/graph_coloring/E0740/claims/2026_08_16_dottedcalculator|claim page]])
derived the same case from version 1's Theorem 1.3 by the same argument; the
preprint does not cite it, the site or the problem. At $g=5$, Theorem 1.3 gives
the [[problems/graph_coloring/E0108/_index|finite girth problem]] a
single-exponential bound in place of Rödl's tower.

**Claimant.** Raphael Steiner. The paper's AI disclosure says that ChatGPT 5.6
Pro was used for proofreading and supplied the idea and a first draft of the
proof of Theorem 1.7, the lower bound on the multicolor Ramsey numbers of odd
cycles on which Theorem 1.3 rests, rewritten by the author; that ChatGPT 5.6 Sol
Ultra gave a first proof of Lemma 4.5, on the strict vector chromatic number,
which the corollary does not use; and that the statements, the proofs of
Theorems 1.3 and 1.5 and the rest of the paper are the author's own.

**Acceptance.** None: the preprint is not refereed, no outside reviewer has
endorsed it, and the site labels the problem OPEN.
