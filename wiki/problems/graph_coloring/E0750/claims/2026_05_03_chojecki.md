***

**Claim.** The answer to [[problems/graph_coloring/E0750/_index|Problem 750]] is
yes: for every $f:\mathbb{N}\to[0,\infty)$ with $f(m)\to\infty$ (the reading the
problem page's Formulation records) there is a graph $G$ of infinite chromatic
number such that every finite subgraph of $G$ on $m$ vertices has an independent
set of size at least $m/2-f(m)$. The note proves a stronger statement (its
Theorem 1.1): for every nondecreasing unbounded $g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}_0$
there is a graph $G$ with $\chi(G)$ infinite such that every finite subgraph $F$
becomes bipartite after deleting at most $g(|V(F)|)$ vertices. The independent
set follows (its Corollary 1.2): after deleting $t\le f(m)$ vertices from an
$m$-vertex subgraph, one side of the remaining bipartition has at least
$(m-t)/2$ vertices.

**Construction.** The building blocks are generalized Mycielski graphs
$M_s(H)$, with $s$ levels over $H$ and one apex. Two estimates drive the
argument: a vertex set $X$ of $M_s(H)$ projecting to $P\subseteq V(H)$ is made
bipartite by deleting at most $s$ times an odd-cycle transversal of $H[P]$
plus possibly the apex, and when $H$ is bipartite every odd cycle of $M_s(H)$
passes through the apex and has length at least $2s+1$. An induction on the
chromatic number then gives, for every $r$ and every such $g$, an
$r$-chromatic generalized Mycielski graph in which every nonempty induced
subgraph $X$ has odd-cycle-transversal number at most $g(|X|)$; the chromatic
number of the recursively generated generalized Mycielski graphs is Stiebitz's
theorem, used as a black box. A disjoint union of such finite graphs with
geometrically scaled profiles is the graph $G$. The note states only that
$\chi(G)$ is infinite; that it equals $\aleph_0$ is this page's observation,
since $G$, a disjoint union of countably many finite graphs, has countably
many vertices.

**Claimant.** The note, *Local odd-cycle transversals in generalized Mycielski
graphs and an Erdős problem on almost-half independent sets*, is dated
2026-05-03 and signed by Przemek Chojecki together with the AI system GPT-5.5
Pro, which the author credits with the proof; it was posted the same day on
the site's discussion thread. The claimant here is the human submitter, with
the system named as the note names it.

by Chojecki, independently of the claimant; on the discussion thread Stijn
Cambie, whom the site's page thanks by name, records a human review of the
proof (2026-07-10), and a ChatGPT check posted on 2026-05-03 found no issues.
