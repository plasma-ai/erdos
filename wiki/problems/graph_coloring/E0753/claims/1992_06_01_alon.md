***

**Claim.** The answer is no: no constant $c>0$ makes
$\chi_L(G)+\chi_L(G^c)>n^{1/2+c}$ hold for every graph $G$ on $n$ vertices.
Corollary 1.2 of
[[../library/graph_coloring/alon_1992_choice_numbers_graphs_probabilistic_approach/_index|Alon 1992]]
gives a constant $b>0$ such that for every $n$ some $n$-vertex graph $G$
satisfies

$$
\chi_L(G)+\chi_L(G^c)\le b\,n^{1/2}(\log n)^{1/2},
$$

which is $o(n^{1/2+c})$ for every $c>0$. The graph is the complete
$r$-partite graph $K_{m*r}$ with $m$ vertices in each part, where
$m=(n\log n)^{1/2}$ and $r=n/m$. Its complement is a disjoint union of $r$
cliques of order $m$, so $\chi_L(G^c)=m$, and Theorem 1.1 of the paper,
$\chi_L(K_{m*r})=\Theta(r\log m)$ for $m,r\ge2$, gives
$\chi_L(G)=O(r\log m)=O((n\log n)^{1/2})$. The paper states that Corollary
1.2 settles this question of Erdős, Rubin and Taylor.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: N. Alon, Choice numbers of graphs: a probabilistic
approach, Combin. Probab. Comput. 1 (1992), no. 2, 107–114; the issue is
dated June 1992, and this page carries the first of that month because the
day is not recorded. Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas Bloom, marks
Problem 753 disproved on the problem page and credits Alon's theorem [Al92];
the community database records the problem as disproved, with a Lean
proof, from 2026-04-14.

**Formalizations.** One Lean 4 development, posted by Matteo Del Vecchio and
held in three places, declares itself a formalization of this result; the three
copies are linked above. Del Vecchio posted it on the site's discussion thread
on 2026-04-14, a proof generated with Harmonic's Aristotle following Alon's
paper. It proves Alon's Proposition 2.1 only in its first case, $r\le m$
(`alon_prop_2_1`, with constant $6$), and applies it to the balanced graph
$K_{m*m}$ on $m^2$ vertices, giving $\chi_L(G)+\chi_L(G^c)\le6m\log m+m$. This
refutes every exponent $1/2+c$, but the development formalizes neither Corollary
1.2's bound $O((n\log n)^{1/2})$ for every $n$ nor the lower bound of Theorem
1.1. The statement file of
[formal-conjectures](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/6fbb54f24ccc2e64dcfaffc28c58950e377110d2/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/753.lean),
as last changed on 2026-09-18, marks the problem solved and points its formal
proof at the copy in Boris Alexeev's lean-proofs collection, which names Alon as
the informal author and is marked as modified from the posting; the erdos-lean
catalog holds a third copy. The site's Lean qualifier refers to these. The
corpus did not build any of them, so this page lists no `formalized` evidence.
