***

**Claim.** There are constants $d_1,d_2>0$ with

$$
d_1\frac{\sqrt n}{\ln n}\le z(S_n)\le d_2\sqrt n
$$

for the orientable surface $S_n$ of genus $n$. This is the genus theorem of
[[../library/graph_coloring/gimbel_1986_three_extremal_problems_cochromatic_theory/_index|Gimbel 1986]],
proved as a disproof of the conjecture that $z(S)$ equals the largest $n$ for
which $K_1\cup K_2\cup\dots\cup K_n$ embeds in $S$, which would give
$z(S_n)=O(n^{1/3})$. The upper bound is the chromatic number of $S_n$, which is
$O(\sqrt n)$. For the lower bound, the complete graph on about $\sqrt{48n}/2$
vertices embeds on $S_n$, so a graph of order about $\sqrt n$ with cochromatic
number $\gg\sqrt n/\log n$, which the paper's order theorem supplies, embeds
on $S_n$ too.

**Covers.** The lower bound $z(S_n)\gg\sqrt n/\log n$, which has the growth
rate the problem asks for, and the upper bound $z(S_n)\ll\sqrt n$, which is off
by the logarithm.
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0759/claims/1997_11_01_gimbel_thomassen|Gimbel and Thomassen 1997]]
later close the gap and determine the growth rate.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: J. Gimbel, Three extremal problems in cochromatic
theory, Rostock. Math. Kolloq. 30 (1986), 73–78; the issue carries only its
year, and this page carries the first of January because the date is not
recorded. The site's curator credits Gimbel [Gi86] with exactly these bounds,
but the site's label settles the problem on Gimbel and Thomassen's result, so
that credit is not `reviewed` evidence. The page rests on the refereed
publication alone.
