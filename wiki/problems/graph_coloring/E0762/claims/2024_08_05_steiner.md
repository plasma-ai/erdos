***

**Claim.** Steiner's Theorem 1.4 answers the question of
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0762/_index|Problem 762]] in the negative: there are
infinitely many graphs $G$ with $\omega(G)<5$, $\zeta(G)=4$ and $\chi(G)=7$.
Every such $G$ has no $K_5$ and cochromatic number at least $4$, yet
$\chi(G)=\zeta(G)+3$, so the bound $\chi(G)\le\zeta(G)+2$ conjectured by Erdős,
Gimbel and Straight fails. The same graphs answer the question of Erdős and
Gimbel (1993) whether only finitely many graphs with $\omega<5$ have
$\chi>\zeta+2$: there are infinitely many.

The construction is Section 3 of the preprint. An auxiliary graph $H$ on $11$
vertices is the join of two $5$-cycles together with one further vertex
adjacent to one vertex of each cycle. Lemma 3.1 records that $\omega(H)<5$,
that $V(H)$ splits into three cliques, and that every proper $6$-coloring of
$H$ uses all six colors on some set $X\subseteq V(H)$ with $\omega(H[X])<4$.
The graph $G$ is obtained from $H$ by adding, for every $X\subseteq V(H)$
with $\omega(H[X])<4$, a nonempty independent set of new vertices whose
neighborhood is exactly $X$; the freedom in the sizes of these sets gives
infinitely many graphs. A clique of $G$ is a clique of $H$ or one new vertex
together with a clique of $H[X]$, so $\omega(G)\le4$; three cliques of $H$
and the independent set of new vertices give $\zeta(G)\le4$, while a
counting argument rules out a cover by three homogeneous sets; and a proper
$6$-coloring of $G$ would use all six colors on some $X$ and leave none for
the vertices attached to $X$, so $\chi(G)=7$, the upper bound being
$\chi(H)+1$.

**Acceptance.** The site's curator, Thomas Bloom, lists Problem 762 as
disproved and credits the disproof to Steiner [St24b], an acceptance
independent of the claimant (`reviewed`). The result appeared as an arXiv
preprint, arXiv:2408.02400, first version 2024-08-05 and second version
2024-08-20, and is published as R. Steiner, On the Difference Between the
Chromatic and Cochromatic Number, SIAM Journal on Discrete Mathematics 39
(2025), no. 4, 2268–2274, doi:10.1137/24M1715180, published online 2025-11-17
(`refereed`); Theorem 1.4 and Section 3 are cited above by their labels in
the arXiv version. The site cites only the arXiv version. The source card is
[[../library/graph_coloring/steiner_2024_difference_between_chromatic_cochromatic_number/_index|Steiner 2024]].

**Formalization.** The Lean file among the links, in Boris Alexeev's repository
`lean-proofs`, declares itself a formalization of Steiner's result, naming
Steiner as its informal author and Aristotle, ChatGPT and Alexeev as its formal
authors. It builds Steiner's graph from the $11$-vertex $H$ and proves
`steiner_graph` (clique number $4$, cochromatic number $4$, chromatic number
$7$) and `not_erdos_762`, the negation of the conjecture, with the axioms
`propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` printed at its end. Its first
version, announced in the site's discussion thread on 2026-02-08, used
`native_decide` for an exhaustive check on $H$; the repository's commit of
2026-05-18 removes that step. The link is pinned to the repository's commit of
2026-06-24, which placed the file in its `v4.29.1` folder. The erdos-lean
catalog holds a variant of the same development, also naming Steiner as its
informal author and Aristotle and ChatGPT as its formalizers, announced in the
thread on 2026-06-03 with the exhaustive step carried out by `decide`, a
replacement the thread post credits to Claude Opus 4.7; it is the second
formalization link, pinned to its commit of 2026-06-10. This corpus has
built neither file, so the formalizations are links and not `formalized`
evidence. The formal-conjectures statement file for the problem marks it solved
and points at the same proof; a statement file is not a formalization.
