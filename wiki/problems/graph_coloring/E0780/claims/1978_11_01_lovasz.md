***

**Claim.** Lovász proves Kneser's conjecture: if $n\ge 2r+t-1$ and the
$r$-subsets of an $n$-set are split into $t$ classes, some class contains two
disjoint sets. In the language of the Kneser graph $KG(n,r)$, whose vertices
are the $r$-subsets of $\{1,\dots,n\}$ and whose edges join disjoint sets,
the theorem says that $\chi(KG(n,r))=n-2r+2$ for $n\ge 2r$; the coloring by
the least element, with the sets inside the last $2r-1$ points given one
further color, shows that the value is sharp. This is the case $k=2$ of
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0780/_index|Problem 780]], whose bound
$n\ge kr+(t-1)(k-1)$ reads $n\ge 2r+t-1$ there.

The proof is topological. The neighborhood complex of a graph has, for every
vertex set with a common neighbor, a simplex; Lovász shows that a graph whose
neighborhood complex is $(k-2)$-connected has chromatic number at least
$k+1$, by the Borsuk–Ulam theorem, and that the neighborhood complex of
$KG(n,r)$ is $(n-2r-1)$-connected. The argument opened the topological
method in combinatorics; the general case of Problem 780, for every $k$, is
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0780/claims/1986_11_01_alon_frankl_lovasz|Alon,
Frankl and Lovász's theorem]], whose Proposition 2.3 composes this case with
the odd-prime cases.

**Covers.** The case $k=2$ of Problem 780, for every $r$ and every $t$: a
$t$-coloring of the $r$-subsets of an $n$-set with $n\ge 2r+t-1$ has two
pairwise disjoint sets of one color. Every $k\ge3$ is outside it.

**Acceptance.** Published in Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series A 25
(1978), no. 3, 319–324 (`refereed`); the issue is dated November 1978, and the
page carries the first of that month. The site's curator, Thomas Bloom, lists
Problem 780 as proved and credits the case $k=2$, which Kneser conjectured, to
Lovász [Lo78] (`reviewed`).
