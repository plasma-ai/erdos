***

**Claim.** Alon proves that almost every graph on $n$ vertices has list
chromatic number $o(n)$, answering the question of
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0799/_index|Problem 799]], which Erdős, Rubin and
Taylor raised in the paper introducing choosability, read at the 1979 West
Coast conference (Congressus Numerantium 26, 1980). Corollary 1.3 of the
paper states that for a constant $c>0$ the random graph $G_{n,1/2}$ satisfies

$$
\chi_L(G_{n,1/2}) \le c\,n\,\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}
$$

with probability tending to $1$; since $G_{n,1/2}$ is a uniformly random
labeled graph on $n$ vertices, this is the statement for almost all graphs.
The main theorem (Theorem 1.1) is that the complete $r$-partite graph
$K_{m*r}$ with $m$ vertices in each class has choice number of order
$r\log m$, proved by a probabilistic splitting of the colors. The corollary
follows because almost surely $G_{n,1/2}$ has a proper coloring with
$n/\log_2 n$ colors and no independent set larger than $2\log_2 n$, so it is
a subgraph of $K_{m*r}$ with $r=n/\log_2 n$ and $m=2\log_2 n$. The paper asks
whether the true order of $\chi_L(G_{n,1/2})$ is that of the chromatic
number, $n/\log n$, or that of this upper bound; it is the first, as
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0799/claims/1999_10_01_alon_krivelevich_sudakov|Alon, Krivelevich and Sudakov 1999]]
shows.

without a day, so the page is dated to the first day of that month. The
