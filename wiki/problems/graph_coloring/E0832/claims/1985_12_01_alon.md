***

**Claim.** The conjecture of Erdős and Hajnal behind the question, that for
each fixed uniformity $r\ge3$ and every $k$ large in terms of $r$ an
$r$-uniform hypergraph with chromatic number $k$ has at least

$$
\binom{(r-1)(k-1)+1}{r}
$$

edges, the edge count of the complete $r$-uniform hypergraph on
$(r-1)(k-1)+1$ vertices, is false. Writing $f(r,s)$ for the least number of
edges of an $r$-uniform hypergraph whose chromatic number is at least $s$,
Alon's Proposition 1 bounds $f(r,s)$ above through Turán numbers, and his
[[../library/graph_coloring/alon_1985_hypergraphs_high_chromatic_number/proposition_3|Proposition 3]]
gives, as $r\to\infty$ and $s/r\to\infty$,

$$
f(r,s)=O\!\left(r^{5/2}\log r\left(\tfrac34\right)^r\binom{(s-1)(r-1)+1}{r}\right),
$$

an exponentially small fraction of the benchmark. The site's remarks report
that the Turán-number bound already refutes the conjecture for every
$r\ge4$. Because the bound shrinks relative to the benchmark as $r$ grows,
no threshold $k_0(r)$ rescues the universal assertion: for any proposed
threshold, Proposition 3 supplies, at some large $r$, a hypergraph whose
exact chromatic number exceeds the threshold and whose edge count is below
the benchmark at that chromatic number. The problem page's Progress section
states that parameter transfer. The answer to the displayed question is
therefore no, with the statement's "complete graph" read as the complete
$r$-uniform hypergraph.

**What the result leaves open.** Proposition 3 needs $r\to\infty$ and
Proposition 1's bound lies above the benchmark at $r=3$, so the fixed case
$r=3$ is not settled by the paper, and the equality clause is moot where the
inequality fails.

**Acceptance.** Published in Graphs and Combinatorics **1** (1985), 387–389,
in the journal's Research Problems column, with proofs of Propositions 1
and 2 and a one-sentence derivation of Proposition 3 from inequality (4) and a
Frankl–Rödl bound on Turán numbers
([[../library/graph_coloring/alon_1985_hypergraphs_high_chromatic_number/_index|source card]]).
The site's curator, Thomas Bloom, marks the problem disproved and names this
paper as the disproof (site page). This corpus checked the
statement of Proposition 3, its parameter range and the transfer to an exact
chromatic number; it has not rewritten or reviewed the proof.

**Formalization.** The Lean file among the links, in Boris Alexeev's repository
`lean-proofs`, declares itself a formalization of a solution to Problem 832,
naming Noga Alon as its informal author and Codex and GPT-5.6 Sol as its
formal authors; a second header in the file credits Aristotle and OpenAI
Codex. Its `theorem not_erdos_832` states the eventual assertion with the
problem's quantifiers, the equality clause included, and refutes it at the
fixed uniformity $r=4$: the counterexamples are complements of a Giraud-type
$K_5^{(4)}$-free construction on two copies of $\mathbb{F}_2^d$, so the file
follows the Turán-number route of Proposition 1, the one behind the site's
remark for $r\ge4$, and not Proposition 3. The file ends by printing the
theorem's axioms. The link is pinned to the repository's commit of
2026-08-31, which changed the file after it was added on 2026-08-17. This
corpus has not built the file, so the formalization is a link and not
`formalized` evidence.
