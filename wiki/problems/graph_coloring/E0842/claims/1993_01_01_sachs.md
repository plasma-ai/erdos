***

**Claim.** Let $G$ be a finite nonempty graph with a Hamilton circuit $H$
such that $G-E(H)$ consists of pairwise disjoint triangles covering
$V(G)$. Sachs writes $\pi(G)$ for the number of distinct partitions of
$V(G)$ into three independent sets induced by proper $3$-colorings and
proves that $\pi(G)$ is odd
([[../library/graph_coloring/sachs_1993_elementary_proof_cycle_plus_triangles_theorem/main_theorem|theorem and corollary]],
printed p. 348). In particular $\pi(G)\ge1$, so every such graph is
properly $3$-colorable: this is the displayed corollary, the
cycle-plus-triangles theorem first proved by Fleischner and Stiebitz
([[problems/graph_coloring/E0842/claims/1992_05_01_fleischner_stiebitz|their claim page]]).
The graph of the question is such a graph, so its chromatic number is at
most $3$ and the answer is yes. The proof is elementary and self-contained;
it does not pass through Alon and Tarsi's orientation criterion, so it is an
independent proof of the same result rather than a formalization or
exposition of the earlier one.

**Acceptance.** Published as H. Sachs, Elementary proof of the
cycle-plus-triangles theorem, in D. Miklós, V. T. Sós and T. Szőnyi (eds.),
Combinatorics, Paul Erdős is Eighty, Vol. 1, János Bolyai Mathematical
Society, Budapest, 1993, 347–359; the venue is an edited volume, not a
journal, and no evidence that it was refereed is recorded, so `refereed` is
not listed
([[../library/graph_coloring/sachs_1993_elementary_proof_cycle_plus_triangles_theorem/_index|source card]]).
The same result circulated as Les Cahiers du GERAD G-94-02 (February 1994),
whose text has not been compared with the chapter. The site credits the
theorem to Fleischner and Stiebitz and does not name Sachs, so there is no
curator credit. The source class, the theorem and the corollary were
checked at statement level; the proof, printed in full in §§2–6 on printed
pp. 349–358, has not been reconstructed or reviewed here. No formalization
of Sachs's own argument is known; the third-party Lean proof linked from
the Fleischner–Stiebitz page follows the Alon–Tarsi route.

**Date.** The volume carries only its publication year, 1993; the page is
dated to the first day of that year.
