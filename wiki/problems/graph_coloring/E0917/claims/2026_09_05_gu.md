**Claim.** There is a sequence of $12$-critical graphs $G_s$ with
$|V(G_s)|\to\infty$ and $e(G_s)/|V(G_s)|^2\to2/5$. Since the formula of the
third question of [[problems/graph_coloring/E0917/_index|Problem 917]]
predicts $f_{12}(n)\sim3n^2/8$ and $2/5>3/8$, the formula fails at $k=12$. As
the forum entry describes the construction, it attaches five of Pegden's
coloring gadgets to a $K_5$-saturated graph whose maximum degree grows
sublinearly: saturation makes the graph $11$-colorable once any edge is
removed, and the degree bound prevents an $11$-coloring of the graph itself.
The entry says that whether $f_6(n)\sim n^2/4$ remains open.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Qiyuan Gu
(account fireflysentinel) on 5 September 2026, giving "GPT 6 Astra, GPT 5.6 Sol
and Claude Opus 5" as the AI used:

> We give a negative answer to the final question in Erdős Problem 917,
> already for k = 12. We construct a sequence of 12-critical graphs with n → ∞
> and (2/5 + o(1))n² edges, whereas the conjectured formula predicts f₁₂(n) ∼
> 3n²/8. The construction combines five copies of Pegden’s coloring module
> with a K₅-saturated graph of sublinear maximum degree. Saturation gives
> 11-colorings after any edge deletion, while the small maximum degree rules
> out an 11-coloring of the whole graph. The separate question whether f₆(n) ∼
> n²/4 remains open. Notes: GPT-6 Astra was used to generate the mathematical
> proofs and draft the manuscript. GPT-5.6 Sol and Claude Opus 5 were used
> only for editorial review of the exposition. GPT-6 Astra was run in a
> research environment containing earlier results produced by GPT-5.6 Sol and
> Claude Opus 5, but those earlier results did not contribute to the final
> mathematical arguments. The author reviewed the final manuscript and takes
> full responsibility for its content.

**Covers.** The third question at $k=12$, a multiple of $3$, where Toft's
constructions
([[problems/graph_coloring/E0917/claims/1970_01_01_toft|accepted claim]])
match the conjectured coefficient and refute nothing. It does not cover $k=6$
or the second question. Version 7's remark "Other chromatic numbers" (p. 6;
[[../library/graph_coloring/gu_2026_twelve_critical_graphs/remark_p6|remark page]]),
outside Theorem 1 and not part of the forum claim, sketches limits that would
exceed the proposed coefficient at every multiple of $3$ from $12$ on, with
the limit $2/5$ again at $k=12$; nothing here credits it.
