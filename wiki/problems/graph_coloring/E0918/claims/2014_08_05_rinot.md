***

**Claim.** Call a graph $(\aleph_0,\kappa)$-chromatic if its chromatic number
is $\kappa$ and every subgraph on fewer vertices than the graph has is
countably chromatic. The main theorem of Rinot's paper, as the zbMATH review
(Zbl 1363.03022) gives it: if $\square_\lambda$ and $2^\lambda=\lambda^+$
hold for an uncountable cardinal $\lambda$, then for every cardinal
$\kappa\le\lambda$ there is an $(\aleph_0,\kappa)$-chromatic graph of size
$\lambda^+$. Take $\lambda=\aleph_\omega$ and $\kappa=\aleph_1$: the graph
has $\aleph_{\omega+1}$ vertices and chromatic number $\aleph_1$, and every
subgraph on $\aleph_\omega$ vertices is countably chromatic, which is a
positive answer to the second question of
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0918/_index|Problem 918]]. Both hypotheses hold
in the constructible universe $L$, where GCH holds and Jensen's principle
$\square_\lambda$ holds for every uncountable $\lambda$. So, relative to the
consistency of ZF, ZFC does not refute the second question: it is not
disprovable.

**Covers.** The second question of Problem 918 (the part `q2`), on its not
disprovable side, as a consistency statement: under
$2^{\aleph_\omega}=\aleph_{\omega+1}$ and $\square_{\aleph_\omega}$, in
particular in $L$, a graph with $\aleph_{\omega+1}$ vertices and chromatic
number $\aleph_1$ whose subgraphs on $\aleph_\omega$ vertices are countably
chromatic exists. That side alone leaves the second question open: the result
does not show that ZFC fails to prove such a graph exists. It does not cover
the first question.
