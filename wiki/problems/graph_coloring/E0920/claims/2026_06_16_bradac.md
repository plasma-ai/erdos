***

**Claim.** Theorem 1.1 of the third arXiv version of Bradač's paper states
that for each fixed $s\ge3$ there is $c_s>0$ with

$$
r(s,k)\ge c_s\frac{k^{s-1}}{(\log k)^{2s-4}}
$$

for all $k\ge2$
([[../library/ramsey_theory/bradac_2026_off_diagonal_ramsey_numbers/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]];
the problem page of [[problems/ramsey_theory/E0986/_index|Problem 986]]
records that result's standing). In the question's notation, with $k$ the
forbidden clique size and $t$ the independence bound, if $r(k,t)>n$ then
some graph on $n$ vertices has no $K_k$ and no independent set of $t$
vertices, and $\chi(G)\,\alpha(G)\ge n$ gives $\chi(G)\ge n/(t-1)$. Taking
$t$ of order $n^{1/(k-1)}(\log n)^{(2k-4)/(k-1)}$, the least value at which
the Ramsey bound exceeds $n$, yields

$$
f_k(n)\gg_k\frac{n^{1-\frac{1}{k-1}}}{(\log n)^{\frac{2k-4}{k-1}}},
$$

so the displayed inequality holds for every $k\ge4$ with $c_k=(2k-4)/(k-1)$. The
first two versions of the paper (27 May and 11 June 2026) proved only $r(s,k)\ge
k^{s-2+o(1)}$ for fixed $s\ge4$, which through the same transfer gives only
$f_k(n)\ge n^{1-1/(k-2)-o(1)}$; the third version (16 June 2026) reached $s-1$,
and the paper credits that step to an internal model at OpenAI, as the source
card records.

**Covers.** The cases $k\ge5$ of the question, which the site credits to
this bound. The theorem's range $s\ge3$ includes $s=4$, where it reproduces
the bound of Mattheus and Verstraete, whose earlier refereed result has its
own claim page,
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0920/claims/2023_06_06_mattheus_verstraete|Mattheus–Verstraete 2023]];
the site credits the case $k=4$ to them.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0986/claims/2026_06_16_bradac|Bradač's claim page for Problem 986]].

**Acceptance.** The site's curator, Thomas Bloom, marks the problem solved and
credits the positive answer for all $k\ge5$ to this bound through Problem 986,
as the site's problem page, last edited 25 July 2026, records; a comment of 30
June 2026 in the problem's discussion thread had pointed out that Problem 986's
new standing implies this one. The paper is an arXiv preprint. Third-party Lean
formalizations exist. Boris Alexeev's `lean-proofs` repository proves the
problem through Bradač's construction in the linked file `Erdos920.lean`; its
formal authors are Codex and GPT-5.6 Sol, and the formal-conjectures statement
file
([920.lean](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/17d2cec2f5bec8eede237a146ac893375daf4faf/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/920.lean))
has cited it as the formal proof of `erdos_920` since that date. The
autoformalization system Trellis reports two runs that formalize the paper,
linked above at the commits read and described on the claim page of
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0986/claims/2026_06_16_bradac|Problem 986]]. This
corpus has not built any of them, so no `formalized` evidence is listed. Its
proof is not reviewed in this corpus.

**Later claims of the corollary.** Two notes posted on the site's
proof-claims page for the problem derive the displayed inequality for every
$k\ge4$ from this theorem, with the explicit exponent $c_k=(2k-4)/(k-1)$;
both present the inequality as a corollary of this theorem and claim
nothing about the Ramsey construction itself, so neither is an independent
result and neither has a claim page of its own. Miroslav
Lžičař's note, Erdős Problem 920 as a corollary of Bradač's off-diagonal
Ramsey bound, version 1.0 of 23 July 2026
([release](https://github.com/mireklzicar/erdos-920/releases/tag/v1.0),

[forum entry](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/920/proof-claims#proof-claim-117)),
was found with GPT 5.6 Sol according to its submission. Moses Lua's
write-up of 25 July 2026
([PDF](https://github.com/moseslua/erdos920/blob/f99fcf3de092d3a1233dcc49dd3dc467eb811332/erdos_problem_extended_writeup_of_subcontributions_during_920.pdf),
