***

Folkman [Fo70b] proves the statement as asked: if $k\ge0$ and every subgraph $H$
of a graph $G$ contains an independent set of size at least $(n-k)/2$, where $n$
is the number of vertices of $H$, then $G$ has chromatic number at most $k+2$.
The question is from the note of Erdős and Hajnal
([[../library/graph_coloring/erdos_1967_kromatikus_grafokrol_chromatic_graphs/_index|Erdős and Hajnal 1967]]);
the case $k=0$ is immediate, since a graph all of whose subgraphs have an
independent set of half their vertices is bipartite, and the authors could not
settle the case $k=1$. The same note shows that the hypothesis cannot be
weakened to a fraction $c<1/2$ of the vertices: for every such $c$ there are
graphs of arbitrarily large chromatic number whose finite subgraphs all have
independent sets of size at least $c$ times their order. The zbMATH record of
Folkman's paper (Zbl 0209.55003) carries no review, so the theorem is stated
here as the site credits it to the paper.

**Acceptance.** Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas Bloom, labels the
problem proved and credits Folkman [Fo70b] with the proof. The paper is J. H.
Folkman, An upper bound on the chromatic number of a graph, Combinatorial
Theory and its Applications, Colloq. Math. Soc. János Bolyai 4 (1970),
437--457 (zbMATH 0209.55003), a contribution to a colloquium proceedings
volume rather than a journal article, so no `refereed` evidence is listed;
the record gives the year and no finer date, so the page is dated to the
first day of that year. Its proof is not reviewed in this corpus.
