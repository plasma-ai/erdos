***

Rödl [Ro77] proves the statement as asked: for every $k$ there is $f(k)$
such that every graph $G$ with chromatic number at least $f(k)$ contains a
triangle-free subgraph with chromatic number at least $k$. The Crossref
record of the paper states the stronger form proved there: for positive
integers $m$ and $n$ there is $\varphi(m,n)$ such that a graph with
$\chi(G)\ge\varphi(m,n)$ contains a complete subgraph on $m$ vertices or a
triangle-free subgraph $H$ with $\chi(H)=n$, and says that this answers a
question of Erdős and Hajnal. Since a complete graph on $m$ vertices contains
triangle-free subgraphs of chromatic number growing with $m$, the clique
alternative also yields the wanted subgraph, so the printed theorem implies
the statement asked. The paper is not held in the library.

**Formalization.** Three Lean 4 files among the links formalize Rödl's theorem.
Parcly Taxel posted the first in the problem's thread on 20 April 2026, a
formalization made with the help of the system Aristotle; the site's curator
answered that post by updating the page, and the site's label gained its Lean
qualifier on that date. Parcly Taxel posted a second file in the thread on 21
April 2026, a formalization of the same proof made earlier without AI help (its
linked revision dates from 20 April 2026); its theorem `rodl_main` cites Rödl's
paper. The file in Boris Alexeev's repository of Lean proofs is a later copy of
the first: it declares itself a formalization of a solution to the problem with
Rödl as its informal author and names Aristotle and Parcly Taxel as its formal
authors, and its final theorem `erdos923` states that for every type $V$ and
every $n$ there is $k$ such that every simple graph on $V$ with chromatic number
at least $k$ has a subgraph with chromatic number at least $n$ and no triangle.
Taking $V$ infinite recovers the question for finite graphs, since a finite
graph is a graph on $V$ with isolated vertices added. The formal-conjectures
catalog's statement file for the problem
([`FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/923.lean`](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/6fbb54f24ccc2e64dcfaffc28c58950e377110d2/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/923.lean),
read at the commit linked) is tagged research solved and points to the
repository copy as its formal proof. This corpus has not built any of the three
files, so they are links, not `formalized` evidence.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: V. Rödl, On the chromatic number of subgraphs of a
given graph, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 64 (1977), no. 2, 370--371, DOI
10.1090/S0002-9939-1977-0469806-4; the Crossref record gives the publication
month June 1977 and no day, so the page is dated to the first of that month.
Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas Bloom, labels the problem PROVED (LEAN)
and credits Rödl [Ro77] with the proof. Nothing on this page rests on this
project's own review.
