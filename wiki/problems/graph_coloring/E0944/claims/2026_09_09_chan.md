***

Alex Chan claims an explicit graph $G$ on $60$ vertices with $\chi(G)=4$,
$\chi(G-v)=3$ for every vertex $v$ and $\chi(G-e)=4$ for every edge $e$: a
$4$-chromatic graph in which every vertex is critical and no edge is. The
repository describes $G$ as the Cayley graph of the group on
$\mathbb{Z}/12\times\mathbb{Z}/5$ with product
$(e,i)(f,j)=(e+f \bmod 12,\ i+2^e j \bmod 5)$ and the six-element connection
set $\{(1,0),(11,0),(5,1),(7,2),(6,0),(6,3)\}$: simple, connected,
vertex-transitive, $6$-regular, with $180$ edges.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Alex Chan
(account Chanalex) on 11 September 2026, giving "ChatGPT sol" as the AI used:

> Graph for k=4

**Covers.** The case $k=4$, $r=1$ of the question, which is Dirac's 1970
conjecture at $k=4$. The claim says nothing about $r\ge2$ at $k=4$; for
$k\ge5$ the repository deduces the $r=1$ statement with Jensen's theorem
([[../library/graph_coloring/jensen_2002_dense_critical_vertex_critical_graphs/_index|Jensen 2002]])
as an explicit hypothesis and formalizes no infinite family, and those cases
were already settled in the literature.

**Standing.** The result was first posted on 9 September 2026, when the
repository `alexgocardinal/dirac-critical-graph` was created public with the
graph data and the Lean certificates in its initial commit; the Harvard
Dataverse package Dirac60 v1.0.0, of Lean certificates and reproducibility
materials, was published on 10 September 2026, and the proof claim was posted
on the erdosproblems.com forum on 11 September 2026 by the user Chanalex, with
the claimant's name Alex Chan, a one-line summary announcing a graph for
$k=4$, a Google Drive manuscript as the proof link and the repository as the
formalization link; its tools field reads "ChatGPT sol" (so ChatGPT is the
system named). The repository's README at the linked commit names an
accompanying manuscript by Chan, "Feasibility in Spectrum Repacking and the
Resolution of Dirac’s Conjecture", describes two Lean 4 certificates built
with Lean 4.19.0 and Mathlib whose axiom reports contain no `sorryAx`, says
that the concrete finite checks use `native_decide`, so the stated trust
boundary includes `Lean.ofReduceBool`, and points to the archived verification
package at Harvard Dataverse. This corpus has not built the certificates, so
no `formalized` evidence is listed, and the site lists the claim without an
acceptance. The review note of the bounty site Conjectures.io on the certified
proof of the whole question
([[problems/graph_coloring/E0944/claims/2026_09_11_kruer_kohlmeyer|claim page]])
describes this claim, and Kenta Kitamura's Lean proof of the same case
published on 10 September 2026
([[problems/graph_coloring/E0944/claims/2026_09_10_kitamura|claim page]]), as
narrower $k=4$, $r=1$ formalizations, and its exposition of that proof says
that Chan and Kitamura retain credit for the case. The claim is `claimed`: no
independent acceptance and no refereed publication is recorded.
