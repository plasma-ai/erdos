***

**Claim.** There is a finite graph $G$ with $\chi(G)=4$ in which every vertex
is critical and no edge is: deleting any vertex lowers the chromatic number to
$3$, while deleting any one edge leaves it at $4$. The repository's witness is
an explicit graph on $48$ vertices, presented as a right Cayley graph of the
group $S_4\times C_2$. The Lean 4 file linked above proves the catalog
statement `Erdos944.erdos_944.variants.dirac_conjecture.k_eq_four` of
formal-conjectures in the form $\exists\,V\,G,\ \mathrm{IsErdos944}\ G\ 4\ 1$;
the repository says that the standalone file depends only on the axioms
`propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` and contains no `sorry` or
`admit`, while its modular Lake build uses `native_decide` for the table
evaluations.

**Submission note.** Posted to the site's forum by Kenta Kitamura on 10
September 2026:

> I, Kenta Kitamura (KitaKen1 on GitHub), have submitted PR #5467 to Formal
> Conjectures, reporting a Lean 4 proof of the remaining $k=4$ case of Dirac's
> conjecture (the $r=1$ variant of Erdős Problem #944).
>
> Jensen proved Dirac's conjecture for every $k\geq 5$. Together with the $k=4$
> Lean proof here, this completes Dirac's conjecture for every $k\geq 4$.
>
> GitHub: https://github.com/KitaKen1/erdos-944-dirac-k4-lean Formal Conjectures
> PR: https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/5467 Lean4Web:
> open the standalone proof
>

> 'Classical.choice', and 'Quot.sound'.
>
> This settles Dirac's $r=1$ conjecture. It does not claim to settle the full
> Erdős Problem #944 for arbitrary $r$.

**Covers.** The case $k=4$, $r=1$ of
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0944/_index|Problem 944]], which is Dirac's 1970
conjecture at $k=4$. The repository notes that together with Jensen's theorem
for $k\ge5$ this settles Dirac's conjecture for every $k\ge4$; it says nothing
about $r\ge2$.

**Claimant.** Kenta Kitamura, who published the repository
`KitaKen1/erdos-944-dirac-k4-lean` on 10 September 2026 and announced it in
the problem's thread the same day; the comment names ChatGPT GPT-6 ASTRA and
Codex GPT-6 ASTRA as the systems that assisted, and the repository's README
says the proof was produced with assistance from OpenAI Codex and ChatGPT
Astra. Alex Chan's explicit $60$-vertex graph for the same case, a different
witness, was public in its repository from 9 September 2026 and has its
[[problems/graph_coloring/E0944/claims/2026_09_09_chan|own claim page]].

**Standing.** Claimed, not accepted. On 18 September 2026 a formal-conjectures
contributor, Jack McCarthy (GitHub Deicyde), approved Kitamura's pull request
after replaying the pinned $48$-vertex certificate. McCarthy found that its
final theorem matches the catalog's critical-vertex and critical-edge
definitions and uses only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, and
judged that it settles the missing $k=4$ case. A maintainer approved, and the
catalog merged the pull request on 21 September 2026, tagging the $k=4$ case of
Dirac's conjecture research solved and citing this file as its formal proof.
This corpus has not built the file, so no `formalized` evidence is listed. The
erdosproblems.com page labels the problem OPEN and says the $k=4$ case is open
even for $r=1$ (page fetched). The review note of the bounty site Conjectures.io
on the certified proof of the whole question
([[problems/graph_coloring/E0944/claims/2026_09_11_kruer_kohlmeyer|claim page]])
describes this file as a narrower formalization of the $k=4$, $r=1$ case, and
its exposition of that proof says that Chan and Kitamura retain credit for the
case. There is no manuscript and no refereed publication. A catalog pull-request
approval checks that a formal statement and its proof replay in the catalog's
build; it is not a named reviewer's examination of the argument, so it is not
`reviewed` evidence, and `formalized` evidence needs this corpus's own build and
audit of the file.
