***

Heinz-Jürgen Voss proves, in *Graphs having circuits with at least two
chords*, that every $K_4$-free graph with chromatic number $4$ contains an odd
cycle with at least two chords (edges of the graph joining two nonconsecutive
vertices of the cycle, the diagonals of
[[problems/graph_coloring/E1091/_index|Problem 1091]]). This answers the
problem's first question in the affirmative. Its theorem is stated here as the
site and the paper of Alexeev, Putterman, Sawhney, Sellke and Valiant state
it. Erdős had asked for one diagonal, and Larson [La79] proved that, together
with the structural conjecture of Bollobás and Erdős it rests on: a $K_4$-free
graph with no odd cycle carrying a diagonal is bipartite, has a cut vertex, or
has a vertex of degree at most $2$. Two chords cannot be raised to three: the
wheel on a pentagon is $K_4$-free, has chromatic number $4$, and no odd cycle
in it has three diagonals.

**Covers.** The first question of Problem 1091: every $K_4$-free graph of
chromatic number $4$ contains an odd cycle with at least two diagonals. It
does not cover the second question, whether a function $f(r)\to\infty$ of
diagonals is forced by local $3$-colorability; that question is answered in
the negative on
[[problems/graph_coloring/E1091/claims/2026_04_08_alexeev_putterman_sawhney_sellke_valiant|the Alexeev–Putterman–Sawhney–Sellke–Valiant page]].

**Acceptance.** Refereed: J. Combin. Theory Ser. B **32** (1982), no. 3,
264–285; the publisher's record dates the issue June 1982 without a day, and
the page's date is the first of that month. Reviewed: Thomas Bloom, the
site's curator, marks the problem solved and records that Voss answered the
first question in the affirmative. The Lean file for the problem in Boris
Alexeev's lean-proofs collection, linked above at its pinned commit, names
OpenAI Codex as its author, credits the theorem to Voss in its docstring and
proves it for finite graphs (`erdos_1091_affirmative`) beside the
counterexample family of the second question; the corpus has not built this
development, so the page lists no `formalized` evidence. The
formal-conjectures catalog had no statement file for the problem on
2026-10-07.
