***

Vojtěch Rödl, in *Nearly bipartite graphs with large chromatic number*,
answers in the negative a question of Erdős and Hajnal: whether some constant
$\varepsilon>0$ forces chromatic number at most $3$ on every graph each of whose
subgraphs $H$ can be made bipartite by omitting at most $\varepsilon|H|$ edges.
As the paper's abstract states the result, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a
graph of chromatic number greater than $3$ in which every subgraph $H$ becomes
bipartite after deleting at most $\varepsilon|H|$ edges; the paper also treats
the analogous problem for hypergraphs, and Rödl notes that László Lovász
answered the graph question independently by a different construction.
Applied to [[problems/graph_coloring/E1092/_index|Problem 1092]] with $r=2$:
given $c>0$ and $N$, take Rödl's graph with $\varepsilon<\min(c,1/N)$. Every
$m$-vertex subgraph is the union of a bipartite graph and at most
$\varepsilon m<cm$ edges, and for $m<N$ the bound $\varepsilon m<1$ leaves no
edge to delete, so the subgraph is itself bipartite. The graph therefore meets
the hypothesis at every size for any edge budget that is at least $cm$ for all
$m\geq N$, yet its chromatic number is at least $4>3$. For a fixed $r\geq3$,
join a clique on $r-2$ vertices to Rödl's graph: after deleting at most
$\varepsilon m$ edges, each $m$-vertex subgraph is a bipartite graph joined to
at most $r-2$ clique vertices, which has chromatic number at most $r$, while
the join has chromatic number at least $r+2>r+1$. So no admissible budget $f_r$
satisfies $f_r(m)\geq cm$ for all large $m$, for any $c>0$ and any fixed
$r\geq2$, and both questions, $f_2(n)\gg n$ and $f_r(n)\gg_r n$, have the
answer no. The site's commentary states the conclusion as $f_r(n)=o(n)$; that
is stronger than what the construction gives, which refutes only budgets that
are linear at every large size, while the definition admits budgets that are
large at some sizes and zero at the others. The deduction for $r=2$ was posted
in the site's thread by Quanyu Tang on 3 November 2025, assuming a budget
linear at every size; the small-subgraph step, $\varepsilon<1/N$, is supplied
by Theorem 2 and Corollary 1 of
[A note on Erdős Problem 1092](https://www.ulam.ai/research/erdos1092.pdf)
(April 2026, no author named), posted in the thread on 28 April 2026 by
Przemek Chojecki, who says it was written by GPT-5.5 Pro. The note proves the
negative answer for every fixed $r\geq2$ by the join with a clique on $r-2$
vertices used above, and has no page of its own.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Combinatorica **2** (1982), no. 4, 377–383; the
publisher's record dates the issue December 1982 without a day, and the
page's date is the first of that month. Reviewed: Thomas Bloom, the site's
curator, marks the problem disproved and credits the disproof to Rödl's
construction as noted in the thread. The site's label adds a Lean
qualification, which the community database also records. The
formal-conjectures statement file for the problem states both questions
with the answer false, leaves their proofs as `sorry` and names no formal
proof. The Lean file in Boris Alexeev's lean-proofs collection, linked above
at its pinned commit, declares itself a formalization of Rödl's solution
with Codex and GPT-5.6 Sol as formal authors. It proves the fixed-size
statement that formal-conjectures replaced on 2026-09-15, in which the edge
budget binds only the subgraphs of one size $m$; the corrected statement,
with the budget imposed at every subgraph size, is the one the pinned
formal-conjectures file states and leaves unproved. Under the fixed-size
specification $f_2(m)=0$ for $m\geq5$, as the file's own docstring says,
because the complete graph on four vertices has no $m$-vertex subgraph and
is not $3$-colorable, so its proof does not pass through Rödl's
construction. Third-party Lean outside the set this corpus has built and
audited gives no `formalized` evidence, and the page lists none.
