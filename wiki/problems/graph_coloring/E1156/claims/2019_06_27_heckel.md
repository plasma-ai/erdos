***

**Claim.** Let $G=G(n,1/2)$. Theorem 3 of A. Heckel, *Non-concentration of
the chromatic number of a random graph*, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 34 (2021), no. 1,
245-260, states that for every constant $c<1/4$ there is no sequence of
intervals of length $n^c$ that contains $\chi(G)$ with high probability.
Equivalently, if $\mathbb{P}(\lvert\chi(G)-f(n)\rvert<\omega(n))\to1$, then
$\omega(n)>n^c$ for infinitely many $n$. The paper presents this as the answer
to Erdős's question in his appendix to Alon and Spencer (1992), which
[[problems/graph_coloring/E1156/_index|Problem 1156]] records. The source card
is [[../library/graph_coloring/heckel_2021_non_concentration_chromatic_number_random_graph/_index|Heckel 2021]].

**Covers.** The consecutive-values version of the first question, which is
Erdős's question in his appendix to Alon and Spencer: for no constant $C$ do
intervals of $C$ consecutive values contain $\chi(G)$ with high probability.
In particular $\chi(G)$ is not concentrated on one value, the case $C=1$ of
the first question. The first question with arbitrary sets of $C\geq2$ values
is not settled, and neither is the second question, since the theorem gives
long intervals only for infinitely many $n$.
