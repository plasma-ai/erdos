***

**Claim.** Let $A$ be an infinite set of pairwise coprime positive integers
with property P. Baier's
[[../library/integer_sequences/baier_2004_6/theorem|Theorem]] (p. 2) gives,
for every $\varepsilon>0$,
$|A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}|<(3+\varepsilon)N^{2/3}(\log N)^{-1}$ for infinitely
many $N$, sharpening Schoen's bound $2N^{2/3}$
([[problems/integer_sequences/E0012/claims/2001_04_01_schoen|Schoen's claim page]]).
So for every $c<1/3$ there are infinitely many $N$ with
$|A\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}|<N^{1-c}$: for pairwise coprime sets the second question
of [[problems/integer_sequences/E0012/_index|Problem 12]] has the answer yes.
Baier's P-sets let the two larger elements coincide; on infinite pairwise
coprime sets the two readings agree, since $a\mid2b$ with $\gcd(a,b)=1$ forces
$a\le2$, and such a set contains neither $1$, which divides every sum, nor
$2$, which divides the sum of any two of its other, odd, elements. Baier's
introduction (p. 1) recalls Schoen's counterexample, the squares of the primes
$p\equiv3\pmod4$, which shows that the exponent cannot go below $1/2$.

**Covers.** The second question for pairwise coprime sets. Not the second
question in general, which
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0012/claims/2026_04_03_deepmind|the DeepMind claim page]]
answers no, and not the first or third.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Integers 4 (2004), #A13.
