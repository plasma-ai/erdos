***

**Claim.** Let $s_1<s_2<\cdots$ be the squarefree numbers. For every real
$0\le\alpha\le2$ there is a constant $B(\alpha)>0$ with

$$
\sum_{s_{n+1}\le x}(s_{n+1}-s_n)^\alpha=B(\alpha)\,x+o(x),
$$

so the limit that [[problems/integer_sequences/E0145/_index|Problem 145]]
asks about exists for every $\alpha$ in that range. The source is P. Erdős,
*Some problems and results in elementary number theory*, Publ. Math.
Debrecen 2 (1951), 103--109, on the card
[[../library/integer_sequences/erdos_1951_problems_results_elementary_number_theory/_index|erdos_1951_problems_results_elementary_number_theory]].
The paper's moment asymptotic, display (23) on p. 107, is sketched for
$\alpha=2$ on p. 109 with constant $\sum_t t^2\beta_t$, where $\beta_t$ is
the density of the squarefree numbers $s_n$ with gap $s_{n+1}-s_n=t$; the
paper states (23) for general $\alpha$ with an unnamed constant and says it
can prove (23) only for $\alpha$ below a constant between $2$ and $3$. The
sketch rests on the sieve bound of Lemma 2 (p. 107), which bounds the number
of $s_n<x$ with gap larger than $t$ by a constant times $x/(t^2(\log t)^2)$,
so that the tail of the moment sum is small for $\alpha\le2$. Hooley's 1973
paper and Chan's 2023 paper, which extend the range, both cite the result as
holding for $0\le\alpha\le2$, and the range stated here is theirs.

**Covers.** Every exponent $0\le\alpha\le2$. The later ranges are on their
own pages: Hooley's $0\le\alpha\le3$
([[problems/integer_sequences/E0145/claims/1973_12_01_hooley|Hooley 1973]]),
Huxley's $0\le\alpha<11/3$
([[problems/integer_sequences/E0145/claims/1997_01_30_huxley|Huxley 1997]])
and Chan's $0\le\alpha<3.75$
([[problems/integer_sequences/E0145/claims/2023_10_12_chan|Chan 2023]]);
exponents $\alpha\ge3.75$ are open unconditionally, and Granville derives
every $\alpha\ge0$ from the abc conjecture
([[problems/integer_sequences/E0145/claims/1998_01_01_granville|Granville 1998]]).

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Publicationes Mathematicae Debrecen, volume 2
(1951), pp. 103--109. The site labels the problem OPEN (page last edited 19
October 2025), so its curator's remark that Erdős proved the statement for
$0\le\alpha\le2$ is commentary on an open problem and not acceptance, and no
`reviewed` evidence is listed. The corpus has not reproved the theorem and
awards no tier of its own.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the claim rests on the cited paper.
