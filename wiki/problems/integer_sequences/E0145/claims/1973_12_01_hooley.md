**Claim.** Let $s_1<s_2<\cdots$ be the squarefree numbers. For every real
$0\le\alpha\le3$ there is a constant $B(\alpha)>0$ with
$\sum_{s_{n+1}\le x}(s_{n+1}-s_n)^\alpha\sim B(\alpha)\,x$, so the limit
that [[problems/integer_sequences/E0145/_index|Problem 145]] asks about
exists for every $\alpha$ in that range. The source is C. Hooley, *On the
distribution of square-free numbers*, Canad. J. Math. 25 (1973), no. 6,
1216--1223, whose abstract states that Erdős's formula, known for
$0\le\gamma\le2$, holds for the wider range $0\le\gamma\le3$, by a method
different from the one that gave similar results for the sums of two squares
and for the integers coprime to a given large modulus. Chan's 2023 paper,
which extends the range further, cites this paper for $0\le\gamma\le3$.

The site's reference key [Ho73] prints a different paper of Hooley's, *On
the intervals between consecutive terms of sequences*, Proc. Sympos. Pure
Math. 24 (1973), 129--140, doi:10.1090/pspum/024/0384742; the moment result
for squarefree gaps that the site's commentary credits to Hooley is the
Canadian Journal paper cited above, and the problem page's reference entry
records the collision.

**Covers.** Every exponent $0\le\alpha\le3$; the exponents $0\le\alpha\le2$
are already covered by
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0145/claims/1951_01_01_erdos|Erdős 1951]], and
the exponents $3<\alpha<3.75$ by
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0145/claims/1997_01_30_huxley|Huxley 1997]]
and [[problems/integer_sequences/E0145/claims/2023_10_12_chan|Chan 2023]].

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Canadian Journal of Mathematics, volume 25, issue
6 (December 1973), pp. 1216--1223; the Crossref record of the DOI gives
these data. The site labels the problem OPEN (page last edited 19 October
2025), so its curator's remark that Hooley extended the result to
$\alpha\le3$ is commentary on an open problem and not acceptance, and no
`reviewed` evidence is listed. The corpus holds no card for this paper, has
not reproved the theorem and awards no tier of its own.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the claim rests on the cited paper.
