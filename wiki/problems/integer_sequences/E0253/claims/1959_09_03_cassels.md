***

Cassels, *On the representation of integers as the sums of distinct summands
taken from a fixed set*, Acta Sci. Math. (Szeged) 21 (1960), 111–124, received
3 September 1959 (the page name's date). Its Theorem II states that for every
$\varepsilon>0$ there is a set $C=\{c_1<c_2<\cdots\}$ of positive integers with
(i) $(c_{n+1}-c_n)/c_n^{1/2+\varepsilon}\to0$, (ii) infinitely many elements of
$C$ in every arithmetic progression, and (iii) $S(n)< \varepsilon n$ for every
$n$, where $S(n)$ counts the integers up to $n$ that are sums of distinct
elements of $C$. Take $\varepsilon\le1/2$; then condition (i) gives
$c_{n+1}/c_n\to1$ (for $\varepsilon>1/2$ it does not); condition (ii) gives
every infinite arithmetic progression infinitely many elements of $C$, each a
sum of distinct elements of $C$; and condition (iii) leaves the represented
integers of upper density at most $\varepsilon$, so infinitely many integers
are not represented. The site's remark describes one sequence with gaps
$a_i^{1/2+o(1)}$ whose represented integers have density $0$; Theorem II
gives, for each fixed $\varepsilon$, gaps $o(c_n^{1/2+\varepsilon})$ and
represented integers of upper density at most $\varepsilon$, which is all the
disproof needs. The sequence therefore satisfies the hypotheses of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0253/_index|Problem 253]] and fails its
conclusion: the implication is false. The variant of [Va99], which asks only
that every infinite progression contain at least one sum of distinct terms, is
equivalent, since every tail of an infinite progression is itself an infinite
progression, so the same sequence refutes it. The paper's digest is the
[[../library/integer_sequences/cassels_1960_representation_integers_as_sums_distinct_summands/_index|library
card]], which records Theorem II with its statement checked against the
paper; its proof (Section 3) is not reviewed here.

**Acceptance.** The result is refereed (Acta Scientiarum Mathematicarum), and
the site's curator, Thomas Bloom, records the problem as disproved by Cassels
with this construction (erdosproblems.com/253, page last edited 23 January
2026, accessed 2026-10-07); the curator had no part in the result. Nothing
here is this project's own review.
