***

**Claim.** J. W. S. Cassels, *On the representation of integers as the sums of
distinct summands taken from a fixed set*, Acta Sci. Math. (Szeged) 21 (1960),
111–124, received 3 September 1959 (the page name's date). With $A(n)$ the
number of elements of $A$ up to $n$, its Theorem I (p. 111) states: if
$(A(2n)-A(n))/\log\log n\to\infty$ and $\sum_{a\in A}\|a\theta\|^2=\infty$ for
every real $\theta$ with $0<\theta<1$, then every sufficiently large integer is
a sum of distinct elements of $A$. Both hypotheses imply those of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0254/_index|Problem 254]]: the first gives
$A(2n)-A(n)\to\infty$, hence $|A\cap[1,2x]|-|A\cap[1,x]|\to\infty$ along real
$x$, and since $\|x\|\le1/2$ we have $\|a\theta\|^2\le\|a\theta\|/2$, so the
second gives $\sum_{a\in A}\|a\theta\|=\infty$. The proof (Section 2) applies
the circle method to a thinned subset of $A$; the paper's digest is the
[[../library/integer_sequences/cassels_1960_representation_integers_as_sums_distinct_summands/_index|library card]].

**Covers.** The sets $A$ that satisfy both of Cassels's hypotheses; for them the
conclusion of Problem 254 holds. Not covered: a set for which
$(A(2n)-A(n))/\log\log n$ does not tend to infinity, or for which
$\sum_{a\in A}\|a\theta\|^2$ converges at some $\theta\in(0,1)$; the problem
asks for the conclusion under its weaker hypotheses.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Acta Scientiarum Mathematicarum (Szeged) 21 (1960).
The site's commentary credits Cassels with the statement under these hypotheses,
but the site labels the problem OPEN, so no `reviewed` evidence is listed.
