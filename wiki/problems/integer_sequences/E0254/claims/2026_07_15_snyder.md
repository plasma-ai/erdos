***

Submitted to the proof-claim tab of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0254/_index|Problem 254]] on 15 July 2026 by Colin
Snyder as a full proof claim produced with GPT 5.6 in a custom harness, as the
tab names the system. The claim is the statement as posed: if
$|A\cap[1,2x]|-|A\cap[1,x]|\to\infty$ and $\sum_{n\in A}\|\theta n\|=\infty$ for
every $\theta\in(0,1)$, then every sufficiently large integer is a sum of
distinct elements of $A$, proved in Lean 4 with Mathlib as
`Erdos254.erdos_254 : Erdos254.Statement`, with the axiom list `propext`,
`Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` and no placeholder, according to the submission
and the hosted write-up. The argument, as the write-up describes it: dyadic growth makes the
set of angles at which the distance series converges countable: for each bound,
two distinct angles close enough together cannot both have mass below it (if
they differ by $\delta$, each element of a dyadic shell at scale about
$1/(8\delta)$ contributes a fixed positive amount at the difference angle, and
the shells grow), so each sublevel set is finite; a countable diagonalization
then splits $A$ into three disjoint classes whose sets of distinct subset sums
are syndetic and a disjoint correction class on which every nonzero angle still
diverges; the theorem of Bergelson, Furstenberg and Weiss, that the sum of two
syndetic sets contains a piecewise Bohr set, is proved in the bundle from finite
cyclic Fourier analysis (Parseval's identity for the discrete Fourier transform
on cyclic groups, spectral measures, ultrafilter limits and Wiener's lemma)
instead of from a Kronecker factor; the correction class is used to shift any
large integer into that Bohr set, the third class covers the bounded gaps that
remain, and the summands are distinct because the classes are disjoint.

**The bundle.** The downloadable archive (124,569 bytes;
its entries are dated 11 to 13 July 2026) holds a Lean project of fifty-eight
files under `Research/`, a checker script, and a record entry dated 11 July
2026. Its `check_answer/Statement.lean` defines the distance to the nearest
integer as $\min(\{x\},1-\{x\})$ with $\{x\}$ the fractional part, the count of
members of $A$ in $[1,x]$ over the finite interval of integers, the dyadic
increment as the difference of the counts at $2x$ and $x$, the partial sums of
the distances over $A\cap[1,N]$, completeness as a threshold beyond which every
integer is the sum of a finite set contained in $A$, and `Statement` as: for
every $A\subseteq\mathbb N$, if the dyadic increment tends to infinity along
integer cutoffs and for every $\theta\in(0,1)$ the partial sums tend to
infinity, then $A$ is complete. That file matches the site's wording in the
integer-cutoff reading; the equivalence with a real cutoff is the bundle's own
argument and is not examined on this page. The checker byte-compares the
statement, rejects placeholders, rebuilds the project and prints the axioms; the
hosted page reports the run's output (8,617 jobs and the axiom line) and an
unsigned section headed "Independent referee", written in the first person on
the hosting platform and naming no outside reviewer, whose author compared the
statement with the formal-conjectures encoding and rebuilt the project. That
section is a statement on the hosting platform, not an outside review. The
corpus has not built, kernel-checked or audited the development.
