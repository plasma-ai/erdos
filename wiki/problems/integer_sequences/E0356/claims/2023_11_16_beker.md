***

Beker, *On a problem of Erdős and Graham about consecutive sums in strictly
increasing sequences*, arXiv:2311.10087 (posted 16 November 2023); Bull. London
Math. Soc. 56 (2024), no. 8, 2749–2759 (online 28 May 2024); the statements
below are cited from arXiv v1. For a finite sequence $a$ let $S(a)$ be the set
of its sums $\sum_{u\le i\le v}a_i$ over nonempty blocks of consecutive terms.
Theorem 1.2 states that there is an absolute $c_1>0$ such that for every $n$
there are integers $1\le a_1<\cdots<a_k\le n$ with $|S(a)|\ge c_1n^2$; this
answers [[problems/integer_sequences/E0356/_index|Problem 356]] yes, for every
$n$ rather than only for large $n$. Theorem 1.3 obtains it from the random
sequence $a_i=3i+\epsilon_i$ with independent signs $\epsilon_i$, and Theorem
1.4 from the explicit sequences $a_i=2i-1$, except $a_i=2i$ when $b$ divides
$i$, for $\log n\le b\le n/(\log n)^2$; both proofs bound the additive energy of
the set of partial sums by a constant multiple of its minimum. Proposition 1.5
shows the trivial bound $n(n+1)/2$ is not sharp: $|S(a)|\le(c_4+o(1))n^2$ with
$c_4=(e^2-1)/(2(e^2+1))$. The paper's digest is the
[[../library/integer_sequences/beker_2023_problem_erdos_graham_about_consecutive_sums/_index|library card]],
which cites arXiv v1 (16 November 2023) and records the statements of its pp.
1–2 without examining the proofs. Konieczny's theorem on permutations of
$\{1,\ldots,n\}$, which the site cites beside this result, concerns the variant
without monotonicity ([[problems/number_theory/E0034/_index|Problem 34]]) and is
not a claim on this question.

Accepted: the result is refereed (Bulletin of the London Mathematical
Society), and Thomas Bloom, the site's curator, records the original problem
as solved in the affirmative by Beker, with the page's thanks to the author
(erdosproblems.com/356, page last edited 16 October 2025, accessed
