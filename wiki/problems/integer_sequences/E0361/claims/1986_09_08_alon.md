***

N. Alon, *Subset sums*, J. Number Theory 27 (1987), no. 2, 196-205, received
8 September 1986 (the page name's date). With $f(n,m)$ the largest size of a
set $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ with no subset summing to $m$, Corollary 2.6
gives $\lceil n/3\rceil+1\le f(n,2n)<n/3+3+(1+\sqrt3)r_3(n)$ for $n\ge2$,
where $r_3(n)=o(n)$ is the largest size of a subset of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ with
no three-term progression. So $f(n,2n)=(1/3+o(1))n$, which the paper says
settles a problem of Erdős and Graham. The lower bound is the set of integers
from $\lfloor2n/3\rfloor$ to $n$. The upper bound applies Proposition 2.5, the
quantitative form of the bounded zero-sum Theorem 1.1 that the later claims on
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0361/_index|Problem 361]] use, to find at most
two blocks of at most three elements whose sums give $2n$. In this problem's
notation, $f_{1/2}(n)=(1/6+o(1))n$ along even $n$.

**Covers.** The first question at $c=1/2$ along even $n$, asymptotically;
nothing about odd $n$ or other $c$. Together with the even integers below
$n/2$, which avoid every odd $n$, it shows that $f_{1/2}(n)/n$ does not
converge, the pair of limits $1/6$ and $1/4$ that Beyer de Ryke's Proposition
5.2 states
([[problems/integer_sequences/E0361/claims/2026_07_25_beyer_de_ryke|claim
page]]).
