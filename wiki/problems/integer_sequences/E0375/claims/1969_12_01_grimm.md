***

**Claim.** There is an $n_0$ such that for every $n\geq n_0$, with
$g=[\log n/(2\log\log n)]$, there are pairwise distinct primes
$p_1,\ldots,p_g$ with $p_i\mid n+i$ for $1\leq i\leq g$. The result is in
C. A. Grimm, A conjecture on consecutive composite numbers, Amer. Math.
Monthly 76 (1969), no. 10, 1126--1128, the paper that states the conjecture
of [[problems/integer_sequences/E0375/_index|Problem 375]]; the statement
above is the form in which Ramachandra, Shorey and Tijdeman report Grimm's
theorem in the introduction of their 1975 paper
([[problems/integer_sequences/E0375/claims/1975_03_01_ramachandra_shorey_tijdeman|claim page]]).
The journal record gives the December 1969 issue and no day, so the page is
dated by the first of that month.

**Covers.** Every run of composites $n+1,\ldots,n+k$ with $n\geq n_0$ and
$k\leq\log n/(2\log\log n)$, for which the problem's distinct primes exist.
Longer runs are not covered, so the problem stays open.

**Acceptance.** The result appeared in a refereed journal, the American
Mathematical Monthly, in 1969: the `refereed` evidence. The site labels the
problem FALSIFIABLE, an open label, so its commentary's credit is not
`reviewed` evidence.
