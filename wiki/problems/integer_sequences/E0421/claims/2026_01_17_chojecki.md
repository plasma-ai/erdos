***

**Claim.** There is a set of positive integers of density one whose
increasing enumeration has distinct products on distinct consecutive blocks;
the answer to the question is yes.

**Submission note.** Posted to the site's forum by Przemyslaw Chojecki on 17
January 2026:

> I think I have the final proof. You can check the preprint here. However, I'm
> not really happy with it because it's pretty tedious. I plan on trying to
> formalize it now in Lean with Aristotle and simplify. If you have any ideas on
> how to make it simpler, I'd be grateful!
>
> Here's why it's tedious: when you try to construct a sequence in question, you
> note that greedy sequence looks promising but leaves no control, so the next
> step is to go dyadic and try something like stepwise-greedy dyadic
> construction. This runs into a wall too (but maybe manageable by some
> combinatorics - this is my hope for making it simpler). So eventually I
> settled for a dyadic Moser-Tardos construction (Lovasz local lemma), but that
> comes with a lot of bookkeeping.
>
> The final construction is:
> - reduce preliminary cases (sprinkling)
> - use geometry to treat logarithmic regime (Castryck-Cluckers-Dittmann-Nguyen,
>   Bilu-Tichy and Hajdu-Tijdeman results)
> - let Moser-Tardos framework deal with the rest of the cases
>
> Any feedback welcome!

**The result.** P. Chojecki posted a write-up presented as a proof in the
site's thread on 17 January 2026 ("I think I have the final proof"), the
PDF `erdos421-long.pdf` served by the author's organization, after a write-up
of 16 January (`erdos421.pdf`) that by its own account pushed the determinant
method into the regime of block length of order $\log N$ without settling
the problem. The argument follows the route of Tao's thread comment of 18
October 2025: a preliminary sprinkling reduces the cases; collisions of block
products are integral points on split-product curves, counted in the
logarithmic regime through the dimension-growth bounds of Castryck, Cluckers,
Dittmann and Nguyen, with the degenerate curves classified by the theorems
of Bilu and Tichy and of Hajdu and Tijdeman; and a dyadic deletion chosen by
the algorithmic Lovász local lemma (Moser--Tardos) removes the remaining
collisions. The author's thread comments describe the January work as
produced in a long conversation with GPT-5.2.

**Why it is rejected.** On 18 January 2026 a thread commenter listed four
problems: the split-product equation did not match the collision it encoded
(it took the wrong endpoint of the later block, so its factors carried the
wrong sign), the forward argument on the $y$ side rested on the same
confusion, and two lemmas of the deletion step counted kept points as if the
sprinkled set contained every integer of an interval; a second commenter
reported further issues flagged by an AI check (ChatGPT). On 19 January the
author acknowledged that some of these were real errors and posted a modular
rewrite, `erdos421-mod.pdf`, with the endpoint corrected, the regularity of
the sprinkled set made explicit and the combinatorics rewritten; on 20
January two commenters reported that AI checks (ChatGPT; Gemini 3) still
found gaps in the rewrite, in the local-lemma step among others, and no
reader confirmed it. Neither version was withdrawn in the thread. On 13 July
2026 the author posted a different, shorter proof on a gap-greedy
construction, which the author presented as the final one and which is the
result recorded on
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0421/claims/2026_07_13_chojecki|Chojecki's accepted claim]];
the January argument is superseded by its author and recorded here as
rejected on the acknowledged errors. Nobody is known to have reviewed either
January version, and neither is held in this corpus.
