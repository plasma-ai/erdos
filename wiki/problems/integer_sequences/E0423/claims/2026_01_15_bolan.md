***

**Claim.** Let $a_n$ be the sequence of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0423/_index|Problem 423]]. Matthew Bolan,
*Hofstader Ulam sequence* [sic], a note dated January 2026 in Bolan's repository
`mjtb49/HofstaderUlam`, posted in the site's thread on 15 January 2026,
proves as its Theorem 1 that for every $N>0$ the sequence does not contain
every integer of the interval $[N,2^{3N+2}]$; so the sequence omits
infinitely many positive integers, equivalently $a_n-n$ is unbounded. The
proof supposes the interval filled, so that the $N+1$ powers of $2$ from
$2^{2N+2}$ to $2^{3N+2}$ are each a sum of consecutive terms; no power of $2$
is a sum of two or more consecutive integers, so each such sum must use terms
below $N$, and by the pigeonhole principle two of the powers use the same
least term. An elementary lemma on the equation $2^b c=(n+1)+\cdots+m$ then
bounds the lengths of the two sums and yields a contradiction. The thread
post says the argument gives a lower bound of only about $n+\log^*n$, with
$\log^*$ the iterated logarithm, and, after reading Tang's note, that the
proof adds nothing beyond some explicit constants. The site's commentary and
the acknowledgments of Tang's paper record the two proofs as independent;
Tang's result, with its quantitative bounds, is on
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0423/claims/2026_01_15_tang|Tang's page]].
