***

**Claim.** If $q_1,q_2,\ldots$ are primes with
$q_{n+1}-q_n\ge q_n-q_{n-1}>0$ for $n\ge2$, then
$\liminf_n q_n/n^2\ge1/S$, where, with $p_1=2<p_2=3<\cdots$ the primes,
$S=\sum_{r\ge1}(p_{r+1}-1)^2/(p_2\cdots p_{r+1})=2.84010\ldots$, a sum over
the odd primes $p_{r+1}$; thus $\liminf_n q_n/n^2\ge0.3521\ldots$, the bound
the site records as $>0.352$. The source is Richter, B., *Über die Monotonie
von Differenzenfolgen*, Acta Arith. 30 (1976), 225--227, on the card
[[../library/integer_sequences/richter_1976_uber_die_monotonie_von_differenzenfolgen/_index|richter_1976_uber_die_monotonie_von_differenzenfolgen]].
The proof compares the sequence with an extremal minorant. Since the gaps
never decrease, equal gaps are consecutive, and a run of equal gaps $d$ is an
arithmetic progression of primes. Such a progression has at most $P(d)-1$
terms, or at most $P(d)$ when it starts at $P(d)$, where $P(d)$ is the least
prime not dividing $d$. So each gap value occurs at most $P(d)-1$ times, and
the prime number theorem gives the minorant's growth $n^2/S$.

**Covers.** The lower bound $\liminf q_n/n^2\ge1/S=0.3521\ldots$. Whether
$q_n/n^2\to\infty$, the question of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0455/_index|Problem 455]], is not addressed.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Acta Arithmetica 30, no. 3 (1976), 225--227. The
site's commentary credits the bound to Richter, but the site labels the
problem OPEN, so the commentary is not `reviewed` evidence.

**Date.** The record gives the year 1976 and no finer date, so the page is
dated to the first day of that year.
