***

**Claim.** For the sequence of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0460/_index|Problem 460]], in both formulations
of the greedy rule (the site's, $a_0=0$ with coprimality over $0\le i<k$, and
the monograph's, $a_0=n$ with $1\le i<k$), write
$S_{<n}(n)=\sum_{a_k<n}1/a_k$ for the truncated sum, $P^-(m)$ for the least
prime factor of $|m|$, and

$$
f(n)=\sum_{\substack{1\le a<n\\ P^-(n-a)>a}}\frac1a.
$$

The note *Erdős Problem #460: a trivial divergence and a nontrivial truncated
lower bound* by Przemyslaw Chojecki (ulam.ai, dated 13 January 2026 and
posted in its revised form on 14 January 2026) asserts two results. Its
Proposition 7: every $a<n$ with $P^-(n-a)>a$ occurs among the terms $a_k<n$,
in either formulation, so $S_{<n}(n)\ge f(n)$ for every $n\ge2$. The proof is
that a prime dividing both $n-a$ and $n-a'$ with $a'<a$ divides $a-a'<a$, so
such an $a$ is admissible at every stage once available and the greedy rule
cannot skip it. Its Theorem 8:

$$
\frac1N\sum_{n\le N}f(n)\gg\log\log N,
$$

hence $\limsup_{n\to\infty}f(n)=\infty$ and
$\limsup_{n\to\infty}S_{<n}(n)=\infty$ in both formulations. The proof changes
variables to $m=n-a$, counts the $a$-rough integers up to $N-a$ by Buchstab's
asymptotic for $a\in[N^{1/3},N^{1/2}]$, and sums $1/(a\log a)$ over that
range. The note's
Remark 11 states that $S_{<n}(n)\to\infty$ would follow from $f(n)\to\infty$,
which it does not prove; its Conjecture 13 would give that. The author
reports using GPT-5.2 for most of the work.

**Submission note.** Posted to the site's forum by Przemyslaw Chojecki on 14
January 2026:

> You're right, in either formulation, if one sums over all $k$ without any
> upper cut-off on $a_k$, then the series is automatically divergent: for every
> prime $p>n$ the term $a=n+p$ must occur in the greedy sequence, because before
> reaching $n+p$ all previously chosen translates satisfy $n-a_i\in[-p+1,n-1]$,
> so none is divisible by $p$ (the only multiple of $p$ in that interval is $0$,
> which never arises as $n-a_i$ in these constructions); hence
> $\gcd(n-(n+p),\,n-a_i)=\gcd(p,\,n-a_i)=1$ for all earlier $i$, making $n+p$
> admissible at the moment it becomes available and therefore impossible for the
> greedy rule to skip. Comparing $\sum_{p>n}1/(n+p)$ with $\sum_p 1/p$ gives
> divergence for every fixed $n\ge2$.
>
> If instead one imposes the natural ''nontrivial'' restriction $a_k<n$ (or
> $a_k\le n$), then the above argument disappears and one can at least prove an
> averaged divergence statement from the forced ''rough'' contribution. Writing
> $P^-(m)$ for the least prime factor of $|m|$ (with $P^-(\pm1)=\infty$), set\[
> f(n):=\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}\frac1a\,\mathbf 1_{\{P^-(n-a)>a\}}, \]so that (by the
> forced-rough lemma) the truncated greedy sum $S_{<n}(n):=\sum_{a_k\le
> n-1}1/a_k$ satisfies $S_{<n}(n)\ge f(n)$ for every $n$ in both formulations.
> Averaging over $n\le N$ and changing variables $m=n-a$ yields $\sum_{n\le
> N}f(n)=\sum_{a\le N-1}\frac1a\,\Phi(N-a,a)$, where $\Phi(x,y)$ counts
> $y$-rough integers $\le x$; Buchstab’s asymptotic for $\Phi(x,y)$ (uniform for
> $a\in[N^{1/3},N^{1/2}]$, where $u=\log(N-a)/\log a\in[2,3]$ and hence
> $\omega(u)\gg1$) gives $\Phi(N-a,a)\gg N/\log a$ on that range, and thus\[
> \frac1N\sum_{n\le N}f(n)\ \gg\ \sum_{N^{1/3}\le a\le N^{1/2}}\frac1{a\log a}\
> \asymp\ \log\log N. \]In particular $\limsup_{n\to\infty}f(n)=\infty$ and
> hence $\limsup_{n\to\infty}S_{<n}(n)=\infty$; what remains open is whether
> $f(n)\to\infty$ (and thus $S_{<n}(n)\to\infty$) along all $n$.
>
> I've re-written the note to discuss both cases and give the full proofs. I
> think this solves the problem. See here:
> https://www.ulam.ai/research/erdos-460-v2.pdf

**Covers.** In both formulations, $S_{<n}(n)\ge f(n)$ for every $n\ge2$, and
$\limsup_{n\to\infty}S_{<n}(n)=\infty$. Whether $S_{<n}(n)\to\infty$ is not
settled, and neither are the two restricted sums of the statement.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Standing.** Claimed. The result is asserted in a dated note posted to the
site's discussion thread; no referee, outside reviewer or formalization is
recorded. The site's commentary records the reduction to $f(n)\to\infty$ and
says that standard estimates on rough numbers give the averaged bound, but
the site labels the problem OPEN, so the commentary is not acceptance. The
full claim the same notes first made is recorded as withdrawn on
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0460/claims/2026_01_13_chojecki|its own page]].
