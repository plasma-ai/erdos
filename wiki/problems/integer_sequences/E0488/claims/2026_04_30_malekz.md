**Submission note.** Posted to the site's forum by Malek Zribi on 30 April 2026:

> For the split-core tripod family $A=\{2u,3v,uv\}$ with $\gcd(u,v)=1$, $v\geq
> 3$ odd, $u\geq 4$, and $3\nmid u$, EP-488 holds: $B_A(n,m) := 2m\,F_A(n) -
> n\,F_A(m) > 0$ for all integers $m > n \geq uv$. This includes every prime
> witness tripod $\{2p,3q,pq\}$ with distinct odd primes $p,q>3$. The family is
> primitive but not $2$-primitive, since $uv$ divides $(2u)(3v)$; a first-pass
> literature search located no explicit prior treatment of this exact family.
>
> Note (PDF, 5 pages): note Prepared with 5.5 Pro; the math has been verified by
> hand and by an independent audit script.

**The claim.** Theorem 1 of the note *EP-488 for Split-Core Tripods*, dated
30 April 2026 and signed Malek Z: for $A=\{2u,3v,uv\}$ with $\gcd(u,v)=1$,
$v\ge3$ odd, $u\ge4$ and $3\nmid u$, the set $A$ is primitive with
$\max A=uv$, and the inequality of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0488/_index|Problem 488]] holds for all
integers $m>n\ge uv$, in the form $2mF_A(n)-nF_A(m)>0$ with $F_A(x)$ the
number of positive integers up to $x$ divisible by a member of $A$. The
proof: the lcm pattern $[2u,uv]=2uv$, $[3v,uv]=3uv$ and
$[2u,3v]=[2u,3v,uv]=6uv$ makes the pair overlap of $2u$ and $3v$ cancel
against the triple overlap in inclusion--exclusion, so
$F_A(x)=\lfloor x/2u\rfloor+\lfloor x/3v\rfloor+\lfloor x/a\rfloor-\lfloor x/2a\rfloor-\lfloor x/3a\rfloor$
with $a=uv$; the terms for $2u$ and $3v$ contribute positively, since
$2m\lfloor n/d\rfloor>n\lfloor m/d\rfloor$ whenever $m>n\ge d$; and the
three remaining terms reduce, with $W(k)=k-\lfloor k/2\rfloor-\lfloor k/3\rfloor$,
$q=\lfloor n/a\rfloor$ and $t=\lfloor m/a\rfloor$, to $2tW(q)\ge(q+1)W(t)$
for $1\le q<t$, proved from the residue formula for $W$ with a finite check
for $q\le7$. The note's comment says it was prepared with 5.5 Pro and that
the mathematics was verified by hand and by an independent audit script,
the claimant's own. A computational search reported in the note's last
section, on a conjectured ratio bound over $2{,}781{,}110$ primitive sets
with no violation, is evidence around the problem and not part of the
claim.

**Covers.** The problem's inequality for the family $\{2u,3v,uv\}$ under
the stated hypotheses, which include every tripod $\{2p,3q,pq\}$ with
distinct odd primes $p,q>3$. Not covered, as the note says: other primitive
sets, families of several tripods, and the cases where one of the
hypotheses fails. The family has three primitive elements, so it lies
inside the class that
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0488/claims/2026_03_20_chojecki|Chojecki's note]]
covers by a different argument; the note reports that a first literature
search found no prior treatment of the family.
