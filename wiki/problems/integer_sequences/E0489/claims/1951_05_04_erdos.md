***

**The claim.** Let $s_1<s_2<\cdots$ be the squarefree numbers, the set $B$ of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0489/_index|Problem 489]] for
$A=\{p^2:p\text{ prime}\}$, and for $t\ge1$ let $\beta_t$ be the density of
the $s_i$ with $s_{i+1}-s_i=t$, which exists (Lemma 1, p. 107). Then

$$
\sum_{s_{i+1}\le x}(s_{i+1}-s_i)^2=x\sum_{t\ge1}t^2\beta_t+o(x),
\qquad\sum_{t\ge1}t^2\beta_t<\infty,
$$

so the mean squared gap tends to a finite limit. This is the case $\alpha=2$
of display (23), p. 107, of P. Erdős, *Some problems and results in
elementary number theory*, Publ. Math. Debrecen 2 (1951), 103--109, received
4 May 1951 (the date this page is named by). The paper conjectures (23),
$\sum_{s_{i+1}\le x}(s_{i+1}-s_i)^\alpha=C_\alpha x+o(x)$, for every $\alpha$,
says that it can be proved for $\alpha<A$ with $A$ between $2$ and $3$, and
sketches the proof for $\alpha=2$: Lemma 2 (p. 107) bounds the number of
$s_i<x$ with gap above $t$ by $c_{17}x/(t^2(\log t)^2)$, because a gap of
length $r$ contains at least $r/16$ integers divisible by the square of a
prime above $t\log t/100$, by the Chebyshev prime count and the convergence
of $\sum1/P^2$; displays (28) and (29), pp. 108--109, then compare the sum
over large gaps with Lemma 1's densities. The card
[[../library/integer_sequences/erdos_1951_problems_results_elementary_number_theory/_index|Erdős (1951)]]
records the result. Erdős's 1961 problem list, the site's source for the
problem
([[../library/number_theory/erdos_1961_unsolved_problems/_index|Erdős (1961)]],
printed pp. 236--237), states the result as (I.28.2), with the normalization
$\frac1n\sum_{s_i<n}$, cites this paper for it, and poses the general question
as (I.28.3) for a sequence with $a_k/k^2\to\infty$, adding that under
$a_k<ck^2$ alone the limit need not exist. The paper's closing remarks
(p. 109) bear on the general case: for $a_1<a_2<\cdots$ with
$\sum1/a_i<\infty$ the gap densities of the non-multiples exist, and for every
$\varepsilon$ the gaps above a constant $c_\varepsilon$ contribute less than
$\varepsilon x$ to the first moment below $x$, but for the $a$'s in the
intervals $[2^k,2^k(1+1/k^2)]$ the normalized $(1+\varepsilon)$-moment of the
gaps is unbounded although $\sum1/a_i<\infty$; whether this can happen for
pairwise coprime $a$'s is left open there.

**Covers.** The instance $A=\{p^2:p\text{ prime}\}$, for which $B$ is the
squarefree numbers: the limit exists and is finite. Not covered: any other
$A$.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the journal publication cited above. The site's
commentary credits Erdős with the existence of the limit in this case; on a
problem the site labels OPEN, that credit is context, not acceptance.
