**The claim.** For $A\subseteq\{2,3,\ldots\}$ with $|A\cap[1,x]|=o(\sqrt x)$
let $F_A=\{b_1<b_2<\cdots\}$ be the integers divisible by no member of $A$
(so $b_1=1$), and for $h\ge1$ let $q_A(h)$ be the density of the $n$ for
which the window $[n,n+h-1]$ contains no member of $F_A$. Theorem 1 of the
note *Erdős Problem #489: An extended-limit theorem, a finiteness
criterion, and broad positive results*: each $q_A(h)$ exists, and

$$
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac1x\sum_{b_i<x}(b_{i+1}-b_i)^2=1+2\sum_{h\ge1}q_A(h)
$$

exists in $[0,+\infty]$, so the limit of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0489/_index|Problem 489]] exists once the value
$+\infty$ is allowed, and it is finite exactly when the $q_A(h)$ are summable.
Corollary 2: if $A$ is pairwise coprime and the multiplicative semigroup it
generates has counting function $x^{\alpha+o(1)}$ for some $\alpha\in(0,1)$,
then $q_A(h)\ll h^{-(2-\alpha)}$, so the limit is finite; this covers the
squarefree numbers, for which Erdős proved the existence of the limit (the
accepted partial claim
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0489/claims/1951_05_04_erdos|Erdős's squarefree
case]]), and the other classical sets of $k$-free numbers. The argument: the
sparseness gives $\sum_{a\in A}1/a<\infty$, so $F_A$ has positive density and
every fixed local pattern of $F_A$ has a frequency, by comparison with the
periodic sieve by the members of $A$ up to a threshold; an exact identity writes
the sum of squared gaps below $x$ as $x-1+r(x)^2+2\sum_h E_h(x)$ with $E_h(x)$
the number of empty windows of length $h$ starting below $x$ and $r(x)$ the
overshoot to the next member of $F_A$; the overshoot is $o(\sqrt x)$, because a
gap of length $G$ forces about $G$ distinct members of $A$ in $(G,u+G]$; and a
dyadic truncation turns the identity into the formula. Corollary 2 applies a
short-interval bound of Gorodetsky, Mangerel and Rodgers to the empty-window
counts. The note closes with a literature survey and states that the
unstructured case, finiteness under the sparseness hypothesis alone, remains
open.

**Covers.** The existence of the limit in $[0,+\infty]$, with the formula
above, for every $A\subseteq\{2,3,\ldots\}$ with $|A\cap[1,x]|=o(\sqrt x)$;
and the finiteness asked for by the problem for every such $A$ that is
pairwise coprime and generates a semigroup of index $\alpha\in(0,1)$. Not
covered: finiteness in general, which the note identifies as the only
remaining issue. The full claim on
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0489/claims/2026_07_15_snyder|Snyder's page]]
asserts finiteness for every such $A$.
