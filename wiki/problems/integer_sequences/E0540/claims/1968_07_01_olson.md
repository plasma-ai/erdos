***

**The claim.** Theorem 1 of J. E. Olson, *An addition theorem modulo $p$*,
J. Combinatorial Theory 5 (1968), no. 1, 45--52, p. 45: if
$a_1,\ldots,a_s$ are distinct nonzero residue classes modulo a prime $p$ and
$s>(4p-3)^{1/2}$, then every residue class, $0$ included, is a sum
$\epsilon_1a_1+\cdots+\epsilon_sa_s$ with each $\epsilon_i$ equal to $0$ or
$1$ and not all $0$. Paged as
[[../library/integer_sequences/olson_1968_addition_theorem_modulo/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
of
[[../library/integer_sequences/olson_1968_addition_theorem_modulo/_index|Olson (1968)]].
A set $A\subseteq\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$ with $|A|\ge2\sqrt p$ either
contains $0$ or consists of more than $(4p-3)^{1/2}$ nonzero residues, and in
both cases has a nonempty zero-sum subset; the statement of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0540/_index|Problem 540]] thus holds for prime
$N$ with the constant $2$, Erdős and Heilbronn's conjectured constant. The
paper prints no received date, so the page is named by the issue, July 1968
according to its Crossref record, with the first day of the month standing in
for the unknown day.

**Covers.** The problem for prime $N$: more than $(4p-3)^{1/2}$ distinct
nonzero residues modulo $p$ have a nonempty zero-sum subfamily. Composite $N$
is not covered.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the journal publication. Reviewed: the site's
curator, Thomas Bloom, who is independent of the author, labels the problem
PROVED (LEAN) and his commentary credits the prime case to this paper;
Szemerédi's 1970 paper records in an editor's footnote that Olson proved the
prime case.
