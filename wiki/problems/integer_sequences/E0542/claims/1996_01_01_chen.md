**Claim.** Let $\rho(n)=\max\sum1/a_i$, the maximum taken over collections
of integers $1<a_1<\dots<a_r$ with $[a_i,a_j]>n$ for all $1\le i<j\le r$.
Then $\limsup\rho(n)\le1.0170166$, improving the constant $c=1.017262\ldots$
of Schinzel and Szekeres's Theorem 2. The paper also observes that Erdős's
conjecture $\rho(n)\to1$ would follow from a real function $h$ with $h(x)=0$
for $x<1$, $h(x)\ge0$ and $S(x)=\sum_{k\ge1}h(x/k)\ge x$ for $x>1$ and
$S(x)/x\to1$, and constructs a function with weaker properties, from which
the constant comes. Since $1.0170166<31/30$ and the element $1$ adds nothing
to the question (a set containing $1$ is $\{1\}$, with sum $1$), every
admissible set has reciprocal sum below $31/30$ once $n$ is large, which
answers the first question of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0542/_index|Problem 542]] yes for all
sufficiently large $n$. The theorem is stated as the zbMATH review of the
paper by I. Z. Ruzsa (Zbl 0870.11013) gives it. The site's commentary states
the result in the explicit form
$\sum1/a<1/3+1/4+1/5+1/7+1/11=1.017099\ldots$ for $n>172509$; that form is
the site's and was not read in the paper. The thread's $\rho_n<1.0170166$
for large $n$ agrees with the review. The source is Y.-G. Chen, *On a
problem of P. Erdős*, Acta Sci. Math. (Szeged) 62 (1996), no. 1--2,
101--114.

**Covers.** The first question for all sufficiently large $n$.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Acta Scientiarum Mathematicarum (Szeged) is a
refereed journal. The site's curator, Thomas Bloom, cites the result in the
problem's commentary, which credits the solution of both questions to
Schinzel and Szekeres. The record gives the year 1996 and no month or day,
so the month and day in the page name are placeholders.
