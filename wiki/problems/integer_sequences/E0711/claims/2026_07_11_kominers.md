***

**Claim.** S. D. Kominers, *Long Intervals Without Distinct Multiples of the
First $n$ Positive Integers*, arXiv:2607.10431v1 (11 July 2026, 19 pages),
announced by the author in the thread of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0711/_index|Problem 711]] the same day. With
$f(n,m)$ the least $h\ge0$ such that $(m,m+h]$ holds distinct
$a_1,\ldots,a_n$ with $i\mid a_i$, and $F(n)=\max_mf(n,m)$, Theorem 1.1
states

$$
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{F(n)-f(n,n)}{n\log n}\ge\frac1e,
$$

so for each fixed $c<1/e$ and all large $n$ some interval of length
$c\,n\log n$ holds no system of distinct multiples of $1,\ldots,n$.
Corollary 1.2 gives $F(n)-f(n,n)>A\,n\log n/\log\log n$ for every fixed
$A>0$ and all large $n$. The site's open interval adds one to every $f(n,m)$
and leaves the difference unchanged. The proof applies the Erdős–Pomerance
smooth-number obstruction at starting points $m\asymp n\log n$, with a
smoothness threshold of order $\log n$ and the local saddle-point estimates
of Hildebrand and Tenenbaum.

**Covers.** The second question, answered yes at the scale $n\log n$;
Remark 1.3 presents this as a strengthening of van Doorn's answer, on
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0711/claims/2026_01_05_van_doorn|van Doorn's claim page]].
It does not cover the first question: the bound is consistent with
$n^{1+o(1)}$, and Proposition 6.2, $\max_{m\le n^C}f(n,m)\le n^{1+o(1)}$ for
each fixed $C>0$, reaches only starting points up to a fixed power of $n$,
so it settles no instance of the first question.

language models assisted with computations, analysis, synthesis and
