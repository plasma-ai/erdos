***

**Claim.** The answer is yes. Theorem 2 of B. Green, *The Cameron-Erdős
conjecture*, Bull. London Math. Soc. 36 (2004), no. 6, 769--778, states
that the number of sum-free subsets of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ is asymptotically
$c(n)2^{n/2}$, where $c(n)$ takes one constant value for odd $n$ and another
for even $n$. This proves Conjecture 1 of Cameron and Erdős, that the count
is $O(2^{n/2})$, and with the trivial lower bound $f(n)\ge2^{\lceil n/2\rceil}$
(every subset of the integers in $(n/2,n]$ is sum-free) it gives
$f(n)=2^{(1+o(1))n/2}$, the asked exponent, in the sharper form of an
asymptotic. The source card
[[../library/integer_sequences/green_2004_cameron_erdos_conjecture/_index|green_2004_cameron_erdos_conjecture]]
digests the proof: a family of $2^{o(n)}$ almost sum-free containers, built
by Fourier-analytic granularization on $\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$, covers every
sum-free subset, and a structural step shows that almost every sum-free
subset consists of odd numbers or lies in $\{\lceil(n+1)/3\rceil,\ldots,n\}$,
whose sum-free subsets Cameron and Erdős had shown to number asymptotically
$c(n)2^{n/2}$. The paper notes that the bound $2^{n/2+o(n)}$ was known earlier
(Alon; Calkin; Erdős and Granville), so the exponent alone predates it, with
its own claim pages
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0748/claims/1990_03_01_calkin|Calkin 1990]]
and [[problems/integer_sequences/E0748/claims/1991_06_01_alon|Alon 1991]];
the site's label rests on the full conjecture.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society,
volume 36, issue 6, pages 769--778, published online 19 October 2004 and in
print in November 2004 (Crossref record of DOI 10.1112/S0024609304003650). Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas Bloom, who is
independent of the author, labels the problem PROVED, and his commentary
(accessed 2026-09-05 and 2026-10-07, the discussion thread and the
proof-claim tab empty at both) credits the bound
$f(n)\ll2^{n/2}$ and the two-valued asymptotic to Green and, independently,
to Sapozhenko, whose result is the sibling page
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0748/claims/2003_01_01_sapozhenko|Sapozhenko 2003]].
Read depth: Theorem 2 and the introduction were read; the proof was not,
and nothing is independently reviewed by this project.
