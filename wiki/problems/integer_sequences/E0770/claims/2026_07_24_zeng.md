***

**Claim.** With $h(n)$ and $P(n)$ as on the page of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0770/_index|Problem 770]] and
$S(n)=\lfloor\sqrt{4n}\rfloor+1$, Jeffrey Zeng's partial proof claim, submitted
to the site's proof-claim tab on 24 July 2026 and made, the listing says,
using an OpenAI internal model, asserts for every $n\ge2$ that

$$
P(n)\le h(n)\le\max\{P(n),S(n)\},
$$

so $P(n)>2\sqrt n$ forces $h(n)=P(n)$, and odd $n$ satisfy $h(n)\le S(n)$.
Its sharper criterion: if $p=P(n)>2$ and the number $C(p)$ of ordered coprime
pairs in $\{1,\ldots,p\}^2$ exceeds $n$, then $h(n)=p$. The listing's Notes
carry the whole ordinary argument: a prime $q$ dividing every $a^n-1$ for
$2\le a\le M$ exceeds $M$ and puts $1,\ldots,M$ into the $n$-torsion subgroup
of $\mathbf F_q^*$, of order at most $n$; when $q>M^2$ the reduced fractions
with numerator and denominator at most $M$ stay distinct in that subgroup,
which has too few elements once $M\ge S(n)$ or $C(M)>n$; when $q\le M^2$, a
signed pigeonhole representation (for even $n$) or the least quadratic
nonresidue bound (for odd $n$) rules $q$ out. The listing says the restriction
is needed, since $n=86$ has $P(n)=3$ and $h(n)=5$, and calls the result
partial, with novelty and priority undetermined. The library's
[[../library/integer_sequences/zeng_2026_collective_coprimality_threshold/_index|source card]]
records the listing, and its
[[../library/integer_sequences/zeng_2026_collective_coprimality_threshold/partial_threshold_theorem|result page]]
reconstructs the proof with its five elementary lemmas, author-recorded.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Jeffrey
Zeng (account jeffzeng) on 24 July 2026, giving "an OpenAI internal model" as
the AI used:

> AI-assisted, Lean-formalized partial result for #770. Use the collective-gcd,
> strict-\(M>2\) definition of \(h(n)\). Let \(P(n)=\max\{p\text{ prime}:p-1\mid
> n\}\) and \(S(n)=\lfloor\sqrt{4n}\rfloor+1\). For every positive \(n\),\[
> P(n)\le h(n)\le\max\{P(n),S(n)\},\qquad P(n)>2\sqrt n\Rightarrow h(n)=P(n).
> \]If \(n\) is odd, \(h(n)\le S(n)\). More sharply, if \(p=P(n)>2\) and
> \(C(p)=\#\{(a,b):1\le a,b\le p,\gcd(a,b)=1\}>n\), then \(h(n)=p\), including
> cases with \(p\le2\sqrt n\). These are partial results: the density, liminf,
> and general \(\varepsilon>0\) questions remain unresolved. Novelty and
> priority have not been determined. AI disclosure: an OpenAI internal model.
> Notes: Put \(G(n,M)=\gcd_{2\le a\le M}(a^n-1)\). If \(q\mid G(n,M)\), then
> \(q>M\), and \(H=\{x\in\mathbf F_q^*:x^n=1\}\) has at most \(n\) elements. For
> \(q>M^2\), reduced fractions \(a/b\), \(1\le a,b\le M\), inject into \(H\).
> Their count is \(C(M)\ge M^2/4+M\), so \(M\ge S(n)\) or \(C(M)>n\) gives a
> contradiction. For \(q\le M^2\) and even \(n\), Dirichlet gives \(x=a/k\) with
> \(1\le k\le M\) and \(0<|a|<M\), so every \(x\ne0\) satisfies \(x^n=1\); hence
> \(q-1\mid n\), contradicting \(q>P(n)\). For odd \(n\), all elements of \(H\)
> are squares and the least quadratic nonresidue is at most \(\lceil\sqrt
> q\rceil\), forcing \(q>M^2\). Fermat gives \(P(n)\le h(n)\). The restriction
> is necessary: \(n=86\) has \(P(n)=3\) but \(h(n)=5\).

**Covers.** The third question for every fixed $\epsilon>1/2$: since
$n^\epsilon>2\sqrt n$ for large $n$, $P(n)>n^\epsilon$ then implies
$h(n)=P(n)$. Not covered: the endpoint $\epsilon=1/2$ and every smaller
positive $\epsilon$, the existence of the densities $\delta_p$ of the first
question, and the limit inferior of the second.
