***

**Claim.** Write $H(x,K)$ for the least number of integers $m\le x$ divisible
by no element of $A$, the minimum taken over all sets $A$ of integers greater
than $1$ with $\sum_{a\in A}1/a\le K$. Ruzsa proves that for every $K\ge1$

$$
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\log H(x,K)}{\log x}=e^{1-K}
$$

(Theorem I), so $H(x,K)=x^{e^{1-K}+o(1)}$, and that there are positive
constants $c_1,c_2$ with

$$
\frac{c_1x}{\log x}<H(x,1)<\frac{x}{(\log x)^{c_2}}
$$

(Theorem II). For a fixed $C>1$ the exponent $e^{1-C}$ is below $1$, so
some admissible $A\subseteq[2,x]$ leaves fewer than $x/(\log x)^c$ integers
unsifted for every $c>0$ once $x$ is large: the answer to the question is no.
For $C=1$ Theorem II gives the asked lower bound with $c=1$, and for
$0<C<1$ the union bound leaves at least $(1-C)x$ integers unsifted, as the
site's commentary notes. The question is therefore answered yes for
$0<C\le1$ and no for $C>1$. Read for every $C>0$, the reading Erdős expected
to hold, the asked bound fails, so the result is a disproof, and the positive
answer for $0<C\le1$ is part of the same theorem. The source is
I. Z. Ruzsa, On the small sieve. II. Sifting by composite numbers, J. Number
Theory 14 (1982), no. 2, 260--268, DOI 10.1016/0022-314X(82)90051-8; the page
is dated to the issue month, April 1982, as the Crossref record gives it, and
the page name uses the first of the month. Library home:
[[../library/primes/ruzsa_1982_small_sieve_ii_sifting_composite_numbers/_index|ruzsa_1982_small_sieve_ii_sifting_composite_numbers]].

**Formulation.** The problem page's corrected Statement excludes $1$ from
$A$, as Ruzsa's definition (1.3) does, and this claim is valued against it.
Ruzsa's sets avoid $1$, so his negative answer for $C>1$ holds for the site's
wording as well. Theorem II's upper bound and the upper bound of Theorem I
both rest on the Schinzel--Szekeres construction, which is why the site names
that example as the reason the lower bound would be best possible.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the paper appeared in the Journal of Number Theory.
Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas Bloom, who is independent of the author,
labels the problem SOLVED, with the page last edited 8 April 2026, and his
commentary attributes the negative answer for fixed $C>1$ and the lower bound
at $C=1$ to this paper, and records that Erdős acknowledged in his 1980 survey
that Ruzsa's construction had overturned his expectation; on 2026-09-05 and on
2026-10-07 the thread held two comments and the proof-claim tab was empty.

**Related results.** Saias (1998) proves the matching upper bound
$H(x,1)\ll x/\log x$, which makes the $C=1$ case sharp but does not bear on
the question's lower bound. Weingartner (2025) sharpens Theorem I to the
exact order $H(x,C)\asymp x^{e^{1-C}}/\log x$ uniformly for $1\le C\le Z$
([[problems/integer_sequences/E0784/claims/2023_10_19_weingartner|Weingartner's claim]]).
Erdős and Ruzsa (1980) show that when $A$ consists of primes a positive
proportion of the integers up to $x$ is always left unsifted; that is a
restricted variant, not this question.
