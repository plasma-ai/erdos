**Claim.** Let $F(n)$ be the largest size of a set $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$
such that $a\nmid bc$ whenever $a,b,c\in A$ with $a\ne b$ and $a\ne c$, the
case $b=c$ included, which the manuscript calls strongly 2-primitive and which
is the site's condition. Theorem 1.1 states that, as $n\to\infty$,

$$
F(n)=\pi(n)+\Bigl(\frac{27}{2}+o(1)\Bigr)\frac{n^{2/3}}{(\log n)^2},
$$

so the constant $C$ the problem asks for exists and equals $27/2$. The
source is P. Chojecki, The second term for strongly 2-primitive sets, a
five-page manuscript served by the author's organization (PDF metadata dated
13 July 2026, posted in the site's thread the same day), with the result page
[[../library/integer_sequences/chojecki_2026_second_term_strongly_2_primitive_sets/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]]
on the
[[../library/integer_sequences/chojecki_2026_second_term_strongly_2_primitive_sets/_index|source card]].
The upper bound (Proposition 2.4) follows Erdős's 1938 argument: every
integer up to $n$ is a product of two members of a basis made of four classes
(the integers up to $n^{3/5}$, the primes in $(n^{3/5},n]$, the products $pq$
of two primes up to $n^{1/3}$, and the products $qr$ of primes $q$ and $r$
with $n^{1/3}<q\le n^{2/5}$ and $r\le n/q^2$), a strongly 2-primitive set has at
most as many members as the basis, and the prime number theorem with partial
summation gives the counts $(9/2+o(1))S$ and $(9+o(1))S$ of the last two
classes, $S=n^{2/3}/(\log n)^2$. The lower bound (Proposition 3.4) packs a
linear family of prime triples with products at most $n$, built from proper
edge-colorings between logarithmic bins of primes near $n^{1/3}$, and takes
the unused primes together with the triple products; the cell weights sum to
$(27/2-o(1))S$.

**Provenance.** The manuscript's byline footnote declares AI assistance in
exploring the argument and writing the text, and the site's commentary
attributes the proof to GPT 5.6 Sol prompted by the author; the manuscript
names one human author, who is the claimant.

**Acceptance.** Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas Bloom, who neither
submitted nor co-wrote the claim, labels the problem PROVED (LEAN) with the
page last edited 14 July 2026, and the curator's commentary credits the
asymptotic to GPT 5.6 Sol prompted by Chojecki, with the manuscript linked; the
curator's thread comment of 14 July 2026 states that the
upper-bound proof is Erdős's own 1938 argument with its constant tracked and
that the lower bound is the same construction as Erdős's, reduced to a linear
3-uniform hypergraph on primes; the page as accessed 2026-09-18 and
2026-10-07 shows thirteen comments, one proof claim and no exposition.
Not refereed: the manuscript was posted to arXiv on 14 July 2026
(arXiv:2607.15306, one version, same title and author; record read
2026-10-02), which is not refereeing, and no journal version or written
expert review of the manuscript was found (Crossref bibliographic query of

developments are linked, and neither is counted as `formalized`. The

repository, linked at the repository's head of 10 September 2026, proves the
