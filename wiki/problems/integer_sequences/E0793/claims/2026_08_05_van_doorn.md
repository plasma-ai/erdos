**Claim.** For $k\ge2$ let $F_k^{\mathrm{rep}}(n)$ be the largest size of a
set $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ in which no member $a$ divides a product
$b_1\cdots b_k$ of members of $A\setminus\{a\}$, repetitions allowed, and
$F_k^{\mathrm{dist}}(n)$ the same with the $b_i$ distinct; let
$S=n^{2/(k+1)}/(\log n)^2$. The theorem `main` of the Lean file states that
for every $k\ge2$ and $\varepsilon>0$, for all large $n$,

$$
\pi(n)+(\Lambda_{k+1}-\varepsilon)S\le F_k^{\mathrm{rep}}(n)\le
F_k^{\mathrm{dist}}(n)\le\pi(n)+(\Lambda_{k+1}+\varepsilon)S,
$$

where $\Lambda_r$ is a dyadic packing constant defined as a supremum, and the
theorem `Lambda_limit` states $\Lambda_r\to e^2$. The case $k=2$ of
$F_k^{\mathrm{rep}}$ is the $F(n)$ of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0793/_index|Problem 793]], whose condition
allows $b=c$, so
$F(n)=\pi(n)+(\Lambda_3+o(1))n^{2/3}/(\log n)^2$: the constant $C$ exists,
and it is the same for the convention requiring $b\ne c$. The file does not
evaluate $\Lambda_3$; Chojecki's theorem gives the value $27/2$
([[problems/integer_sequences/E0793/claims/2026_07_13_chojecki|its claim page]]).
The file also states two self-contained corollaries for large $k$, with
constants $e^2\pm\varepsilon$.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Wouter van
Doorn (account Woett) on 5 August 2026, giving "GPT-5.5 Pro and Aristotle" as
the AI used:

> Let $F_k(n)$ denote the cardinality of the largest set $A \subseteq \{1, 2,
> \ldots, n\}$ such that $a \mid b_1b_2 \cdots b_k$ with $a, b_1, \ldots, b_k
> \in A$ implies $a \in \{b_1, b_2, \ldots, b_k\}$. Then Erdős conjectured the
> existence of a constant $c_k$ such that
> $$
> F_k(n) = \pi(n) + \frac{(c_k + o(1))n^{2/(k+1)}}{\log^2 n}.
> $$
> The case $k = 2$ is the original problem and was solved (with constant $c_2 =
> 27/2$) here and formalized here. This proof claim is mainly to record that the
> generalization is now also formalized with a constant $c_k$ that converges to
> $e^2$ as $k$ goes to infinity. Notes: I feel a bit guilty sharing this, as I
> have not digested the proof myself and don't expect to in the near future. But
> I hope it's sufficiently interesting anyway.
