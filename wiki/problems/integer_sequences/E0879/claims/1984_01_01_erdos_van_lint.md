***

**Claim.** In *On two unconventional number theoretic functions and on some
related problems* (Calcutta Math. Soc. Diamond-cum-Platinum Jubilee
Commemoration Volume, Part I (1984), 113–121; library card
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/erdos_1984_two_unconventional_number_theoretic_functions_related/_index|erdos_1984_two_unconventional_number_theoretic_functions_related]]),
Erdős asks on p. 120 whether for $n>n_0(r)$ some element of an optimal
admissible set must have more than $r$ prime factors, and states, right after
his joint result with van Lint on $G(n)$, that the case $r=1$ is easy: for
large $n$ an optimal set contains an element that is not a prime power. He
adds that a simple computation, which he did not carry out, would find the
largest $n$ for which every element of an optimal set is a prime power. No
proof is given. The site credits the result to Erdős and van Lint. In the
notation of [[problems/integer_sequences/E0879/_index|Problem 879]] this is
the second question at $k=2$.

**Covers.** The second question at $k=2$ only.

**Standing.** Claimed: the result is stated without proof, and the volume is a
society's commemoration volume, not a journal, so it is not listed as
refereed. The site's credit on a problem it labels OPEN is commentary, not
acceptance.
