***

**Claim.** P. Erdős, *On consecutive integers*, Nieuw Arch. Wisk. (3) 3
(1955), 124--128, Theorem 1, printed p. 124: "There is a constant $c_1>1$ so
that $f(k)\le c_1\frac{k}{\log k}$ (1). In other words the sequence
$u+1,u+2,\ldots,u+t$, $t=[c_1\frac{k}{\log k}]$, $u\ge k$ has at least one
prime $>k$"
([[../library/factorials_binomials/erdos_1955_consecutive_integers/theorem_1|result page]]),
where $f(k)$ is the function of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0961/_index|Problem 961]]. The paper leaves
$c_1$ unspecified; the constant $3$ in the site's display $f(k)<3k/\log k$ is
Erdős's 1976 restatement of this theorem. The journal record gives the year
and no day, so this page carries the first of January.

**Covers.** The upper bound $f(k)\le c_1k/\log k$ for some absolute constant
$c_1>1$; the order of $f(k)$ stays open.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Nieuw Archief voor Wiskunde. The site labels the
problem OPEN, so its commentary gives no `reviewed` evidence.
