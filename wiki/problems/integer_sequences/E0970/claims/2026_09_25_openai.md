***

**Claim.** The manuscript *A quadratic bound for Jacobsthal's function* of
the OpenAI mathematics release, dated 25 September 2026 and authored by
OpenAI (its intake card is
[[../library/integer_sequences/openai_2026_quadratic_bound_jacobsthal_function/_index|openai_2026_quadratic_bound_jacobsthal_function]],
with the statement on its
[[../library/integer_sequences/openai_2026_quadratic_bound_jacobsthal_function/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1 page]]),
states as its main theorem, and claims to prove, that Jacobsthal's function
$h(k)$, the least $m$ such that every block of $m$ consecutive integers
contains an integer coprime to any given positive $n$ with at most $k$
distinct prime factors, satisfies

$$
h(k)\le C\,\frac{k^2}{(\log\log 3k)^2}\qquad\text{for every }k\ge1,
$$

with one absolute constant $C>0$, uniformly over the set of prime divisors and
the position of the block. This contains Jacobsthal's conjecture
$h(k)\ll k^2$, the displayed question of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0970/_index|Problem 970]], with an
iterated-logarithm saving. Of it, the quadratic bound $h(k)\le Ck^2$ is
accepted here through the Lean declaration `erdos_970_quadratic` named below;
the saving by $(\log\log 3k)^2$ is the manuscript's claim. The argument sieves
one forbidden class at each prime up to $z$ with a lower-bound sieve at the
critical parameter $2$ (Theorem 1.2 of the manuscript, a survivor count on an
interval of length $z^2/(\log z)^2$), then removes the remaining large divisor
primes by an upper sieve; the constant is not made explicit and, by the
manuscript's stated conventions, need not be effective. The release's
formalization page for its family 021 says that the order of magnitude of
$h(k)$ is not determined.

**Covers.** The displayed question "is it true that $h(k)\ll k^2$?" is
answered yes. One absolute $C>0$ gives $h(k)\le Ck^2$ for every $k\ge1$.
Here $h$ counts $n$ by distinct prime factors, and a block of consecutive
integers may start at any integer. The order-of-magnitude question, which
carries the OPEN label, is not settled.

**Formalization.** The release's Lean tree at the pinned revision proves the
declaration `OAI.Erdos970.Erdos970Final.erdos_970_quadratic`, in
`OAI/NumberTheory/Jacobsthal/Conclusions/QuadraticBound.lean`, the main
result the release's catalog `lean/formalization.yaml` lists for this
manuscript. Its statement, `JacobsthalQuadratic`, fixes one real $C>0$
before $k$ and asks, for every $k\ge1$, for some $m\le Ck^2$ with
`IsJacobsthalBound k m`; that predicate says that for every $n>0$ with at
most $k$ distinct prime factors and every integer $a$, some $a+i$ with
$0\le i<m$ has $\gcd(|a+i|,n)=1$. The predicate holds for every larger $m$
once it holds for $m$, so the declaration states $h(k)\le Ck^2$; it is not
vacuous, since $m=0$ fails at $n=1$; counting distinct prime factors is the
Formulation of the problem page, and the bound also holds when factors are
counted with multiplicity; signed starting points are harmless. The
comparator challenge `ComparatorChallenges/Jacobsthal.lean`, with its
configuration `ComparatorChallenges/Jacobsthal.json`, pins the same
statement in the challenge form. The release also proves the stronger
declaration `erdos_970_iterated_log` (the bound displayed above, in
`Conclusions/IteratedLogBound.lean`, challenge
`ComparatorChallenges/JacobsthalImproved.lean`), which would also suffice
because $\log\log 3k>0$ for $k\ge1$; this corpus has not built or
axiom-checked that declaration, and the accepted claim rests on the
quadratic declaration alone.
