***

**Claim.** Let $p(a,q)$ be the least prime congruent to $a$ modulo $q$. The
manuscript *A Complete Candidate Proof of Erdős Problem 971* by KyungMin Han
(revised 26 July 2026) states, as its main theorem, that there are absolute
constants $c,C>0$ and $q_0$ such that for every $q\ge q_0$

$$
\#\{a\bmod q:\ (a,q)=1,\ p(a,q)>(1+c)\,\phi(q)\log q\}\ \ge\ C\,\phi(q),
$$

which is the question of [[problems/integer_sequences/E0971/_index|Problem 971]]
answered yes. The author presents it as a candidate proof, unconditional
except for the correctness of two cited published theorems, and asks for
review by specialists.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by KyungMin
Han (account Dogcake) on 25 July 2026, giving "GPT 5.6 Pro" as the AI used:

> For\[ R=\left\lfloor\frac{\phi(q)\log q}{q}\right\rfloor, \qquad X=qR,
> \]write\[ N_a(X)=\#\{p\le X:p\equiv a\pmod q\}, \quad
> C_2=\sum_{(a,q)=1}\binom{N_a(X)}2, \quad C_3=\sum_{(a,q)=1}\binom{N_a(X)}3.
> \]The proposed analytic route is\[ V(X;q)\gg X\log q \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
> C_2\gg\phi(q), \]using the pointwise Friedlander-Goldston lower bound for
> Hooley's variance, and\[ C_3\ll\phi(q), \]using a uniform upper-bound sieve
> for the three forms\[ p,\qquad p+rq,\qquad p+sq, \]followed by a uniform
> average of their singular series. At primes dividing 'q', the local factor is
> exactly '(1-1/ℓ)^{-2}', so the resulting '(q/φ(q))^2' factor is cancelled by
> the number of available shift pairs.

**Argument, as the claimant describes it.** Take the cutoff $X=qR$ with
$R=\lfloor\phi(q)\log q/q\rfloor$, so that the primes up to $X$ number about
$\phi(q)$, one per reduced class on average, and let $N_a(X)$ count the primes
up to $X$ in the class $a$. The second factorial moment
$\sum_a\binom{N_a(X)}{2}$ over reduced classes is bounded below by a constant
times $\phi(q)$, from the pointwise lower bound of Friedlander and Goldston
(1996) for Hooley's variance of primes in progressions; the third factorial
moment $\sum_a\binom{N_a(X)}{3}$ is bounded above by a constant times
$\phi(q)$, from an upper-bound sieve for the three linear forms $p$, $p+rq$,
$p+sq$ (cited to an explicit prime-tuple bound of Dubbe, 2024) followed by an
average of the singular series over the shift pairs, where the local factors
at primes dividing $q$ produce a factor $(q/\phi(q))^2$ that the number of
shift pairs cancels. The two moment bounds force a positive proportion of
classes to hold at least two primes below $X$; since the total count of primes
is $\phi(q)+o(\phi(q))$, a positive proportion of classes hold none; and the
prime number theorem bounds the primes added when the cutoff grows to
$(1+c)\phi(q)\log q$, so a positive proportion of empty classes survive. The
author names the normalization and range of the variance bound, the uniformity
of the three-form sieve and the singular-series average for smooth moduli as
the steps most in need of checking.

**Formalization.** The Lean 4 file `lean/Erdos971Forum.lean` of the author's
repository (Lean 4.27.0 with Mathlib, single file importing only Mathlib)
proves the finite reduction and nothing analytic: the theorem
`prime_moment_method_to_least_prime_classes` fixes one modulus $d$, two
cutoffs $X_0\le X_1$ and real constants, and takes five hypotheses as
parameters: a lower bound $\alpha\phi(d)$ for the second factorial moment at
$X_0$, an upper bound $\beta\phi(d)$ for the third, an inequality among
$\alpha$, $\beta$, $\delta$ and a truncation level (the moment method's gap
condition), a total of at most $(1+\varepsilon)\phi(d)$ primes in reduced
classes up to $X_0$, and at most $\eta\phi(d)$ primes added between the
cutoffs. From these it concludes that at least
$(\delta-\varepsilon-\eta)\phi(d)$ reduced classes modulo $d$ have least
congruent prime beyond $X_1$; it also proves that a class holds no prime up to
$X$ exactly when its least congruent prime exceeds $X$, using Mathlib's
Dirichlet theorem. The companion `AnalyticTargets.lean` states the four
analytic hypotheses as propositions, in eventual form in $d$ with
$\varepsilon\to0$, without proving them; the gap condition is a check on the
constants. The author's verification record reports a GitHub-hosted build with
axiom closure `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` for the three
principal theorems and no `sorry`, and says plainly that this is a formal
proof of the conditional finite implication and not of the analytic
manuscript. The Lean file declares no `sorry`, `axiom` or `native_decide`;
this corpus has not built it. The formalization does not reach the problem's
statement, so it is not counted as evidence.
