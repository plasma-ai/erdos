***

**Claim.** For every integer $k\ge2$ there is a constant $c_k>0$ with

$$
\sum_{p<x} n_k(p)\sim c_k\frac{x}{\log x},
$$

where $n_k(p)$ is the least $k$th power nonresidue modulo $p$ for
$p\equiv1\pmod k$ and $n_k(p)=0$ for the other primes. That convention is
the one the precise Statement of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0980/_index|Problem 980]] adopts (see the
Convention paragraph below).

**Source.** P. D. T. A. Elliott, A problem of Erdős concerning power residue
sums, Acta Arith. 13 (1967), fasc. 2, 131–149
([[../library/integer_sequences/elliott_nd_problem_erdos_concerning_power_residue_sums/_index|card]]).
The page's date is the editorial receipt date printed at the end of the
paper, 1967-01-09; the paper's first page and the publisher's record both
date the volume 1967, and the running header prints the fascicle as XIII.2.

**What is proved.** Theorem 1 of the paper is stronger than the claim: for
every integer $k>0$ and every exponent $a<4e^{1-1/k}$,

$$
\sum_{p<x} n_k(p)^a\sim C_{k,a}\frac{x}{\log x},
$$

and when $k$ is an odd prime the constant is the series
$C_{k,a}=\sum_{r\ge1}k^{-r}q_r^a$ over the primes $q_1<q_2<\cdots$. The claim
is the case $a=1$, where $4e^{1-1/k}>1$ for every $k\ge1$. The proof combines
a large-sieve count of the primes with a large least nonresidue (the step
Erdős had identified as the obstacle for $k>2$) with Galois-theoretic lemmas
on linear disjointness and on when a radical lies in a cyclotomic field, which
control the density of primes for which a given prime $q$ is a $k$th power
nonresidue. Elliott notes that Barban had stated the result without proof
(The 'Large Sieve' method and its applications in the theory of numbers,
Russian Math. Surveys 21 (1966), 49–104, at pp. 61–62).

**Convention.** Elliott defines $n_k(p)$ for $p\equiv1\pmod k$ and sets
$n_k(p)=0$ for the other primes, the convention of the problem's precise
Statement; Erdős's 1961 formulation and the site's statement do not restrict
$p$. For prime $k$ the two readings agree, since a prime
$p\not\equiv1\pmod k$ has every residue a $k$th power and no $k$th power
nonresidue. For composite $k$ a prime $p\not\equiv1\pmod k$ with
$\gcd(k,p-1)=d>1$ does have a least $k$th power nonresidue, equal to
$n_d(p)$; the literal sum that includes these primes is the variant the
problem page records under Formulation, and Elliott's theorem does not reach
it. The site records the problem as proved by this paper without remarking on
the convention.

**Earlier case.** Erdős proved the case $k=2$ in 1961, with the constant
$c_2=\sum_{j\ge1}p_j/2^j$ over the primes $p_1<p_2<\cdots$, and conjectured
the general case
([[../library/integer_sequences/erdos_1961_szamelmeleti_megjegyzesek/_index|card]],
equations (3) and (4);
[[problems/integer_sequences/E0980/claims/1961_01_01_erdos|claim page]]); the
constant $c_2$ is the number of
[[problems/irrationality/E0251/_index|Problem 251]].
