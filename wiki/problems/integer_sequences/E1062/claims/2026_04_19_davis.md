***

**Claim.** Corollary 4 of Damek Davis, Forbidden subgraphs in divisor graphs
and an Erdős divisibility problem, arXiv:2604.17613 (19 April 2026), gives
$f(n)=c_2n+o(n)$ for an effectively computable constant $c_2$, with an explicit
error term, and the growth rate $q(n)=\beta_2^{n+o(n)}$ of the number of
fork-free subsets of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, through McNew's theorem on local
statistics of divisor graphs. The sentence after the corollary leaves the
irrationality of $c_2$ open. The
[[../library/integer_sequences/davis_2026_forbidden_subgraphs_divisor_graphs/_index|library card]]
records the paper; no file of it is held.

**Covers.** The first question, how large $f(n)$ can be, in asymptotic form:
$f(n)=c_2n+o(n)$ for an effectively computable constant $c_2$, so that
$\lim f(n)/n$ exists; the claim value is `answered` because the result
determines that size. It does not cover the irrationality question, and the
paper gives no closed form for $f(n)$; its numerical estimate (Section 5.1,
p. 7) is the bound $c_2\ge0.6729$, which with Lebensold's $c_2\le0.6736$ leaves
a gap of about $7\times10^{-4}$.

**Standing.** The thread post of 19 April 2026 by the author announces the
paper as a partial solution, links the write-up on GitHub (the pinned preprint
link above, at its first commit of that day) and credits the initial solution
to GPT 5.4 pro; on 20 April 2026 Nat Sothanaphan reported that a standard check
found no issues, and on 4 May 2026 the site's curator wrote that, if correct,
it is not a full solution, since the irrationality question remains. The
site's label stayed OPEN, there is no refereed version, and no body has
accepted the paper so the claim stays `claimed`. The later
Conjectures.io proof
([[problems/integer_sequences/E1062/claims/2026_09_21_jenw1n|claim page]])
reproves the existence of the limit on its way to irrationality.
