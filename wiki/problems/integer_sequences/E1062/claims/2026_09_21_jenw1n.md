**Claim.** The limit $\lim f(n)/n$ exists and is irrational, where $f(n)$ is
the largest size of a subset of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ in which no element divides two
other distinct elements. The accepted formal statement is the
formal-conjectures clause `Erdos1062.erdos_1062.parts.ii` with its open answer
fixed to true: there is an $l$ with $f(n)/n\to l$ and $l$ irrational. The same
file proves on the way an exact closed formula for $f(n)$ for every $n$,
summing over the integers $q\le n$ coprime to $6$ the largest fork-free set of
$\{2,3\}$-smooth numbers up to $n/q$ with a correction at finitely many scales
per $q$, and identifies the limit as an explicit series,
$0.6729656511994\ldots$, inside Lebensold's bracket $[0.6725,0.6736]$; the
irrationality comes from a finiteness theorem for small integer combinations of
numbers $2^a3^b$ (a parametric Subspace Theorem for the places $\infty$, $2$
and $3$) proved inside the file. The
[[problems/integer_sequences/E1062/_index|problem page]] states the formula,
the series and the proof's four steps in full.

whether $\lim f(n)/n$ is irrational. The reviewed target asserts that $f(n)/n$
