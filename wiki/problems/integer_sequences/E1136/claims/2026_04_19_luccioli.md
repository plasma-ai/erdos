**Claim.** The gist `Erdos1136.lean` linked above (243 lines, importing
Mathlib), posted to the discussion thread of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E1136/_index|Problem 1136]] on 19 April 2026
by the forum user Lorenzo Luccioli, who writes that they asked Aristotle to
formalize the solution to the problem and posts the code it produced,
defines $A$ as the positive integers whose odd part is $3\pmod4$ and proves
`erdos_1136`: there is a set $A\subset\mathbb N$ with $a+b\ne2^k$ for all
$a,b\in A$ and $k\ge0$ and some $N_0$ with $|A\cap[1,n]|>n/3$ for every
$n\ge N_0$. That statement is weaker than the question, which asks for lower
density above $1/3$: a count exceeding $n/3$ for all large $n$ allows lower
density exactly $1/3$. The file's lemma `density_lower_bound_general`,
$|A\cap[1,n]|\ge3\lfloor n/8\rfloor$ for every $n$, gives lower density at
least $3/8>1/3$ and so the question's answer, and the proof of `erdos_1136`
derives the headline bound from it with $N_0=64$; the headline theorem does
not state it. The file's header names no author, no source and no prover;
only the post names Aristotle. Its text contains no `sorry`, `axiom`
declaration or `native_decide`. The page rests on the file's text at the
pinned revision; nothing was built, kernel-checked or audited here.
