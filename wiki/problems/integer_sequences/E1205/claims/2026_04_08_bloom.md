***

**Claim.** For the function $F(x)$ of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E1205/_index|Problem 1205]], the largest
multiplicity that some choice of one residue class $a_n\pmod n$ for every
$n\le x$ guarantees for every integer $m\le x$,

$$
\log x-O\bigl(\sqrt{\log x\log\log x}\bigr)\le F(x)\le\log x+O(1),
$$

so $F(x)\sim\log x$. This answers "estimate $F(x)$" to the first order and
is the site's reading of its SOLVED label, defined on the page as a
resolution other than a proof or disproof. The argument, in the words of
this compilation, has three steps. Upper bound: the class of $n$ holds
about $x/n$ of the integers up to $x$, so the average number of congruences
an integer $m\le x$ satisfies is $\sum_{n\le x}1/n+O(1)=\log x+O(1)$, and
some $m$ sits at or below the average. Random lower bound: choose the
classes for $n\le x/2$ uniformly at random; a fixed $m\le x$ lies in the
class of $n$ with probability $1/n$, so its expected count is $\log x+O(1)$,
and a Chernoff-type concentration bound makes the probability of a count
below $\log x-O(\sqrt{\log x\log\log x})$ at most $(\log x)^{-2}$, leaving
at most $O(x/(\log x)^2)$ exceptional integers for some fixed choice.
Greedy repair: each modulus $n\in(x/2,x]$ can choose its class to contain
any one prescribed integer $m\le x$; spreading these about $x/2$ moduli over
the $O(x/(\log x)^2)$ exceptions covers each exception $\gg(\log x)^2$
times. The commentary says the argument was suggested by the comments on
the prime-modulus analogue, Problem 689, which remains open.
