***

**Claim.** Question (i) of
[[problems/integer_sequences/E1209/_index|Problem 1209]] is answered no for
every integer shift, and question (ii) for nonnegative shifts, by a variant of
the construction of [[problems/integer_sequences/E0429/_index|Problem 429]].
Take $a_1=2$ and, for $k\ge2$, a prime $a_k>a_{k-1}$ with
$a_k+k\equiv0\pmod{q_k}$ for some prime $q_k\nmid k$; Dirichlet's theorem
supplies such primes, since $\gcd(k,q_k)=1$, and $a_k$ may be taken as large as
any prescribed growth demands. For $n=0$ every $n+a_k$ is prime. For $n=k\ge2$,
$k+a_k$ is a multiple of $q_k$ and exceeds $q_k$ once $a_k$ is chosen above
$q_k$, so it is composite; for $n=1$, $1+a_2$ is even and greater than $2$.
Every negative shift fails at $k=1$, since $n+a_1=n+2\le1$. So $0$ is the only
integer shift making every term prime, and one such shift does not force
infinitely many, however fast the sequence grows. With $q_k^2$ in place of $q_k$
the term $k+a_k$ is divisible by $q_k^2$ for every $k\ge2$, so the nonnegative
shifts making every term squarefree are among $0$ and $1$, finitely many, which
answers (ii). The commentary states the construction and its growth in two
sentences; the checks of the shifts $n=1$ and $n=k$ above are the problem page's
elementary reading of it.

**Covers.** Question (i) for every integer shift, since a negative shift fails
at $k=1$ ($n+a_1=n+2\le1$), and question (ii) for nonnegative shifts: one shift
making every $n+a_k$ prime, or one nonnegative shift making every $n+a_k$
squarefree, does not force infinitely many. Under the convention that shifts are
positive, the sequences must first be translated (Barschkis's Corollary 2.2).
Not covered: negative shifts in (ii), which the construction does not exclude
($n=-1$ gives $n+a_1=1$, and $a_k-1$ is uncontrolled) and Barschkis's note does,
on
[[problems/integer_sequences/E1209/claims/2026_04_15_barschkis|its claim page]];
question (iii.a), which the commentary credits to Barschkis and GPT; and
questions (iii.b), (iii.c) and (iii.d), which stay open.

problem page, unsigned, whose recommended citation names the site's curator,
T. F. Bloom, as the page's author; the page is filed under that name. The
site's revision history shows two superseded revisions: the construction
entered the page in the edit of 8 April 2026 (the revision without it was

**Standing.** Pending. The label is the claimant's own: the curator who labels
the problem wrote the construction, so the page's label is no independent review
and no `reviewed` evidence is listed. No paper or preprint of the construction
exists (the proof-claim tab was empty on 2026-09-18). Since 19 September 2026
(formal-conjectures pull request 6065), the collection's own file proves its
statements `erdos_1209.parts.i` and `.parts.ii`, over shifts in $\mathbb N$. It
uses a variant of this construction: Dirichlet primes $p\equiv-k\pmod q$ for a
prime $q>k$, so that the shift $k$ fails at the $k$-th term. The pull request
calls it the construction in the docstring. This corpus has not built or audited
that Lean, so it is a `formalization` link, not `formalized` evidence.
Barschkis's note also excludes every nonzero integer shift for squarefree
values, and its Lean file proves that statement; its standing is recorded on its
own page and is not evidence for this one. Nothing here is this project's own
review.
