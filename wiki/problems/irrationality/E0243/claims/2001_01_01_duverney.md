***

**Claim.** D. Duverney, *Irrationality of fast converging series of rational
numbers*, J. Math. Sci. Univ. Tokyo 8 (2001), 275--316, received 13
September 2000. Corollary 3.2 (printed p. 287) states: let $u_n$ be positive
integers with

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}u_n=+\infty
\quad\text{and}\quad
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Bigl(\frac{u_{n+1}}{u_n^2}-1\Bigr)\ \text{convergent},
$$

and let $a_n\in\{-1,1\}$ for every $n$; then $\sum_{n\ge0}a_n/u_n$ is
rational if and only if

$$
u_{n+1}=u_n^2-\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}u_n+\frac{a_{n+2}}{a_{n+1}}
$$

for every $n\ge N$. With every $a_n=1$ this is $u_{n+1}=u_n^2-u_n+1$. The
paper introduces the corollary as a partial answer to its question (2.15),
Erdős's question of [[problems/irrationality/E0243/_index|Problem 243]],
which it locates at p. 64 of the Erdős--Graham monograph and p. 105 of
Erdős's 1988 survey, and proves it (Section 5.2) by Mahler's method in the
form of Loxton and van der Poorten, the paper's tool for fast converging
series. The source card
[[../library/irrationality/duverney_2001_irrationality_fast_converging_series_rational_numbers/_index|duverney_2001_irrationality_fast_converging_series_rational_numbers]]
records the publication.

**Covers.** The sequences of Problem 243 for which
$\sum_n(a_{n+1}/a_n^2-1)$ converges; the convergence implies the problem's
hypothesis $a_{n+1}/a_n^2\to1$, so these are instances of the problem, and
the corollary decides them, with the recurrence as the exact condition for
rationality. Not covered: the sequences whose relative error tends to $0$
without being summable.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: J. Math. Sci. Univ. Tokyo 8 (2001), 275--316. The
site labels the problem OPEN and records the corollary in its remark, added
after a thread comment of 11 October 2025 pointed to it, so no `reviewed`
evidence is listed. The proof is not checked here.
