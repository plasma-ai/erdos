# problems/irrationality/E0250/claims

[[problems/irrationality/E0250/_index|..]]

[[problems/irrationality/E0250/claims/1995_09_18_duverney|1995_09_18_duverney]]: Duverney's 1995 note proves that (q-1)^2 times the sum of sigma(n) over q^n
is irrational for every integer q other than -1, 0 and 1; the case q = 2 is
the series of Problem 250, so the answer is yes; refereed and reviewed.

[[problems/irrationality/E0250/claims/1996_03_07_nesterenko|1996_03_07_nesterenko]]: Nesterenko's 1996 theorem that at least three of q, P(q), Q(q), R(q) are
algebraically independent for 0 < |q| < 1 makes P(1/2) = 1 - 24 times the
sum of sigma(n) over 2^n transcendental, hence the sum irrational.

[[problems/irrationality/E0250/claims/2001_11_08_zudilin|2001_11_08_zudilin]]: Zudilin (Mat. Sb. 193 (2002)) proves the q-analog of zeta(2) irrational at
q = 1/p with irrationality measure at most 4.0787; at q = 1/2 this is the
sum of sigma(n) over 2^n; refereed.

[[problems/irrationality/E0250/claims/2006_04_13_postelmans_van_assche|2006_04_13_postelmans_van_assche]]: Postelmans and Van Assche (J. Number Theory 126 (2007)) prove 1, zeta_q(1)
and zeta_q(2) linearly independent over Q for q = 1/p, p at least 2; at
q = 1/2 this makes the sum of sigma(n) over 2^n irrational; refereed.

[[problems/irrationality/E0250/claims/2008_09_15_smet_van_assche|2008_09_15_smet_van_assche]]: Smet and Van Assche (Acta Arith. 138 (2009)) prove zeta_q(2) irrational at
q = 1/p with measure at most 10 pi^2/(5 pi^2 - 24); at q = 1/2 this is the
sum of sigma(n) over 2^n; refereed.

[[problems/irrationality/E0250/claims/2025_10_21_romanlelan|2025_10_21_romanlelan]]: A note linked in the site's thread claims the sum of sigma(n) q^n
transcendental for every algebraic q with 0 < |q| < 1; the curator showed
the same day that the argument does not reach the series itself.
