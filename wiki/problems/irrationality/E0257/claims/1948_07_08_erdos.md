***

**Claim.** The single Theorem of P. Erdős, *On arithmetical properties of
Lambert series*, J. Indian Math. Soc. (N.S.) 12 (1948), 63--66, received
8 July 1948, states that for every integer $t$ with $|t|>1$ both $f(1/t)$ and
$g(1/t)$ are irrational, where

$$
f(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{x^n}{1-x^n},\qquad
g(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{x^n}{1-x^n}\sin\frac{n\pi}{2}.
$$

Since $f(1/t)=\sum_{n\ge1}1/(t^n-1)$, the theorem at $t=2$ is the case
$A=\mathbb N$ of [[problems/irrationality/E0257/_index|Problem 257]], answered
yes; the site's remarks state this case through the identity
$\sum_{n\ge1}1/(2^n-1)=\sum_{n\ge1}\tau(n)/2^n$, with $\tau(n)$ the number of
divisors of $n$. For $d\ge1$ and $A=d\mathbb N$, the positive multiples of
$d$, the series is $\sum_{k\ge1}1/(2^{dk}-1)=f(1/2^d)$, so the theorem at
$t=2^d$ answers these instances yes as well. The proof is written for
$f(1/t)$: with $k=[(\log n)^{1/10}]$ and the consecutive primes above
$(\log n)^2$, the system of simultaneous congruences (2) produces a block of
consecutive integers $r$ whose divisor counts $d(r)$ are divisible by high
powers of $t$, so that the base-$t$ expansion of $\sum_rd(r)/t^r$ contains at
least $k/2$ consecutive zeros for arbitrarily large $k$ and never terminates;
the case of negative $t$ is said to follow by the same method, and the proof
for $g(1/t)$ by this method and Chowla's. The closing remark lists the
analogous series for $\phi(n)$, for the sum of divisors and for the number of
prime factors as presenting difficulties. The source card is
[[../library/irrationality/erdos_1948_arithmetical_properties_lambert_series/_index|erdos_1948_arithmetical_properties_lambert_series]].
Erdős's 1968 theorem for pairwise coprime supports with convergent reciprocal
sum is the accepted partial claim on
[[problems/irrationality/E0257/claims/1965_12_13_erdos|its own page]].

**Covers.** The support $A=\mathbb N$ and, through the theorem at the base
$t=2^d$, every set of multiples $A=d\mathbb N$ with $d\ge1$. Not covered: every
other infinite support; the other settled classes are listed on the problem
page.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the Journal of the Indian Mathematical Society, new
series, volume 12 (1948), pp. 63--66, received 8 July 1948 as the paper's
header prints. The site labels the problem OPEN, and its remark crediting the
paper with the case $A=\mathbb N$ is commentary on an open problem, not
acceptance, so no `reviewed` evidence is listed. The proof is not checked
here.

**Formalization.** A Lean 4 theorem in Will Cook's plectis-erdos repository,
`erdos_257_variants_tsum_top` at the lines of the `formalization` link, states
that $\sum_{n\ge1}\tau(n)/2^n$ is irrational, the form of the catalog's variant;
the same file derives it, and an every-integer-base form
`erdos_1049_variants_geq_2_integer`, from the repository's theorem for
$\sum_{n\ge1}1/(b^n-1)$ at every integer base $b\ge2$, which its header
describes as Erdős's 1948 theorem, so the declaration is a link on this page and
not an independent claim. The formal-conjectures catalog (the `record` link,
pinned to its commit of 2026-09-23) tags its variant
`erdos_257.variants.tsum_top`, the irrationality of $\sum_n\tau(n)/2^n$,
research solved with that declaration as its formal proof, while its main
statement `erdos_257` stays research open. This corpus has not built the
development or audited its statement against the theorem, so the claim carries
no `formalized` evidence.
