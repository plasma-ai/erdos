***

**Claim.** Theorem 3 (p. 6) of P. Erdős, *Some problems and results on the
irrationality of the sum of infinite series*, J. Math. Sci. 10 (1975), 1--7,
states that if $n_k>0$ and $n_k\equiv0\pmod{2^{2^k}}$ for every $k$, then

$$
\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{1}{n_k}
$$

is irrational; the paper notes that the sequence $n_k$ is not assumed
monotone. On pp. 2--3 Erdős recalls the property $P$ he and Straus
considered, that a sequence $n_1<n_2<\cdots$ has property $P$ when
$\sum_k1/m_k$ is irrational for every choice of positive integers $m_k$
divisible by $n_k$, which is the notion of an irrationality sequence in
[[problems/irrationality/E0262/_index|Problem 262]] with $m_k=t_ka_k$; he
writes that they wondered whether $n_k=2^{2^k}$ has property $P$ and that he
will prove this conjecture, which Theorem 3 does. So $a_n=2^{2^n}$ is an
irrationality sequence, with $\limsup(\log_2\log_2a_n)/n=1$. The proof
reorders the $m_k$ into a monotone sequence, reduces by Theorem 1 to the case
$\limsup m_k^{1/2^k}<\infty$, and uses that at least two of $m_1,\ldots,m_k$
are divisible by $2^{2^{k-1}}$, so the least common multiple $N_k$ of
$m_1,\ldots,m_k$ is at most $2^{-2^{k-1}}$ times their product; along a
subsequence where $m_k$ is nearly as large as the growth allows, the product
times the tail tends to $0$, which rationality forbids. The paper adds that
property $P$ is of interest only when $\lim n_k^{1/2^k}<\infty$, and that
Erdős did not know whether a sequence with property $P$ can grow slowly, the
problem's question. The source card is
[[../library/irrationality/erdos_1976_problems_results_irrationality_sum_infinite_series/_index|erdos_1976_problems_results_irrationality_sum_infinite_series]],
and the result page is
[[../library/irrationality/erdos_1976_problems_results_irrationality_sum_infinite_series/theorem_3|Theorem 3]].

**Covers.** The attained half of the answer: an irrationality sequence can grow
as slowly as $2^{2^n}$, so $\limsup(\log_2\log_2a_n)/n=1$ is attained. Not
covered: the lower bound, that no irrationality sequence has
$\limsup(\log_2\log_2a_n)/n<1$, which is Hančl's theorem on
[[problems/irrationality/E0262/claims/1991_01_01_hancl|its claim page]].

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the Journal of Mathematical Sciences, volume 10
(1975), pp. 1--7. The site's remarks credit this paper with the example, but
the SOLVED (LEAN) label credits Hančl with the answer, so no `reviewed`
evidence is listed. The proof is not checked here.
