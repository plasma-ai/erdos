***

**Claim.** Kovač, Vjekoslav and Tao, Terence, *On several irrationality
problems for Ahmes series*, Acta Math. Hungar. 175 (2025), 572–608, on the card
[[../library/irrationality/kovac_2024_several_irrationality_problems_ahmes_series/_index|kovac_2024_several_irrationality_problems_ahmes_series]].
Theorem 2.5: if $(a_n)$ is a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers
with $\sum_n 1/a_n<\infty$ and

$$
\liminf_{n\to\infty} a_n^2\sum_{k>n}\frac{1}{a_k^2}>0,
$$

then $(a_n)$ is not an irrationality sequence of this type, that is, there is a
bounded sequence of integers $b_n$ with $b_n\ne0$ and $a_n+b_n\ne0$ for all $n$
such that $\sum_n 1/(a_n+b_n)$ is rational. The paper's definition includes
the condition $b_n\ne0$, which its footnote 3 adds to the formulation of Erdős
and Graham to make the question about $2^n$ meaningful. Corollary 2.6: a
strictly increasing sequence of positive integers with
$\limsup_n a_{n+1}/a_n<\infty$ is not an irrationality sequence of this type,
which covers $a_n=2^n$. Section 5 makes the case $a_n=2^n$ explicit: the sums
$\sum_n 1/(2^n+b_n)$ over all $(b_n)$ with values in $\{1,\dots,5\}$ fill a
closed interval containing $3/4$, so some such $(b_n)$ gives
$\sum_n 1/(2^n+b_n)=3/4$. The arXiv record 2406.17593 carries the paper from
its third version, posted 2024-11-27, which first states Theorem 2.5 and
Corollary 2.6 and first names Tao as coauthor; versions 1 and 2 are Kovač's
single-author note *On simultaneous rationality of two Ahmes series*, whose
second version (2024-07-10) already proves as its Theorem 2 that $2^n$, and
every sequence with a uniform bound in place of the liminf condition, is not an
irrationality sequence of this type.

**Formalization.** Three public Lean 4 files declare themselves formalizations
of this result, none built or audited in this corpus. Kovač's file of
2025-12-17 in Kovač's own repository (the first `formalization` link) states
that Aristotle (Harmonic) auto-formalized Kovač's blueprint of the paper's proof
and generated the rest of the file; its `main_theorem` proves that some
$b_k\in\{1,\dots,5\}$ make $\sum_k 1/(2^k+b_k)$ rational, and Kovač announced
it on the site's discussion thread the same day (the first `discussion` link).
Boris Alexeev's lean-proofs file `Erdos264b` of 2025-12-18 (the second
`formalization` link) adapts it to prove the formal-conjectures statement
`erdos_264.parts.i`, the negation of the irrationality-sequence property for
$2^n$, and cites the paper as the original human proof. The combined file of
2026-08-25 in the same repository (the third `formalization` link) merges it
with the independent proof recorded on
[[problems/irrationality/E0264/claims/2025_12_18_alexeev|the Alexeev page]] and
names Kovač and Tao as its informal authors.

**Covers.** The powers-of-two part: the answer is no, $a_n=2^n$ is not an
irrationality sequence of this type. Not covered: the factorial part. For
$a_n=n!$ the quantity $(n!)^2\sum_{k>n}(k!)^{-2}$ tends to $0$ and the ratios
$a_{n+1}/a_n=n+1$ are unbounded, so neither Theorem 2.5 nor Corollary 2.6
applies; the paper's Theorem 2.7 gives only some irrationality sequence of this
type asymptotic to $n!$, not a statement about $n!$ itself.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Acta Math. Hungar. 175 (2025), 572–608. The site's
commentary credits Kovač and Tao with the result but labels the problem OPEN,
so that remark is not listed as `reviewed` evidence, and the Lean files give no
`formalized` evidence. The corpus has not reproved the theorem and awards no
tier of its own.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the claim rests on the cited paper.
