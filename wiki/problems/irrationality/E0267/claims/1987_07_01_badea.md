***

**Claim.** Corollary 4: $\sum_{n\ge1}1/F_{2^n+1}$ is irrational, so the
instance $n_k=2^k+1$ of [[problems/irrationality/E0267/_index|Problem 267]],
whose index ratios are at least $5/3$, has answer yes. The paper's main
Theorem states that if $(a_n)$ and $(b_n)$ are sequences of positive integers
with $a_{n+1}>(b_{n+1}/b_n)a_n^2-(b_{n+1}/b_n)a_n+1$ for all large $n$, then
$\sum b_n/a_n$ is irrational; the proof writes the sum as a limit of rationals
$A_n/P_n$ with $P_n=a_1\cdots a_n$ and applies Brun's criterion for
irrationality of limits of increasing rational sequences. Corollary 4 verifies
the criterion for $a_n=F_{2^n+1}$ and $b_n=1$ through the Fibonacci identity
$F_{2k+1}=F_k^2+F_{k+1}^2$, which gives $F_{2^{n+1}+1}\ge F_{2^n+1}^2$.
Section 5 of the paper answers two questions of Erdős and Graham in this way,
the second being Corollary 5, the irrationality of $\sum_{n\ge1}1/L_{2^n}$
over the Lucas numbers, which lies outside this problem. The source is C.
Badea, *The irrationality of certain infinite series*, Glasgow Math. J. 29
(1987), no. 2, 221–228, on the card
[[../library/irrationality/badea_1987_irrationality_certain_infinite_series/_index|badea_1987_irrationality_certain_infinite_series]].

**Covers.** The instance $n_k=2^k+1$: the answer is yes. Not covered: every
other index sequence. The instance meets the condition of Badea's 1993
corollary with equality, although its ratios are below $2$
([[problems/irrationality/E0267/claims/1993_01_01_badea|the Badea page]]).

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Glasgow Mathematical Journal, volume 29, issue 2
(July 1987). The site's commentary credits Badea with the irrationality of this
sum but labels the problem OPEN, so that commentary is not listed as
`reviewed` evidence. The corpus has not reproved the theorem and awards no
tier of its own.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the claim rests on the cited paper.
