# problems/irrationality/E1001/claims

[[problems/irrationality/E1001/_index|..]]

[[problems/irrationality/E1001/claims/1958_01_01_erdos_szusz_turan|1958_01_01_erdos_szusz_turan]]: Theorem III of the 1958 paper that posed the problem proves that the limit
of S(N,A,c) exists and equals 12A log c/pi^2 whenever 0<A<c/(1+c^2), the
range in which the approximation intervals do not overlap.

[[problems/irrationality/E1001/claims/1962_05_01_kesten|1962_05_01_kesten]]: Theorem 2 of Kesten's 1962 paper proves that the limit of S(N,A,c) exists
for Ac<=1 and gives closed forms on c/(1+c^2)<=A<=min(1/2,1/c) and on
1/2<=A<=1/c, extending the proposers' sparse-range value.

[[problems/irrationality/E1001/claims/1966_01_01_kesten_sos|1966_01_01_kesten_sos]]: Kesten and Sós show in 1966 that the measure S(N,A,c) converges as N grows,
for every A>0 and c>=1, without finding the value of the limit; this is the
existence half of the question.

[[problems/irrationality/E1001/claims/2006_01_01_xiong_zaharescu|2006_01_01_xiong_zaharescu]]: Xiong and Zaharescu reprove in 2006 that the limiting measure exists for
every A>0 and c>=1 and give a formula for it as a finite alternating sum
of double integrals, from which the closed forms in the known ranges follow.

[[problems/irrationality/E1001/claims/2008_08_01_boca|2008_08_01_boca]]: Boca's 2008 paper proves, according to its abstract, that the limiting
measure S(N,A,c) exists and identifies it for all A>0 and c>1.
