***

**Claim.** For an integer $q>1$ and a nonzero rational $r$ with $r\ne q^m$
for every $m\ge1$,

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{q^n-r}
$$

is irrational (Theorem 4 of the paper, in its sign convention; equivalently
$\sum_{n\ge1}(q^n+r)^{-1}$ is irrational for nonzero rational $r\ne-q^m$). With
$q=2$ and $r=3$, which is no power of $2$, the series $\sum_{n\ge1}1/(2^n-3)$ is
irrational, answering [[problems/irrationality/E1050/_index|Problem 1050]] yes.
The paper is Peter B. Borwein, On the irrationality of $\sum(1/(q^n+r))$, J.
Number Theory 37 (1991), no. 3, 253--259, received 1987-05-25, revised
1989-10-09 and published in the March 1991 issue (the page's date); its
introduction names the series $\sum1/(2^n-3)$, which Erdős and Graham had listed
as unresolved, as a special case. The card
[[../library/irrationality/borwein_1991_irrationality_1_qn_r/_index|borwein_1991_irrationality_1_qn_r]]
summarizes the argument: Padé approximants to the $q$-logarithm
$L_q^*(x)=\sum_{m\ge1}x/(q^m-x)$, whose denominators have integer coefficients
and controlled growth, are combined with the dilation identity
$L_q^*(qx)=L_q^*(x)+x/(1-x)$ and a clearing factor to produce nonzero integer
linear forms in $1$ and $L_q^*(r)$ that tend to zero, which rationality of
$L_q^*(r)=r\sum_{n\ge1}(q^n-r)^{-1}$ forbids. A remark of the paper adds that
the value is not a Liouville number. Borwein gave a second, self-contained proof
in On the irrationality of certain series, Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc.
112 (1992), no. 1, 141--146, received 1991-11-12 and published in the July 1992
issue (the date of the second `paper` link): its Theorem 1 proves
$\sum_{n\ge1}1/(q^n+r)$ irrational for every integer $|q|>1$ and nonzero
rational $r\ne-q^n$ by a contour-integral argument, and its introduction says
that the 1991 paper resolved the series $\sum1/(2^n-3)$; the card
[[../library/irrationality/borwein_1992_irrationality_certain_series/_index|borwein_1992_irrationality_certain_series]]
summarizes the argument. The classical case $q=2$, $r=1$ is Erdős's 1948 theorem
that $\sum1/(2^n-1)$ is irrational (card
[[../library/irrationality/erdos_1948_arithmetical_properties_lambert_series/_index|erdos_1948]]).

**Formalization.** A third party formalized the result: `erdos_1050` in
`LeanGallery/NumberTheory/Erdos1050/Statement.lean` of
https://github.com/gotrevor/lean-gallery, pinned above at the commit of
2026-07-05 that last touched the folder. The file names Trevor Morris as its
author and Borwein's 1991 paper, beside the 1992 paper, as the resolving theorem
it formalizes, specialized to $q=2$, $r=-3$, so it is a link on this page rather
than an independent claim; the formal-conjectures catalog describes the
development as formalized by Trevor Morris with Claude Code and Harmonic's
Aristotle. Its theorem states that $\sum_{n\ge0}1/(2^{n+1}-3)$ is irrational,
the problem's series reindexed from $n\ge1$; the file says the proof reduces the
literal series to the tail $\sum_{n\ge0}1/(2^{n+2}-3)$ by discarding the
rational first term $-1$, and that its axioms should be only `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. The catalog (the `record` link, pinned to
the commit of 2026-09-18 that last touched its file) tags its statement
`erdos_1050` as `research solved` and cites this file as the formal proof,
together with the development's proofs of Borwein's general theorem and of
Erdős's 1948 case as variants. No build of the development, printout of its
axioms or audit of its definitions against the problem is recorded in this
repository, so the claim carries no `formalized` evidence and the file's own
account of its axioms is reported, not warranted.
