***

Kovač proves, as Theorem 1, that the set $X\subseteq\mathbb R^3$ of
[[problems/number_theory/E0268/_index|Problem 268]], the triples

$$
\Bigl(\sum_{n\in A}\frac1n,\ \sum_{n\in A}\frac1{n+1},\ \sum_{n\in A}\frac1{n+2}\Bigr)
$$

over infinite $A\subseteq\mathbb N$ with $\sum_{n\in A}1/n<\infty$, has
nonempty interior, which is exactly the question's affirmative answer. The
proof is constructive: after a linear change of variables the series becomes
a perturbed vector series with leading terms $(1/n,2/n^2,2/n^3)$, and a
convergence game played against the perturbation errors shows that every
point of a small open ball is represented; Section 5 exhibits a ball of
radius $10^{-24}$. The two-dimensional case, the pairs
$(\sum1/n,\sum1/(n+1))$, is the older theorem of Erdős and Straus that the
question of Erdős and Graham sought to extend; Kovač and Tao's later
extension to every dimension has
[[problems/number_theory/E0268/claims/2024_11_27_kovac_tao|its own page]].

The paper's digest is the library card
[[../library/number_theory/kovac_2024_set_points_represented_harmonic_subseries/_index|Kovač 2024]].
