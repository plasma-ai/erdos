***

Halász proves, as Theorem 2 of his 1977 paper, that for $n$ vectors
$\mathbf a_1,\ldots,\mathbf a_n\in\mathbb R^d$ with
$|\mathbf a_k-\mathbf a_{k'}|\ge1$ for $k\ne k'$ such that, for some
$\delta>0$ and every unit vector $\mathbf e$, at least $\delta n$ of them
satisfy $|(\mathbf a_k,\mathbf e)|\ge1$, at most $c(\delta,d)2^nn^{-1-d/2}$ of
the $2^n$ signed sums $\sum_k\varepsilon_k\mathbf a_k$ ($\varepsilon_k=\pm1$)
lie in any open unit ball. His remark after the theorem records that this
confirms a conjecture Erdős had communicated to him: with
$\mathbf a_k=(a_k,1)$ and $|a_k-a_{k'}|\ge1$ the count is at most
$c2^nn^{-2}$, which is the Sárközy--Szemerédi problem with the number of plus
signs also fixed, and he names this question as the starting point of his
investigations in higher dimensions.

For the second question of
[[problems/number_theory/E0362/_index|Problem 362]], with
$A=\{a_1<\cdots<a_N\}\subseteq\mathbb N$: the subsets $S\subseteq A$ with
$|S|=l$ and $\sum S=t$ are the sign vectors whose sum
$\sum_k\varepsilon_k(a_k,1)$ equals $(2t-\sum A,\,2l-N)$, so their number is
at most the number of signed sums in the unit ball around that point. The
vectors $(a_k,1)$ are $1$-separated, and the fixed-size count does not change
when $A$ is translated, so after moving the middle element of $A$ to $0$ at
least $N/4$ of the vectors satisfy the condition of the theorem for $N\ge6$;
the count is therefore at most $c(1/4,2)2^N/N^2$, with $N\le5$ covered by
the trivial bound. That reduction is the problem page's authored line,
detailed on the library's result page, and is not spelled out in Halász's
remark.

**Covers.** The second question of Problem 362: for every $N$-element
$A\subseteq\mathbb N$, every $l$ and every $t$, the number of subsets of $A$
of size $l$ with sum $t$ is $\ll2^N/N^2$ with an absolute constant. The first
question has
[[problems/number_theory/E0362/claims/1965_01_01_sarkozy_szemeredi|Sárközy and Szemerédi's page]].

**Formalization.** The statement file of the formal-conjectures project
(`FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/362.lean`) states the second question as
its declaration `erdos_362.variants.fixed_card`, marks it solved and points,
through its `formal_proof` attribute, at a Lean 4 file in Boris Alexeev's
`lean-proofs` repository, linked above at the commit the attribute pins. That
file declares itself a formalization of a solution to Problem 362, names
András Sárközy, Endre Szemerédi and Gábor Halász as its informal authors
and Codex and GPT-5.6 Sol as its formal authors, and cites this paper for
the fixed-cardinality estimate. The second conjunct of its theorem `erdos_362`, a
bound $C\,2^N/N^2$ on the number of subsets of $A$ of size $l$ with sum $t$
(`fixedCardSubsetSumFiber A l t`) over all nonempty finite
$A\subseteq\mathbb N$ and all $l$ and $t$, is this claim; the first conjunct
is the claim of
[[problems/number_theory/E0362/claims/1965_01_01_sarkozy_szemeredi|Sárközy and Szemerédi's page]].
The file contains no `sorry`, no `axiom` command and no `native_decide` at
the pinned commit. This project has not built the file or audited its
statement against the problem, so it supplies no `formalized` evidence; the
site's label PROVED (LEAN) refers to this development.

**Acceptance.** The paper is refereed: G. Halász, *Estimates for the
concentration function of combinatorial number theory and probability*,
Period. Math. Hungar. 8, no. 3--4 (September 1977), 197--211, received
29 January 1976. The conjecture is Erdős's, stated in 1965 and again in 1973
as never proved. The site's curator, Thomas F. Bloom, marks Problem 362
proved and credits this paper with the affirmative answer to the second
question, as a consequence of its multidimensional theorem. The page is
dated by the first day of the issue month, since the paper's first posting
carries no finer date.
