***

**Claim.** For every $\delta>0$ there is a number $C(\delta)$ such that

$$
N(X,\delta)<C(\delta)\,X^{1/2}\qquad\text{for all }X\ge1,
$$

where $N(X,\delta)$ is the largest number of points in a disc of radius $X$
whose pairwise distances all lie at distance at least $\delta$ from the
nearest integer, as [[problems/number_theory/E0465/_index|Problem 465]]
defines it. This is the Theorem of Konyagin's paper (p. 630), translated
from the Russian statement as the result page
[[../library/number_theory/konyagin_2001_distances_between_points_plane/theorem|theorem]]
translates it;
the digest is on the source card
[[../library/number_theory/konyagin_2001_distances_between_points_plane/_index|konyagin_2001_distances_between_points_plane]].

**What it settles.** Both questions of the problem. The bound is $o(X)$,
which is the first question. For the second, given $\varepsilon>0$ one has
$C(\delta)X^{1/2}\le X^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ as soon as
$X\ge C(\delta)^{1/\varepsilon}$, so $N(X,\delta)<X^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ for
all large $X$, which is what $N(X,\delta)<X^{1/2+o(1)}$ asks (an authored
one-line remark). Konyagin's introduction states the question of Erdős and
Graham in exactly this form and names a positive answer as the paper's aim.
No exponent below $1/2$ holds for all small $\delta$: Sárközy's Theorem 1 of
1976 gives $N(X,\delta)>X^{1/2-\delta^{1/7}}$, compiled on
[[problems/number_theory/E0466/_index|Problem 466]], and the lower exponent
$1/2-\delta^{1/7}$ tends to $1/2$ as $\delta\to0$. The first question had
been settled in 1976 by Sárközy's bound of order $X/\log\log X$, the
[[problems/number_theory/E0465/claims/1976_01_01_sarkozy|accepted partial claim]]
beside this page.

**Argument, in outline.** Exponential sums
$A_k(\varphi)=\sum_je(k(x_j\cos\varphi+y_j\sin\varphi))$ averaged over the
direction $\varphi$; the inequality
$\sum_kd_k\int_0^{2\pi}|A_k(\varphi)|^2\,d\varphi\ge0$ for nonnegative
weights; Bessel's identity, which turns each cross term into
$2\pi J_0(2\pi k\,d(P_i,P_j))$; the asymptotic expansion of $J_0$; Lemma 1
(p. 632), a cosine polynomial with nonnegative coefficients that is negative
together with the absolute value of its conjugate on
$[2\pi\delta,2\pi(1-\delta)]$; and weights $d_k=(k/2)^{1/2}c_k$ that make the
off-diagonal contribution at most $-A(2X)^{-1/2}n^2$ against error terms
$O(n)$, whence $n=O(X^{1/2})$. The proof (pp. 630--633) is not checked
here.

on the plane (in Russian), Mat. Zametki 69 (2001), no. 4, 630--633, Brief
Communications, received 21 September 2000 and revised 5 October 2000;
English translation, About distances between points on the plane, Math.
Notes 69 (2001), no. 3--4, 578--581 (not held). Reviewed: the site's curator,
