***

**Claim.** The request of [[problems/number_theory/E0482/_index|Problem 482]],
results like the Graham--Pollak identity for $\sqrt m$ and other algebraic
numbers, is answered for every positive real $w$. Theorem 1.2 of the 2005 paper
gives, for every $w>0$ and every integer $j\ge1$, two families of two-step
recurrences $u_1=1$, $u_{n+1}=\lfloor a(u_n+\varepsilon_n)\rfloor$, whose
multiplier alternates between $a$ and $b=2/a$ with the parity of $n$ and whose
shifts are $1/2$ (one family allows any $\varepsilon\in[1/3,2/3)$ on even
steps), such that the differences $u_{2n+1}-2u_{2n-1}$ are the binary digits of
$w$; the Graham--Pollak recurrence is the case $j=1$, $\varepsilon=1/2$,
$w=\sqrt2$ of the family allowing any $\varepsilon$ (Case I). Theorem 1.3 does
the same in every integer base $g\ge2$, with $u_{2n+1}-gu_{2n-1}$ the base-$g$
digits of $w$. Theorem 3.1 of the 2006 paper extends the construction to integer
starting values $m$ and integer parameters $(l,k)$, with $(m,l)$ in six explicit
cones, which require $m\ge1$ or $m\le-2$, and $(g-1)\mid(k-1)l$; Theorems 3.3
and 3.4 give two further binary families; and Corollary 3.5 identifies, for
every integer $m\notin\{-1,0\}$, which number's binary digits the original
recurrence with $u_1=m$ produces. The statements are quoted on the result pages
[[../library/number_theory/stoll_2005_families_nonlinear_recurrences_related_digits/theorem_1_2|theorem_1_2]],
[[../library/number_theory/stoll_2005_families_nonlinear_recurrences_related_digits/theorem_1_3|theorem_1_3]],
[[../library/number_theory/stoll_2006_problem_erdos_graham_concerning_digits/theorem_3_1|theorem_3_1]],
[[../library/number_theory/stoll_2006_problem_erdos_graham_concerning_digits/theorem_3_3|theorem_3_3]]
and
[[../library/number_theory/stoll_2006_problem_erdos_graham_concerning_digits/corollary_3_5|corollary_3_5]];
the digests are on the source cards
[[../library/number_theory/stoll_2005_families_nonlinear_recurrences_related_digits/_index|stoll_2005_families_nonlinear_recurrences_related_digits]]
and
[[../library/number_theory/stoll_2006_problem_erdos_graham_concerning_digits/_index|stoll_2006_problem_erdos_graham_concerning_digits]].
Algebraicity plays no role: the theorems hold for all of $\mathbb R^+$, so every
$\sqrt m$ and every positive algebraic number is covered. They construct
families and do not classify every recurrence with the digit property, which the
request never asked for. The inductive proofs were read for structure only and
are not checked here.
