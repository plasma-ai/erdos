# problems/number_theory/E0952/claims

[[problems/number_theory/E0952/_index|..]]

[[problems/number_theory/E0952/claims/1997_01_01_gethner_stark|1997_01_01_gethner_stark]]: Gethner and Stark prove, from any starting point, that no walk to infinity
on the Gaussian primes has steps of length at most 2, a negative answer for
every step bound up to 2.

[[problems/number_theory/E0952/claims/2019_08_27_das|2019_08_27_das]]: An arXiv note of August 2019 claimed that no infinite sequence of distinct
Gaussian primes off the axes has bounded steps; its author withdrew it in
September 2024, saying that the paths of its Theorem 3 miss primes.

[[problems/number_theory/E0952/claims/2024_01_16_stumpenhusen|2024_01_16_stumpenhusen]]: A three-page arXiv preprint of January 2024 claimed that no bounded-step
walk on the Gaussian primes reaches infinity; its author withdrew it the
next day, saying that the width of the moat was not correctly computed.

[[problems/number_theory/E0952/claims/2026_09_26_openai|2026_09_26_openai]]: The release manuscript proves that for every step bound D the components of
the distance-D graph on Gaussian primes have bounded size, so no infinite
bounded-step sequence of distinct Gaussian primes exists; Lean proof built here.
