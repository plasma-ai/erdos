***

**Claim.** For every $\epsilon$ with $0<\epsilon\le1$ there is a constant
$c(\epsilon)$ such that the eventual-time thresholds of the Legendre-symbol
partial sums have mean value $c(\epsilon)$ over the primes:
$\pi(x)^{-1}\sum_{p\le x}g(\epsilon,p)\to c(\epsilon)$ as $x\to\infty$,
where $g(\epsilon,p)$ is the least $t$ such that
$\bigl|\sum_{n\le m}(n/p)\bigr|<\epsilon m$ for every $m\ge t$. The proof
ends with the quantitative form
$\sum_{p\le x}g(\epsilon,p)=c(\epsilon)\pi(x)(1+O((\log\log x)^{-1/8}))$
and identifies $c(\epsilon)=\sum_{\mu\ge1}\mu d_\mu$, where $d_\mu$ is the
limiting frequency of the primes with $g(\epsilon,p)=\mu$. Since
$\pi(x)\sim x/\log x$, this is the asymptotic
$\sum_{p<x}f_\epsilon(p)\sim c_\epsilon x/\log x$ asked by
[[problems/number_theory/E0981/_index|Problem 981]], Erdős's display (80)
of 1965, with the two-sided threshold $g$ in place of the one-sided
threshold $f$ of Erdős and of the problem's statement, and with
$c(\epsilon)\ge1$ because every $g(\epsilon,p)\ge1$. The theorem is quoted
from the printed page on the result page
[[../library/number_theory/elliott_1969_conjecture_erdos_concerning_character_sums/theorem_p165|theorem_p165]];
the digest is on the source card
[[../library/number_theory/elliott_1969_conjecture_erdos_concerning_character_sums/_index|elliott_1969_conjecture_erdos_concerning_character_sums]].

**The one-sided threshold.** The paper opens with Erdős's one-sided threshold
$f(\epsilon,p)$ and the conjecture as its display (1), notes $f(1,p)=n_2(p)$,
the least quadratic nonresidue, and then proves the theorem for the two-sided
$g(\epsilon,p)$, saying of the original definition only that simple changes in
the argument give a similar result (p. 164); no adaptation is printed. Termwise
$f(\epsilon,p)\le g(\epsilon,p)$, which does not by itself transfer the
asymptotic. The problem's wording, the one-sided form, therefore rests on the
printed theorem together with the author's remark, as the problem page's Status
records. For $\epsilon\ge1$ the one-sided form is settled without this paper:
the problem page's Formulation shows that for $\epsilon>1$ both thresholds equal
$1$ for every odd prime, and records the identity $f(1,p)=n_2(p)$ that makes the
instance $\epsilon=1$ Erdős's theorem (78) under its Origin.

**Argument, in outline.** The primes $p\le x$ whose two-sided sums reach
$\epsilon m$ for some $m\ge N$ are few by a fourth-moment large-sieve
bound (Lemma 7); for the rest, $g(\epsilon,p)=\mu\le N$ is decided by the
symbols $(n/p)$ for $n\le N$, so by the residue of $p$ modulo $N!$, and
with $N=[\sqrt{\log\log x}]$ the Siegel--Walfisz theorem gives each $\mu$
its limiting frequency $d_\mu$. The proof was not checked here beyond this
outline.
