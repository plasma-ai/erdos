***

**Claim.** Theorem 1 (p. 2): for all $n\ge4$, $f(n)\le\lfloor n/4\rfloor+d$
with $d=1,2,2,4$ for $n\equiv0,1,2,3\pmod4$, from explicit badly ordered
pairs around $1/2$ (for $n=4m$, the fractions $(2m-1)/(4m)$ and $2m/(4m-1)$,
at index distance $m+2$). Theorem 2 (p. 5): fractions of order $n$ at index
distance at most $\frac n{12}(1-4n^{-1/3})$ are similarly ordered, so
$f(n)\ge(\frac1{12}-o(1))n$, by optimizing Erdős's 1943 argument with a
lemma on the arithmetic progressions formed by the Farey neighbors of a
fraction of small denominator, and Dress's discrepancy bound. The Conjecture
(p. 2): $f(n)>n/4$ for all $n\ge4$ and $f(n)=\lfloor n/4\rfloor+d$ for all
$n\ge92$, checked for $n\le5000$, the exceptions below $92$ being
$n=7,9,11,15,19,23,25,27,31,35,39,49,51,63,91$. The paper defines $f(n)$ as
the site does (p. 1): the largest integer such that $a_k/b_k$ and $a_l/b_l$
are similarly ordered whenever $|l-k|\le f(n)$, so its theorems are
statements about the $f(n)$ of [[problems/number_theory/E1005/_index|Problem
1005]] and need no bridge. The theorems are compiled on the result pages
[[../library/number_theory/doorn_2025_improved_bounds_mayer_erdos_phenomenon_similarly/theorem_1|theorem_1]]
and
[[../library/number_theory/doorn_2025_improved_bounds_mayer_erdos_phenomenon_similarly/theorem_2|theorem_2]];
the digest is on the card
[[../library/number_theory/doorn_2025_improved_bounds_mayer_erdos_phenomenon_similarly/_index|doorn_2025_improved_bounds_mayer_erdos_phenomenon_similarly]].

**Covers.** The upper bound $f(n)\le\frac n4+O(1)$, with the explicit $d$,
which is the upper half of the asymptotic $f(n)=(\frac14+o(1))n$, together
with the lower bound $(\frac1{12}-o(1))n$. The matching lower bound
$(\frac14-o(1))n$, which decides the problem's question, is on
[[problems/number_theory/E1005/claims/2026_07_04_cipollini|Cipollini's page]];
the exact-value conjecture for $n\ge92$ is not part of the problem and is
claimed on
[[problems/number_theory/E1005/claims/2026_07_28_wang_xie_zhao|the Wang--Xie--Zhao page]].

**Acceptance.** Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas F. Bloom, labels the
problem solved and credits van Doorn, in the problem's commentary, with
the bounds $(\frac1{12}-o(1))n\le f(n)\le\frac n4+O(1)$ and the conjecture
that the upper bound is optimal, the resolution being these bounds together
with Cipollini's lower bound; the curator neither wrote nor submitted the
result. Not refereed: arXiv:2509.00121v1 (28 August 2025) is the only
version, and no journal record was found (Crossref, 2026-09-18); the bounds
are also the formula lines of OEIS A386893, van Doorn's own entry. The
proofs were read for structure only, and nothing here is independently
reviewed by this project.

**Depends on.** Nothing on the wiki; the theorems are proved in the preprint
linked above.
