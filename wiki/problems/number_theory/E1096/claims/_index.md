# problems/number_theory/E1096/claims

[[problems/number_theory/E1096/_index|..]]

[[problems/number_theory/E1096/claims/1998_04_01_erdos_komornik|1998_04_01_erdos_komornik]]: The 1998 theorem that the gaps of the ordered sums of distinct powers of q
tend to zero for every q in (1, 2^(1/4)] other than the square root of the
second Pisot number, the first resolution; refereed, credited by the site.

[[problems/number_theory/E1096/claims/2011_03_23_akiyama_komornik|2011_03_23_akiyama_komornik]]: The theorem that the gaps of the ordered sums of distinct powers of q tend
to zero for every q in (1, 2^(1/3)], closing the point Erdős and Komornik
had left out; refereed in the Journal of Number Theory (2013).

[[problems/number_theory/E1096/claims/2011_11_10_feng|2011_11_10_feng]]: Feng's theorem that the gaps tend to zero for every q in (1, sqrt 2) whose
square is not a Pisot number; with Siegel's smallest Pisot number this
answers the question for every q below about 1.151; refereed in JEMS 2016.

[[problems/number_theory/E1096/claims/2026_04_16_acosta_de_leon|2026_04_16_acosta_de_leon]]: A two-page note of 16 April 2026, posted on a data repository and linked
from the problem's thread, that deduces the answer for every q below the
square root of the smallest Pisot number from Feng's Theorem 1.4; pending.
