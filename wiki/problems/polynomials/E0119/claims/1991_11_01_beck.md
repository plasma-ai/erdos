***

**Claim.** There is an absolute $c>0$ such that for every sequence
$z_1,z_2,\dots$ on the unit circle,

$$
\max_{n\le N}M_n>N^c\qquad\text{for all large }N,
$$

where $M_n=\max_{\lvert z\rvert=1}\lvert\prod_{i\le n}(z-z_i)\rvert$. Hence
$M_n>n^c$ for infinitely many $n$: if $M_n\le n^c$ for every $n>n_0$, then
$\max_{n\le N}M_n\le\max(C,N^c)$ with $C=\max_{n\le n_0}M_n$, which equals
$N^c$ for large $N$, against Beck's strict inequality. This is
Beck, *The modulus of polynomials with zeros on the unit circle: a problem of
Erdős*, Ann. of Math. (2) 134 (1991), no. 3, 609–651, cited as [Be91] on the
problem page; the quantifiers, absolute constants $c,c_0>0$ with
$\max_{n\le N}M_n>c_0N^c$ for all $N$ and all sequences, are as the zbMATH
review (Zbl 0747.11031) states them, and shrinking $c$ absorbs $c_0$ for
large $N$. It answers the second question of
[[problems/polynomials/E0119/_index|Problem 119]] yes. The exponent cannot
be $1$: Erdős gave a sequence with $M_n\le n+1$ for every $n$ (recorded in
Hayman's collection [Ha74]), and Linden [Li77] a sequence with
$M_n\ll n^{1-c}$ for some $c>0$. The value of Beck's constant is not known:
the site's discussion (the curator's comment of 2026-07-19) records that
nobody has worked it out and presumes that Beck did not compute it.

**Covers.** The second question, that $M_n>n^c$ for infinitely many $n$ for
some $c>0$, and with it the first, already answered by
[[problems/polynomials/E0119/claims/1980_03_01_wagner|Wagner 1980]]. It does
not cover the third question, on the sum $\sum_{k\le n}M_k$, which
[[problems/polynomials/E0119/claims/2026_07_14_korsky|Korsky 2026]]
answered.
