# problems/polynomials/E0119/claims

[[problems/polynomials/E0119/_index|..]]

[[problems/polynomials/E0119/claims/1980_03_01_wagner|1980_03_01_wagner]]: Wagner proves that for every sequence on the unit circle the maximum modulus
of the partial products exceeds a fixed power of log n infinitely often, so
is unbounded; answers the first question, refereed and credited by the site.

[[problems/polynomials/E0119/claims/1991_11_01_beck|1991_11_01_beck]]: Beck proves that for every sequence on the unit circle the largest of the
first N maximum moduli exceeds N^c for all large N, with an absolute c>0;
answers the second question, refereed in the Annals, credited by the site.

[[problems/polynomials/E0119/claims/2026_07_14_korsky|2026_07_14_korsky]]: Korsky, working with GPT 5.6-Pro, proves that for every sequence on the unit
circle the sum of the first N maximum moduli is at least a constant times
N^(5/4)/sqrt(log N), answering the third question; accepted by the site.

[[problems/polynomials/E0119/claims/2026_08_29_korsky|2026_08_29_korsky]]: Korsky, working with GPT-5.6 Pro, claims that for every sequence on the unit
circle the first N maximum moduli sum to at least (e^(-1/2)+o(1)) N^(3/2),
believed sharp; a shared write-up on the site's thread, credited by the site.
