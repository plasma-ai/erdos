***

**Claim.** For every $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ there is $N_0$ such that every
length $N\ge N_0$ admits signs
$\varepsilon_0,\dots,\varepsilon_{N-1}\in\{-1,1\}$ with

$$
(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt N\le\Bigl\lvert\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\varepsilon_k z^k\Bigr\rvert
\le(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt N
\qquad\text{for every }\lvert z\rvert=1,
$$

the real points $z=\pm1$ included (the main theorem of the release's manuscript
*Ultraflat real Littlewood polynomials*, 2026-10-05, carded as
[[../library/polynomials/openai_2026_ultraflat_real_littlewood_polynomials/_index|Ultraflat
real Littlewood polynomials]]). A companion manuscript of the same date,
*Nearly minimal maxima and positive minima of Littlewood polynomials*, carded
as
[[../library/polynomials/openai_2026_nearly_minimal_maxima_positive_minima_littlewood_polynomials/_index|Nearly
minimal maxima and positive minima of Littlewood polynomials]], claims the
weaker two-sided bound $\sqrt N/16\le\lvert P(z)\rvert\le(1+\eta)\sqrt N$ for
every $\eta>0$ and every large $N$. The release names OpenAI as the author of
both manuscripts; its README says that its manuscripts and proof artifacts were
produced by an internal OpenAI model and that the collection includes results
at different stages of verification. Either statement answers
[[problems/polynomials/E0228/_index|Problem 228]] yes for every large degree
and is strictly stronger than the accepted answer of
[[problems/polynomials/E0228/claims/2019_07_22_balister_bollobas_morris_sahasrabudhe_tiba|Balister
et al. 2020]], whose constants are fixed but far from $1$ (its Theorem 2.1
gives $2^{-160}\sqrt n\le\lvert P(z)\rvert\le2^{12}\sqrt n$ on the circle for
centered polynomials $\sum_{k=-2n}^{2n}\varepsilon_kz^k$, of degree $4n$): the
ultraflat theorem forces the ratio $\lvert P(z)\rvert/\sqrt N$ to $1$ uniformly
on the circle, and the companion improves the constants to $1/16$ below and
$1+\eta$ above. The same family bears on
[[problems/polynomials/E1150/_index|Problem 1150]], which asks whether the
maximum modulus of every such polynomial exceeds $(1+c)\sqrt n$ for one fixed
$c>0$; that page carries its own account. The release describes its route as
relaxed coefficients in $[-1,1]$ with small defect, a coefficient cap built
from quadratic-phase waves sampled off an auxiliary torus polynomial with
Pippenger–Spencer packing, and a defect-sensitive rounding to signs by the
Spencer and Lovett–Meka partial colorings.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Standing.** The claim is a manuscript statement and stays `claimed`. The
release's `lean/` folder (the formalization link, at the pinned revision)
proves two statements from the earlier manuscript *Asymptotically minimal
maxima of real Littlewood polynomials* (2026-09-23, carded as
[[../library/polynomials/openai_2026_asymptotically_minimal_maxima_real_littlewood_polynomials/_index|Asymptotically
minimal maxima of real Littlewood polynomials]]):
`OAI.AsymptoticallyMinimalLittlewood.main`, that for every $\eta>0$ and every
large $N$ some real signing has
$\max_{\lvert z\rvert=1}\lvert P(z)\rvert\le(1+\eta)\sqrt N$, and
`OAI.AsymptoticallyMinimalLittlewoodFiniteFlatness.main`, that one all-length
family of real signings has the $L^p$ mean of
$\bigl\lvert\lvert P_N(z)\rvert/\sqrt N-1\bigr\rvert$ over the circle tending
to $0$ for every finite $p>0$; the comparator challenges
`AsymptoticallyMinimalLittlewood.lean` and `LittlewoodFiniteFlatness.lean` of
the same folder pin the two statements. Neither declaration states a lower bound
for $\lvert P(z)\rvert$ on the circle, which is the half of the question that
the accepted proof made hard and that the $L^p$ statement leaves open (an $L^p$
mean near $0$ allows zeros on the circle); the two October 5 manuscripts that
carry the lower bound have no Lean in the release. The release's family document
for that formalization says its results are existential, with no effective rate
and no signing algorithm. Neither declaration bounds $\lvert P(z)\rvert$ from
below, so the formalization settles nothing this problem asks. An upper bound of
order $\sqrt N$ is classical (Rudin–Shapiro); the release's sharper
$(1+\eta)\sqrt N$ bound answers [[problems/polynomials/E1150/_index|Problem
1150]] but does not touch the lower half of the question. The formalization link
is therefore recorded for the stronger claim's provenance and contributes no
`formalized` evidence. No outside review, referee report or acceptance by the
site is recorded.
