**Claim.** The answer to Problem 230 is no. Kahane [Ka80] constructs, for
every $n$, a polynomial $P(z)=\sum_k a_kz^k$ of degree $n$ with complex
coefficients of modulus one such that

$$
\lvert P(z)\rvert=(1+o(1))\sqrt n
$$

uniformly on the unit circle, with a relative error that vanishes as the
degree grows; such polynomials are called ultraflat. Given any $c>0$, a large
$n$ then gives a unimodular polynomial with
$\max_{\lvert z\rvert=1}\lvert P(z)\rvert<(1+c)\sqrt n$, so no constant $c>0$
makes the displayed inequality of
[[problems/polynomials/E0230/_index|Problem 230]] hold for every $n\ge2$. The
site's polynomial carries the coefficients $a_1,\ldots,a_n$; a shift of the
index range multiplies $P$ by a power of $z$, which changes nothing on the
circle, and one coefficient more or fewer changes the maximum by at most one,
which is $o(\sqrt n)$. The lower bound $\sqrt n$ is Parseval's identity, and
Hayman's 1974 list [Ha74], where the question is Problem 4.31, attributes the
conjecture to Erdős and Newman; Erdős himself expected the answer yes.
Littlewood had obtained $(1+o(1))\sqrt n$ away from a small arc, Körner [Ko80]
introduced probabilistic ideas for passing from coefficients bounded by one to
unimodular ones, and Kahane obtained the uniform statement with relative error
$O(n^{-1/17}\sqrt{\log n})$, as the introduction of Bombieri and Bourgain
reports (digested on the card
[[../library/polynomials/bombieri_2009_kahane_ultraflat_polynomials/_index|bombieri_2009_kahane_ultraflat_polynomials]],
printed pp. 627--628). Their footnote 1 (p. 627) says that Theorem 2 of Byrnes
is incorrect and that its use invalidates the proofs of Körner's Theorems 6
and 7, while his Lemma 2, the basic tool for unimodularity, remains valid.
Kahane's paper is not held; the statement above follows the site's commentary
and the introduction of Bombieri and Bourgain (pp. 627--628). The sharper
construction of
[[problems/polynomials/E0230/claims/2009_06_30_bombieri_bourgain|Bombieri and Bourgain]]
is a second, independent disproof; the coefficients in both are general points
of the unit circle, so neither settles the real-sign question of
[[problems/polynomials/E1150/_index|Problem 1150]], which the release's
[[problems/polynomials/E0230/claims/2026_09_23_openai|real-sign construction]]
addresses.
