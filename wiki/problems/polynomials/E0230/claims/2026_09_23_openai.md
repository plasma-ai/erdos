**Claim.** Write $m_N$ for the minimum, over signs
$\varepsilon_0,\ldots,\varepsilon_{N-1}\in\{-1,1\}$, of
$N^{-1/2}\max_{\lvert z\rvert=1}\lvert\sum_{k<N}\varepsilon_kz^k\rvert$;
Parseval's identity gives $m_N\ge1$. Theorem 1.1 of OpenAI, *Asymptotically
minimal maxima of real Littlewood polynomials*, OpenAI Math Release preprint,
23 September 2026, at the pinned revision of the release repository (the
preprint link), states that
$m_N\to1$: for every $\eta>0$ there is $N_0$ such that every integer
$N\ge N_0$ admits signs $\varepsilon_0,\ldots,\varepsilon_{N-1}$ with

$$
\max_{\lvert z\rvert=1}\Bigl\lvert\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\varepsilon_kz^k\Bigr\rvert
\le(1+\eta)\sqrt N .
$$

The manuscript is paged at
[[../library/polynomials/openai_2026_asymptotically_minimal_maxima_real_littlewood_polynomials/theorem_1_1|the result page]]
of
[[../library/polynomials/openai_2026_asymptotically_minimal_maxima_real_littlewood_polynomials/_index|its card]].
Real signs are coefficients of modulus one, so this refutes the constant asked
for in [[problems/polynomials/E0230/_index|Problem 230]]: given $c>0$, take
$\eta=c/2$ and $n\ge\max(N_0,2)$, and put $a_k=\varepsilon_{k-1}$ for
$1\le k\le n$; the site's polynomial is $z$ times the sum above, has the same
modulus on the circle, and its maximum is at most
$(1+c/2)\sqrt n<(1+c)\sqrt n$. The site's question was already answered no by
Kahane with complex unimodular coefficients, and this is a second route with
coefficients from the smaller class $\{-1,1\}$, so the same theorem answers the
real-sign question of [[problems/polynomials/E1150/_index|Problem 1150]]
negatively; that problem's page is the place for that claim. The theorem is
one-sided and existential: it gives no lower bound for $\lvert P\rvert$, no
convergence rate and no signing algorithm, and the manuscript notes that
Erdélyi's additive gap $\max\lvert P\rvert^2\ge N+(N-1)^{1/3}/38$ is compatible
with it. The proof first builds relaxed coefficients in $[-1,1]$ with small
defect from signs by sampling quadratic-phase waves off a real trigonometric
polynomial on an auxiliary torus, packing their Fourier supports with the
Pippenger--Spencer coloring theorem, then rounds them to signs with a
defect-sensitive discrepancy bound, using the partial-coloring method of Beck
and Spencer in the arbitrary-vector form verified through the theorem of Lovett
and Meka (Sections 2--6). The same manuscript derives unbounded binary merit
factors through all lengths, a uniquely ergodic binary Morse shift with simple
spectrum whose zero-coordinate spectral measure is absolutely continuous ($L^2$
density), and $L^p$ flatness for every finite $p$, and its Appendix A disputes
nonflatness claims in preprints of el Abdalaoui; none of these is part of this
claim. Two later release manuscripts of 5 October 2026 strengthen the
construction without Lean: *Nearly minimal maxima and positive minima of
Littlewood polynomials*
([[../library/polynomials/openai_2026_nearly_minimal_maxima_positive_minima_littlewood_polynomials/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]]
of
[[../library/polynomials/openai_2026_nearly_minimal_maxima_positive_minima_littlewood_polynomials/_index|its card]])
adds the lower bound $\sqrt N/16\le\lvert P(z)\rvert$ to the same upper bound,
and *Ultraflat real Littlewood polynomials*
([[../library/polynomials/openai_2026_ultraflat_real_littlewood_polynomials/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
of
[[../library/polynomials/openai_2026_ultraflat_real_littlewood_polynomials/_index|its card]])
states that for every $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ and every large $N$ some signs give
$(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt N\le\lvert P(z)\rvert\le(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt N$ on the
whole circle, the points $z=\pm1$ included, a real-sign ultraflat family
stronger than the constant-factor flatness of
[[problems/polynomials/E0228/_index|Problem 228]]. Those two statements are
recorded here as the same claimant's unformalized companions; the accepted
claim is Theorem 1.1 of 23 September.

**Formalization.** The release's Lean tree (folder `lean/` at the pinned
revision, with the release's pinned Lean and Mathlib) proves
`OAI.AsymptoticallyMinimalLittlewood.main : MainStatement` in
`OAI/Analysis/Littlewood/Main.lean`, where the comparator challenge
`ComparatorChallenges/AsymptoticallyMinimalLittlewood.lean` defines

```lean
def littlewoodValue {N : ℕ} (ε : Fin N → ℝ) (z : ℂ) : ℂ :=
  ∑ k : Fin N, (ε k : ℂ) * z ^ (k : ℕ)

def IsRealSigning {N : ℕ} (ε : Fin N → ℝ) : Prop :=
  ∀ k, ε k = -1 ∨ ε k = 1

def MainStatement : Prop :=
  ∀ η : ℝ, 0 < η → ∃ N₀ : ℕ, 1 ≤ N₀ ∧ ∀ N : ℕ, N₀ ≤ N →
    ∃ ε : Fin N → ℝ, IsRealSigning ε ∧
      ∀ z : ℂ, ‖z‖ = 1 → ‖littlewoodValue ε z‖ ≤ (1 + η) * Real.sqrt N
```

and its configuration `AsymptoticallyMinimalLittlewood.json` pins the
declaration `OAI.AsymptoticallyMinimalLittlewood.main` (its
`definition_names` list is empty) and permits only the axioms `propext`,
`Quot.sound` and `Classical.choice`; the release's catalog
`lean/formalization.yaml` lists the declaration under this manuscript, and
the release's family note pairs it with a second comparator,
`LittlewoodFiniteFlatness.lean`, for the $L^p$ statement, which is not part
of this claim. The bridge from the declaration to the site's question, the
factor $z$, the passage from the pointwise bound to the maximum and the
choice $\eta=c/2$ with $n\ge\max(N_0,2)$, is elementary and is not in Lean.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki: the proof is self-contained in the
manuscript and its Lean tree, and the Lean statement rests on Mathlib's
finite sums, complex norm and `Real.sqrt` alone.
