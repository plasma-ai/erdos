***

Schinzel [Sc87] proves the conjecture of Rényi, first published by Erdős,
that the least number $Q_k$ of nonzero coefficients of the square of a
polynomial with exactly $k$ nonzero complex coefficients tends to infinity
with $k$, in the explicit form

$$
Q_k > \frac{\log\log k}{\log 2}.
$$

The bound is the case $l=2$ of his Theorem 1: for a field $K$, a polynomial
$f\in K[x]$ with $T\ge2$ terms whose power $f^l$ has $t$ terms, and either
$\operatorname{char}K=0$ or $\operatorname{char}K>l\deg f$,

$$
t \ge l+1+\frac{1}{\log 2}\log\Bigl(1+\frac{\log(T-1)}{l\log 4l-\log l}\Bigr).
$$

The problem's $f(k)$ is the same minimum taken over rational polynomials
only, so $f(k)\ge Q_k$ and $f(k)\to\infty$, which answers
[[problems/polynomials/E0485/_index|Problem 485]] yes. The proof inducts on
$t$, using Hajós's lemma on the number of terms forced by a zero of high
multiplicity, a reduction of $f^l\in K[x^d]$ to $f\in K[x^d]$, and a sequence
of differential operators; the
[[../library/polynomials/schinzel_1987_number_terms_power_polynomial/_index|source card]]
digests the paper, whose Theorem 2 treats positive characteristic. Schinzel
notes that the bound is far from Erdős's upper bound $Q_k<C_1k^{1-C_2}$
([[../library/polynomials/erdos_1949_number_terms_square_polynomial/_index|card]]).
The paper was received by the journal on 1985-11-22, as its last page
records, and published in 1987.

no. 1, 55–70. Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas F. Bloom, records the
problem as solved by Schinzel with this bound in the problem's commentary
(page last edited 2026-04-08, read 2026-10-07), and Schinzel and Zannier's

([[problems/polynomials/E0485/claims/2009_03_31_schinzel_zannier|Schinzel and Zannier 2009]])

theorem `schinzel_support_bound` for the square case in an imported
development; the file ends with an axiom print. The site labels the problem
PROVED, with no Lean qualification, and the formal-conjectures statement
file for the problem points to that proof. This corpus has neither built the
development nor audited its statement, so no `formalized` evidence is
