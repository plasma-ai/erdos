***

**Claim.** For every monic polynomial $f\in\mathbb{C}[z]$ of degree $2$, $3$
or $4$, the set $\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\lvert f(z)\rvert\le1\}$ can be covered by
disks the sum of whose radii is at most $2$. This is the question of
[[problems/polynomials/E0509/_index|Problem 509]] restricted to degree at
most four. The claim was registered on the site's proof-claims page on
2026-07-19 by Boon Qing Hong as a partial claim, with a write-up in a shared
PDF, linked above, that the claimant says was mainly generated by ChatGPT
(the claim's tools line names Chat GPT-5.5 Pro and 5.6 Sol). That PDF is a
different document from the claimant's strategy note on the general
question, carded as
[[../library/polynomials/hong_2026_strategy_proposal_covering_lemniscates_erdos_problem/_index|Hong 2026]],
which proposes a route and proves no case; this claim rests on the PDF
linked above.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Boon Qing
Hong (account Qing_Hong) on 19 July 2026, giving "Chat GPT-5.5 Pro, 5.6 Sol" as
the AI used:

> Here is a result on the problem up to quartic polynomials, mainly generated by
> ChatGPT. - The quadratic case is trivial. - The cubic case can be solved by
> bounding the variables. - The quartic case can be solved by bounding as well,
> followed by a computer-assisted proof to bound $\mathcal{F}(X, Y, Z).$ (Code
> is included in PDF) GPT also suggested an MST approach as a promising approach
> among several approaches.

**Covers.** Degrees $2$, $3$ and $4$ only; the claimant calls the quadratic
case trivial. (Degree $1$ is not part of the claim; this page notes only that
it is immediate, the set being a disk of radius $1$.) The general question,
every degree, is untouched; the known general bounds are Cartan's $2e$ and
Pommerenke's $2.59$ in place of $2$, and Pommerenke proved that $2$ suffices
when the open set $\{|f|<1\}$ is connected, the accepted partial claim on
[[problems/polynomials/E0509/claims/1959_01_01_pommerenke|Pommerenke 1959]]
([[../library/analysis/pommerenke_1959_some_problems_erdos_herzog_piranian/_index|library card]],
cited on the problem page as [Po59]), and more generally when the closed set
$\{|f|\le1\}$ is connected, the accepted partial claim on
[[problems/polynomials/E0509/claims/1961_01_01_pommerenke|Pommerenke 1961]].

**Argument, as the claim summarizes it.** The cubic case is settled by
bounding the variables, and the quartic case by bounding as well, followed
by a computer-assisted bound on a function the write-up calls
$\mathcal{F}(X,Y,Z)$, with the code included in the PDF. The summary adds
that the system also suggested an approach through minimum spanning trees
as a promising route to the general question; that suggestion is not a
result and is not covered by this claim. The write-up is behind a sign-in;
this account follows the site's claim entry (2026-10-07).
