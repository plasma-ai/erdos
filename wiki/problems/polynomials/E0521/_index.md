# Problem 521

[[problems/polynomials/_index|..]]

[[problems/polynomials/E0521/claims/_index|claims/]]: The 6 claim pages of Problem 521, one per claimant's result; the problem's standing derives from them.

***

**Statement.** Let $(\epsilon_k)_{k\geq 0}$ be independently uniformly chosen at
random from $\{-1,1\}$. If $R_n$ counts the number of real roots of
$f_n(z)=\sum_{0\leq k\leq n}\epsilon_k z^k$ then is it true that, almost surely,

$$
\lim_{n\to \infty}\frac{R_n}{\log n}=\frac{2}{\pi}?
$$

**Formulation.** The site's Statement draws the coefficients from $\{-1,1\}$.
Its commentary records that Erdős's source [Er61, p. 252] is ambiguous between
$\{-1,1\}$ and $\{0,1\}$, the constant being $1/\pi$ in the second case. In the
site's discussion on 7 September 2025 the curator wrote that Erdős "was most
likely intending to ask whether, if $\epsilon_k\in\{-1,1\}$ is an infinite
sequence chosen uniformly at random, and $R_n(f)$ counts the number of real
roots of $\sum_{0\leq k\leq n}\epsilon_k z^k$, then
$\lim_{n\to\infty} R_n(f)/((2/\pi)\log n)=1$", that the curator was "minded to
keep the problem as it's written here" with the caveat that it is not strictly a
problem of Erdős, and, after Tao read the source's binary-digit construction as
$\{0,1\}$-valued, that the curator was "leaning towards Erdős intending
$\{-1,1\}$ as the explanation that implies fewer mistakes on his part". The
Statement is therefore the site's wording unchanged, the reading the curator
adopted; no Statement (precise) is needed, since the wording already fixes
$\{-1,1\}$. The $\{0,1\}$ version, where the constant would be $1/\pi$ and which
has no claim, is a variant and does not enter the standing. Under the Statement
the answer is no: almost surely $R_n/\log n$ has lower limit $1/\pi$ and upper
limit at least $2/\pi$, so it does not converge to $2/\pi$
([[problems/polynomials/E0521/claims/2026_08_26_alexeev|Alexeev 2026]], accepted
on the Lean build). The site's label OPEN (page last edited 19 October 2025)
reflects that neither claim on its proof-claims tab has been reviewed, not a
different reading of the question.

**Status.** Disproved. The site labels the problem OPEN as of 2026-10-06 (page
last edited 19 October 2025), which reflects that neither of the two claims on
its proof-claims tab has been reviewed, not a different reading of the question.
The tab carries two full proof claims, both disproofs, with no comments and no
acceptance, and the site's remarks cite [EO56], [Er61] and [Do24]. The derived
standing is `solved`/`disproved` for the Statement, through the Lean development
with Codex as formal author on
[[problems/polynomials/E0521/claims/2026_08_26_alexeev|Alexeev 2026]], which
this corpus built and audited: almost surely $R_n/\log n$ has lower limit
$1/\pi$ and upper limit at least $2/\pi$, so it does not converge almost surely
to $2/\pi$. Five further full claims of the negative answer remain pending, none
refereed or independently reviewed: the working note on
[[problems/polynomials/E0521/claims/2026_04_30_kovac|Kovač 2026]] (generated by

## Current assessment

The question, as the site states it, asks whether $R_n/\log n\to2/\pi$ almost
surely for one infinite sequence of independent uniform signs, where $R_n$
counts the real roots of the degree-$n$ partial polynomial. The site records
that the source [Er61, p. 252] is ambiguous between $\{-1,1\}$ and $\{0,1\}$
coefficients, the constant being $1/\pi$ in the second case; the thread's
discussion of September 2025 between the site's curator and Tao found the
literal text to use binary digits while the section's other problems use
signs. The site's Statement is the $\{-1,1\}$ version, its commentary notes
the $\{0,1\}$ alternative, and the curator leans towards $\{-1,1\}$. Every
claim recorded here addresses the $\{-1,1\}$ reading; the $\{0,1\}$ variant
has no claim. What is known: Erdős and Offord [EO56] give $(2/\pi+o(1))\log n$
real roots with high probability, and Do [Do24] proves the strong law
$R_n[-1,1]/\log n\to1/\pi$ for the roots in $[-1,1]$. Comments of September
2025 observed that the available concentration bounds give only polynomial
tails, too weak for an almost-sure statement, and suggested the law might
fail. In April 2026 the thread developed a disproof strategy: Letwin's
Gaussian-process limit of the rescaled polynomial near the endpoints of
$[-1,1]$, Tao's buckets, and Kovač's suggestion to look for degrees with no
real root outside $[-1,1]$. Every claim follows that route: infinitely many
degrees have no exterior real root (a cone or record event of the walk built
from the reversed coefficients, with positive probability in the first note
and almost surely in the three later notes and the Alexeev development, while
Snyder's Lean theorem proves only the negation), so the lower limit is $1/\pi$
by Do's law, while convergence in probability keeps the upper limit at least
$2/\pi$. A note posted by Kovač on 2026-04-25 claims to settle the same
question for standard Gaussian coefficients, which is a question of Pritsker
rather than this problem. On the thread for Problem 522 (21 April 2026) Letwin
wrote that Letwin's own solutions include this problem and that Michelen and
Yakir have a stronger result implying Problem 522; no manuscript was found.
The claims differ in rigor and in authorship: two are AI-generated notes
posted by their prompters, two are Lean developments without a write-up (one
credited to Snyder, one with Codex as formal author in Alexeev's repository),
and of the two on the tab, Sneiderman's describes itself as a synthesis of
earlier arguments while An and Lin's is self-contained. None has been checked
by a named reader on the thread. The Lean development in Alexeev's repository,
which this corpus built and audited, proves the lower limit $1/\pi$ and the
upper limit at least $2/\pi$ almost surely, so the problem is disproved; the
written notes and Snyder's development have not been examined here, and the
$\{0,1\}$ variant, which does not enter the standing, remains open.

## Known Results

- [EO56]: for $\pm1$ coefficients, $R_n=(2/\pi+o(1))\log n$ with probability
  tending to one; this is convergence in probability, not the almost-sure law
  asked here.
- [Do24]: for one infinite sequence of signs, almost surely
  $R_n[-1,1]/\log n\to1/\pi$ for the roots in $[-1,1]$; the roots outside
  $[-1,1]$ depend on the last coefficients and are not covered.
- Accepted on a Lean build here:
  [[problems/polynomials/E0521/claims/2026_08_26_alexeev|Alexeev 2026]], a
  Lean development with Codex as formal author, proves that for $\{-1,1\}$
  coefficients, almost surely, $R_n/\log n$ has lower limit $1/\pi$ and upper
  limit at least $2/\pi$, counting distinct real roots, so the almost-sure
  law fails.
- Claimed, not accepted: $R_n/\log n$ does not converge almost surely, with
  lower limit $1/\pi$ (and upper limit at least $2/\pi$) for $\{-1,1\}$
  coefficients, on
  [[problems/polynomials/E0521/claims/2026_04_30_kovac|Kovač 2026]] (positive
  probability of the lower limit, by a cone-record criterion),
  [[problems/polynomials/E0521/claims/2026_05_07_kwon_zou|Kwon–Zou 2026]]
  (almost surely, by a Lévy zero–one argument),
  [[problems/polynomials/E0521/claims/2026_07_21_sneiderman|Sneiderman 2026]]
  (almost surely, by a finite-prefix restart),
  [[problems/polynomials/E0521/claims/2026_08_01_an_lin|An–Lin 2026]]
  (almost surely infinitely many even degrees with no exterior root, by a
  reflected random walk),
  and [[problems/polynomials/E0521/claims/2026_07_23_snyder|Snyder 2026]] (a
  Lean theorem, linked by formal-conjectures).
- Claimed variant, not this problem: for standard Gaussian coefficients the
  almost-sure law is claimed to fail with positive probability of a lower
  limit at most $1/\pi+\delta$, in the AI-generated note of 2026-04-25
  recorded on the Kovač page, which answers a question posed at the 2019 AIM
  workshop on random polynomials; unreviewed.
