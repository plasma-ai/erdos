***

**Claim.** For independent uniform signs $\epsilon_k\in\{-1,1\}$ and $R_n$
the number of zeros of $P_n(z)=\sum_{k\le n}\epsilon_kz^k$ in the closed
unit disk, counted with multiplicity,

$$
R_n=\frac{n}{2}+O_\omega\bigl(n^{399/400}\bigr)
$$

almost surely, so $R_n/(n/2)\to1$ almost surely, which answers
[[problems/polynomials/E0522/_index|Problem 522]] in the affirmative. The
first note, posted on the site's discussion thread on 2026-04-20 after
Przemek Chojecki ran remarks of Kovač and Letwin through GPT-5.4 Pro, gave
the exponent $149/150$; the second, *A strong law for the roots of random
Littlewood polynomials* under Chojecki's name, produced with GPT-5.5 Pro and
posted on 2026-04-27 with Letwin's permission as a streamlined note, gives
$399/400$. Both notes count zeros through Jensen's formula and use the
reciprocal polynomial for the upper bound. The first note, which says it
follows the route proposed on the thread, compares logarithmic means at the
radii $1$ and $1-n^{-1-\alpha}$, controls small values of $\lvert P_n\rvert$
with the log-integrability theorem of Nazarov, Nishry and Sodin, and gets
almost-sure convergence of smoothed means from a smooth Berry–Esseen bound
and a subsequence argument modeled on Angst and Poly. The revised note, which
proves the stated $n^{399/400}$ bound, compares the means at the radii
$1-n^{-401/400}$ and its reciprocal, quotes only the Nazarov–Nishry–Sodin
theorem and proves its smooth central-limit estimate by a Lindeberg
replacement. Nat Sothanaphan's AI check of the first note stopped halfway
after finding several issues, the simplest an exponent in Proposition 7 that
does not follow from the stated parameters, and the author revised. The
author wrote that they were not able to formalize the argument.

**Standing.** Claimed. The thread's discussion of 2026-04-20 and 2026-04-21
concerned priority: Letwin had announced a more general paper that would
imply the problem and mentioned a separate paper of Michelen and Yakir,
neither of which has appeared; Chojecki wrote that they take no claim for
the problem and offered the notes for anyone's use, a disclaimer of credit
that this page does not read as a withdrawal of the result, and the thread
records no check of the revised note. No proof claim was registered on the
site's proof-claims tab and no refereed or arXiv version was found; the
formal-conjectures statement of the problem cites the note as a proof posted
in April 2026, and Kitamura's Lean repository credits it, with the Kwon–Zou
note, as an informal proof of the $\{-1,1\}$ statement. The site's label
records a Lean formalization of a later claim and no human acceptance (OPEN
