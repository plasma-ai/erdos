***

Konyagin [Ko94] proves, as his Theorem 1, that for every $\varepsilon>0$ the
probability that a random polynomial $f$ of degree $n$ with independent
uniform $\pm1$ coefficients has $m(f)=\min_{|z|=1}|f(z)|>n^{-1/2+\varepsilon}$
tends to zero as $n\to\infty$. Thus $m(f)\le n^{-1/2+o(1)}$ for all but
$o(2^n)$ of the sign choices. This is the first answer to the first question
of [[problems/polynomials/E0525/_index|Problem 525]], since a bound below
$1$ gives $|f(z)|<1$ somewhere on the circle for all but $o(2^n)$ sign
choices, and it gives the upper half of the answer to the second: the
typical minimum modulus is at most $n^{-1/2+o(1)}$. Konyagin's introduction (printed p. 80) places the theorem after Littlewood's
conjecture that $m(f)\le\varepsilon\sqrt n$ for most $f$, Kashin's proof of
it through the bound $n^{1/2}(\log n)^{-1/3}$ ([[problems/polynomials/E0525/claims/1987_01_01_kashin|Kashin 1987]]) and Odlyzko's
unpublished $n^{1/3+\varepsilon}$, and it confirms Odlyzko's conjecture that
$n^{-1/2+\varepsilon}$ holds; the site's commentary and the introduction of
Cook and Nguyen's paper credit the first question to Kashin instead, a
conflict the Kashin page records. The proof reduces the minimum over the
circle to the values of the polynomial at a chosen finite set of points and
bounds the resulting small-ball probabilities; the
[[../library/polynomials/konyagin_1994_minimum_modulus_random_trigonometric_polynomials_coefficients/_index|source card]]
digests the paper, which is in Russian. The page's date is the day the paper
was received by the journal, 1994-06-20, as its last page records.

**Covers.** For every $\varepsilon>0$, the proportion of degree-$n$ sign
polynomials with $m(f)>n^{-1/2+\varepsilon}$ tends to zero; hence the first
question's answer, yes, and the upper bound $m(f)\le n^{-1/2+o(1)}$ for
typical $f$. The lower bound of [[problems/polynomials/E0525/claims/1999_08_27_konyagin_schlag|Konyagin and Schlag 1999]] shows the exponent $-1/2$ optimal, and
the limit law at scale $n^{-1/2}$ is [[problems/polynomials/E0525/claims/2021_01_18_cook_nguyen|Cook and Nguyen 2021]]. The claim value is `proved`: a
bound proved, which also answers the first question yes.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the result rests on the refereed paper
linked above.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the paper appeared in Matematicheskie Zametki
(1994), 80–101, 158. Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas F. Bloom, records
the bound $m(f)\le n^{-1/2+o(1)}$ as Konyagin's improvement in the problem's
commentary, and Cook and Nguyen's refereed paper of 2021
records the theorem in its introduction as the bound preceding theirs. The
theorem statement is checked against the paper; the proof is not
compiled in this wiki. No formal proof of this bound on its own is held or
audited in this repository, so no `formalized` evidence is listed.
