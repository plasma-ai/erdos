# problems/polynomials/E0525/claims

[[problems/polynomials/E0525/_index|..]]

[[problems/polynomials/E0525/claims/1987_01_01_kashin|1987_01_01_kashin]]: Proves that a random plus-minus one polynomial of degree n has minimum
modulus on the unit circle at most the square root of n over the cube root
of log n with probability tending to one; refereed, 1987.

[[problems/polynomials/E0525/claims/1994_06_20_konyagin|1994_06_20_konyagin]]: Proves that a random plus-minus one polynomial of degree n has minimum
modulus below n to the minus one half plus epsilon with probability tending
to one, the first answer to the first question; refereed, 1994.

[[problems/polynomials/E0525/claims/1999_08_27_konyagin_schlag|1999_08_27_konyagin_schlag]]: Proves that a random plus-minus one polynomial of degree n has minimum
modulus below epsilon over root n with limiting probability at most a
constant times epsilon, so the exponent minus one half is optimal.

[[problems/polynomials/E0525/claims/2021_01_18_cook_nguyen|2021_01_18_cook_nguyen]]: Proves that the minimum modulus of a random plus-minus one polynomial,
scaled by the square root of the degree, has the same exponential limit law
as in the Gaussian case, which contains the first question's answer.
