***

**Claim.** Theorem 2 of the paper: let $a$ be a nonzero integer such that
$-a$ is not a perfect square. Then, as $x\to\infty$,

$$
\sum_{\substack{n\le x\\ n^2+a>0}}d(n^2+a)
=A_2(a)\,x\log x+A_3(a)\,x+O\bigl(x^{8/9}\log^3x\bigr),
$$

where $d$ is the divisor function and $A_2(a)$ and $A_3(a)$ are explicit
constants depending on $a$: $A_2(a)=2f_{-a}(1)$ for a Dirichlet series
$f_{-a}(s)$ that the theorem writes in terms of $\zeta(s)/\zeta(2s)$ and the
$L$-functions of the quadratic characters attached to $-a$, and $A_3(a)$
involves $f_{-a}(1)$, $f'_{-a}(1)$ and Euler's constant. In the notation of
[[problems/polynomials/E0975/_index|Problem 975]], for $f(x)=x^2+a$ with
$-a$ not a perfect square, which is exactly the condition that $f$ be
irreducible over $\mathbb Z$, and for which $f(n)\ge1$ for all large $n$,

$$
\sum_{n\le X}\tau(f(n))\sim c(f)\,X\log X,\qquad c(f)=A_2(a)>0,
$$

the constant being positive because the sum is $\gg X\log X$. The proof
writes the sum as three sums, two of which give the main terms, and bounds
the third through a new exponential sum over the roots of the congruence
$\nu^2\equiv-a\pmod k$, estimated with the theory of binary quadratic forms;
Section 9 derives from the same estimate that the roots $\nu/k$ are
uniformly distributed (Theorem 3). The introduction says that the case
$-a=k^2$ is excluded because $n^2-k^2$ factors and the sum then has order
$x\log^2x$, that the elementary formula $A_2(a)x\log x+O(x)$ had been
commonly realized and proved by Scourfield with a weaker error term, and
that similar but more complicated methods give the asymptotic for
$\sum d(an^2+bn+c)$; the paper proves it only for $n^2+a$.

**Covers.** The quadratics $f(x)=x^2+a$ with $a\ne0$ and $-a$ not a perfect
square: for every such $f$ the asymptotic of the problem holds with
$c(f)=A_2(a)$. Not covered: the other irreducible quadratics, for which
McKee proves the asymptotic with the constant written in class numbers, for
the monic ones in
[[problems/polynomials/E0975/claims/1995_05_01_mckee|1995]] and
[[problems/polynomials/E0975/claims/1999_01_01_mckee|1999]] and for some
non-monic ones in
[[problems/polynomials/E0975/claims/1997_01_30_mckee|1997]]; and every
degree three or more, where only the order of magnitude $X\log X$ is known
and the problem is open.

**Acceptance.** C. Hooley, *On the number of divisors of quadratic
polynomials*, Acta Math. 110 (1963), 97--114, received 5 April 1963, a
refereed journal (`refereed`); the record gives only the year, so the page
is dated to its first day. The site's reference [Ho63] prints the title with
"a quadratic polynomial"; the record's title is the plural. The curator of
erdosproblems.com, Thomas Bloom, credits the quadratic case to this paper
in the problem's commentary, but the site labels the problem OPEN, so that
credit is not acceptance of this partial claim. Lapkova (arXiv:1704.02498,
Section 1) restates Hooley's constant for $x^2+C$ and McKee's for the
monic irreducible quadratic.
