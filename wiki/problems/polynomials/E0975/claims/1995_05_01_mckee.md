***

**Claim.** Let $b$ and $c$ be integers with $\Delta=b^2-4c<0$, and let
$H^*(\Delta)$ be the Hurwitz class number. Then

$$
\sum_{n\le x}d(n^2+bn+c)=\lambda\,x\log x+O_\Delta(x),\qquad
\lambda=\frac{12H^*(\Delta)}{\pi\sqrt{-\Delta}},
$$

where $d$ is the divisor function; this is the theorem as the zbMATH review
of the paper (Zbl 0841.11050) states it. The proof writes the number of
roots of $n^2+bn+c\equiv0\pmod k$ as a weighted count of proper
representations of $k$ by reduced binary quadratic forms of discriminant
$\Delta$. In the notation of
[[problems/polynomials/E0975/_index|Problem 975]], for $f(x)=x^2+bx+c$ with
$\Delta<0$, which is irreducible and positive at every integer,

$$
\sum_{n\le X}\tau(f(n))\sim c(f)\,X\log X,\qquad c(f)=\lambda>0.
$$

**Covers.** The monic quadratics $x^2+bx+c$ with $b^2-4c<0$. For even $b$
the polynomial is a translate of $x^2+a$ with $a>0$, already covered by
[[problems/polynomials/E0975/claims/1963_01_01_hooley|Hooley's theorem]];
the new instances are those with odd $b$, such as $x^2+x+1$. Not covered:
the monic quadratics of positive non-square discriminant, which are
[[problems/polynomials/E0975/claims/1999_01_01_mckee|McKee's 1999 theorem]];
the non-monic quadratics; and every degree three or more, where the problem
is open.

**Acceptance.** J. McKee, *On the average number of divisors of quadratic
polynomials*, Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc. 117 (1995), no. 3,
389--392, a refereed journal (`refereed`); the record dates the issue to May
1995 without a day, so the page is dated to the first day of that month. The
curator of erdosproblems.com, Thomas Bloom, cites this paper as [Mc95] in
