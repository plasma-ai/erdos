***

**Claim.** Let $b$ and $c$ be integers with $\Delta=b^2-4c>0$ not a perfect
square. Then

$$
\sum_{n\le x}d(n^2+bn+c)=\lambda\,x\log x+O(x),\qquad
\lambda=\frac{12H^*(\Delta)\log\epsilon_\Delta}{\pi^2\sqrt\Delta},
$$

where $d$ is the divisor function, $H^*(\Delta)$ is a weighted class number
and $\epsilon_\Delta$ is the fundamental unit of discriminant $\Delta$. The
zbMATH review of the paper (Zbl 0923.11132) states the theorem as the
determination of $\lambda$ for $\Delta>0$, completing the case $\Delta<0$
of [[problems/polynomials/E0975/claims/1995_05_01_mckee|McKee's 1995
paper]]; the formula for $\lambda$ is as Lapkova (arXiv:1704.02498, (1.3))
restates it. Because the automorphism groups of the forms of positive
discriminant are infinite, the paper defines $H^*(\Delta)$ through the
index of a subgroup of the automorphism group rather than its order; for
$\Delta<0$ this agrees with the earlier definition except at $\Delta=-3$
and $-4$. In the notation of
[[problems/polynomials/E0975/_index|Problem 975]], for $f(x)=x^2+bx+c$ with
$\Delta>0$ not a square, which is irreducible and positive for all large
$n$,

$$
\sum_{n\le X}\tau(f(n))\sim c(f)\,X\log X,\qquad c(f)=\lambda>0.
$$

**Covers.** The monic quadratics $x^2+bx+c$ with $b^2-4c$ positive and not
a perfect square. For even $b$ the polynomial is a translate of $x^2+a$
with $a<0$ and $-a$ not a square, already covered by
[[problems/polynomials/E0975/claims/1963_01_01_hooley|Hooley's theorem]];
the new instances are those with odd $b$, such as $x^2+x-1$. Not covered:
the non-monic quadratics of positive discriminant, and every degree three
or more, where the problem is open.

**Acceptance.** J. McKee, *The average number of divisors of an irreducible
quadratic polynomial*, Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc. 126 (1999),
no. 1, 17--22, a refereed journal (`refereed`); the record dates the issue
to January 1999 without a day, so the page is dated to the first day of that
month. The curator of erdosproblems.com, Thomas Bloom, credits this paper as
[Mc99] in the problem's commentary, but the site labels the problem OPEN, so
that credit is not acceptance of this partial claim.
