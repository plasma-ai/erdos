**Claim.** For every monic $f(z)=\prod_{i\le n}(z-z_i)$ with
$\lvert z_i\rvert\le1$, some root $z_j$ is the center of a disk of radius
$(\log2)/n$ contained in $\{z:\lvert f(z)\rvert<1\}$, so
$\rho(f)\ge(\log2)/n$; with $f(z)=z^n-1$, whose inradius is at most
$(\pi/2)/n$, this gives $\inf_{\deg f=n}\rho(f)=\Theta(1/n)$ and answers
the second question of [[problems/polynomials/E1039/_index|Problem 1039]] in
the affirmative. The argument, posted on the site's discussion thread on
2026-05-07 by Liam Price with GPT-5.5 Pro named as the system used, is a
product estimate: if no such disk exists, there are points $w_j$ with
$\lvert f(w_j)\rvert\ge1$ and $\lvert w_j-z_j\rvert<c/n$ for some
$c<\log2$, and the double product $\prod_j\prod_i\lvert w_j-z_i\rvert$,
which is at least $1$, is at most $(e^c-1)^n<1$. The constant $\log2$ is
sharp for disks centered at roots, as $z^n-1$ shows. Nat Sothanaphan posted
a streamlined account on 2026-05-08
([notes](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1ik7Nf9Jvo5C4xINBP8G-guGk8-9gNVOm/view)),
and the site's curator, Thomas Bloom, stated on 2026-05-11 the inequality
$\prod_j\lvert f(w_j)\rvert\le((1+\epsilon)^n-1)^n$ whenever
$\lvert z_i-w_i\rvert\le\epsilon$ for all $i$, from which the bound follows,
asking whether it is new and for a Lean proof of it. Kenta Kitamura's Lean 4
development of 2026-05-15, based on Price's write-up and the thread,
formalizes the argument, with assistance from Codex 5.5 disclosed.

**Covers.** The lower bound $\rho_n\ge(\log2)/n$ for the minimal inradius
$\rho_n=\inf_{\deg f=n}\rho(f)$, hence, with $z^n-1$, the exact order
$\rho_n=\Theta(1/n)$ and a yes to the second question. Read as its source
states it (the problem page's Formulation), the first question asks for the
asymptotic behavior of $\rho_n$; this claim gives its order and not the sharp
constant $\lim n\rho_n$, which is the later full claim on
[[problems/polynomials/E1039/claims/2026_09_06_geng_qiu|Geng–Qiu 2026]].

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Standing.** Claimed. Sothanaphan wrote on the thread that they had digested
the argument and could vouch for its correctness (2026-05-08) and, after
Kitamura's formalization, that an AI check confirmed it (2026-05-17); Bloom
wrote that Bloom was sure the proof in the note was fine while asking for a
formal proof of the inequality. The site labels the problem OPEN (page last
edited 27 December 2025, before these postings), no proof claim was
registered on the site's proof-claims tab, no refereed or arXiv version of
the note exists, and the Lean development was neither built nor audited
here; the corpus records the endorsements and does not read them as the
site's acceptance. The argument is reproduced, with credit to Price,
Sothanaphan, Bloom and Kitamura, as Section 2 of the Geng–Qiu preprint.
