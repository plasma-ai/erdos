**Claim.** For every $C>0$ there are $\epsilon>0$ and $n_0$ such that for
every $n\ge n_0$ and every choice of nodes $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in[-1,1]$,
counted with multiplicity, there are labels $y_1,\ldots,y_n\in[-1,1]$ such
that every real or complex polynomial $P$ with $\deg P<(1+\epsilon)n$ and
$P(x_i)=y_i$ for at least $(1-\epsilon)n$ indices has
$\max_{[-1,1]}\lvert P\rvert>C$. This is Theorem 1.1 of the note *A
Bernstein-density proof of Erdős's robust interpolation obstruction* (29
April 2026, no author byline, hosted at ulam.ai), the assertion of
[[problems/polynomials/E1133/_index|Problem 1133]] with repeated nodes
allowed. The card
[[../library/polynomials/anon_2026_bernstein_density_proof_erdos_s_robust/_index|anon_2026_bernstein_density_proof_erdos_s_robust]]
digests the argument. Beurling's theorem, in the real-line form of
Ortega-Cerdà and Seip, says that a separated sequence interpolates the
Bernstein space $B_1$ only when its upper uniform density is below $1/\pi$;
by compactness this yields a finite obstruction (Proposition 3.1): for each
$C$ there are $L$ and $\eta>0$ such that any $L$ reals (with multiplicity) of
diameter at most $\pi(1+\eta)L$ receive labels in $[-1,1]$ that no
complex-valued $f\in B_1$ of sup norm at most $C$ takes at all of them. After
$x=\cos\theta$ the nodes are cut into consecutive blocks of $L$; more than
$\epsilon n$ blocks have angular span at most
$\pi(1+\eta)L/\lceil(1+\epsilon)n\rceil$, each is loaded with a forbidden
pattern, and after angular rescaling a polynomial of norm at most $C$ fitting
a whole good block would give a function in $B_1$ of norm at most $C$ taking
the forbidden labels. The note gives no quantitative dependence of $\epsilon$
on $C$; the later manuscript of
[[problems/polynomials/E1133/claims/2026_09_13_yang|Yang]], which cites it as
an unsigned draft and reuses its angular reduction and grouping, supplies that
dependence.
