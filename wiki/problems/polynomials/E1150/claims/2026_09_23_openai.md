***

**Claim.** The answer to [[problems/polynomials/E1150/_index|Problem 1150]]
is no: no constant $c>0$ makes every $\pm1$ polynomial of every large degree
$n$ exceed $(1+c)\sqrt n$ in maximum modulus on the unit circle. Write $m_N$
for the minimum, over signs $\varepsilon_0,\ldots,\varepsilon_{N-1}\in\{-1,1\}$,
of $N^{-1/2}\max_{\lvert z\rvert=1}\lvert\sum_{k<N}\varepsilon_kz^k\rvert$;
Parseval's identity gives $m_N\ge1$. Theorem 1.1 of OpenAI, *Asymptotically
minimal maxima of real Littlewood polynomials*, OpenAI Math Release preprint,
23 September 2026, at the pinned revision of the release repository (the
preprint link), states that $m_N\to1$: for every $\eta>0$ there is $N_0$ such
that every integer $N\ge N_0$ admits signs
$\varepsilon_0,\ldots,\varepsilon_{N-1}$ with

$$
\max_{\lvert z\rvert=1}\Bigl\lvert\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\varepsilon_kz^k\Bigr\rvert
\le(1+\eta)\sqrt N .
$$

The manuscript is paged at
[[../library/polynomials/openai_2026_asymptotically_minimal_maxima_real_littlewood_polynomials/theorem_1_1|the result page]]
of
[[../library/polynomials/openai_2026_asymptotically_minimal_maxima_real_littlewood_polynomials/_index|its card]].
The bridge to the Statement is elementary: a sum of $N$ terms with
coefficients $\pm1$ is a polynomial of degree $n=N-1$ with coefficients
$\pm1$. Given $c>0$ take $\eta=c/2$ and $N_1\ge N_0$ so large that
$(1+c/2)\sqrt N\le(1+c)\sqrt{N-1}$ for every $N\ge N_1$; then for every
degree $n\ge N_1-1$ the theorem's polynomial of length $n+1$ has maximum
modulus at most $(1+c)\sqrt n$, so the inequality of the Statement fails for
every large $n$, not merely for some. The theorem is one-sided and
existential: the manuscript's remark after Theorem 1.1 says that it gives no
lower bound for $\lvert P\rvert$ on the circle, the release's family document
says that it gives no convergence rate and no signing algorithm, and the
manuscript notes that the
additive gap $\max\lvert P\rvert^2\ge N+(N-1)^{1/3}/38$ of Erdélyi 2026 (card
[[../library/polynomials/erdelyi_2026_erdos_problem_about_maximum_modulus_littlewood_polynomials_unit_circl/_index|erdelyi_2026_erdos_problem_about_maximum_modulus_littlewood_polynomials_unit_circl]])
is compatible with it, that excess being $o(N)$. The proof first builds
relaxed coefficients in $[-1,1]$ with small defect from signs by sampling
quadratic-phase waves off a real trigonometric polynomial on an auxiliary
torus, packing their Fourier supports with the Pippenger–Spencer coloring
theorem, then rounds them to signs with a defect-sensitive discrepancy bound
in the partial-coloring method of Spencer and Lovett and Meka (Sections
2–6). The same manuscript derives unbounded binary merit factors through all
lengths, a uniquely ergodic binary Morse shift with simple spectrum whose
zero-coordinate spectral measure is absolutely continuous ($L^2$ density),
and $L^p$ flatness for every finite $p$, and its Appendix A disputes
nonflatness claims in preprints of el Abdalaoui (el Abdalaoui's three claims
of an answer to this problem have rejected pages:
[[problems/polynomials/E1150/claims/2016_09_12_el_abdalaoui|2016]],
[[problems/polynomials/E1150/claims/2025_04_30_el_abdalaoui|April 2025]] and
[[problems/polynomials/E1150/claims/2025_09_04_el_abdalaoui|September 2025]]);
none of these is part of this claim. Two later release manuscripts of
5 October 2026 strengthen the construction without Lean: *Nearly minimal
maxima and positive minima of Littlewood polynomials*
([[../library/polynomials/openai_2026_nearly_minimal_maxima_positive_minima_littlewood_polynomials/theorem_1_1|Theorem 1.1]]
of
[[../library/polynomials/openai_2026_nearly_minimal_maxima_positive_minima_littlewood_polynomials/_index|its card]])
adds the lower bound $\sqrt N/16\le\lvert P(z)\rvert$ to the same upper
bound, and *Ultraflat real Littlewood polynomials*
([[../library/polynomials/openai_2026_ultraflat_real_littlewood_polynomials/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
of
[[../library/polynomials/openai_2026_ultraflat_real_littlewood_polynomials/_index|its card]])
states that for every $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ and every large $N$ some signs
give $(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt N\le\lvert P(z)\rvert\le(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt N$ on
the whole circle, $z=\pm1$ included, which is the two-sided ultraflat
polynomial the site's commentary names. Each contains the upper bound and so
the answer no, but their two-sided statements are claimed, not accepted, and
are recorded on
[[problems/polynomials/E0228/claims/2026_10_05_openai|the pending claim page of Problem 228]];
their links on this page are provenance for the stronger claims and
contribute no evidence. The same theorem is a second disproof of
[[problems/polynomials/E0230/_index|Problem 230]], recorded on
[[problems/polynomials/E0230/claims/2026_09_23_openai|that problem's claim page]].

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki: the proof is self-contained in the
manuscript and its Lean tree, and the Lean statement rests on Mathlib's
finite sums, complex norm and `Real.sqrt` alone.

**Acceptance.** A disproof of Problem 1150; its Lean declaration was built by
this corpus's verification with the three standard axioms and its statement
audited for fidelity. Formalized: the declaration
`OAI.AsymptoticallyMinimalLittlewood.main` of the release's `lean/` folder (file
`OAI/Analysis/Littlewood/Main.lean`, with `MainStatement`, `littlewoodValue` and
`IsRealSigning` in `Model.lean`) proves exactly the display above: for every
real $\eta>0$ there is $N_0\ge1$ such that for every $N\ge N_0$ some
$\varepsilon:\mathrm{Fin}\,N\to\mathbb R$ with every value $-1$ or $1$ has
$\lVert\sum_k\varepsilon_kz^k\rVert\le(1+\eta)\sqrt N$ for every complex $z$ of
norm $1$. This corpus's verification built the solution module and the
comparator challenge
`ComparatorChallenges/AsymptoticallyMinimalLittlewood.lean`, which pins the
declaration, from the release at the pinned revision, printed the declaration's
axioms, which were exactly `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, and
found the comparator fingerprint of the pinned challenge statement identical to
the solution's; the result was recorded on 2026-10-07. The statement audit that
`formalized` requires is this corpus's own: a statement-fidelity audit unfolded
`littlewoodValue` and `IsRealSigning`, checked the quantifiers and the coercions
(the natural $N$ to a real under the square root, the real signs to complex
coefficients, `Fin` indices to exponents) for junk values and found none,
compared the declaration with the problem page's Statement (the coefficient
class, the degree $N-1$ against the Statement's $n$, the circle, the bound and
the quantifiers over $c$ and $n$) and with the formal-conjectures statement
`erdos_1150`
(https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/6fbb54f24ccc2e64dcfaffc28c58950e377110d2/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/1150.lean):
its eventual range in $n$, its coefficient class fixed by the natural degree,
and its supremum over the circle against the declaration's pointwise bound; it
noted that the bound is not trivial since for small $\eta$ it beats the
Rudin–Shapiro polynomials, and judged that the declaration refutes the question
through the bridge above. The release's family document says the results are
existential, with no effective rate and no signing algorithm. Not reviewed: no
outside reviewer or independent acceptance of the result is recorded; the site's
label is OPEN with an empty proof-claims tab. Not refereed: the manuscripts are
unrefereed release preprints with no arXiv version. The release's own README
says that its manuscripts were produced by an internal OpenAI model and are at
different stages of verification. Only the pinned revision is described; later
revisions are unexamined.
