**Claim.** For each $n$ let $X_n\subset[-1,1]$ have $n$ distinct points, and
let $r_n\ge0$ be integers with $r_n/n\to0$. Theorem 1 of the write-up
*Almost everywhere divergence of polynomial interpolation with sublinear
excess degree* (Zenodo record 10.5281/zenodo.22549777, version 7 of
2026-09-06 under the concept record 10.5281/zenodo.22312935, whose record
lists its creator as anonymous) states that
there is a continuous $f:[-1,1]\to\mathbb R$ such that every sequence of real
polynomials $p_n$ of degree at most $n+r_n$ with $p_n=f$ on $X_n$ satisfies

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\lvert p_n(x)\rvert=\infty
\quad\text{for almost every }x\in[-1,1],
$$

the exceptional null set depending on the sequence. This answers
[[problems/polynomials/E1152/_index|Problem 1152]] yes, in a stronger form:
given $\epsilon(n)\to0$, put $r_n=\max\{0,\lceil\epsilon(n)n\rceil-1\}$, so
that $\deg p_n<(1+\epsilon(n))n$ implies $\deg p_n\le n+r_n$, and
unboundedness at $x$ excludes $p_n(x)\to f(x)$. The same $f$ serves every
admissible sequence, as the question requires, and the quantifier order
matches: the null set may depend on $(p_n)$. The write-up presents the
theorem as the extension of the Erdős–Vértesi almost-everywhere divergence
of Lagrange interpolation, for arbitrary arrays, to non-unique interpolation
with $o(n)$ excess degree, in contrast with the theorem of Erdős, Kroó and
Szabados [EKS89] in the site's commentary, that for a fixed $\epsilon>0$
suitable arrays give every continuous $f$ uniformly convergent interpolants
of degree below $(1+\epsilon)n$. Its inputs are a construction of Olevskii
and Ulanovskii in Bernstein spaces and the uniform Christoffel–Darboux kernel
asymptotics of Kriecherbauer, Schubert, Schüler and Venker; a finite
interpolation construction forces large values on a fixed fraction of a
local interval uniformly over the admissible corrections, finitely many such
data sets rule out common sets of bounded values, and the Baire category
theorem gives one $f$.

> functions, weighted Christoffel-Darboux kernel asymptotics, a sign-change
