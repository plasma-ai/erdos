**Claim.** For every fixed interval $I=[a,b]$ with $-1\le a<b\le1$ there are
a constant $C_I\ge0$ and a threshold $n_I$ such that for every $n\ge n_I$
and every choice of $n$ distinct nodes in $[-1,1]$ the Lebesgue function
$\lambda(x)=\sum_k|l_k(x)|$ satisfies

$$
\sup_{x\in I}\lambda(x)\ge\frac2\pi\log n-C_I
$$

(Theorem 1.10(i), equation (1.26), p. 9 of the third arXiv version). The
constant and threshold depend on $I$ but not on the nodes. Since the $l_k$
are polynomials, $\lambda$ is continuous and the supremum over the compact
interval is a maximum; for $n\ge\max\{n_I,2\}$ the choice
$\varepsilon_n=(C_I+1)/\log n\to0$ gives the strict inequality
$\max_{x\in[a,b]}\lambda(x)>(2/\pi-\varepsilon_n)\log n$, which is the
question of [[problems/polynomials/E1153/_index|Problem 1153]] answered yes,
with an additive $O(1)$ remainder in place of the $o(1)\log n$ asked for.
The exact source statement and this elementary transfer are recorded on
[[../library/polynomials/tao_2026_local_bernstein_theory_lower_bounds_lebesgue/theorem_1_10_i_transfer|the transfer page]]
of the source card
[[../library/polynomials/tao_2026_local_bernstein_theory_lower_bounds_lebesgue/_index|Tao 2026]];
the transfer, and only the transfer, was independently reviewed on
2026-09-06. The proof localizes Bernstein's theory to functions holomorphic
in a rectangle, bounded and real on the lower edge and with controlled
growth on the other edges (Theorem 1.6), and combines residue
representations with estimates at macroscopic, mesoscopic and microscopic
scales; Tao's own proof has not been compiled or reviewed in this corpus.
Part (ii) of the same theorem gives the integral bound
$\int_I\lambda\ge(4|I|/\pi^2)\log n-o(\log n)$. Erdős and Turán posed the
question [ErTu61]; on the whole interval Bernstein [Be31] asserted the
sharp bound $(2/\pi-o(1))\log n$ (proving it in full only for trigonometric
interpolation), Erdős and Turán proved $(2/\pi)\log n-O(\log\log n)$
[ErTu61, (3.13)], and Erdős [Er61c] improved the loss to $O(1)$ (his page
[[problems/polynomials/E1153/claims/1961_01_01_erdos|Erdős 1961]]); Erdős and
Szabados [ErSz78] obtained
$\max_{[a,b]}\lambda\gg\log n$ on fixed subintervals without the sharp
coefficient.

**Acceptance.** Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas F. Bloom, labels the
problem proved and records in its commentary that Tao resolved the question with
the bound $(2/\pi)\log n-O(1)$ on every fixed interval (problem page last edited
2026-04-01, accessed 2026-10-07). Not refereed: the manuscript is a preprint,
first submitted to arXiv on 2026-03-23, with the third version dated 2026-04-22
and no journal acceptance recorded as of the arXiv API response of 2026-09-06
recorded on the source card. No `formalized` evidence: Boris Alexeev's
`lean-proofs` repository holds a Lean file, added on 2026-08-20 and linked above
at a pinned commit, that declares itself a formalization of a solution to the
problem. It names Tao as informal author and Codex and GPT-5.6 Sol as formal
authors. Its theorem `Erdos1153.erdos_1153` states the $\varepsilon$ form of the
question, with a threshold uniform in the nodes, and not the additive $O(1)$
bound. This corpus has not built or audited it. The Lean development of
[[problems/polynomials/E1153/claims/2026_08_31_yang|Yang 2026]] declares itself
an alternative proof, not a formalization of this manuscript, and has its own
pending page. Tao first posted a manuscript of the solution in the problem's
discussion thread on 2026-02-27 (the first February link above); readers
reported errors and one proof gap in it the same day, and Tao posted a revised
copy that day (the second February link), announcing a complete rewrite. The
arXiv paper of 2026-03-23, announced in the thread on 2026-03-24, is that
rewrite and the source the curator credits; further small corrections reported
on 2026-03-24 were to be addressed in a revision, and no comparison of the
February reports with the third version is recorded. The author's disclosures of
AI assistance, on p. 14 of the preprint, are recorded on the source card and
confer no correctness credit.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the result rests on the preprint linked
above, and the transfer page is a library record, not a premise.
