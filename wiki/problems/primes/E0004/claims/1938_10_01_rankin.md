***

**Claim.** Write $G(X)$ for the largest gap between consecutive primes below $X$
and $\log_k$ for the $k$-fold iterated logarithm. R. A. Rankin, *The difference
between consecutive prime numbers*, J. London Math. Soc. 13 (1938), no. 4,
242--247, proves

$$
G(X)\ge(c+o(1))\,\frac{\log X\log_2X\log_4X}{(\log_3X)^2}
$$

with $c=1/3$. The value of the constant is as the introductions of Ford, Green,
Konyagin and Tao (arXiv:1408.4505, p. 2) and of the 2018 paper of Ford, Green,
Konyagin, Maynard and Tao (see
[[../library/integer_sequences/ford_2018_long_gaps_between_primes/_index|its library card]])
record it. Since $\log p_n\sim\log n$, the bound gives, for every $C$ with
$0<C<1/3$, infinitely many $n$ with
$p_{n+1}-p_n>C\log n\log_2n\log_4n/(\log_3n)^2$, the question of
[[problems/primes/E0004/_index|Problem 4]] answered yes for those $C$. Later
work raised $c$ to $\tfrac12e^\gamma$ (Schönhage), $e^\gamma$ (Rankin),
$1.31256e^\gamma$ (Maier and Pomerance) and $2e^\gamma$ (Pintz), before the 2014
theorems of
[[problems/primes/E0004/claims/2014_08_20_ford_green_konyagin_tao|Ford, Green, Konyagin and Tao]]
and of [[problems/primes/E0004/claims/2014_08_21_maynard|Maynard]] made it
arbitrary.

**Covers.** Every $C$ with $0<C<1/3$: for each such $C$ there are infinitely
many $n$ with the question's inequality.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki.

**Acceptance.** Refereed publication: J. London Math. Soc. 13 (1938), no. 4,
242--247, doi:10.1112/jlms/s1-13.4.242. Not reviewed: the site's commentary
mentions Rankin's result, but the site's PROVED label credits the solution to
Maynard and to Ford, Green, Konyagin and Tao.
