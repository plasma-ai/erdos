**Claim.** Both clauses of the corrected Statement of
[[problems/primes/E0049/_index|Problem 49]] hold.
Let $M(x)$ be the largest size of a set $\{a_1<\cdots<a_t\}$ of positive
integers up to $x$ with $\phi(a_1)\le\cdots\le\phi(a_t)$. Theorem 1.1 of
T. Tao, *Monotone Nondecreasing Sequences of the Euler Totient Function*,
states: "We have
$M(x) \leq \left(1 + O\left(\frac{\log_2^5 x}{\log x}\right)\right) \frac{x}{\log x}$
for all $x \geq 10$", and, by the prime number theorem, in the form (1.2),

$$
\pi(x)\le M(x)\le\left(1+O\left(\frac{(\log\log x)^5}{\log x}\right)\right)\pi(x),
$$

the lower bound coming from the primes, on which $\phi(p)=p-1$ increases. A
set on which $\phi$ is strictly increasing is in particular one on which it is
nondecreasing, so every strict example $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ has
$\lvert A\rvert\le M(N)=(1+o(1))\pi(N)$, and since $\pi(N)=o(N)$, also
$\lvert A\rvert=o(N)$
([[../library/primes/tao_2024_monotone_nondecreasing_sequences_euler_totient_function/strict_transfer|the strict transfer]]).
This answers both clauses of the corrected Statement; it does not decide
Erdős's exact conjecture, recorded on the problem page, that the primes are a
largest strict example.

**Covers.** Both clauses of the corrected Statement: every strict example
$A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ has $\lvert A\rvert<(1+o(1))\pi(N)$, and so
$\lvert A\rvert=o(N)$. Erdős's exact conjecture, that the primes are a
largest strict example, is not part of the corrected Statement and is not
covered.
