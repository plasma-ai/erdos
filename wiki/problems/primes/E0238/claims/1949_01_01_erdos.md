***

**Claim.** For every constant $c_2>0$ there is a constant $c_1=c_1(c_2)>0$
such that, for all sufficiently large $x$, there are more than $c_1\log x$
consecutive primes $p_k<p_{k+1}<\dots<p_{k+r}<x$ whose successive gaps
$p_{k+i+1}-p_{k+i}$ all exceed $c_2$; since the primes increase, every two of
them then differ by more than $c_2$. This is Theorem 3 of P. Erdős, On some
applications of Brun's method, Acta Univ. Szeged. Sect. Sci. Math. 13 (1949),
57–63, stated there with constants $c_5$ (the gap) and $c_6=c_6(c_5)$ (the
run length $[c_6\log n]$). The proof counts the pairs of consecutive primes
below $n$ at distance at most $c_5$ by Schnirelmann's sieve bound,
$O(n/(\log n)^2)$, and compares that count with $\pi(n)$: the small gaps are
too few to break every run of $[c_6\log n]$ primes when $c_6$ is small in
terms of $c_5$. The library's
[[../library/primes/erdos_1949_applications_brun_s_method/_index|card for the
paper]] records the theorem beside the paper's two results on the least
prime in a progression.

**Covers.** The instances of
[[problems/primes/E0238/_index|Problem 238]] with $c_1$ below a threshold
that depends on $c_2$: for every $c_2>0$ the answer is yes whenever
$0<c_1<c_6(c_2)$. The problem asks whether the answer is yes for every
pair $c_1,c_2>0$, that is, whether the threshold can be removed; Theorem 3
does not reach that, and the site records the problem as open.

**Acceptance.** The paper is refereed: it appeared in the journal Acta
Universitatis Szegediensis, Sectio Scientiarum Mathematicarum, volume 13
(1949), pages 57–63. The curator of erdosproblems.com, Thomas Bloom, records
in the site's commentary that Erdős proved the statement for every $c_2>0$
when $c_1>0$ is small enough in terms of $c_2$; the site's label for the
problem is OPEN, so that commentary credits the partial result without
settling the problem, and it is not listed as acceptance evidence.

**Depends on.** Nothing on this wiki; the argument is the paper's own.
