***

**Claim.** Let $0<\epsilon<1$. There is an infinite sequence of primes
$p_1<p_2<\cdots$ such that, if $n_1<n_2<\cdots$ is the sequence of all
positive integers composed of these primes, then

$$
n_{i+1}-n_i> n_i^{1-\epsilon}
$$

for every $i$. This is
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/tijdeman_1973_integers_many_small_prime_factors/theorem_7|Theorem 7]]
of R. Tijdeman, *On integers with many small prime factors*, Compositio Math.
26 (1973), no. 3, 319–330, display (13) on p. 326, restated in the
introduction on p. 320; the paper is digested on
[[../library/arithmetic_functions/tijdeman_1973_integers_many_small_prime_factors/_index|its library card]],
which is the source of this claim. The site's commentary records the weaker
form $a_{i+1}-a_i\gg a_i^{1-\epsilon}$. Since the gaps tend to infinity, the
theorem answers the question of [[problems/primes/E0240/_index|Problem 240]]
in the affirmative; the paper says it settles a conjecture of Wintner in the
affirmative and proves much more, citing the conjecture from Erdős's 1965
survey, which matches the site's note that Wintner put the question to Erdős.
The sequence is built by induction from $p_1=3$. Given $p_1<\cdots<p_r$ with
$b-a>a^{1-\epsilon}$ for all $a<b$ composed of them, the next prime is taken
from a range $[T/2,T]$, for a large $T$, outside a small exceptional set. If
$a<b$ are composed of $p_1,\ldots,p_r$ and a prime $p$ of that range and
satisfy $0<b-a<a^{1-\epsilon}$, a lower bound for the linear form in
logarithms $\log(b/a)$, an estimate of Baker that was then unpublished and
later appeared in Acta Arith. 24 (1973), 33–36, bounds the exponents of the
pair: $a\le p^{C_7/2}$ for an effective constant $C_7$, so the exponent of $p$
is at most $C_7$ and each other exponent at most $C_7\log T$. For each fixed
pattern of exponents the primes $p$ that admit such a pair lie in an interval
of length at most $Ta^{-\epsilon}\le 4T^{1-\epsilon}$, so at most
$4T^{1-\epsilon}(C_7+1)^{2r+2}(\log T)^{2r}$ primes of the range are excluded,
fewer than the $3T/(10\log T)$ primes the range contains once $T$ is large,
and $p_{r+1}$ is any remaining prime. The earlier theorems of the paper give,
for a finite set of primes, effective gap bounds of the shape
$n_{i+1}-n_i\gg n_i/(\log n_i)^C$ by the Gel'fond–Baker method.
