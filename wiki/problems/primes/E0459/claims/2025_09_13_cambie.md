***

**Claim.** Read $f(u)$, as the site's remark and every formalization do, as
the least $v>u$ all of whose prime factors divide $u$; the site states this
is equivalent to the definition of
[[problems/primes/E0459/_index|Problem 459]]. For $u\geq 2$ the integer
$u+1$ has a prime factor not dividing $u$, and $u^2$ has none, so

$$
u+2\leq f(u)\leq u^2.
$$

Stijn Cambie's observations, credited by the site, are these. When $u=p$ is
prime, $f(p)=p^2$, since the only candidates are the powers of $p$. When $u$
is even, every power of $2$ is a candidate, so $f(u)\leq 2^k$ for the least
power $2^k>u$; for $u=2^k-2$ with $k\geq 2$ that power is $u+2$, so
$f(u)=u+2$. Both bounds are therefore attained infinitely often and $f$ has
no regular order of growth. For the typical $n$, however, $f(n)=(1+o(1))n$:
for every $\varepsilon,\delta>0$ there is an $x_0$ such that, for every
$x\geq x_0$, at least $(1-\delta)x$ of the integers $n\leq x$ satisfy
$f(n)<(1+\varepsilon)n$. Cambie's argument, posted in the site's discussion
on 6 February 2026, fixes $M$ so that all but a $\delta/2$ proportion of the
integers are divisible by two distinct primes $p,q<M$; for each such pair,
because $\log p/\log q$ is irrational, the integers of the form $p^aq^b$ have
consecutive ratios tending to $1$, so for every large $n$ divisible by $pq$
one of them lies in $(n,(1+\varepsilon)n]$ and has all its prime factors
dividing $n$.

The problem asks only to estimate $f(u)$, and the site's remark says that
the estimates Erdős and Graham had in mind are not clear; the curator marks
the problem solved because these observations answer the natural readings
of the question. The exact order of the exceptional set, and any finer
statistic of $f$, are outside this claim.

**Acceptance.** The site's curator, Thomas F. Bloom, marks Problem 459
solved and credits Cambie's observations, the `reviewed` evidence; no
journal publication is recorded. The page is dated by the earliest record of
the remark crediting Cambie: the Wayback Machine snapshot of the site's page
taken on 13 September 2025, linked above as the record, shows the label
SOLVED, the remark and the acknowledgment of Cambie, while the snapshot of
15 September 2024 shows the problem open without the remark; the community
database (teorth/erdosproblems) lists the problem as solved, as of its last
update of the entry on 31 August 2025. The proof of the almost-all estimate
was posted in the site's discussion on 6 February 2026, and the observations
may be older than any of these dates.

**Formalization.** Three Lean files, linked above, formalize these statements
for the function $f$ defined as above, the earliest only in part; all three
are formalizations of the results the site credits to Cambie, so they are
links on this page and not claims of their own. Boris Alexeev's
`Erdos459b.lean` in their `lean-proofs` repository, pinned at the commit of
6 February 2026 in the link, carries no header, author line or attribution;
Alexeev's post in the site's discussion of 6 February 2026 presents it as a
formalization of some of the results in the problem's description, which are
those credited to Cambie. The file proves the two bounds, $f(p)=p^2$, the
bound $2^k$ for even $u$, $f(2^k-2)=2^k$, and that each bound is attained
infinitely often; the post says that it is not complete, lacking the
almost-all estimate. The same repository's later
`src/latest/ErdosProblems/Erdos459.lean`, pinned in the link at a commit of
24 August 2026, carries a header that names Cambie as the informal author and
Aristotle, Alexeev and van Doorn as the formal authors. It proves every
statement of Alexeev's earlier file and, as its theorem `erdos_459`, the
almost-all estimate. Wouter van Doorn's `ErdosProblem459.lean` in their
`Lean-files` repository, pinned at the commit of 11 March 2026, is Alexeev's
file extended with the almost-all estimate, formalized by Aristotle
(Harmonic): its `main_theorem` states, for $\varepsilon,\delta>0$, an $x_0$
beyond which at least $(1-\delta)x$ of the $n\leq x$ have
$f(n)<(1+\varepsilon)n$, under `leanprover/lean4:v4.24.0`; its 724 lines
contain no `sorry`, `axiom` or `native_decide` token. The `formal_proof`
attribute of the statement in `google-deepmind/formal-conjectures` names van
Doorn's file for the two bounds and for $f(p)=p^2$. This corpus has built none
of these files, so no `formalized` evidence is listed, and the
formal-conjectures statement file is not a formalization link.

**Depends on.** Nothing beyond the postings linked above.
