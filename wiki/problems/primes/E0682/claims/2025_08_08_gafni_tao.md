***

**Claim.** Call the index $n$ exceptional when no integer $m$ with
$p_n<m<p_{n+1}$ has least prime factor $p(m)\geq p_{n+1}-p_n$. Theorem 1.1
of Gafni and Tao bounds the number of exceptional $n$ with $p_n\in[X,2X]$ by
$O(X/(\log X)^2)$. Summing over dyadic ranges, the exceptional $n\leq N$
number $O(N/\log N)$, a proportion $O(1/\log N)$ as the paper notes, which
is $o(N)$, so the exceptional set has density zero and the question of
[[problems/primes/E0682/_index|Problem 682]] has the answer yes: for almost
all $n$ some $m\in(p_n,p_{n+1})$ has
$p(m)\geq p_{n+1}-p_n$. The method is a moment computation for the count of
rough numbers in short intervals, with the higher moments controlled through
the Montgomery–Soundararajan asymptotics for singular series; the second
moment alone already gives $O(X/(\log X)^{4/3-o(1)})$ exceptions, enough for
the density statement. Under a form of the prime tuples conjecture the paper
sharpens the count to $\sim cX/(\log X)^2$ for an explicit constant $c>0$.
The paper's digest is the
[[../library/primes/gafni_2025_rough_numbers_between_consecutive_primes/_index|source
card]].

Whether the "almost all" can be dropped is open. Assuming Schinzel's
hypothesis H (Dickson's conjecture suffices for these two linear forms),
Erdős showed, in the passage from his 1979 paper that Gafni and Tao quote,
that the statement fails for infinitely many $n$: when $2183+30030d$ and
$2201+30030d$ are both prime they are consecutive primes, since every
integer strictly between them is divisible by one of the primes up to $13$,
and each such integer then has least prime factor at most $13$, below the
gap $18$. Unconditionally, it is open whether infinitely many gaps contain
no such integer (Gafni and Tao, Remark 1.2).
