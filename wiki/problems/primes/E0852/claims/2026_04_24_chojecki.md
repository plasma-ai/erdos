**Claim.** Theorem 1 of P. Chojecki, *A Power Lower Bound for Runs of
Distinct Prime Gaps* (a six-page note dated 24 April 2026), states that there
is an absolute constant $c_0>0$ such that, for all sufficiently large $x$, the
function $h(x)$ of [[problems/primes/E0852/_index|Problem 852]] satisfies

$$
h(x)\ge c_0(\log x)^{1/3},
$$

and in particular $h(x)>(\log x)^c$ for every fixed $c<1/3$ and all sufficiently
large $x$. The proof uses no hypothesis. It first proves the bound at $M=\pi(N)$
(Proposition 5): among the blocks of $H$ consecutive gaps starting at $n\le
M-H$, Markov's inequality applied to the total block length leaves a positive
proportion with $p_{n+H}-p_n\le AH\log N$; if such a block repeats a gap $q$ at
offsets $a<b$ from $p_n$, the five numbers $p_n$, $p_n+a$, $p_n+a+q$, $p_n+b$,
$p_n+b+q$ are prime, and the Selberg upper-bound sieve (Lemma 2, cited from
Friedlander and Iwaniec and from Lichtman and Teräväinen) together with an
average of singular series over affine families (Lemma 3, a special case of
Lichtman and Teräväinen's Corollary 2.6) bounds the number of such
configurations with offsets at most $Y$ by $\ll Y^3+NY^3/(\log N)^5$, with the
degenerate four- and three-prime cases handled alike. With $Y\asymp H\log N$
this is smaller than the number of short blocks once $H$ is a small multiple of
$(\log N)^{1/3}$. Passing to the index variable through $p_M\sim M\log M$ gives
the theorem. The note says (Introduction and Remark 6) that the argument uses
only that the endpoints of the equal gaps are prime, not that the gaps are
consecutive, and that this is the source of the exponent $1/3$. The author's
thread post of the same day credits the result to GPT-5.5 Pro, and reports the
system's further remark that $h(x)=o(\log x)$ may fail under a form of the
uniform Hardy-Littlewood conjecture, with $h(x)\sim1.32323\log x$ as a guess;
the note itself contains neither.

**Submission note.** Posted to the site's forum by Przemek Chojecki on 24 April
2026:

> GPT-5.5 Pro got a nice partial result showing that\[ h(x)\gg (\log x)^{1/3}.
> \]The idea is this: we look at blocks of $H$ consecutive prime gaps whose
> total length is at most a constant multiple of $H\log N$. A positive
> proportion of starting positions have this property. If such a block has two
> equal gaps, then five explicitly related numbers must be prime:\[ m,\quad
> m+a,\quad m+a+q,\quad m+b,\quad m+b+q. \]For fixed $a,b,q$, the Selberg sieve
> (in a form proved by Lichtman--Teravainen) gives an upper bound of the
> expected order $N(\log N)^{-5}$, up to the singular series. Averaging the
> singular series over the three parameters $a,b,q$ then shows that the number
> of bad blocks is\[ \ll \frac{NY^3}{(\log N)^5} \]when all offsets are at most
> $Y$. Since in our application $Y\asymp H\log N$, this is smaller than the
> number of available starting positions as soon as $H$ is a sufficiently small
> multiple of $(\log N)^{1/3}$.
>
> On the other hand, GPT-5.5 also noted that $h(x)= o(\log x)$ might be wrong if
> we assume a form of uniform Hardy-Littlewood conjecture and more likely
> something like $h(x)≤(C+o(1))\log x$ is plausible with a guess at possible
> asymptotics being
> $$
> h(x) \sim 1.32323 \log x
> $$

**Covers.** The first of the problem's two particular questions, answered
yes: $h(x)>(\log x)^c$ holds for every fixed $c<1/3$. The note gives no upper
bound, does not estimate $h(x)$, and does not address whether
$h(x)=o(\log x)$. An independent argument posted the same day,
[[problems/primes/E0852/claims/2026_04_24_turturean|Turturean's four-prime count]],
reaches the same bound.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki: the inputs are the cited sieve
estimates of Friedlander and Iwaniec and of Lichtman and Teräväinen.

**Standing.** Claimed. The note is posted on the author's site and is not
refereed; the site's label is OPEN, its commentary records only that Brun's
sieve gives $h(x)\to\infty$, and its proof-claims tab lists nothing for the
problem, so the curator records no acceptance. No independent review of the
argument is recorded.
