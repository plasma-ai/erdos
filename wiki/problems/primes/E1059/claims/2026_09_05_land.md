***

**Claim.** The manuscript "A proposed solution to Erdős Problem 1059: Prime
avoidance for a sparse set of shifts" (5 September 2026) states as its
Theorem 1 that for any function $K(X)=o(\log X)$, every sufficiently large
$X$ and any $k\le K(X)$ distinct integers $h_1,\dots,h_k\in[1,X]$, some
prime $p\in(2X,3X]$ has $p-h_i$ composite for every $i$. Its Corollary 1
takes $X=m!$ and the shifts $1!,2!,\dots,m!$: for every large $m$ there is
a prime $p\in(2\cdot m!,3\cdot m!]$ with $p-i!$ composite for each $i\le m$,
and since every $k$ with $k!<p$ then satisfies $k\le m$, these primes answer
[[problems/primes/E1059/_index|Problem 1059]] in the affirmative. The
argument gives one prime per interval $(2X,3X]$, so it asserts infinitude and
not a positive proportion of the primes. The tab entry describes the method
as an adaptation of the truncated-product construction of the OpenAI
long-gaps note recorded on
[[problems/primes/E0004/claims/2026_09_04_openai|the release's Problem 4 page]],
with a coefficient estimate for primitive characters that allows averaging
over primes through Vaughan's mean value theorem. This page rests on the
manuscript's statements; its proof is not checked here.

> framework from OpenAI’s Improved long gaps between primes, adding a
> primitive-character coefficient estimate to permit prime averaging via
> Vaughan’s theorem. Formalization coming...

no site acceptance and no outside review was found (site and thread accessed

**Lean.** The claimant's one comment on the tab, of 6 September 2026, points
to the repository linked above as a formalization that compiles with no
`sorry` and no axioms beyond `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.
The repository's README names the terminal theorem
`Erdos1059.infinitelyManyFactorialAvoidingPrimes`, that the set of primes
$p$ with $p-k!$ composite for every $k!<p$ is infinite, reports the same
three axioms from its build, uses the toolchain `leanprover/lean4:v4.34.0-rc2`,
and says that no independent replication or external audit is recorded and
that no human audit of the correspondence between the Lean statements and
the manuscript is recorded. This corpus has not built or audited the
development, so no `formalized` evidence is listed.

**Depends on.** Nothing on the wiki; the manuscript's outside inputs are the
