***

**Claim.** The note "The large-prime-divisor route to Erdős Problem #1201"
(1 May 2026) works in the range $1/2<\alpha<1$, where an integer of size
about $X$ has at most one prime factor above $X^\alpha$. For
$0\le j<h$ let $L_{j,X}(n)$ be the number of primes $p>X^\alpha$ dividing
$n+j$, and for $J\subseteq\{0,\dots,h-1\}$ let $T_J(X)$ be the sum over
$X<n\le2X$ of $\prod_{j\in J}L_{j,X}(n)$. The hypothesis the note names
$\mathrm{LPD}(\alpha,h)$ is that $T_J(X)=\lambda_\alpha^{|J|}X+o(X)$ for
every such $J$, with $\lambda_\alpha=\log(1/\alpha)$. Its Theorem 1.3:
assuming $\mathrm{LPD}(\alpha,h)$, the number of $n\le N$ with
$P^+(n(n+1)\cdots(n+h-1))\le n^\alpha$ is $\rho(1/\alpha)^hN+o(N)$, so the
natural density of that set exists and equals $\rho(1/\alpha)^h$. With
$\alpha=1-\varepsilon$ this gives the set of
[[problems/primes/E1201/_index|Problem 1201]] the natural density
$1-\rho(1/(1-\varepsilon))^h$, which exceeds $1-\eta$ for $h$ large; the
natural-density variant recorded on the problem page would follow for
$0<\varepsilon<1/2$. Theorem 1.4 says that the two-point case
$J=\{i,j\}$ of the hypothesis is equivalent to the asymptotic
$\rho(1/\alpha)^2X+o(X)$ for the number of $X<n\le2X$ with both
$P^+(n+i)$ and $P^+(n+j)$ at most $X^\alpha$, at every scale $X$. The
note's abstract says that it does not claim an unconditional proof of the
hypothesis and that the hypothesis is exactly the missing fixed-shift,
all-scales input. The statements above are those of the note's Section 1;
its proofs have not been checked.

**Hypothesis.** $\mathrm{LPD}(1-\varepsilon,h)$ for every $h$ and every
$0<\varepsilon<1/2$: an unproven asymptotic for the joint distribution of
large prime divisors of $h$ consecutive integers at every scale. The
hypothesis at every $h$ is needed because Theorem 1.3 gives the density
$1-\rho(1/(1-\varepsilon))^h$ only at an $h$ where
$\mathrm{LPD}(1-\varepsilon,h)$ holds, and reaching $1-\eta$ for every
$\eta$ needs an $h$ with $\rho(1/(1-\varepsilon))^h<\eta$, so arbitrarily
large $h$; as $\mathrm{LPD}(\alpha,h)$ implies $\mathrm{LPD}(\alpha,h')$
for $h'<h$, the sets $J$ shrinking, that is the hypothesis at every $h$.
The note's Section 1 phrases its deduction as using the hypothesis for some
$h$ chosen large, which as a statement of the hypothesis is too weak. Even
granting the hypothesis for every $h$ and every $\varepsilon<1/2$, the
natural-density form of the question for $\varepsilon\ge1/2$ is not
reached: the method needs $\alpha>1/2$, and a superset of a set of natural
density at least $1-\eta$ need not have a natural density, so the
conditional result is also partial in $\varepsilon$. Terence Tao wrote in
the thread on 1 May 2026 that the note's results are conditional on an
unproven and difficult hypothesis, the one the note names LPD, and the
claimant confirmed the conditional nature of the results the same day. The
unconditional lower-density statement, the precise Statement, is
[[problems/primes/E1201/claims/2026_04_30_chojecki|Chojecki's claim]] and is
not conditional.

**Standing.** Przemek Chojecki posted the note in the site's thread on 1
May 2026, presenting it as written by GPT-5.5 Pro; Chojecki is the claimant as
its submitter, and GPT-5.5 Pro is the system Chojecki names. The note itself
carries no byline and, as of 2026-10-07, has no refereed publication, no
formalization and no outside review, and the site's label is unchanged (OPEN).
The claim is rejected: under the unproven hypothesis LPD it gives the
natural-density variant only for $0<\varepsilon<1/2$, which implies the
precise Statement only in that range, a result the unconditional claim already
covers.

**Depends on.** Nothing on the wiki; the note's unconditional part rests
on the same Matomäki--Radziwiłł input as the claim linked above.
