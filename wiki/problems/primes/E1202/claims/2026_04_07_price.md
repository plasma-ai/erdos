***

Price, working with GPT-5.4 Pro, answers
[[problems/primes/E1202/_index|Problem 1202]] in the negative. The question
asks, for fixed $\epsilon,\eta>0$, whether some $k$ makes every choice of $k$
primes $p_1<\cdots<p_k<n^{1-\epsilon}$, each with a forbidden set $A_i$ of
$(p_i-1)/2$ classes modulo $p_i$, leave at most $\epsilon n$ integers
$m\leq n$ outside every $A_i$. The construction shows that no $k$ works. In
the quantitative form the linked Lean file proves, for every $k$ there are
$n$, primes $p_1<\cdots<p_k<n^{9/10}$ and sets $A_i$ of $(p_i-1)/2$ classes
each such that more than $n/10$ integers in $[1,n]$ survive, so the statement
fails at $\epsilon=1/10$ for every $\eta$; the exponent $9/10$ and the bound
$n/10$ are the file's choices, not a form the manuscript is held to state.
The primes are taken from one short interval and each $A_i$ is an interval of
residues, aligned so that a striped set of positive density avoids every
forbidden class; the surviving set contains a long arithmetic progression.
The site's commentary reports the construction in quantitative form: for any
$c>0$ and $m>\sqrt{n\log n}$ it gives $k\gg_c m^2/(n\log n)$ primes in
$(m,2m]$ with half their classes forbidden and at least $(1/2-c)n$ survivors
up to $n$, so half the classes modulo $k$ primes of size $O_k(\sqrt{n\log n})$
can be sifted while a positive proportion of $[1,n]$ remains. That form is
recorded as the site states it and was not checked. The large sieve
gives the affirmative answer when $p_k<n^{1/2}$, so the counterexample lives
in the range between $n^{1/2}$ and $n^{1-\epsilon}$ that Erdős asked about.
