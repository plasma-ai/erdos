***

**Claim.** There is a constant $c$ such that some 2-coloring of the integers
has no monochromatic arithmetic progression of common difference $d$ with
more than $cd$ terms, for every $d$; so the best $f$ of
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0187/_index|Problem 187]] satisfies $f(d)<cd$.
Erdős states the bound in Section 2 (printed p. 121) of P. Erdős, Problems
and results on combinatorial number theory, A Survey of Combinatorial Theory
(Fort Collins, 1971), North-Holland (1973), 117--138, and sketches the
coloring: for a quadratic irrational $\alpha$, put $n$ in the first class
when the fractional part of $n\alpha$ is below $\frac12$ and in the second
otherwise. Along a progression of difference $d$ the fractional parts advance
by $\{d\alpha\}$, which the inequality $|\alpha-p/q|>c_1/q^2$ keeps at least
$c_1/d$ away from every integer, so a monochromatic run has length $O(d)$.
The passage is recorded on the
[[../library/additive_combinatorics/erdos_1973_problems_results_combinatorial_number_theory/_index|chapter's library card]].
Erdős repeats the bound in his 1976 and 1980 surveys and, with Graham, in
their 1979 and 1980 surveys, as the problem page records.

**Covers.** The upper bound $f(d)=O(d)$ on the best $f$ alone, superseded by
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0187/claims/1980_11_01_beck|Beck's bound]]
$(1+o(1))\log_2d$. Not covered: any lower bound, and the determination of the
best $f$.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the claim rests on the cited chapter.

**Standing.** The chapter appears in a proceedings volume, and no evidence
that the volume was refereed is on record, so no `refereed` evidence is
listed. The site's curator credits the bound to Erdős in the problem's
commentary, but the site labels the problem OPEN, so that credit is not
acceptance. The claim stays `claimed`; the year is the chapter's only date,
so the page is named by the first day of 1973. The corpus records no check of
the argument beyond the sketch above.
