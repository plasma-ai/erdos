# problems/ramsey_theory/E0551/claims

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0551/_index|..]]

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0551/claims/1973_02_01_bondy_erdos|1973_02_01_bondy_erdos]]: Theorem 4 of Bondy and Erdős (1973): R(C_k,K_n) = (k-1)(n-1)+1 whenever
k >= n^2-2, the first infinite range of the identity; refereed and credited
by the site's commentary.

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0551/claims/2004_04_27_nikiforov|2004_04_27_nikiforov]]: Theorem 1 of Nikiforov's preprint arXiv:math/0404501v1 (journal version
Combin. Probab. Comput. 14 (2005)): R(C_k,K_n) = (k-1)(n-1)+1 whenever
n >= 4 and k >= 4n+2, a linear threshold; refereed.

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0551/claims/2018_07_17_keevash_long_skokan|2018_07_17_keevash_long_skokan]]: Theorem 1.1 of Keevash, Long and Skokan (IMRN 2021) gives an absolute C with
R(C_k,K_n) = (k-1)(n-1)+1 whenever k is at least C log n / log log n, so
the identity holds for all large n; finitely many pairs remain unchecked.

[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0551/claims/2026_09_25_openai|2026_09_25_openai]]: The OpenAI release's preprint of 25 September 2026 proves R(C_k,K_n) =
(k-1)(n-1)+1 for every k at least n at least 3, with R(C_3,K_3) = 6, by a
reduction to 3,099 finite pattern instances; accepted on its built Lean proof.
