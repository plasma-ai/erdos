***

**Claim.** Write $f(n)=R(C_4,K_{1,n})$, the Ramsey number of
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0552/_index|Problem 552]] with $S_n=K_{1,n}$.
T. D. Parsons, *Ramsey graphs and block designs. I*, Trans. Amer. Math.
Soc. 209 (1975), 33--44, proves in Theorem 1 (p. 41) that
$f(n)\le n+\sqrt{n-1}+2$ for all $n\ge2$ and $f(q^2+1)\le q^2+q+2$ for all
$q\ge1$, with

$$
f(q^2+1)=q^2+q+2
$$

for every prime power $q$ (including $q=1$), and in Theorem 2 (pp. 41--42),
which the paper attributes jointly to the author and S. L. Lawrence, that

$$
f(q^2)=q^2+q+1
$$

for every prime power $q$. The lower bounds come from the polarity graph of
the projective plane over $GF(q)$; the upper bound counts pairs of vertices
through common neighbors in a $C_4$-free graph. Since $f$ is an integer,
the general bound is $f(n)\le n+\lceil\sqrt n\rceil+1$, the upper end of the
problem's window. The statements are recorded on the result pages
[[../library/ramsey_theory/parsons_1975_ramsey_graphs_block_designs_i/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
and
[[../library/ramsey_theory/parsons_1975_ramsey_graphs_block_designs_i/theorem_2|Theorem 2]]
of the library home
[[../library/ramsey_theory/parsons_1975_ramsey_graphs_block_designs_i/_index|parsons_1975_ramsey_graphs_block_designs_i]].

**Covers.** The value of $f(n)$ at $n=q^2+1$ and $n=q^2$ for every prime
power $q$: there $f(n)=n+\lceil\sqrt n\rceil$ and $f(n)=n+\lceil\sqrt
n\rceil+1$. The value at every other $n$, and the second question, whether
$f(n)\le n+\sqrt n-c$ for infinitely many $n$, are not settled by it.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the theorems rest on the paper's own
lemmas and the Friendship Theorem.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the paper is a journal publication in the
Transactions of the American Mathematical Society, volume 209 (1975), the
`refereed` evidence; the record carries no month, so this page is dated to
the first day of the year. The site's curator lists these families in the
commentary, but the site's label OPEN settles neither the problem nor a
declared part of it, so `reviewed` is not listed.
