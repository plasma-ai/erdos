***

**Claim.** Every graph $G$ with no $K_4$ minor, that is, every graph of
treewidth at most $2$, is Ramsey size linear in the sense of
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0566/_index|Problem 566]]: for every graph $H$
with $m$ edges and no isolated vertices,

$$
R(G,H)\le624\,v(G)\,m.
$$

The claimant describes the method as an adaptation of two tools from the paper
of Bradač, Gishboliner and Sudakov (the library's
[[../library/ramsey_theory/bradac_2022_ramsey_size_linear_graphs_related_questions/_index|source card]]),
triangle removal and an averaging step. Three further results are stated in the
claimant's summary: a connected graph whose edge count exceeds its vertex count
by at most two is Ramsey size linear unless $K_4$ or $K_4^*$ ($K_4$ with one
edge subdivided, the $H_5$ of Problem 567) is a subgraph of it; one graph in
each of the pairs $\{K_4^*,W_4\}$ and $\{W_5^-,W_5\}$ fails to be Ramsey size
linear while all of its proper subgraphs are (the site's Problem 79); and an
exhaustive computation over the graphs with at most eight vertices leaves, by
the claimant's count, $49$ minimal graphs undecided under the density
hypothesis, which fall into eleven core types, the code and tables being
supplementary material of the preprint.

**Covers.** The corrected Statement of Problem 566 (every subgraph on $k\ge2$
vertices has at most $2k-3$ edges) for the graphs without a $K_4$ minor. Every
$2$-tree has exactly $2k-3$ edges and satisfies the hypothesis with equality, so
the claim covers the extremal instances of that family; the density question for
all graphs satisfying the hypothesis is not claimed.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the argument adapts the methods of
Bradač, Gishboliner and Sudakov rather than invoking a paged theorem.
