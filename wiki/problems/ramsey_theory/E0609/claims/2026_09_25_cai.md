**Claim.** In the notation of
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E0609/_index|Problem 609]], $f(3)=5$: every
$3$-coloring of the edges of $K_9$ has a monochromatic odd cycle of length
at most $5$, and some $3$-coloring has none of length $3$. The lower bound
is the classical $R(3,3,3)=17>9$, which gives a $3$-coloring of $K_9$ with
no monochromatic triangle. For the upper bound the write-up supposes a
$3$-coloring of $K_9$ with no monochromatic $C_3$ or $C_5$ and argues in
three steps. First, no color class is bipartite: a bipartite class has a
side with at least five vertices, inside which the other two colors would
each have to be bipartite (an odd cycle on five vertices has length $3$ or
$5$), and two bipartite graphs cannot cover $K_5$, since assigning each
vertex its pair of sides gives five vectors in $\{0,1\}^2$, two of which
coincide. Second, each class, having odd girth $7$ or $9$, has at most $12$
edges: a $C_7$ in it is induced, the two vertices off the cycle each send
at most two edges to it, and the edge between them adds one, for
$7+2+2+1=12$; a class with no $C_7$ has a chordless Hamiltonian $C_9$ with
nine edges. Third, the $36$ edges force each class to have exactly $12$
edges and the first shape; the admissible $12$-edge graphs form a single
isomorphism class with $9!/8=45{,}360$ labeled copies, and a direct
enumeration finds no three pairwise edge-disjoint copies covering $K_9$. A
SAT encoding with one variable per edge and color, forbidding each
monochromatic $C_3$ and $C_5$, is reported unsatisfiable, and the same
encoding with only triangles forbidden satisfiable.

**Covers.** The value $f(3)=5$, the case $n=3$ of the problem. The
estimation of $f(n)$ as $n$ grows, the problem's question, is untouched,
as the claim itself says; the asymptotic bounds on the problem page are
unaffected.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the argument rests on the classical
value $R(3,3,3)=17$ and on the finite checks described in the write-up.
