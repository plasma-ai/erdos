**Claim.** [[problems/ramsey_theory/E0801/_index|Problem 801]] asks whether a
graph $G$ on $n$ vertices with no independent set on more than $n^{1/2}$
vertices must have a set of at most $n^{1/2}$ vertices spanning
$\gg n^{1/2}\log n$ edges. Alon answers yes.
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/alon_1996_independence_numbers_locally_sparse_graphs_ramsey/theorem_1_2|Theorem 1.2]]
of *Independence numbers of locally sparse graphs and a Ramsey type problem*
states that if the independence number of $G$ is smaller than
$\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor$, then some set of $\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor$ vertices of
$G$ contains at least $\Omega(\sqrt n\log n)$ edges; the abstract gives the
conclusion with an absolute constant $c'>0$, as at least $c'\sqrt n\log n$
edges. The paper says the bound is tight and settles Erdős's problem, the
tightness being
[[../library/extremal_graph_theory/alon_1996_independence_numbers_locally_sparse_graphs_ramsey/proposition_3_1|Proposition 3.1]]:
for every $1<m\le n$ there is a graph on $n$ vertices with independence
number below $m$ in which every $m$-set spans at most $cm\ln(en/m)$ edges,
which at $m=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor$ is $O(\sqrt n\log n)$. The proof splits on
the average degree: a dense graph has a random $\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor$-set
with the required expected edge count, and in a sparse one either some
neighborhood is dense enough or a cleaned random subset is triangle-free, so
that the Ajtai--Komlós--Szemerédi bound on its independence number forces the
edges. Erdős posed the question in 1979 for $f(n;m)$ at the threshold
$m=[n^{1/2}]$, the hypothesis being that every set of $m$ vertices contains
an edge, which is Alon's hypothesis $\alpha(G)<m$.

**Scope.** Full. Alon states the theorem with Erdős's hypothesis
$\alpha(G)<\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor$. The site's wording, no independent set on
more than $n^{1/2}$ vertices, also admits graphs with
$\alpha(G)=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor$, which the theorem as printed does not cover;
that boundary case follows from the printed theorem by a twin blow-up recorded
in the problem page's Current assessment as an authored reduction, and Alon's p.
7 says that his proof extends to every threshold $n^\epsilon\le m\le n/2$. The
Lean development linked above states the result under the site's non-strict
hypothesis.
