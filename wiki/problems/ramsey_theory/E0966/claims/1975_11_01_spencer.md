**Claim.** Spencer's
[[../library/ramsey_theory/spencer_1975_restricted_ramsey_configurations/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
(J. Combinatorial Theory Ser. A 19 (1975), p. 279) states that for all $k$
and $c$ there is a $V$-set $A$ containing no arithmetic progression of
length $k+1$, where a $V$-set is a set of integers every $c$-coloring of
which yields a monochromatic arithmetic progression of $k$ terms. With
$c=r$ this answers [[problems/ramsey_theory/E0966/_index|Problem 966]] yes
for every $k,r\ge2$: the set $A$ has no non-trivial progression of length
$k+1$, and every $r$-coloring of it has a monochromatic non-trivial
progression of length $k$. The set is
$A=\{a_0+a_1p+\dots+a_{n-1}p^{n-1}:0\le a_i<k\}$ for a prime $p>k$ and
$n$ the Hales--Jewett dimension for $k$ and $c$; it is finite, lies in
$\{0,\dots,p^n-1\}$, and a translation by $1$ places it in the positive
integers. Both clauses concern progressions with nonzero difference. The
proof is half a page: a $c$-coloring of $A$ is a $c$-coloring of the cube
$k^n$, whose monochromatic line, given by the Hales--Jewett theorem, is a
$k$-term progression in $A$; and a progression $x,x+d,\dots,x+kd$ in $A$
is impossible, because at the position of the lowest nonzero base-$p$
digit of $d$ the digits of the terms run through $k+1$ distinct residues
modulo $p$ while the digits of elements of $A$ take only $k$ values.

**Scope.** Full: the theorem is the problem's statement for every $k$ and
$r$, the site's $k,r\ge2$ included.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the result rests on the cited paper
and the Hales--Jewett theorem it invokes.

**Acceptance.** Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas Bloom, marks the problem
PROVED (LEAN) and, in the problem's commentary, credits the result to Spencer
through Erdős's announcement; the Lean proof behind the label is described under
Formalization. The curator's credit rests on Erdős's note, not on a reading of
Spencer's paper, which the commentary does not cite and the thread does not
discuss. Refereed: the paper appeared in J. Combinatorial Theory Ser. A 19
(1975), no. 3, 278--286 (the Crossref record dates the issue November 1975
without a day, so the page's day is a placeholder). Erdős's 1975 Bordeaux paper
announced the result in an added-in-proof note that gives no reference
([[../library/additive_combinatorics/erdos_1975_problems_results_combinatorial_number_theory/_index|source card]]);
the identification of Spencer's paper as the published proof is the problem
page's, and Spencer's acknowledgment thanks Erdős for his conjectures. The
Semantic Scholar list of 28 citing works records no dispute.

**Formalization.** The Lean proof behind the site's suffix is an
independent proof that Aristotle (Harmonic) generated from the statement
alone and the forum user JoshuaB posted to the site's thread on 25 February
2026 (post 4472, linked above). Its repository copy, the file
`src/v4.29.1/ErdosProblems/Erdos966.lean` in Boris Alexeev's repository
`plby/lean-proofs` (linked above), names Spencer and Aristotle as informal
authors and Aristotle and JoshuaB as formal authors, and proves the
statement with colorings of $\mathbb N$ in place of colorings of $A$. Its
construction is the one above with base $2k$ in place of the prime $p$. The
corpus has not built or audited the file and no statement-fidelity review of
it exists, so the page lists no `formalized` evidence.

clause, and the proof for its two steps, not step by step. Nothing here is
