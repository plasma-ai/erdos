***

**Claim.** Theorem 1 of *On the ratio of $R(k,\ell)$ and $R(k,\ell+1)$*, a
three-page manuscript hosted by OpenAI, states that for every fixed integer
$k\ge2$

$$
\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{R(k,\ell+1)}{R(k,\ell)}=1,
$$

where $R(k,\ell)$ is the least $N$ such that every graph on $N$ vertices
has a $K_k$ or an independent set of $\ell$ vertices. For $k\ge3$ this is
the statement of [[problems/ramsey_theory/E1014/_index|Problem 1014]];
the case $k=2$ is $R(2,\ell)=\ell$. Remark 1 adds that the proof gives, for
each fixed $k\ge2$, a constant $c_k>0$ with
$R(k,\ell+1)/R(k,\ell)\le1+\ell^{-c_k}$ for all large $\ell$, without a
value for any $c_k$. The claimant is OpenAI: the manuscript names no
author, and its abstract attributes the proof to an internal model at
OpenAI. The theorem and remark are paged at
[[../library/ramsey_theory/openai_2026_ratio_consecutive_ramsey_numbers/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
and
[[../library/ramsey_theory/openai_2026_ratio_consecutive_ramsey_numbers/remark_1|Remark 1]]
of the library's
[[../library/ramsey_theory/openai_2026_ratio_consecutive_ramsey_numbers/_index|source card]].
The proof (pp. 2--3) takes a graph $G$ on $R(k,\ell+1)-1$ vertices with no
$K_k$ and independence number at most $\ell$, so that every vertex has at
least $R(k,\ell+1)-R(k,\ell)-1$ neighbors; dependent random choice, in
the Fox--Sudakov form, extracts a set $U$ in which every
$\lceil k/2\rceil$-subset has at least $R(\lfloor k/2\rfloor,\ell+1)$
common neighbors, so $U$ spans no $K_{\lceil k/2\rceil}$ and no
independent $(\ell+1)$-set; the Erdős--Szekeres upper bound and a
probabilistic lower bound on $R(k,\ell)$ make the two bounds on $|U|$
incompatible unless $(R(k,\ell+1)-R(k,\ell))/R(k,\ell+1)\to0$.

**Standing.** Accepted. Reviewed: the site's curator, T. F. Bloom, relabeled
the problem PROVED (LEAN) on 24 April 2026, the day after a thread comment
linked the manuscript, with commentary crediting the solution to an internal
model at OpenAI and stating the quantitative bound the proof gives; the
curator is independent of the claimant, and this crediting is the one
evidence kind listed. The manuscript has no refereed publication, no arXiv
version and no written expert review (Crossref and arXiv searches of
2026-09-18, recorded on the problem page), so `refereed` is not listed; its
Lemma 2, the probabilistic lower bound $R(k,\ell)\gg_k(\ell/\log\ell)^{k/2}$,
is stated without proof or citation (it follows from Spencer's 1977
Theorem 2.2, recorded on the problem page).
The thread also records a formalization report and one reader's request for
an expert review before the label changes, and the proof-claim tab is empty.
The page is named by the PDF's metadata date, 22 April 2026, the earliest
date attached to the posting; the manuscript's text is undated.

**Formalizations.** Two external Lean developments prove the theorem for
their own definitions of the Ramsey number at the pinned commits; neither
is built or audited here. The file
`src/v4.29.1/ErdosProblems/Erdos1014.lean` of Boris Alexeev's repository
`plby/lean-proofs` (the repository head of 2026-09-18; 3,511 lines) names the internal model as
informal author and Codex and Boris Alexeev as formal authors, and its
header's URL list points at OpenAI's GPT-5.5 announcement, the file's
pointer rather than the manuscript's attribution; it defines
`ramseyNumber k l` over `SimpleGraph (Fin n)` and proves
`erdos1014 (k : ℕ) (hk : 3 ≤ k) : Tendsto (fun l => (ramseyNumber k (l + 1) : ℝ) / ramseyNumber k l) atTop (𝓝 1)`
with no `sorry`, its closing comment recording the three standard axioms;
the thread comment of 23 April 2026 announced it, and the
formal-conjectures statement `erdos_1014` (a `sorry` body over
`SimpleGraph.classicalRamsey`) names it in its `formal_proof` attribute.
The repository `maokami/ramsey-ratio-lean` (head of 29 April 2026,
toolchain v4.28.0-rc1) defines `ramsey k ℓ` as an infimum, proves
`ramsey_ratio_tendsto_one` for every $k\ge2$ and the Remark 1 form
`ramsey_ratio_quantitative`, ends with `#print axioms`, and says in its
README that it reproduces this manuscript. The three definitions of the
Ramsey number differ and no bridge was reviewed, so `formalized` is not
listed.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the manuscript's proof rests on the
