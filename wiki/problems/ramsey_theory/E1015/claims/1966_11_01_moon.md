***

**Claim.** The
[[../library/ramsey_theory/moon_1966_disjoint_triangles_chromatic_graphs/theorem_p259|Theorem]]
(p. 259) of J. W. Moon, *Disjoint triangles in chromatic graphs*, Math.
Mag. 39 (1966), no. 5, 259--261. Write $\mu(G_n)$ for the largest number of
vertex-disjoint monochromatic triangles in a two-coloring $G_n$ of the
edges of $K_n$. Then $[\tfrac13n]-1\le\mu(G_n)\le[\tfrac13n]$ for every
$G_n$, and $\mu(G_n)=[\tfrac13n]$ when $n\equiv2\pmod3$ and $n\ge8$. The
closing remark (p. 261) says that the lower bound is attained whenever
$n\ge3$ and the second part does not apply, and prints no example. In the
leftover count of
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E1015/_index|Problem 1015]], where $f(n,3)$ is the
maximum of $n-3\mu(G_n)$, this gives $f(n,3)\le4$ for every $n$ other than
$5$ (at most $3$ for $n\equiv0$ and $4$ for $n\equiv1\pmod3$ by the first
part, and $2$ for $n\equiv2\pmod3$, $n\ge8$, by the second), and
$f(n,3)=2$ for $n\equiv2\pmod3$, $n\ge8$. Math. Mag. 39, no. 5, is the
November--December issue, so the page's day is a placeholder.

**Covers.** The case $t=3$, $f(3)=4$, the site's value. The upper bound is
Moon's theorem. Moon leaves to the reader the colorings that attain $4$ for
$n\equiv1\pmod3$; the $k=3$ case of the Figure 6 coloring of Burr, Erdős
and Spencer (1975),
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E1015/claims/1975_01_01_burr_erdos_spencer|their claim page]],
supplies them. Nothing for $t\ge4$ or for the two closing questions.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the theorem rests on the paper's own
case analysis of $K_8$ and the fact that every two-coloring of $K_6$ has a
monochromatic triangle.

**Acceptance.** Refereed journal publication in Mathematics Magazine 39
(1966), no. 5, 259--261, which is the `refereed` evidence. The site's label
SOLVED rests on Burr, Erdős and Spencer, so the curator's credit of
$f(3)=4$ to Moon is not listed as `reviewed`. The proof (pp. 259--261) is
followed on the library's result page, with the claims about its Figures
2--4 taken as printed; nothing here is independently reviewed.
