**Claim.** Both constants in the known bounds on $F(n)$ of
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E1182/_index|Problem 1182]] are improved:
$F(n)\ge n+\lfloor(n-1)/6\rfloor$ for every $n\ge4$, where the 1980 theorem
of Burr, Erdős, Faudree, Rousseau and Schelp gives $(17n+1)/15$, and
$F(n)\le11n/2$, sharpened to $F(n)\le5.03n$, for all large $n$, where
Brandt gives $84n$.

The lower bound reuses the 1980 argument, which strips a sparse connected
graph down to a dense core and bounds the Ramsey number of the core, and
feeds it Sidorenko's theorem $R(K_3,H)\le2e(H)+1$, a sharper input than the
1980 paper had; with it the arithmetic tolerates a core of up to $6k$
edges whenever $k\le(n-1)/6$, and every connected graph with at most
$n+\lfloor(n-1)/6\rfloor$ edges stays triangle-good.

The upper bound exhibits, for large $n$, connected graphs with about
$11n/2$ edges that are not triangle-good, namely almost all $11$-regular
graphs $H$ on $n$ vertices. The witness coloring of $K_{2n-1}$ takes a
balanced blow-up of $C_5$, which has no triangle, as its red graph; $H$ is
a blue subgraph exactly when its vertices can be partitioned into the five
blow-up classes, each of size at most $\lceil(2n-1)/5\rceil$, with no edge
of $H$ joining two classes consecutive on the pentagon. The claimant
computes the expected number of such partitions of a random $d$-regular
graph as $\exp(n\max\Psi_d+o(n))$ for an explicit function $\Psi_d$ and
shows $\max\Psi_{11}\le-0.05$ by a branch-and-bound search over the
domain of $\Psi_{11}$ in exact arithmetic, pruned by a dual bound and a
convexity argument; the expectation then tends to zero, so almost every
$11$-regular graph has no blue copy and $R(K_3,H)>2n-1$. For the $5.03n$
bound a random $10$-regular graph with $0.03n$ random edges added takes
the place of $H$; the claimant notes that $d=10$ alone falls short and that
about $5.02n$ is the method's limit.

**Covers.** The constants: if the claim holds, $7/6\le\liminf F(n)/n$ and
$\limsup F(n)/n\le5.03$, against the $17/15$ and $84$ of the problem page,
and the closing question's negative answer follows again, independently of
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E1182/claims/1996_12_01_brandt|Brandt 1996]]; as
on that page, the claim value follows the closing question's polarity, a
no to "is it true that $F(n)/n\to\infty$?". The order of $f(n)$ and the
exact growth of $F(n)$ are not addressed.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E1182/claims/1980_01_01_burr_erdos_faudree_rousseau_schelp|Burr, Erdős, Faudree, Rousseau and Schelp 1980]]
for the reduction the lower bound reuses; the lower bound also rests on
Sidorenko's theorem, the upper bound on the certificate in the repository.
