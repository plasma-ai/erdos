**Claim.** There is a $2$-coloring of $\mathbb{N}$ under which no infinite
set $A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots\}$ has all its multilinear expressions, the sums
$\prod_{i\in S_1}a_i+\cdots+\prod_{i\in S_k}a_i$ over pairwise disjoint
nonempty finite index sets other than the single terms $a_i$, in one color
class; so the answer to
[[problems/ramsey_theory/E1198/_index|Problem 1198]] is no. The coloring
comes from Smith's Theorem 3.17 (Partitions and ($m$ and $n$) sums of
products, J. Combin. Theory Ser. A 72 (1995), no. 1, 77--94), as the site's
thread states it: for distinct $m_1,\ldots,m_r$ there is a partition of
$\mathbb{N}$ into $r$ cells such that no cell contains both an
$SP_{m_i}$-set and an $SP_{m_j}$-set for $i\ne j$, where $SP_n((x_t))$ is
the set of sums of $n$ products over increasing blocks of a sequence
$x_1<x_2<\cdots$. With $r=2$, $m_1=2$ and $m_2=3$, every element of
$SP_2((a_t))$ and of $SP_3((a_t))$ is a multilinear expression of $A$ with
two or three terms, so a class containing all multilinear expressions of
$A$ would contain both sets, which the partition forbids. The thread's
other route, from Smith's two-cell statement for one product against a sum
of two products through the substitution $b_i=a_{2i}a_{2i+1}$, gives the
same answer. Both deductions use only that the configurations named above are
multilinear expressions other than single terms; they are written out as an
authored derivation on the problem page.

**Read depth.** The paper is not held, although the Crossref record carries
the publisher's open-archive license; the zbMATH, Crossref and OpenAlex
records serve no review text or abstract. Nothing of it was read; the
definition of $SP_n$, Theorem 3.17 and the two-cell statement are exactly
as the thread quotes them, and the deduction is valid for those statements.
Reopening condition: Smith's text read at Theorem 3.17 and at the two-cell
statement, which may also show whether the author's later two-cell paper
(SIAM J. Discrete Math., 2001) gives a more direct theorem.
