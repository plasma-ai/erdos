***

Jeff Kahn proves, in *On a problem of Erdős and Lovász. II: $n(r)=O(r)$*
([[../library/set_systems/kahn_1994_problem_erdos_lovasz_ii/_index|card]]), that
the function $f$ of [[problems/set_systems/E0021/_index|Problem 21]], written
$n(r)$ in the paper, satisfies $n(r)=O(r)$: the least size of an intersecting
family of $r$-sets such that every set of at most $r-1$ elements is disjoint
from some member is at most a constant times $r$, which is the inequality
$f(n)\ll n$ the problem asks for. The theorem is stated through a fixed prime
power $K$: for all sufficiently large $t$ and prime powers $q\equiv3\pmod4$ with
$q<t\leq(1+K^{-2})q$, the value $r=Kq+t$ has $n(r)\leq5(K^2+K)t$, and every
sufficiently large $r$ has this form. The constant is about $5K$ and is not
evaluated. The construction is given in dual form, an $r$-regular hypergraph on
$5(K^2+K)t$ vertices in which every two vertices share an edge and whose edge
cover number is $r$, built from $5$-regular bipartite expander-like graphs and
transversal designs $\mathrm{TD}(K,t)$, whose existence for large $t$ is the
transversal-design form of the Chowla–Erdős–Straus theorem on mutually
orthogonal Latin squares (Kahn cites Wilson 1974 for the equivalence). Erdős
and Lovász had posed the problem with the bounds
$\frac83n-3\leq f(n)\ll n^{3/2}\log n$
([[../library/graph_coloring/erdos_1975_problems_results_3_chromatic_hypergraphs_related/_index|card]]),
and Kahn had earlier lowered the upper bound to $O(n\log n)$ [Ka92b]; both upper
bounds take random lines of a projective plane of order $n-1$, so they assume
such a plane exists, as it does when $n-1$ is a prime power. Kahn's 1994 paper
calls the lower bound $\frac83n-O(1)$ still unimproved. The site's commentary
reports that the truth has been speculated to be $3n+O(1)$ and cites [Ka94], but
the paper makes no such conjecture: its remark that a constant is probably $3$
(p. 126) concerns the number $Cr\log r$ of random lines of a projective plane
needed in its Theorem 1.2, not $f(n)$. A 2026 preprint of Sivashankar
(arXiv:2606.24878) claims the lower bound $f(n)\geq3n-4$ and, through Kahn's
hypergraph edge-coloring theorem, $f(n)\geq(\frac{41-\sqrt{19}}{12}-o(1))n$,
which would refute that speculation; it is unrefereed and is recorded on the
problem page, since it does not bear on whether $f(n)\ll n$. Exact small values:
$f(1)=1$ and $f(2)=3$ are trivial, $f(3)=6$ was known and is reproved, and
$f(4)=9$ is proved, by
[[../library/set_systems/tripathi_2014_note_uniform_intersecting_families_maximum_transversal/_index|Tripathi]]
(the site credits both $f(3)$ and $f(4)$ to him), and $f(5)=13$ with
$13\leq f(6)\leq18$ by
[[../library/set_systems/barat_2021_intersecting_hypergraphs_maximal_covering_number/_index|Barát]];
these bound the function at single arguments and are not part of the claim.

received 15 April 1992; the publisher's record dates the issue January 1994
without a day, and the page's date is the first of that month. Reviewed: Thomas
Bloom, the site's curator, marks the problem proved and credits the solution to
Kahn [Ka94]. The site's label adds a Lean qualification. The formal-conjectures
