***

**Claim.** Péter Frankl proves the conjecture of Erdős and Sós in
[[../library/set_systems/frankl_1977_families_finite_sets_intersect_singleton/_index|On families of finite sets no two of which intersect in a singleton]]:
for $k\ge4$ and $n>n_0(k)$, every family $\mathcal F$ of $k$-element
subsets of an $n$-element set with $|\mathcal F|>\binom{n-2}{k-2}$ contains
two members $A,B$ with $|A\cap B|=1$. The threshold is sharp, since the
$\binom{n-2}{k-2}$ sets containing a fixed pair pairwise meet in at least two
points. Theorem 2 of the paper (printed p. 132) gives the explicit range
$n>n_0(k)+2\binom{n_0(k)}{k}$ and the structure at the threshold: a family
with no two members meeting in one point has fewer than $\binom{n-2}{k-2}$
members or is exactly the family of all $k$-sets through a fixed pair. The
proof analyzes the links $\mathcal F_x$ and their $\Delta$-systems
(sunflowers). Katona had proved the case $k=4$; his proof is unpublished and
the site records it without a source. For $k=3$ the analogous statement
fails, as Erdős and Sós observed, since there are $n$ triples on $n$ points
with no two meeting in one point when $4\mid n$.

**Formulation.** This is the corrected Statement of
[[problems/set_systems/E0702/_index|Problem 702]], which carries Erdős's own
range $n>n_0(k)$, and the claim is full for it. The site's wording drops that
range and is false for $k+1\le n\le3k-4$, as the problem page's Notes
record; Frankl's theorem says nothing about those $n$.

**Acceptance.** Refereed, reviewed and formalized. Refereed: Bull. Austral.
Math. Soc. **17** (1977), no. 1, 125–134; the issue is dated August 1977, and
the page is dated to the first day of that month. Reviewed: Thomas Bloom, the
site's curator, marks the problem proved and credits the proof for all $k\ge4$
to Frankl [Fr77]. The library card records the paper's results; no proof review
is recorded.

with the definitions its type reaches (`IsUniform`, that every member has $k$
elements; `HasSingletonIntersection`, that two members meet in exactly one
point; and `twoStarBound`, $\binom{n-2}{k-2}$), and the fingerprint of the built
theorem was found identical to the challenge. The statement was audited clause
by clause against the corrected Statement: subsets of `Fin n` stand for subsets
of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$; both require $k\ge4$; a threshold $n_0$ with the conclusion
for every $n\ge n_0$ is equivalent to one with the conclusion for $n>n_0(k)$;
the bound is $\binom{n-2}{k-2}$, and the truncated subtraction $n-2$ matters
only for $n\le1$, where no family meets the hypothesis; and the conclusion
allows $A=B$, which cannot meet itself in one point since $|A|=k\ge4$. The
theorem is therefore the corrected Statement, and the module's source closure
contains no `sorry`, no axiom and no `native_decide`. The build certifies that
statement only: it does not formalize the structure clause of Theorem 2, that at
the threshold the family of all $k$-sets through a fixed pair is the only
extremal family, which rests on the refereed paper alone.

**Formalization.** Boris Alexeev's repository holds a Lean 4 development, added
on 2026-08-18, whose header calls it a formalization of a solution to the
problem, names Frankl as the informal author and the AI systems Codex and
GPT-5.6 Sol as the formal authors, and cites a write-up `tex/702.tex` for the
mathematical proof and the formalization map. Its main theorem is the eventual
statement: for every $k\ge4$ there is $n_0$ such that for $n\ge n_0$ a family of
$k$-element subsets of an $n$-element set with more than $\binom{n-2}{k-2}$
members has two members meeting in exactly one point. A second named theorem
records that the site's wording, with no range on $n$, fails, with the family of
all four-element subsets of a five-element set as the counterexample; it is not
Frankl's result and is recorded on
[[problems/set_systems/E0702/claims/2026_08_18_alexeev|a rejected claim page]].
This corpus's verification built and audited that development, its main theorem
is the corrected Statement, and the page carries `formalized` evidence for it,
as the Acceptance section records.
