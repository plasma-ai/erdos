***

**Claim.** The answer to [[problems/set_systems/E0722/_index|Problem 722]] is
yes: for fixed $k>r\ge1$ there is $n_0(k,r)$ such that for every $n>n_0$
satisfying $\binom{k-i}{r-i}\mid\binom{n-i}{r-i}$ for all $0\le i<r$ there is
a family of $k$-subsets of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ containing every $r$-subset
exactly once, an $r$-$(n,k,1)$ design or Steiner system $S(r,k,n)$. This is
the existence conjecture for designs, in Keevash's *The existence of designs*
([[../library/set_systems/keevash_2014_existence_designs/_index|card]]). It
is the case $G=K_n^r$ of Keevash's Theorem 1.4, which gives a
$K_q^r$-decomposition of every $K_q^r$-divisible, typical $r$-uniform hypergraph
$G$ on $n>n_0$ vertices with density at least $n^{-\alpha}$; Theorem 1.10
extends the conclusion to designs of any fixed multiplicity $\lambda$. The
method, randomized algebraic construction, builds an approximate decomposition
and absorbs the leftover with algebraically structured configurations. Before
it, the question was settled only for small parameters: Kirkman for
$(r,k)=(2,3)$, Hanani for $(3,4)$, $(2,4)$ and $(2,5)$, and Wilson for $(2,k)$
with every $k$, each with its own claim page. An independent second proof, by
iterative absorption, is
[[problems/set_systems/E0722/claims/2016_11_21_glock_kuhn_lo_osthus|Glock, Kühn, Lo and Osthus's designs by iterative absorption]].

**Acceptance.** Reviewed: the site's curator, Thomas Bloom, labels the problem
proved and credits the general case to Keevash [Ke14], independently of its
author; Glock, Kühn, Lo and Osthus's refereed memoir presents its own argument
as a new proof of Keevash's theorem. The preprint is arXiv:1401.3665, posted
2014-01-15, the date of this page; its fourth version (2024-11-27) says it
incorporates referee comments, but the arXiv record lists no journal
reference, so the page lists no `refereed` evidence. The proof was not
reconstructed in this corpus.

**Formalization.** Boris Alexeev's repository holds a Lean 4 development,
added on 2026-08-20, whose header calls it a formalization of a solution to
the problem, names Peter Keevash as the informal author and "Codex" and
"GPT-5.6 Sol" as the formal authors. Its top-level theorem `erdos_722` states
the problem in the problem's own form: for all $0<r<k$ there is $n_0$ such
that every $n\ge n_0$ satisfying $\binom{k-i}{r-i}\mid\binom{n-i}{r-i}$ for
all $i<r$ carries a family of $k$-subsets of an $n$-element set containing
each $r$-subset exactly once; the argument is carried in separate modules,
and the file is linked above at its commit. This corpus has not built or
audited that development, so the page lists no `formalized` evidence; the
acceptance rests on the curator's credit.
