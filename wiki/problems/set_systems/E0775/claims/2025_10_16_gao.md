***

**Claim.** The answer to [[problems/set_systems/E0775/_index|Problem 775]] is
no. Theorem 1.1 of Gao's paper states that for every $k\ge3$ and every constant
$C\ge0$ there is $N(k,C)$ such that every $k$-uniform hypergraph on
$n\ge N(k,C)$ vertices has at most $n-C$ distinct sizes of cliques, a clique
being a maximal complete subhypergraph. Writing $g(n,k)$ for the largest number
of distinct clique sizes in a $k$-uniform hypergraph on $n$ vertices, this
says $g(n,k)\le n-\omega(1)$, so for $k=3$ no constant $C$ admits a
$3$-uniform hypergraph on $n$ vertices with $n-C$ distinct clique sizes for
infinitely many $n$. The site's commentary records that Erdős had constructed
$3$-uniform hypergraphs with at least $n-\log_* n$ distinct clique sizes, so
the gap between $n$ and $g(n,3)$ grows, but slowly. The graph case is
different: there $g(n,2)=n-\log_2 n+O(1)$, the lower bound by Spencer and the
upper bound by Moon and Moser, both on refereed pages linked from the problem
page.

The proof introduces $(k,C)$-layered trees, trees with a vertex ordering
$v_0,\dots,v_t$ in which every vertex is adjacent to an earlier one, every
vertex lies within distance $k$ of $v_0$, and the degree of $v_i$ is at most
$2^{C+i}$. Lemma 2.2 shows by induction on $k$ that such a tree has a bounded
number of vertices, and a hypergraph with too many distinct clique sizes is
shown to contain a layered tree larger than that bound. The
[[../library/set_systems/gao_2025_cliques_hypergraphs/_index|source card]]
holds the digest.

**Claimant.** Jun Gao, *On cliques in hypergraphs*, arXiv:2510.14804, posted
16 October 2025 (v1), revised 17 October (v2) and 31 October 2025 (v3). The
arXiv record lists no journal reference, so the paper is not recorded as
refereed.

**Acceptance.** The site's curator, Thomas Bloom, lists Problem 775 as
disproved and credits the negative answer to Gao [Ga25], in the general form
the commentary states, that the number of distinct clique sizes in a
$k$-uniform hypergraph on $n$ vertices falls short of $n$ by an unbounded
amount (`reviewed`). The paper was
reported in the problem's forum thread on 17 October 2025, and the site's page
was updated to credit it.

**Formalization.** The Lean file among the links, in Boris Alexeev's
repository `lean-proofs`, declares itself a formalization of a solution to
Problem 775, naming Jun Gao as its informal author and Aristotle and Lorenzo
Luccioli as its formal authors; it formalizes Theorem 1.1 as `main_theorem`
and the $k=3$ specialization as `erdos_problem_775`. Luccioli announced the
formalization in the problem's forum thread on 19 April 2026, with the code at
the gist among the links, pinned to the revision they posted, and the repository
copy is pinned to the commit of 24 June 2026 that last touched its path. A
later forum comment of 3 June 2026 reports that the gist did not compile under
Lean v4.28.1 and offers a patched copy; that copy is not a posting of the
claimant's result and is not linked. This corpus has not built any of these
files, so the formalization is a link and not `formalized` evidence. The
formal-conjectures statement file for the problem marks it solved and points at
the repository copy; a statement file is not a formalization.
