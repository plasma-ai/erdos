***

**Claim.** [[problems/set_systems/E0834/_index|Problem 834]] asks whether a
$3$-critical $3$-uniform hypergraph can have every vertex of degree at least
$7$, and its source does not say what $3$-critical means. Li's paper settles
the question under both meanings documented in the site's commentary, with
opposite answers.

Under the transversal meaning, $H$ is $3$-critical when some set of three
vertices meets every edge, no set of two vertices does, and for every edge $e$
some pair of vertices meets every edge other than $e$ ($\tau(H)=3$ and
$\tau(H-e)\le2$ for every edge $e$). Theorem 1.1 of the paper proves
that such a $3$-uniform hypergraph has a vertex of degree at most $6$, so the
answer is no; the bound is sharp, attained by the complete $3$-uniform
hypergraph on five vertices. The argument (Theorem 3.2) pairs each edge with a
two-vertex transversal of the rest and applies Bollobás's set-pairs inequality,
which bounds the number of edges by $\binom{5}{2}=10$; with at least five
vertices the degree sum then forces a vertex of degree at most $6$.

Under the chromatic meaning, $H$ is $3$-critical when its weak chromatic
number is $3$ and deleting any edge or any vertex leaves a $2$-colorable
hypergraph. Theorem 1.2 exhibits a $3$-uniform hypergraph on nine vertices with
$22$ edges and degree sequence $(10,7,7,7,7,7,7,7,7)$ that is not
$2$-colorable, has a proper $3$-coloring, and has an explicit proper
$2$-coloring after each of the $22$ edge deletions and nine vertex deletions
(Theorem 4.1 and Propositions 4.5 and 4.6), so the answer is yes.

The claim value is `answered` because the result is neither a proof nor a
disproof of one statement: the two readings of the question receive opposite
answers, and the paper settles both. The
[[../library/set_systems/li_2025_erdos_lovasz_problem_3_critical/_index|source card]]
holds the digest and one page per result.

**Claimant.** Ruiliang Li, *On an Erdős–Lovász problem: 3-critical 3-graphs
of minimum degree 7*, arXiv:2512.24850, posted 31 December 2025 (v1, 16
pages). The arXiv record lists no later version and no
journal reference, so the paper is not recorded as refereed.
