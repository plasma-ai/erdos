***

**Claim.** Kleitman's paper bounds the number of members of a family of
subsets of an $n$-element set that contains no $k$ pairwise disjoint members,
and shows the bounds best possible when $n=mk$ and when $n=mk-1$. Its
$r$-uniform case at $n=rk$ is the case of
[[problems/set_systems/E1020/_index|Problem 1020]] that the site and the
later literature credit to the paper: a family of $r$-subsets of an $rk$-set
with no $k$ pairwise disjoint members has at most

$$
\binom{rk-1}{r}=\Bigl(1-\frac1k\Bigr)\binom{rk}{r}
$$

members, so $f(rk;r,k)=\binom{rk-1}{r}$, the clique term of the conjecture,
which at $n=rk$ equals the covering term. Kolupaev and Kupavskii record
that at $n=(s+1)k$ in their notation the conjectured inequality is easy and
was proved by Kleitman, which is why Frankl's range on
[[problems/set_systems/E1020/claims/2017_10_01_frankl|Frankl 2017]] needs no
lower bound on the matching number. The paper is D. J. Kleitman, Maximal
number of subsets of a finite set no $k$ of which are pairwise disjoint,
J. Combinatorial Theory 5 (1968), 157–163.

**Covers.** The case $n=rk$ for every $r\ge3$ and $k\ge2$. It says nothing
about other $n$; the neighboring range above $rk$ is the claim on
[[problems/set_systems/E1020/claims/2017_10_01_frankl|Frankl 2017]].

**Acceptance.** Refereed: the paper appeared in the Journal of Combinatorial
Theory in 1968 (volume 5, issue 2); the record dates the issue to September
