**Claim.** Let $s>k\ge2$ and $(s+1)k\le n<(s+1)(k+k^{-2k-1}/2)$. Then every
family of $k$-subsets of an $n$-set with matching number at most $s$ has at
most $\binom{(s+1)k-1}{k}$ members, the size of the clique of all $k$-sets
inside an $((s+1)k-1)$-set. This is the paper's theorem as Kolupaev and
Kupavskii quote it (their Theorem 1.1). In the notation of
[[problems/set_systems/E1020/_index|Problem 1020]], with $r$ for the
uniformity and $k-1$ for the matching number,

$$
f(n;r,k)=\binom{rk-1}{r}
\qquad\Bigl(k-1>r,\ rk\le n<k\bigl(r+\tfrac{1}{2r^{2r+1}}\bigr)\Bigr),
$$

the conjectured value in that range, where the clique term is the larger.
The hypothesis $k-1>r$ costs nothing: for $k-1<2r^{2r+1}$ the range of $n$
is the single value $n=rk$, which is the case on
[[problems/set_systems/E1020/claims/1968_09_01_kleitman|Kleitman 1968]]. The
paper is P. Frankl, Proof of the Erdős matching conjecture in a new range,
Israel J. Math. 222 (2017), 421–430.

**Covers.** The range $k-1>r$ and $rk\le n<k(r+1/(2r^{2r+1}))$. The site
records it as $kr\le n\le k(r+1/(2r^{2r+1}))$, without the hypothesis on
$k$ and with a weak upper inequality. The window in $n$ was widened to
$k(r+1/(100r))$, for $r\ge5$ and $k-1>101r^3$, on
[[problems/set_systems/E1020/claims/2022_06_03_kolupaev_kupavskii|Kolupaev and Kupavskii 2023]].
