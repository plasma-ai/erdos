***

**Claim.** Theorem 3 of Wood's paper gives, for all integers $r\ge2$ and
$d\ge1$, an $r$-uniform hypergraph that is triangle-free and $d$-degenerate yet
has chromatic number $d+1$; a hypergraph is $d$-degenerate when every
subhypergraph has a vertex in at most $d$ edges, and a triangle is three edges
whose union has $r+1$ vertices. With $r=t$ and $d=2$ this gives, for every
$t\ge2$, a $t$-uniform hypergraph without property B in which every nonempty
vertex set $X$ contains at most $2(|X|-1)<2|X|$ edges: delete the vertices of
the induced subhypergraph one at a time, each in at most two remaining edges,
and charge every edge to its first deleted vertex. So a constant $c_t$ for which
the implication of [[problems/set_systems/E1022/_index|Problem 1022]] holds must
satisfy $c_t<2$, and the answer to the problem is no. The paper does not mention
the problem; it presents the theorem as showing that the greedy bound
$\chi\le d+1$ for $d$-degenerate hypergraphs is sharp at every uniformity. The
[[../library/set_systems/wood_2013_hypergraph_colouring_degeneracy/theorem_3|result page]]
on the source card records the statement, the inductive construction of Lemma 4,
and the application to the problem.

**Claimant.** David R. Wood, *Hypergraph Colouring and Degeneracy*,
arXiv:1310.2972, posted 10 October 2013 (v1) and revised to v3 on 15 August
2014; the arXiv record lists no journal reference, so the paper is not
recorded as refereed.

**Acceptance.** The site's curator, Thomas Bloom, names Wood's construction
[Wo13b] in the problem's commentary as the counterexample that refutes the
problem and bounds every valid constant below $2$ (`reviewed`). The paper was
pointed out in the problem's forum thread on 24 January 2026 by KoishiChan,
whose own construction had already been accepted
([[problems/set_systems/E1022/claims/2025_12_04_koishichan|claim page]]), with
the remark that the problem's counting condition is equivalent to degeneracy;
Bloom replied on 25 January 2026 that the condition is weaker than
$c_t$-degeneracy, giving the family of all $(n-1)$-subsets of an $n$-set as
an example, and that degeneracy implies the condition, which is all the
disproof needs. No Lean development declares itself a formalization of Wood's
construction.
