**Claim.** $g(n)\asymp n^{1/2}$, with explicit constants: Theorem 2.3 of
[[../library/discrete_geometry/furedi_1991_maximal_independent_subsets_steiner_systems_planar_sets/_index|Maximal independent subsets in Steiner systems and in planar sets]]
states that

$$
\frac{2\sqrt3}{9}\sqrt n<g(n)<2\sqrt n,
$$

where $g(n)$ is the least, over all maps $f$ from the pairs of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$
to $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ with $f(x,y)\notin\{x,y\}$, of the largest independent set,
as the problem defines it. The theorem is Section 2.2 of a paper whose main
subject is independent subsets of planar point sets. Füredi recalls the question
of Erdős and Hajnal [ErHa58] with their bounds
$c_6n^{1/3}<g(n)<c_7\sqrt{n\log n}$, the lower from the greedy algorithm and the
upper from the mean over random $f$, and observes that although the triples
$\{i,j,f(i,j)\}$ resemble a Steiner triple system, the true order of $g(n)$ is
not $\sqrt{n\log n}$, so the large-girth hypothesis in the
Komlós–Pintz–Szemerédi inequality cannot be dropped. The lower bound is a
special case of Spencer's theorem [Sp72], that a $3$-uniform hypergraph on $n$
vertices with average degree $d$ has an independent set of size at least
$c\,n/\sqrt d$. The upper bound is an explicit map: partition $\{1,\ldots,n\}$
into $a=\lceil\sqrt n\rceil$ blocks $V_i=\{(i,1),\ldots,(i,b_i)\}$ with
$b_1\le\cdots\le b_a=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor$, and for $i<j$, $x\ne y$ and
$y\le b_j$ set $f((i,x),(j,y))=(j,x)$, the other values arbitrary. If $I$ is
independent and $I_i$ is the set of second coordinates of $I\cap V_i$, then for
$i<j$ the sets $I_i$ and $I_j$ cannot meet when $|I_j|>1$, which gives
$|I|\le\lceil\sqrt n\rceil+\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor-1$. As printed, the partition
needs $\lceil\sqrt n\rceil\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor\ge n$, which fails when
$m^2+m<n<(m+1)^2$ with $m=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor$, for example at $n=7$. For
those $n$, blocks of up to $m+1$ points cover $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, and the same
argument gives $|I|\le2m+1$. Since $n\ge m^2+m+1$, $2m+1<2\sqrt n$, so the
theorem stands. This answers the question as asked, the order of $g(n)$; the
asymptotic constant is not determined. The same upper bound was proved
independently later by Conlon, Fox and Sudakov, whose paper records Füredi's
priority and whom the site credits; their page is
[[problems/set_systems/E1025/claims/2015_07_02_conlon_fox_sudakov|Conlon, Fox and Sudakov 2016]].

**Depends on.**
[[problems/set_systems/E1025/claims/1972_05_01_spencer|Spencer's lower bound]]
supplies $g(n)\gg n^{1/2}$, which the paper derives as a special case of
Spencer's theorem and does not reprove.
