**Claim.** In the problem's precise Statement, $c=1$, and the least ratio is
known exactly for every length. For a sequence $x$ of $n$ distinct positive
reals let $M(x)$ be the largest sum of a monotone subsequence, and let $c(n)$
be the infimum of $M(x)/\sum_ix_i$ over all such sequences. Then

$$
c(n)=\frac{k}{k^2+a}\qquad\text{for }n=k^2+2a+1,\ -k<a\le k,\ k\ge2,
$$

every $n\ge3$ other than $n=4$ being written this way for exactly one such
pair $(k,a)$. In particular $c(k^2)=1/k$: $k^2$ distinct positive reals
summing to $1$ have a monotone subsequence of sum at least $1/k$, and this is
sharp in the limit.
Since $c(n)\sqrt n\to1$, the largest constant $c$ with
$M(x)>(c-o(1))\,n^{-1/2}\sum_ix_i$ for all sequences is $1$, and the problem's
assumption that the $x_i$ be distinct and positive loses nothing, as negative
or zero terms can be dropped. The values agree with the exact ratios computed
in the site's thread for $n\le9$, verified there to $n\le11$, and with the
pattern Terence Tao found in the thread with the tool AlphaEvolve from
examples for $n$ up to $16$.

**The proof.** The lower bound $c(n)\ge k/(k^2+a)$ turns the sequence into a
packing of axis-parallel squares, in the manner of Seidenberg's 1959 proof of
the Erdős–Szekeres theorem: with $S_i$ and $T_i$ the largest sums of an
increasing and of a decreasing subsequence ending at $x_i$, the squares
$[S_i-x_i,S_i]\times[T_i-x_i,T_i]$ have pairwise disjoint interiors and lie in
$[0,M(x)]^2$, so a bound on the total side length of $n$ axis-parallel squares
packed in a unit square bounds $\sum x_i$ in terms of $M(x)$. That bound is the
axis-parallel case of Erdős's square-packing problem,
[[problems/discrete_geometry/E0106/_index|Problem 106]], proved by Baek,
Koizumi and Ueoro [BKU24] with an embedding argument of Praton; the Lean
file proves the needed form itself (`baek_koizumi_ueoro`), so the claim rests
on no other page of this corpus. The upper bound
$c(n)\le k/(k^2+a)$ is Alexeev's construction of $n$ numbers clustered at two
values, arranged in blocks so that every monotone subsequence takes few terms
from each cluster; for $n=k^2$ it is the construction posted by Stijn Cambie
in the thread in September 2025, which gave $c\le1$. The thread records the
collaboration: Tao's numerical table and conjectured pattern, Alexeev's
closed form and construction, Lawrence Wu's identification of the packing
problem and of [BKU24], and Tao's blog post of 8 December 2025 with a complete
informal proof.

**Formalization.** The Lean file among the links declares itself a
formalization of a solution to Problem 1026, says that Aristotle generated all
of its formal proofs, and names Lean 4.24.0 with the matching Mathlib. Its
`theorem exists_monotone_subseq_sum_ge` is the $k^2$ statement, its
`theorem c_opt_eq_k_div_sq_add_a` the exact value above for $n=k^2+1+2a$ with
$k>1$ and $-k<a\le k$, for the infimum `c_opt` of the ratio over sequences of
$n$ distinct positive reals, and its construction theorems give the matching
upper bounds. The file contains no `sorry` and no axiom declaration and ends
without an axiom print; Alexeev's post says the kernel check takes their laptop
over half an hour and that the file, stitched from several Aristotle runs,
defines $c$ three times. The link is pinned to the commit of 31 March 2026;
the file was added on 7 December 2025 and received the general theorem on 12
December 2025. The formal-conjectures statement file for the problem, as of
its commit of 18 September 2026, marks it solved and, on its weighted-variant
statement, points at the same file of the same repository under a later
toolchain folder (`v4.29.1`), unpinned; a statement file is not a
formalization.
