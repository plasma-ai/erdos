***

**Claim.** The largest number of edges of a $3$-uniform hypergraph on $n$
vertices in which no three edges span at most five vertices is
$(1\pm o(1))\,n^2/5$ (Glock 2019, main theorem, as the arXiv abstract and the
Crossref record state it; the paper is not held in the library). In the site's
notation, $\mathcal F_5$ is the family of $3$-graphs with $5$ vertices and $3$
edges, and a $3$-graph contains a member of $\mathcal F_5$ exactly when some
three of its edges span at most five vertices, a member with an isolated
vertex being three edges on four vertices. Hence

$$
\mathrm{ex}_3(n,\mathcal F_5)=\Bigl(\frac15+o(1)\Bigr)n^2,
$$

not $(1+o(1))n^2/6$: the displayed asymptotic fails at $k=5$ with
$\mathcal F_k$ the single family the site's wording defines, so the site's
wording, an assertion about every $k\ge5$, is false. The transfer from the
theorem to the site's wording is the identification of the two forbidden
families just made. The theorem says nothing about $k\ge6$; the limit at $k=6$
is $7/36$
([[problems/set_systems/E1076/claims/2022_09_28_glock_joos_kim_kuhn_lichev_pikhurko|the (6,4) page]]),
a second refutation of that wording.

**Why it is rejected.** The result is correct, but it answers the site's
wording, the single family $\mathcal F_5$, not the corrected Statement of
[[problems/set_systems/E1076/_index|Problem 1076]], whose family is
cumulative: under the corrected Statement a $3$-graph avoiding
$\mathcal F_4\cup\mathcal F_5$ is linear, so the theorem says nothing against
it, and the page does not count toward the problem's standing. The problem
page's Notes credit the result.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: S. Glock, Triple systems with no three triples
spanning at most five points, Bull. Lond. Math. Soc. 51 (2019), no. 2,
230–236, published online 27 November 2018 after the arXiv posting of
6 September 2018. The paper's abstract presents the result as the case $k=5$
of Brown, Erdős and Sós's conjecture that the limit of $f^{(3)}(n;k,k-2)/n^2$
exists. The site does not cite the paper on this problem and its curator makes
no statement about it; the proof is unreviewed.

**Formalizations.** None of this theorem is known. The file for the problem
in Boris Alexeev's lean-proofs collection names Glock as its informal author
in a header added on 23 August 2026, but its own docstring describes it as a
self-contained disproof that does not rely on Glock's approximate packing
theorem: it proves the weaker lower bound $92/529>1/6$ at $k=5$ by an
explicit construction. It is an independent proof and has its own claim
page, [[problems/set_systems/E1076/claims/2026_08_17_alexeev|Alexeev 2026]].
