**Claim.** Write $\kappa=(2^{\aleph_0})^+$ and read $[\lambda]^\kappa$ as
the subsets of size $\kappa$, the site's reading. The answer posted on
MathOverflow (question 511508, "On Erdős Problem #598") argues that
$\lambda\to[\kappa]^\omega_\kappa$ for an uncountable $\lambda\ge\kappa$
implies that $0^\sharp$ exists: the relation gives a weaker relation on
finite subsets, equivalent to the existence, for every structure on a set
$M$ of size $\lambda$ with a distinguished $P\subseteq M$ of size $\kappa$,
of an elementary substructure $N$ of size $\kappa$ with $N\cap P$ a proper
subset of $P$; applied to $L_\lambda$ with $P=\kappa$, the transitive
collapse of $N$ gives a nontrivial elementary embedding
$L_\alpha\to L_\lambda$ with critical point below $\kappa$, and such an
embedding yields $0^\sharp$ (Jech, Set Theory, Theorem 18.27). Hence in
every model without $0^\sharp$, in particular in $L$, the coloring asked
by [[problems/set_theory/E0598/_index|Problem 598]] exists for every
infinite $m$: the answer is yes. On the other side, the forcing of Garti
and Hayut (The first omitting cardinal for Magidority, Math. Log. Q. 65
(2019), 95–104;
[[../library/set_theory/garti_2019_first_omitting_cardinal_magidority/_index|library card]])
gives, from the consistency of an elementary embedding
$j:V_{\lambda+1}\to V_{\lambda+1}$ (the axiom I1), a model with
$\kappa=\aleph_2$ in which $\lambda\to[\kappa]^\omega_\kappa$ for some
$\lambda$, a negative instance. So the problem, read as one question about
every infinite $m$, is independent of ZFC relative to the consistency of
I1.

**Submission note.** Posted to the site's forum by Fanxin Wu on 7 June 2026:

> Based on this MO discussion the problem is independent modulo large cardinals.
> More precisely, the positive answer holds in the constructible universe $L$ so
> is consistent. The negative answer is consistent assuming the consistency of
> something called rank-into-rank axiom, which is pretty high in the large
> cardinal hierarchy; the exact large cardinal needed is probably open, but it's
> at least zero sharp.
>
> I would also like to propose distinguishing problems like this from
> problems that are independent but don't involve large cardinals,
> namely #1119 and #1127.

**Hypothesis.** The negative half is relative to a rank-into-rank axiom,
far above the consistency strength of ZFC; the positive half holds in $L$
outright, since $0^\sharp$ does not exist there. The answer leaves open the
exact consistency strength of $\lambda\to[\kappa]^\omega_\kappa$, which
lies between $0^\sharp$ and I1, and notes that Laver's earlier consistency
result may use the other reading of $[\lambda]^\kappa$, by order type. Wu
calls the independence essentially folklore.
