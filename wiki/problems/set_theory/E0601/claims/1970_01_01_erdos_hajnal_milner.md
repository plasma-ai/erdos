***

**Claim.** Theorem 7 of the paper (§6, printed p. 358): let $S$ be an
ordered set of type $\omega\theta<\omega_1^{\omega+2}$ and let $G=(S,E)$
be any graph on $S$ with no infinite path; then $S$ has an independent set
$S'$ of the same type $\omega\theta$. Every limit ordinal is of the form
$\omega\theta$, so every limit $\alpha<\omega_1^{\omega+2}$ has the
property asked by [[problems/set_theory/E0601/_index|Problem 601]]: a graph
on $\alpha$ has an infinite path or an independent set of order type
$\alpha$. The proof uses the paper's Theorem 5 on set mappings, and the
remark after the theorem says that this is what restricts the type below
$\omega_1^{\omega+2}$: Theorem 5 fails for larger types of cardinality
$\aleph_1$, while the authors suspect that Theorem 7 holds for arbitrary
$\theta$ and cannot prove it. Erdős records the result under Problem 10 of
[[../library/set_theory/erdos_1987_problems_finite_infinite_graphs/_index|Erdős 1987]]
(printed p. 226).

**Covers.** Every limit ordinal $\alpha<\omega_1^{\omega+2}$, positively.
It says nothing about $\alpha=\omega_1^{\omega+2}$, the first case the
paper leaves open and the case Erdős later priced at $250, nor about any
larger limit ordinal.

**Source.** P. Erdős, A. Hajnal and E. C. Milner, Set mappings and
polarized partition relations, in Combinatorial theory and its
applications, I (Proc. Colloq., Balatonfüred, 1969), Colloquia Mathematica
Societatis János Bolyai 4, North-Holland, Amsterdam, 1970, pp. 327–363;
Zbl 0215.329; the site's key [EHM70]. The paper link is the open scan in
the Rényi Institute's Erdős archive; the
[[../library/set_theory/erdos_1970_set_mappings_polarized_partition_relations/_index|source card]]
records the paper. The volume carries only the year, so this page is dated
the first of January 1970. Nothing on this page is independently reviewed
by this project.

**Acceptance.** Reviewed: the theorem is restated as proved and built on
in the refereed papers of Jean A. Larson
([[problems/set_theory/E0601/claims/1986_04_01_larson|1986]],
[[problems/set_theory/E0601/claims/1987_09_01_larson|1987]],
[[problems/set_theory/E0601/claims/1990_04_01_larson|1990]]) and of
Baumgartner and Larson
([[problems/set_theory/E0601/claims/1990_04_01_baumgartner_larson|1990]]),
whose zbMATH reviews repeat it: a documented acceptance by named experts
independent of the authors. Not `refereed`: the colloquium proceedings
carry no evidence that their papers were refereed. The site's commentary
credits the authors with the result, but the site labels the problem OPEN,
so the curator's credit is not acceptance of a claim.
