**Claim.** There is a graph $X$ with chromatic number $\aleph_1$ such that
no uncountable set of its vertices induces an infinitely connected subgraph,
where a graph is infinitely connected when any two of its vertices are
joined by infinitely many pairwise disjoint paths. Since a graph of
chromatic number $\aleph_1$ is uncountable, $X$ has no infinitely connected
subgraph of chromatic number $\aleph_1$, and the question of
[[problems/set_theory/E1067/_index|Problem 1067]] has a negative answer in
ZFC alone. This is Theorem 3.5 of arXiv:1409.2922v1, posted 2014-09-09,
published as Dániel T. Soukup, Trees, ladders and graphs, J.
Combin. Theory Ser. B 115 (2015), 96--116; Theorem 4.3 of the same version
strengthens the construction so that any two incomparable vertices of the
underlying tree are separated by a finite set, so every uncountable vertex
set contains two vertices joined by only finitely many disjoint paths. The
[[../library/set_theory/soukup_2015_trees_ladders_graphs/_index|source card]]
records the statements.

**Argument, in outline.** For a stationary costationary set
$S\subseteq\omega_1$, the tree $T=T(S)$ of the paper's Section 2 has no
uncountable chains and no branching at limit levels, and $G(T)$ is its
comparability graph. A ladder system on $T$ assigns to each vertex $t$ a
set $C_t$ of predecessors of $t$ that is either finite or a cofinal sequence
of order type $\omega$ (Definition 3.1), and defines the subgraph
$X_{\underline{C}}$ of $G(T)$ whose edges join each $t$ to the members of
$C_t$. The system is transitive when $C_t\cap s^\downarrow\subseteq C_s$ for
every $s\in C_t$ (Definition 3.2), so that each $C_t$ spans a complete
subgraph; a path in $X_{\underline{C}}$ then contains a path that is the
union of two monotone paths (Lemma 3.3). The separation property comes from
transitivity and the two tree properties (Lemma 3.4): two incomparable
vertices $s,t$ of an uncountable set $A$ have a greatest common initial
part $r$, because $T$ does not branch at limits, and a suitable $s'\in A$
with $r<s'\leq s$ is separated from $t$ within $A$ by
$\{r\}\cup(r^\downarrow\cap C_{s'})$, a finite set because $C_{s'}$ has order
type at most $\omega$ and only its part below $r$ enters. The uncountable
chromatic number is not a consequence of the tree alone: the proof of
Theorem 3.5 builds a transitive ladder system by induction over the
accumulation points of $S$, choosing at each level $\delta\in S$ the ladders
of continuum many vertices against an enumeration of the countable colored
subsets of the levels below, and an elementary-submodel argument (Claim
3.5.1) then shows that every coloring of $X_{\underline{C}}$ by $\omega$
colors gives an edge with both ends of one color. The proof was not
reconstructed here.

**Context.** Erdős and Hajnal asked in 1966, for graphs with $\aleph_1$
vertices and chromatic number $\aleph_1$, whether an infinitely connected
subgraph of chromatic number $\aleph_1$ must exist
([[../library/set_theory/erdos_1966_chromatic_number_graphs_set_systems/_index|source card]]),
and in 1985 (Discrete Math. 53 (1985), 281--285) asked the question as the
problem states it; Komjáth showed the 1966 version independent of ZFC.
[[problems/set_theory/E1067/claims/2013_01_03_komjath|Komjáth]], A note on
chromatic number and connectivity of infinite graphs, Israel J. Math. 196
(2013), 499--506, showed earlier that a negative answer to the 1985 question
is consistent with ZFC, by forcing a graph of size and chromatic number
$\aleph_1$ with no uncountable infinitely connected subgraph (correcting the
forcing of Komjáth's 1988 paper). Soukup's theorem needs neither forcing nor
extra axioms. A later, shorter construction is
[[problems/set_theory/E1067/claims/2024_02_08_bowler_pitz|Bowler and Pitz's example]],
which is independent of this one.

**Acceptance.** The result appeared in a refereed journal, the *Journal of
Combinatorial Theory, Series B*, in 2015, the `refereed` evidence; the text
cited here is the arXiv preprint (v1), not compared with the published
version. The site's curator, Thomas Bloom, marks the problem DISPROVED (LEAN)
and records in the commentary that Soukup constructed a counterexample using
no extra set-theoretic assumptions: that curator credit is the `reviewed`
evidence. The Lean development recorded on the Bowler and Pitz page
formalizes their example, not this construction; neither the site's thread
nor the formal-conjectures file at its revision of 2026-10-07 names a
formalization of this construction, and this project has built or audited
none.
