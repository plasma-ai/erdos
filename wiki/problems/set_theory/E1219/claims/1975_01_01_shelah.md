**Claim.** [[problems/set_theory/E1219/_index|Problem 1219]] asks whether
$\sum_k 2^{\aleph_{n_k}}\to(\aleph_\omega)^2$, with two colors, for an
increasing sequence $(n_k)$ of integers such that $2^{\aleph_{n_k}}$ is
strictly increasing and $2^{\aleph_{n_0}}>\aleph_\omega$. Shelah's Corollary
1.3 (p. 1260) states: if
$\aleph_\omega<2^{\aleph_{n(0)}}<2^{\aleph_{n(1)}}<\cdots$ then

$$
\sum_{n<\omega}2^{\aleph_n}\to(\aleph_\omega,\aleph_\omega)^2 ,
$$

and the Remark after it records that this answers Problem 3 of the 1971
Erdős--Hajnal list. The two sums are the same cardinal: a countable sum of
infinite cardinals is its supremum, $n\mapsto2^{\aleph_n}$ is nondecreasing
and $n_k\to\infty$, so $\sum_{n<\omega}2^{\aleph_n}=\sup_k2^{\aleph_{n_k}}
=\sum_k2^{\aleph_{n_k}}$, and the relation is the catalog's. The corollary is
the case $\lambda=\aleph_\omega$, $\kappa=\omega$ of Theorem 1.2 (p. 1260):
if $\kappa\to(\kappa)^2_2$, $\kappa=\operatorname{cf}\lambda$, and
$\langle2^\mu:\mu<\lambda\rangle$ is not eventually constant but eventually
at least $\lambda$, then $\sum_{\mu<\lambda}2^\mu\to(\lambda)^2_2$, indeed
$\to(\lambda,\lambda,\omega)^2$; here $\kappa\to(\kappa)^2_2$ is Ramsey's
theorem. The proof takes half a page from the paper's Canonization Lemma 1.1
(pp. 1258--1260), which reduces a two-coloring of pairs on a union of blocks
$A_i$ of size $(2^{\mu(i)})^+$ to a coloring of pairs of indices, to which
Ramsey's theorem is applied. Section 0 (p. 1257) describes Problem 3 as the
only open case, for infinite cardinals, of $\lambda\to(\mu)^2_2$, so the
corollary completes that discussion; this answers the problem in the
affirmative.

**Misprint in Theorem 1.2.** The printed hypothesis says the powers are
eventually at least $\kappa$, a misprint for $\lambda$: the proof chooses
$\mu(i)$ with $2^{\mu(i)}\ge\lambda$, and as printed the theorem would assert
$\aleph_\omega\to(\aleph_\omega)^2_2$ whenever $2^{\aleph_n}=\aleph_{n+1}$
for all $n$, which fails for every singular cardinal. Corollary 1.3 carries
the catalog's hypothesis $2^{\aleph_{n(0)}}>\aleph_\omega$ explicitly, so
the claim does not depend on this reading. Hajnal's stronger conjecture
$\sum_k2^{\aleph_{n_k}}\to(\aleph_\omega,4)^3$ (Conjecture 1A, p. 1261) is a
separate question, recorded as still unproven by Komjáth (2025), and is not
part of this claim.
