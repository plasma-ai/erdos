***

**Claim.** Let $F(k)$ be the number of $k$-element sets of positive integers
whose reciprocals sum to $1$, each counted once (the increasing $k$-tuples
$1\le n_1<\cdots<n_k$ with $1/n_1+\cdots+1/n_k=1$). The manuscript *Short
Egyptian fractions* of the OpenAI mathematics release (OpenAI, 25 September
2026; the release attributes it to an internal OpenAI model and names no
individual author, and its README says that its manuscripts were produced
by an internal OpenAI model and are at different stages of verification)
states as its Corollary 1.2 that there are absolute
constants $c,C>0$ and $k_0$ with

$$
ck\ \le\ \log\log F(k)\ \le\ Ck
\qquad\text{for every } k\ge k_0 ,
$$

that is, $F(k)=\exp(\exp(\Theta(k)))$. The lower half is the new content: it
removes the $1/\log k$ from the recorded lower bound $\exp(\exp(ck/\log k))$
of Konyagin and of Elsholtz. The upper half was known, and the manuscript's own
explicit bound $F(k)\le k^{2^k-1}$ is weaker than the Elsholtz–Planitzer bound
the problem page records. The constants are not made explicit. The route: the
manuscript's Theorem 1.1 (the accepted claim of
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0304/_index|Problem 304]]) applied to $(Q-1)/Q$, for
$Q$ the product of the first $r$ odd primes, gives a distinct expansion of $1$
of length $O(\log r)$ containing a denominator with at least $2^r$ divisors;
splitting that denominator over its proper divisors gives about $2^r$ distinct
expansions of one length, and an injective padding of the largest denominator
reaches every larger length. The statement, its locators and a structural
reading of the proof are on
[[../library/unit_fractions/openai_2026_short_egyptian_fractions/corollary_1_2|the result page of the source card]],
whose card
[[../library/unit_fractions/openai_2026_short_egyptian_fractions/_index|records the release's provenance and attestations]];
the prose proof is recorded there at the level of its structure, with its
steps unverified.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0304/claims/2026_09_25_openai|The release's Theorem 1.1]]
supplies the short expansion of $(Q-1)/Q$; in the Lean development
`counting_double_log_order` is proved from `main_double_log_order`, the
declaration that page records.

**Covers.** The double-exponential order of $F(k)$, the number of $k$-element
sets of positive integers with reciprocal sum $1$: there are $c,C>0$ with
$ck\le\log\log F(k)\le Ck$ for all large $k$, so $F(k)=\exp(\exp(\Theta(k)))$.
This replaces the recorded lower bound $\exp(\exp(c'k/\log k))$; the upper half
was already known (Elsholtz–Planitzer). Not settled: an asymptotic formula,
$F(k)$ up to constant factors, or the constant in $\log\log F(k)$ (the
monograph's guess $c_0^{2^{k(1-\varepsilon)}}$).

**Acceptance.** The evidence is `formalized`. The release's Lean development at
the pinned revision declares, in
`lean/OAI/NumberTheory/EgyptianFractions/Main.lean`, the theorem
`OAI.Problem337.counting_double_log_order`, which states
$ck\le\log\log F(k)\le Ck$ with the quantifiers
$\exists c,C>0\ \exists k_0\ \forall k\ge k_0$, and the theorem
`OAI.Problem337.one_expansions_finite_and_bounded`, which proves for every
$k$ that the set of expansions is finite with $F(k)\le k^{2^k-1}$ and that
every denominator of a $k$-term expansion of $1$ satisfies $n_i\le k^{2^{i-1}}$.
Here `OneExpansions k` is the set of strictly increasing tuples `Fin k → ℕ`
with entries at least $1$ and reciprocal sum exactly $1$, which correspond one
to one with the $k$-element sets the problem counts, and `F k` is its `ncard`;
the finiteness statement excludes the junk value $0$ of `ncard` on an infinite
set, and the lower bound $ck\le\log\log F(k)$ with $ck>0$ excludes it a second
time since `Real.log 0 = 0`. The corpus's verification built both declarations
and checked their axioms, which are `propext`, `Classical.choice` and
`Quot.sound` only, and the comparator challenge
`lean/ComparatorChallenges/EgyptianFractions.lean` of the release pins these
statements and definitions, with which the solution module's fingerprints were
identical. The namespace `Problem337` is the release's internal label and is
unrelated to catalog Problem 337.

The claim is partial because the problem asks for good estimates and the
declaration fixes only the order of the double logarithm: no asymptotic formula,
no estimate of $F(k)$ up to constant factors and no value of the constant in
$\log\log F(k)$ (the monograph's guess $c_0^{2^{k(1-\varepsilon)}}$ would mean a
slope tending to $\log2$) follows from it. No other acceptance evidence was
found on 2026-10-07: the manuscript was not refereed and had no arXiv version,
no outside review of it was recorded, and the site's page showed the label
OPEN (last edited 27 September 2025) with no proof claim on its tab on
2026-10-07.
