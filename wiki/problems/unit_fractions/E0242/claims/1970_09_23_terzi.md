***

**Claim.** Every prime $n$ coprime to $120120$ that is not congruent modulo
$120120$ to one of the $198$ residues of the paper's Table 2 satisfies
Rosati's condition (2) or (3), so that $4/n=1/x+1/y+1/z$ has a solution in
positive integers. This is the outcome of the first algorithm of D. G. Terzi,
*On a conjecture by Erdös-Straus*, BIT 11 (1971), 212--216, received
23 September 1970, the date this page carries: the abstract (p. 212) states
it for primes, and congruence (9) with
[[../library/unit_fractions/terzi_1971_conjecture_erdos_straus/table_2|Table 2]]
(p. 214) lists the $198$ classes left open. The paper allows repeated terms;
the Formulation of [[problems/unit_fractions/E0242/_index|Problem 242]]
records how a representation becomes one with three distinct terms.

**Covers.** The primes coprime to $120120$ outside the $198$ classes, and,
since a representation for a prime scales to one for every multiple of it,
every $n>2$ with such a prime factor. The page does not cover a composite
$n$ outside the $198$ classes whose prime factors all lie inside them: the
classes are not closed under multiplication, for example
$66049\cdot30241\equiv32449\pmod{120120}$ with $66049$ and $30241$ in Table 2
and $32449$ not. The site's commentary, which credits Terzi with every $n$
outside the classes, therefore says more than the paper's statement for
primes. Nor does the page cover the paper's verification of all $n\le10^8$
(p. 215), an author's report of a computation that [ElTa13] finds
incomplete and that the problem page records under Finite verification.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: BIT 11 (1971). The site labels the problem
FALSIFIABLE, an open problem, so its commentary's credit to Terzi is no
`reviewed` evidence. The statement and Table 2 are recorded on the library's
result page; the algorithm is recorded in outline only, and the proof is not
independently reviewed.
