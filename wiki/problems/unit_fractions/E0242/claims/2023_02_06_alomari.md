***

**Claim.** M. W. Alomari's note *A simple direct proof of the Erdős–Straus
conjecture* (Authorea, 6 February 2023, v2 21 February 2023; OSF,
10 February 2023; Research Square, 15 February 2023) claims that $4/n$ is a
sum of three unit fractions for every $n\ge2$, and that Sierpiński's $5/n$
conjecture follows the same way. Through the Formulation of
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0242/_index|Problem 242]] this is a claim of the
whole problem.

**Depends on.** No page of this wiki.

**Why it is rejected.** Section 2 of
[Research Square v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-2579186/v1) (printed
p. 2) argues by contradiction with a least upper bound. Three steps fail.

1. *Wrong quantifier.* The set
   $A=\{n(xy+xz+yz)\}$ ranges over $n\ge2$ as well as $x,y,z$, and the
   proof ends with existence "for some" $n\ge2$. That is witnessed by $n=4$,
   $(x,y,z)=(2,3,6)$ and is not the conjecture, which fixes $n$ first.
2. *No supremum.* The proof sets $\sup A=\alpha=4xyz$, an expression in
   the set's own variables. $A$ is unbounded ($n=2$, $x=y=z=t$ gives
   $6t^2$), and failure of equality does not put every triple on one side
   of it.
3. *The construction overshoots.* The proof needs an element of $A$ strictly
   between $\alpha-(xy+xz+yz)$ and $\alpha$. With $x=m$, $y=m+p$, $z=m+q$
   and $S=mq+pm+pq$ its candidate is $(4m^3+4m)S$, which exceeds
   $\alpha=4m(m+p)(m+q)$: for $m=p=1$, $q=2$ it is $40>24$.

The note says nothing in the argument is special to the numerator $4$. Applied
with the universal quantifier to numerator $8$, it would represent $8/11$,
$8/17$, $8/131$ and $8/241$, none of which is a sum of three unit fractions
([Pomerance–Weingartner, arXiv:2511.16817v2, Table 1, p. 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.16817v2#page=13);
[[../library/unit_fractions/pomerance_2025_exceptions_erdos_straus_schinzel/_index|source card]]).

**Standing.** Rejected: **proof defective; statement unresolved**. No
counterexample to Problem 242 and no verification tier are asserted.

**Read depth.** Section 2 and the closing discussion of Research Square v1
(printed pp. 1–2; closing discussion p. 3), checked against the PDF. Authorea v2 was not examined and is not compared.
