# problems/unit_fractions/E0242/claims

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0242/_index|..]]

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0242/claims/1950_01_01_oblath|1950_01_01_oblath]]: Obláth (Mathesis 59 (1950)) proves that $4/n$ is a sum of three unit fractions
whenever $n+1$ has a prime factor congruent to $3$ modulo $4$, a set of
density one. The paper is refereed, and the result is known second-hand.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0242/claims/1970_09_23_terzi|1970_09_23_terzi]]: Terzi (BIT 11 (1971)) proves that $4/n$ is a sum of three unit fractions for
every prime $n$ coprime to $120120$ outside $198$ listed residue classes, and
so for every multiple of such a prime. The paper is refereed.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0242/claims/2023_02_06_alomari|2023_02_06_alomari]]: Rejected: the supremum argument never fixes $n$, its set has no supremum,
and its key construction overshoots the bound it needs. Interpreted as a
proof for every denominator, its extension to arbitrary numerators would
contradict known exceptions for $8/n$. The conjecture remains unresolved.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0242/claims/2025_08_29_mihnea_dumitru|2025_08_29_mihnea_dumitru]]: An arXiv computation report of August 2025 by Mihnea and Dumitru states that
the conjecture holds for every prime up to $10^{18}$, hence for every $n$ up
to $10^{18}$. It is unrefereed, and the computation has not been rerun.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0242/claims/2025_11_07_dyachenko|2025_11_07_dyachenko]]: Rejected: the lattice lemma behind the claimed unconditional step is false
as stated, the lattice step does not establish a point satisfying the
quadratic constraint a solution needs, and the covering route assumes its
covering. The paper's conclusion says the infinite case is not proven constructively
and its covering sections are conditional. The conjecture remains unresolved.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0242/claims/2026_02_12_bradford|2026_02_12_bradford]]: Rejected: Lemmas 1–2 are correct identities on congruence classes of
primes, but the paper does not show that the classes cover all primes, and
its listed classes miss primes in each of Mordell's six residual classes.
The conjecture remains unresolved.
