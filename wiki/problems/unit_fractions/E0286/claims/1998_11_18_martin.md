**Claim.** For every sufficiently large $k$ there are integers
$n_1<\cdots<n_k$ in an interval of width $(e-1+o(1))k$ with
$1=1/n_1+\cdots+1/n_k$, which answers the question of
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0286/_index|Problem 286]] as the site states it.
The source is Martin's
[[../library/unit_fractions/martin_2000_denser_egyptian_fractions/theorem_2|Theorem 2]]
(Denser Egyptian fractions, Acta Arith. 95 (2000), no. 3, 231--260;
arXiv:math/9811112, 18 November 1998): for every positive rational $r$ and
all $t\ge t_0(r)$ the least largest denominator in a $t$-term representation
of $r$ by distinct unit fractions is
$M_t(r)=t/(1-e^{-r})+O_r(t\log\log3t/\log3t)$. At $r=1$ this gives, for
every $k\ge3$, a $k$-term representation of $1$ whose denominators all lie
in $[2,M_k(1)]$ with $M_k(1)=(e/(e-1)+o(1))k$. Since
$e/(e-1)\approx1.582<e-1\approx1.718$, the interval $[2,M_k(1)]$ has width
below $(e-1)k$ for every large $k$, so the required interval exists with
$o(1)=0$. This one-line deduction is the corpus's own; Martin's paper states
Theorem 2 as the resolution of
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0285/_index|Problem 285]]. It discusses the width
only as the least width $M'_t(r)$ of a $t$-term representation (pp. 2--3):
Croot's result gives $M'_t(r)=t+O_r(t\log\log t/\log t)$ for infinitely many
$t$, and no analogue of Theorem 2 valid for all $t$ is obtained for it. The
paper does not draw the deduction above.

**Readings.** The site's question asks for an interval of width
$(e-1+o(1))k$ containing the denominators, and the deduction answers it. The
1980 monograph, p. 33, printed the question as an equality for the least
width, $\min\{x_n-x_1\}=(e-1)n+o(n)$; under that reading the same bound
shows that the least width is at most $M_k(1)-2=(e/(e-1)+o(1))k$, below
$(e-1)k$, so the printed equality is false for large $k$, as Croot's
introduction notes, and Croot's own form of the question asks whether the
least width is $\sim k$. The problem page's Formulation records the
difference; this page answers the site's wording.

**Acceptance.** The paper is published in Acta Arithmetica, a refereed
journal (Crossref record of DOI 10.4064/aa-95-3-231-260), which is the
`refereed` evidence. The site's curator credits the problem to Croot, not
to Martin, so no `reviewed` evidence is listed for this page. Proof
coverage: as on the Problem 285 claim page, the reduction of Theorem 2 to
Propositions 5 and 6 is recorded on the theorem page, and the proofs of the
propositions have not been checked.

**Formalization.** The `formalization` link is a public Lean 4 proof of the
every-large-$k$ statement in Boris Alexeev's lean-proofs repository (file
of 2026-08-17, pinned to the commit of 2026-09-15), whose header names
Croot and Martin as the informal authors and Codex and GPT-5.6 Sol as the
formal authors. Its theorem `erdos_286` takes the route above: it imports
the repository's formalization of Martin's upper bound and uses the
inequality $e/(e-1)<e-1$ (`densityConstant_lt_exp_sub_one`) to place the
denominators in $[1,1+(e-1)k]$, with the error function identically zero;
the file contains no `sorry`. formal-conjectures added
`ErdosProblems/286.lean` on 2026-09-20 with this file as its
`formal_proof`, and the community database records the problem as
formalized since that date. The corpus has not built or audited the
development, so the claim lists no `formalized` evidence.
