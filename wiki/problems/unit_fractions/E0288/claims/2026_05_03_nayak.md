**Claim.** For an interval $[u,v]$ of positive integers write
$H([u,v])=\sum_{n=u}^v1/n$. Theorem 3.2 of the note states that if two
intervals $I,J$ intersect and $H(I)+H(J)$ is an integer, then, up to
swapping them, the pair is one of $([1,1],[1,1])$, $([1,2],[1,2])$,
$([1,2],[2,2])$ and $([2,2],[2,2])$, and that all four pairs have an integer
sum. So, in the formulation of
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0288/_index|Problem 288]], which lets the two
intervals overlap or coincide, the pairs with intersecting intervals are
finitely many. The note's argument: the union of two intersecting intervals
is an interval $[r,s]$ on which the sum has coefficients $1$ or $2$; when
$s\ge3$ and the union has at least two elements, Bertrand's postulate or the
Sylvester--Schur theorem gives an odd prime dividing exactly one denominator
in $[r,s]$ (its Lemma 3.1), whose term is then the unique term of lowest
$p$-adic valuation, so the sum is not an integer; when the union is a single
element $\{r\}$, both intervals are $[r,r]$ and the sum $2/r$ is an integer
only for $r=1,2$; the remaining unions inside $\{1,2\}$ are checked
directly. The note's Sections 4 to 18 treat the disjoint case, with
intervals $[a,b]$ and $[c,d]$, $b<c$: every disjoint
solution has upper length $d-c+1<b$, in fact $o(b)$ along any infinite
family, and every denominator $c,\ldots,d$ divides
$\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,b)$; mirror congruences for large prime powers
and a lower bound for the denominator of $H([c,d])$ follow, and for fixed
upper length $L=2$ (its Section 14) a solution must have $c=P_0u_0$,
$c+1=P_1u_1$ with
$P_0,P_1$ carrying almost all of the large primes and $u_0,u_1$ small smooth
cofactors. The note states that it does not claim the disjoint case.

> inequalities, and CRT/smoothness conditions.
>
> For example, for any disjoint solution with intervals $[a,b]$ and $[c,d]$, the
> upper denominators must divide
> $$
> \Lambda_b = \operatorname{lcm}(1,2,\ldots,b).
> $$
> In the sufficiently large fixed length case, the remaining problem is reduced
> to a pretty rigid smooth CRT condition. In the special case $L=2$, this gives
> an obstruction of the form
> $$
> c = P_0u_0,\qquad c+1 = P_1u_1,\qquad P_1u_1-P_0u_0=1,
> $$
> where the $P_i$ contain most of the large prime factors, while the $u_i$ are
> small smooth cofactors.
>
> I would really appreciate any comments, especially on whether the $L=2$
> obstruction can be pushed further.

**Covers.** The pairs of intervals that intersect: among them exactly four,
all inside $\{1,2\}$, have an integer sum. Not covered: the disjoint pairs,
for which the note proves necessary conditions and no finiteness; the
problem's statement, and its singleton case with the singleton outside the
other interval, remain open.

**Standing.** Claimed. The note, "A Research Note on Harmonic Sums over Two
Integer Intervals: Intersecting intervals and reductions in the disjoint
case", dated May 2026 and written, by its title page, with assistance from
GPT-5.5 Thinking, was announced in the problem's discussion thread on 3 May
2026 by its author, who is the claimant; it is a PDF on Google Drive with no
arXiv or journal record. It is not on the site's proof-claim tab, which is
empty, and the site's label is OPEN, so no curator,
referee or named mathematician has accepted it. Two replies of the same day
suggest a compression of the length-$2$ condition and report that an
automated check flagged one minor issue in the note, without saying which.
The statement of Theorem 3.2 and the structure of its proof are compiled
above; the corpus records no check of the proof.
