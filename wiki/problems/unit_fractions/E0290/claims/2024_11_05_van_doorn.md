**Claim.** Write $\sum_{a\le n\le b}1/n=u_{a,b}/v_{a,b}$ in lowest terms. For
every integer $a\ge1$ there is $b>a$ with $v_{a,b+1}<v_{a,b}$, and the least
such $b$ is at most linear in $a$. In van Doorn's convention, where $b(a)$ is
the least $b>a$ with $v_{a,b}<v_{a,b-1}$ (one more than the site's $b(a)$),
van Doorn's Corollary 1 gives $b(a)\le6(a-1)$ for all $a>1$, from the $3$-adic
valuation of the block ending at $2\cdot3^{k+1}$ when $3^k<a\le3^{k+1}$, and
Theorem 2 gives $b(a)\le4.374(a-1)$ for all $a\ge6$. In the other
direction Theorem 6 gives $\liminf_{a\to\infty}(b(a)-a)/\log a\ge1/2$ for
every periodic integer numerator sequence, and Theorem 8 places the limit
inferior for the classical case strictly between $0.54$ and $0.61$. So the
answer to the existence question is yes, and $b(a)$ grows linearly:
$a+0.54\log a<b(a)<4.374a$ for all large $a$. The problem asks a yes-or-no
question and a growth question; the paper answers the first with a proof and
the second with a two-sided estimate, so the value is solved. The same paper
settles the existence question for every periodic numerator sequence that is
not identically zero (Corollary 2 and Theorem 5); the case $a=1$ had been
settled earlier by Shiu
([[../library/unit_fractions/shiu_2016_denominators_harmonic_numbers_revised/_index|card]]).

**Acceptance.** The site's curator, Thomas Bloom, labels the problem proved
and credits its resolution to this paper on the problem page (last edited 28
December 2025); the curator is not an author, and that credit is the
`reviewed` evidence. The paper has no journal version (the arXiv record and a
Crossref query,), so `refereed` is not listed. Three Lean
files in the author's repository accompany the paper and are linked above as
formalizations of its results: `ErdosProblem290.lean`, written by van Doorn
with the prover Aristotle and finished and cleaned up by Boris Alexeev,
proves that for every $a>0$ some $b$ with $a<b\le6a$ has
$v_{a,b}<v_{a,b-1}$, without `sorry`, its closing comment listing the axioms
`propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`; `ErdosProblem290lower.lean`
proves, without `sorry` and with no declared axiom, that for all large $a$ the
denominator does not drop at any $b<a+\log a/20$, a weaker constant than
Theorem 6's $1/2$; and `ErdosProblem290lowertight.lean` proves a converse for
a periodic numerator sequence of period $t$, infinitely many drops at some
$b<a+(1+\varepsilon)t(t+1)\varphi(t)\log a$, under one declared axiom that the
author says follows from the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions.
None of these files was built or audited by this corpus, so `formalized` is
not listed; the formal-conjectures file for the problem is a statement with a
`sorry` body and is not a formalization. The finer growth of $b(a)-a$ is the
subject of the author's two later partial claims,
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0290/claims/2026_08_31_van_doorn|the exact lower limit]]
and
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0290/claims/2026_09_24_van_doorn|the almost-all lower bound]].
