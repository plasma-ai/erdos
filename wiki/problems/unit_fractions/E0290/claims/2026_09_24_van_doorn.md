**Claim.** With $b(a)$ the least $b>a$ at which the lowest-terms denominator
$v_{a,b}$ of $\sum_{a\le n\le b}1/n$ satisfies $v_{a,b}<v_{a,b-1}$ (the
author's convention, one more than the site's), Theorem 1 of the note states
that for almost all $a$

$$
b(a)>a+\exp\Bigl(\tfrac12\sqrt{\log a\log\log a}\Bigr),
$$

so that for every fixed $C$ the set of $a$ with $b(a)\le a+(\log a)^C$ has
density zero. The proof rests on the note's Lemma 2: with $L_n$ the least
common multiple of $1,\ldots,n$, $v_{a,b}/v_{a,b-1}\ge b/\gcd(b,L_{b-a})^2$,
so a drop at $b$ forces $\gcd(b,L_{b-a})>\sqrt b$; for each shift $n$ up to
the exponential the integers $a\in[x,2x)$ with a drop at $a+n$ are counted
through the divisors $d>\sqrt x$ of $L_n$ by Rankin's trick and a weak form of
the prime number theorem, and the count summed over $n$ is $o(x)$.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Wouter van
Doorn (account Woett) on 24 September 2026, giving "GPT-5.6 Sol" as the AI used:

> We prove that for almost all $a$ we have
> $$
> b(a) > a + \exp\left(\frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\log a \log \log a} \right).
> $$
> In particular, contrary to some earlier speculation, for all $C$ we have that
> the set of $a$ for which $b(a) \le a + (\log a)^C$ holds has density $0$. I
> still believe that $b(a) \le a + O_{\varepsilon}(a^{\varepsilon})$ holds for
> all $\varepsilon > 0$. Let $L_n$ be the least common multiple of the first $n$
> positive integers. The proof of the lower bound rests on the observation that,
> if the denominator of the sum decreases at $b$, then $\gcd(b, L_{b-a}) >
> \sqrt{b}$. For a large $x$ and a given $n \le \exp\left(\frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\log
> 2x \log \log 2x} \right)$, we then count the number of possible values of $a
> \in [x, 2x)$ for which $b := a + n$ has this property that $\gcd(b, L_n) >
> \sqrt{b}$. Applying Rankin's trick, PNT and then summing over all possible
> $n$, we find our zero density result after some algebra.

**Covers.** The size of $b(a)-a$ for a density-one set of $a$. It leaves the
existence question to the 2024 paper and the limit inferior to the 2026
preprint, and gives no upper bound; the author conjectures in the note that
$b(a)<a+a^{\varepsilon}$ for every $\varepsilon>0$ and all large $a$. The
result is a proved lower bound on a density-one set, so the value is proved;
it does not determine how $b(a)$ grows.

**Standing.** A four-page note in the author's GitHub repository
`Woett/Mathematical-shorts` (uploaded 24 September 2026), not posted to
arXiv, with no journal record and no independent review; it is not held in
the library; its statements are compiled from the posted PDF and its proof
is not checked, so the claim is pending. The author filed it on the site's
proof-claim tab the same day, without a scope label, naming the system
GPT-5.6 Sol; the note's declaration of AI usage credits ChatGPT 5.6-Sol Pro
with the proof of the main result and points to the machine write-up, "A
density-one lower bound for the first decrease of a harmonic denominator",
in the author's repository linked above. A thread comment of the same day
states the result as holding for infinitely many $a$, weaker than the note's
almost all.
