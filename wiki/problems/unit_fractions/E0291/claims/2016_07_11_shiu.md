**Claim.** Write $H_n=\sum_{k\le n}1/k=c_n/d_n$ in lowest terms and
$D_n=\mathrm{lcm}(1,\ldots,n)=d_nq_n$; in the notation of
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0291/_index|Problem 291]], $q_n=(a_n,L_n)$. Shiu's
[[../library/unit_fractions/shiu_2016_denominators_harmonic_numbers_revised/theorem_1|Theorem 1]](iii)
states that $d_n<d_{n-1}$ for infinitely many $n$; since
$d_{n-1}\le D_{n-1}\le D_n$, every such $n$ has $q_n>1$. Shiu's
[[../library/unit_fractions/shiu_2016_denominators_harmonic_numbers_revised/theorem_2|Theorem 2]]
is the exact criterion behind this: for an odd prime $p\le n$, $p\mid q_n$ if
and only if the leading digit $m$ of $n$ in base $p$ satisfies $p\mid c_m$.
Pairing $1/j$ with $1/(p-j)$ shows $p\mid c_{p-1}$ for every odd prime, so
every $n\ge p$ whose leading digit in base $p$ is $p-1$ has $p\mid q_n$; for
$p=3$ these are the $n$ with $2\cdot3^a\le n<3^{a+1}$, $a\ge1$, a set of
positive lower density. Hence $(a_n,L_n)>1$ for infinitely many $n$: the second
question of the problem is answered in the affirmative.

**Covers.** The second question, that $(a_n,L_n)>1$ occurs for infinitely
many $n$, together with the criterion that says for which $n$ a given odd
prime divides $(a_n,L_n)$. Not covered: the first question, whether
$(a_n,L_n)=1$ occurs for infinitely many $n$, which the same paper states as
a conjecture (with the count of such $n\le x$ of order $x/\log x$) and does
not prove.

problem's commentary that the second question is answered affirmatively and
states the leading-digit criterion as a necessary and sufficient condition;
Bloom credits the base-$3$ case to Stefan Steinerberger and cites Shiu's
preprint only for the heuristic count of the complementary set, so the
curator's credit goes to Steinerberger's observation, which has its own
pending page,

disclosed on that page, and the attribution to Shiu rests on the paper
itself. The paper is an unrefereed preprint
(arXiv:1607.02863, v1 of 11 July 2016, v2 of 30 July 2024; no journal record
