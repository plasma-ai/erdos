# problems/unit_fractions/E0291/claims

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0291/_index|..]]

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0291/claims/2016_07_11_shiu|2016_07_11_shiu]]: Shiu's 2016 preprint proves that the reduced denominator of the n-th
harmonic number is smaller than lcm(1, ..., n) for infinitely many n, with
the exact prime-by-prime criterion; the second question is answered yes.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0291/claims/2022_01_26_wu_yan|2022_01_26_wu_yan]]: Wu and Yan's 2022 paper proves, assuming that the numbers 1/log q over
distinct primes q are linearly independent over Q, that (a_n, L_n) > 1 on a
set of upper density 1; the hypothesis is open, so the page derives nothing.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0291/claims/2024_11_07_steinerberger|2024_11_07_steinerberger]]: Steinerberger's observation, recorded in the site's commentary, that 3 divides
(a_n, L_n) whenever the leading digit of n in base 3 is 2; it answers the
second question of Problem 291, that (a_n, L_n) > 1 infinitely often, yes.

[[problems/unit_fractions/E0291/claims/2026_02_05_van_doorn|2026_02_05_van_doorn]]: Van Doorn's note of February 2026 proves that for every periodic sequence of
non-zero numerators the numerator over the lcm shares a factor with it
infinitely often, with a Lean proof by Aristotle; it covers the second question.
