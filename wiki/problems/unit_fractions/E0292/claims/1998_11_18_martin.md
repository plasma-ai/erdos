**Claim.** Let $A$ be the set of integers $n$ for which some representation
$1=\sum_{i=1}^k1/m_i$ with $1\le m_1<\cdots<m_k=n$ exists, and
$B=\mathbb N\setminus A$. Martin's Theorem 4 states that for every positive
rational $r$ the set $\mathcal L_1(r)$ of integers $x>r^{-1}$ that cannot be
the largest denominator of an Egyptian fraction representation of $r$ has
density zero, and that for $x\ge3$ its counting function satisfies

$$
\frac{x\log\log x}{\log x}\ll_r L_1(r;x)\ll_r\frac{x\log\log x}{\log x}.
$$

At $r=1$ the set $\mathcal L_1(1)$ is $B$ (the integer $1$ lies in $A$ by the
one-term representation), so $|B\cap[1,x]|\asymp x\log\log x/\log x$ and $A$
has density $1$: the answer to the problem's question is yes. The proof also
describes $B$: for large $x$, every $n\le x$ with a prime factor exceeding
$Cx/\log x$ lies in $B$, and every element of $B$ below $x$ is at most
$x/\log x$ or has a prime-power factor exceeding $x\log^{-24}x$, which is the
precise form of Martin's remark that the excluded integers are the tiny
multiples of prime powers.
