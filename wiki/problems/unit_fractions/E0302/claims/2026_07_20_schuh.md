***

**Claim.** Let $f(N)$ be the extremal function of
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0302/_index|Problem 302]], the largest size of a
set $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ with no three distinct elements $a,b,c$
satisfying $1/a=1/b+1/c$. The linked text states

$$
f(N)\le\Bigl(\frac{373}{420}+o(1)\Bigr)N\approx0.8881\,N .
$$

Its route, as it describes it: a five-element configuration carrying three
solutions is embedded in a tile $S$ of $23$ integers of the form $2^i3^j$
(excluding $1$); the omissions that a solution-free set must make from $S$
are catalogued at nine thresholds; the dilates $tS$ for $t=2^{6u}3^{4v}d$
with $(d,6)=1$ are pairwise disjoint, and such $t$ have density $12/35$;
summing the forced omissions over the dilates gives the bound. The text
compares its constant with $25/28$, which it calls the previously applicable
bound: the site's argument for
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0301/_index|Problem 301]] uses only the three
two-term relations inside $\{2,3,4,6,12\}$ and so holds for this problem
too; the claimed constant would improve both it and the $9/10$ the site
records here.

**Covers.** An upper bound for the estimate of $f(N)$. It does not bear on
the particular question, which
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0302/claims/2025_03_25_cambie|Cambie's construction]]
answers in the negative, and it bounds $\limsup f(N)/N$ above by $373/420$
without determining the constant.

proof-claim tab on 20 July 2026 by the account 15Redstones; the tab credits
