***

**Claim.** For integers $0<a<b$ let $N(a,b)$ be the least $n$ for which
$a/b=1/x_1+\cdots+1/x_n$ with integers $0<x_1<\cdots<x_n$, and let
$N(b)=\max_{1\le a<b}N(a,b)$, the function of
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0304/_index|Problem 304]]. Erdős's paper of 1950
proves two theorems about it. Theorem 1 (p. 195): there is a constant $c_1$
with

$$
N(a,b)<c_1\,\frac{\log b}{\log\log b}
$$

for every $0<a<b$; the proof gives $c_1=8$ for $b>4096$ (p. 202). Theorem 2
(p. 195): for every positive integer $b$,

$$
N(b-1,b)>\log\log b-1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\frac1{b-2}\sum_{a=1}^{b-2}N(a,b)>\tfrac12(\log\log b-1).
$$

Together, $\log\log b\ll N(b)\ll\log b/\log\log b$, the bounds the site's
commentary credits to the paper, and the average of $N(a,b)$ over $a$ is
$\gg\log\log b$. Theorem 1 writes $a/b$ over $n!$ with $(n-1)!<b\le n!$ and
uses that every integer below $n!$ is a sum of at most $n$ distinct divisors
of $n!$. Theorem 2 rests on the paper's Theorem 5, that in any representation
of $1$ by $n$ distinct unit fractions every denominator is below the $n$th
Sylvester number, so a representation of $1$ containing $1/b$ with $n$ terms
forces $\log\log b<n$. The statements, locators and proof sketches are paged
at
[[../library/unit_fractions/erdos_1950_az_egyenlet_egesz_szamu_megoldasairol_diophantine/theorem_1|Theorem 1]]
and
[[../library/unit_fractions/erdos_1950_az_egyenlet_egesz_szamu_megoldasairol_diophantine/theorem_2|Theorem 2]].

**Covers.** The bounds $\log\log b\ll N(b)\ll\log b/\log\log b$ and the
average bound only. Not covered: the question whether $N(b)\ll\log\log b$,
which the paper states as probable (p. 195) and which
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0304/claims/2026_09_25_openai|the OpenAI release's accepted claim]]
answers. The upper bound was improved to $\ll\sqrt{\log b}$ by
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0304/claims/1985_01_01_vose|Vose]]; the lower
bound is also proved in Lean, by a different argument, on
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0304/claims/2026_09_01_thepriceisright|the Aristotle proof's page]].

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the claim rests on the cited paper.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: P. Erdős, *Az $1/x_1+\cdots+1/x_n=a/b$ egyenlet
egész számú megoldásairól*, Mat. Lapok 1 (1950), 192--210 (in Hungarian,
with an English summary on p. 210; MR 13,280b), a journal publication. The
site's commentary credits the bounds to the paper, but the site labels the
problem OPEN, so that credit is not `reviewed` evidence. The proofs are
summarized, not verified, on the library pages.
