***

**Claim.** Every rational $a/b$ with $0<a<b$ has a representation
$a/b=1/x_1+\cdots+1/x_n$ in integers $0<x_1<\cdots<x_n$ with
$n\ll\sqrt{\log b}$ terms; so $N(b)\ll\sqrt{\log b}$ for the function of
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0304/_index|Problem 304]], superseding the bound
$\log b/\log\log b$ of
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0304/claims/1950_01_01_erdos|Erdős's 1950 paper]].
As the review Zbl 0558.10015 (Ming-Chit Liu) describes the paper, Vose first
proves, by an argument of Erdős's 1950 paper, that there is an increasing
sequence $N_k$ of positive integers such that every integer $m$ with
$1<m<N_k$ is a sum of at most $O(\sqrt{\log N_{k-1}})$ distinct divisors of
$N_k$, and then derives the bound from it. Van Doorn and Tang restate the
theorem as their Lemma 2.2, with $N_K=4^{\alpha K^2}(p_1\cdots p_K)^2$ and
denominators dividing, or $b$ times divisors of, $N_K$
([[../library/unit_fractions/doorn_2025_smallest_denominator_not_contained_unit_fraction/section_3|Section 3]]),
and Liu and Sawhney restate it (arXiv:2404.07113v1, p. 3). The paper itself
is not held; the statement is taken from the review and these restatements.

**Covers.** The upper bound $N(b)\ll\sqrt{\log b}$ only. Not covered: the
question whether $N(b)\ll\log\log b$, which
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0304/claims/2026_09_25_openai|the OpenAI release's accepted claim]]
answers and which implies this bound.

**Depends on.** Nothing in this wiki; the claim rests on the cited paper.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: M. D. Vose, *Egyptian fractions*, Bull. London
Math. Soc. 17 (1985), no. 1, 21--24, doi:10.1112/blms/17.1.21, a journal
publication. The site's commentary credits the bound to the paper, but the
site labels the problem OPEN, so that credit is not `reviewed` evidence.
