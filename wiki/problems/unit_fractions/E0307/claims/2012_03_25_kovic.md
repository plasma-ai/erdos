***

**Claim.** For a solution $(P,Q)$ of
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0307/_index|Problem 307]], the products
$n=\prod P$ and $m=\prod Q$ satisfy $n'=m$ and $m'=n$, where $x\mapsto x'$
is the arithmetic derivative: for squarefree $n$, $n'=n\sum_{p\in P}1/p$,
which is the problem page's $N(P)=\prod Q$, and likewise $m'=N(Q)=\prod P$.
So a solution is a two-cycle of the arithmetic derivative that is not a
fixed point. Kovič (§3.2, pp. 7--9) proves the following of such cycles,
after recalling Ufnarovski and Åhlander's result (J. Integer Seq. 6 (2003))
that the two members are squarefree with disjoint sets of prime factors.

- Proposition 16 (p. 8): the system $n'=m$, $m'=n$ has no solution with
  both $m$ and $n$ products of two primes; so no solution has
  $|P|=|Q|=2$.
- A computer search (p. 8) found no solution of $x''=x$ with $x<10000$; the
  fixed points $p^p$, which also satisfy it, are excluded tacitly. So both
  products of a solution are at least $10^4$.
- Proposition 17 (p. 8): if $n'=m$, $m'=n$ and the smaller member $n<m$ is
  odd, then $n$ has at least nine prime factors and
  $n>3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11\cdot13\cdot17\cdot19\cdot23\cdot29$. So
  $2\notin P$ with $\prod P<\prod Q$ forces $|P|\ge9$.
- Proposition 19 (p. 9): if both members are odd, with $r_1$ and $s_1$ the
  numbers of prime factors of $n$ congruent to $1$ and to $-1$ modulo $4$,
  and $r_2$, $s_2$ the same for $m$, then
  $(r_1-s_1)(r_2-s_2)\equiv1\pmod 4$.

**Covers.** These necessary conditions, each of which refutes every
solution it excludes: a partial no to the existence question. Not covered:
whether a solution exists. Proposition 18 (p. 8), which asserts
$r+s\ge34$ for the numbers $r$ and $s$ of prime factors of $n$ and $m$
(with one member having at least $17$ prime factors), is not established
by its proof: the proof combines an upper bound on $m$ with a lower bound
on $n$ to get $p_1q_s<rs$, while the bounds
$rn/p_r<m<rn/p_1$ and $sm/q_s<n<sm/q_1$ give only $p_1q_1<rs<p_rq_s$, as
Bonfioli's manuscript shows (§14, p. 63;
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0307/claims/2026_06_13_bonfioli|its claim page]]).
The conclusion $r+s\ge34$ itself follows from Rosen's bound
$|P\cup Q|\ge59$, recorded on the problem page.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: J. Kovič, The arithmetic derivative and
antiderivative, J. Integer Seq. 15 (2012), Article 12.3.8; received 19 May
2011, revised 18 March 2012, published 25 March 2012 (the journal's
article page). The site labels the problem VERIFIABLE and does not cite
the paper, so no `reviewed` evidence is listed. This claim is partial, so
the problem stays open.
