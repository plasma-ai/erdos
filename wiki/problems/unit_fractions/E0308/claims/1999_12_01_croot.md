***

**Claim.** Let $n(N)$ be the largest integer such that every integer
$1\le n\le n(N)$ is a sum of distinct unit fractions with denominators at
most $N$, and $H_N=\sum_{n\le N}1/n$. Then, for all large $N$,

$$
\Bigl\lfloor H_N-\tfrac92(1+o(1))\tfrac{(\log\log N)^2}{\log N}\Bigr\rfloor
\le n(N)\le
\Bigl\lfloor H_N-\tfrac12(1+o(1))\tfrac{(\log\log N)^2}{\log N}\Bigr\rfloor.
$$

This is the Main Theorem of Croot's paper, on the library's
[[../library/unit_fractions/crootiii_1999_questions_erdos_graham_about_egyptian_fractions/main_theorem|result page]].
In the notation of [[problems/unit_fractions/E0308/_index|Problem 308]],
with $N(N)$ the set of representable positive integers, $f(N)$ the smallest
positive integer not in it and $m_N=\lfloor H_N\rfloor$: every element of
$N(N)$ is at most $H_N$, so $N(N)\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m_N\}$; the lower
floor gives $\{1,\ldots,m_N-1\}\subseteq N(N)$ for large $N$; and writing
$H_N=m_N+\delta_N$, the theorem puts $m_N$ in $N(N)$ when
$\delta_N>(\frac92+o(1))(\log\log N)^2/\log N$ and outside it when
$\delta_N<(\frac12+o(1))(\log\log N)^2/\log N$ (the paper's p. 2, on the
[[../library/unit_fractions/crootiii_1999_questions_erdos_graham_about_egyptian_fractions/conjecture_p2|conjecture page]]).
Hence for all large $N$ the set $N(N)$ is $\{1,\ldots,m_N-1\}$ or
$\{1,\ldots,m_N\}$ and $f(N)=n(N)+1\in\{m_N,m_N+1\}$.

**Covers.** Both questions of the problem's corrected Statement, for all
sufficiently large $N$: the smallest missing integer $f(N)$ is $m_N$ or
$m_N+1$, and the representable integers form an initial segment
$\{1,\ldots,m\}$, with $m\in\{m_N-1,m_N\}$. The case is decided by the
fractional part $\delta_N$ of $H_N$ whenever $\delta_N$ lies outside the
window between $(\frac12+o(1))(\log\log N)^2/\log N$ and
$(\frac92+o(1))(\log\log N)^2/\log N$. Not covered: the exact value of $f(N)$
inside that window, which Croot's conjecture that the upper floor is the truth
would decide, and the initial-segment property for $N$ below the theorem's
range; the problem page records both as the problem's variants.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0309/claims/1997_12_01_yokota|Yokota's 1997 theorem]]
supplies, in the proof of the lower bound, the representability of the
integers below a fixed bound (the paper's references [5] and [6]).

**Route.** Lower bound: starting from the full harmonic sum, remove as few
terms as possible to leave an integer, prime power by prime power from the
top, with the removed reciprocal mass controlled by a quantity built from
prime powers (Propositions 1 and 3); every integer between the value so
reached for a fixed $N_0$ and the value for $N$ is reached at some
intermediate denominator bound, and the integers below the fixed value are
representable by Yokota's 1997 theorem (the library's
[[../library/unit_fractions/yokota_1997_number_integers_representable_sum_unit_fractions_ii/theorem_1|Theorem 1]],
recorded at statement depth; its 1998 Corrigendum is not held). Upper
bound: a $p$-adic argument shows that no denominator in an integer
reciprocal sum has a prime factor above $N\log\log N/\log N$, and the
reciprocal mass of the integers with such a factor exceeds
$\frac12(1+o(1))(\log\log N)^2/\log N$.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Croot, III, Ernest S., On some questions of Erdős
and Graham about Egyptian fractions, Mathematika 46 (1999), no. 2,
359--372. The publisher's record dates the issue December 1999 and gives
no day; the day in this page's name is the first of that month. Reviewed:
the site's curator, Thomas Bloom, marks Problem 308 proved and credits
Croot's theorem as essentially solving it in the problem's commentary;
the site's displays attach the two floors to $f(N)$ where the theorem
bounds $n(N)=f(N)-1$, as the problem page records. The locators are pages
of the author's fourteen-page typescript, posted on the author's papers
page (the second link); the journal text has not been compared, the proof is
recorded in outline only, and no independent review of it is recorded in
this corpus.

**Formalization.** The file `src/latest/ErdosProblems/Erdos308.lean` in
Boris Alexeev's `lean-proofs` collection at the pinned commit (the third
link) declares itself a Lean formalization of a solution to Problem 308,
with Croot and Yokota as informal authors and Codex, GPT-5.6 Sol (OpenAI
Codex) as formal authors. Its theorem `erdos_308` states that for all
large $N$ the represented positive integers are exactly
$\{1,\ldots,m_N-1\}$ with first missing integer $m_N$, or exactly
$\{1,\ldots,m_N\}$ with first missing integer $m_N+1$; its
`eventually_represented_shape` is the two-set alternative and its
`eventually_interval_coverage` the lower floor's consequence that every
positive integer up to $m_N-1$ is representable. These are the statements
of the Covers paragraph, the two questions of the problem's corrected
Statement; the final theorem does not decide between
the two cases, and the two floors appear in the file as definitions
(`CrootIntervalStatement`, `CrootCardinalityBounds`) rather than as its
theorem, the proof resting on a companion module's construction that the
top file calls Croot's. The corpus did not build the file, and only its
top file is recorded, so no `formalized` evidence is listed; the
formal-conjectures statement file for the problem points at this file.
