***

**Claim.** Let $F(N)$ be the number of positive integers of the form
$1/n_1+\cdots+1/n_k$ with $1\le n_1<\cdots<n_k\le N$, the count of
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0309/_index|Problem 309]]. For all large $N$,

$$
\Bigl(1-\frac{5\log\log N}{\log N}\Bigr)\log N\ \le\ F(N)\ <\ \log N+1,
$$

that is, $F(N)\ge\log N-5\log\log N$, the upper bound being the trivial one.
Hence $F(N)\sim\log N$: the count is not $o(\log N)$, the second question is
answered no, and the first is answered to leading order. This is Theorem 1
of the paper, in the paper's notation $|N(n)|$, which counts the empty sum
$0$ and so exceeds $F(n)$ by one without affecting the asymptotic bounds,
and $\log_2$ for the iterated logarithm, on the library's
[[../library/unit_fractions/yokota_1997_number_integers_representable_sum_unit_fractions_ii/theorem_1|result page]].
The proof opens by stating more (p. 167): for large $N$ every positive
integer up to $\log N-5\log\log N$ is representable, so $N(N)$ contains that
initial segment; Croot's 1999 paper states this range citing the 1998
Corrigendum and uses the paper with its Corrigendum for the integers below a
fixed bound. The printed steps reach only the integers up to
$\log N-(3+o(1))\log N/\log\log N$ (see Route). The theorem sharpens
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0309/claims/1990_06_01_yokota|Yokota's 1990 bound]]
$F(N)\ge(\frac12-o(1))\log N$, the first disproof, which the paper's
introduction (p. 162) reports as having settled the question.

**Depends on.**
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0309/claims/1990_06_01_yokota|Yokota's 1990 theorem]]:
the paper's Lemma 4 (p. 164), the bound on the largest prime of the
divisor set used to represent $a$, is quoted as Theorem 1 of the 1990
paper without proof.

**Route.** For a large integer $a$, denominators are drawn from the
divisors of a product of prime powers $p^{2^i}$ and consecutive primes
chosen so that the reciprocal sum over the divisors up to a cutoff falls
just short of $a$; the deficit is a rational whose numerator is written,
through the paper's Lemma 5, as a sum of distinct divisors of the product,
which supplies the missing reciprocals with denominators at most
$a^4\exp[a(1+3/\log a)]$. The paper then says that this being at most $N$
implies $a\le\log N(1-5\log\log N/\log N)$ (p. 168), the converse of the
step the theorem needs; the bound is at most $N$ only for $a$ up to
$\log N-(3+o(1))\log N/\log\log N$, enough for $F(N)\sim\log N$, so the
answer no stands, but not for the stated error term $5\log\log N$ (the
bound itself also follows from Croot's Main Theorem; see Acceptance).

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Yokota, H., On number of integers representable
as a sum of unit fractions, II, J. Number Theory 67 (1997), no. 2,
162--169, with a Corrigendum, J. Number Theory 72 (1998), 150. The
publisher's record dates the issue December 1997 and gives no day; the day
in this page's name is the first of that month. Reviewed: the site's
curator, Thomas Bloom, marks Problem 309 disproved and credits this
paper's lower bound, $\log N-O(\log\log N)$, in the problem's commentary.
Theorem 1 and the opening of its proof are recorded at statement depth,
the rest of the proof in outline only; the Corrigendum is not held, so
what it corrects is not recorded. No independent review of the proof is
recorded in this corpus. The same conclusion follows from
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0309/claims/1999_12_01_croot|Croot's Main Theorem]]
and from
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0309/claims/2002_10_01_yokota|Yokota's 2002 Corollary 1]],
each on its own claim page.
