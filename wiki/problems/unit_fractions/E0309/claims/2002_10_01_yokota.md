***

**Claim.** Let $F(N)$ be the count of
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0309/_index|Problem 309]], the number of positive
integers that are sums of distinct unit fractions with denominators at
most $N$. For all large $N$,

$$
F(N)\ \ge\ \Bigl\lfloor H_N-\Bigl(\frac{\pi^2}3+o(1)\Bigr)\frac{(\log\log N)^2}{\log N}\Bigr\rfloor,\qquad H_N=\sum_{n\le N}\frac1n.
$$

The integer part is needed: every positive integer up to the bound inside
it is representable, by the deduction on the library's corollary page from
the paper's bound on the least $N$ at which a given integer becomes
representable. The right side is at least $\log N+\gamma-1-o(1)$, so the
count is not $o(\log N)$ and the second question is answered no. With
Croot's upper floor
$F(N)\le\lfloor H_N-(\frac12+o(1))(\log\log N)^2/\log N\rfloor$ (quoted in
the same corollary from Croot's paper, not reproved) and his
initial-segment description (his p. 2), for large $N$ the count is $m_N-1$
or $m_N$, where $m_N=\lfloor H_N\rfloor$. It is $m_N$ when the fractional
part of $H_N$ exceeds $(\frac{\pi^2}3+o(1))(\log\log N)^2/\log N$, $m_N-1$
when it is below $(\frac12+o(1))(\log\log N)^2/\log N$, and undetermined
in between. The lower bound is the first display of Corollary 1 on the
library's
[[../library/unit_fractions/yokota_2002_number_integers_representable_sums_unit_fractions_iii/corollary_1|result page]],
where the paper states it for $|N(n)|$, whose set includes the empty sum
$0$, so that $|N(n)|=F(n)+1$; printed without the integer part, the bound
holds for that count and fails for $F(n)$ infinitely often, whenever the
fractional part of $H_N$ exceeds
$(\frac{\pi^2}3+o(1))(\log\log N)^2/\log N$. The paper deduces it from its
[[../library/unit_fractions/yokota_2002_number_integers_representable_sums_unit_fractions_iii/theorem_1|Theorem 1]],
the same bound for the representations whose denominators lie in a
prescribed divisor set $D(t)$, through $|N^*(n)|\le|N(n)|$. The
corollary's second display, an upper bound on the least $N$ at which a
given integer becomes representable, bears on
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0308/_index|Problem 308]] and is not part of
this claim.

**Depends on.**
[[../library/unit_fractions/yokota_2002_number_integers_representable_sums_unit_fractions_iii/corollary_1|the deduction on the library's Corollary 1 page]].

**Route.** Given a large integer $a$, choose the stage $t$ at which the
reciprocal sum over the divisor set $D(t)$ first exceeds $a$ by a
prescribed small amount; the paper's Lemmas 4 and 5 bound the reciprocal
mass outside $D(t)$ and the growth of the sum from one stage to the next,
which places $a$ within $(\frac{\pi^2}3+o(1))(\log\log p)^2/\log p$ of
$\log L(t)+\gamma$ for the stage's largest prime $p$ and cutoff $L(t)$;
Lemma 3 writes the deficit as a sum of distinct divisors of the stage's
product, whose cofactors are the denominators removed from the full sum
over $D(t)$ to leave exactly $a$. Hence $a$ is representable with
denominators at most $L(t)$, and the paper concludes (p. 357) its count
bound for the representations with denominators in $D(t)$; that every
integer up to $H_N-(\frac{\pi^2}3+o(1))(\log\log N)^2/\log N$ is
representable with denominators at most $N$ is the deduction on the
library's corollary page from the paper's bound on $F(a)$.

**Acceptance.** Refereed: Yokota, H., On the number of integers representable
as sums of unit fractions, III, J. Number Theory 96 (2002), no. 2, 351--372.
The publisher's record dates the issue October 2002 and gives no day; the day
in this page's name is the first of that month. Reviewed: the site's curator,
Thomas Bloom, marks Problem 309 disproved and records this bound in the
problem's commentary as the best lower bound known, credited to this paper;
Bloom is independent of the author. Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 are recorded at
statement depth, the proof in outline only, and no independent review of it is
recorded in this corpus. The disproof is also carried by
[[problems/unit_fractions/E0309/claims/1997_12_01_yokota|Yokota's 1997 theorem]]
and by
