**Claim.** Theorem 1 of Will Sawin's preprint, *Sets of unit fractions
without two members whose average is a unit fraction* (arXiv:2607.15419v1,
16 July 2026), the
[[../library/unit_fractions/sawin_2026_sets_unit_fractions_without_two_members_average_unit_fraction/theorem_1|result page]]
of the
[[../library/unit_fractions/sawin_2026_sets_unit_fractions_without_two_members_average_unit_fraction/_index|source card]]:
for a positive integer $N$ let $A_N$ be the set of $a\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$ such
that $a+b\nmid2ab$ for every $b\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$ with $b\ne a$ and
$\Omega(b)\le\Omega(a)$, where $\Omega$ counts prime factors with
multiplicity. Then distinct members $a,b$ of $A_N$ satisfy $a+b\nmid2ab$,
and there is an absolute constant $c>0$ with $|A_N|>cN$ for all large $N$.
In the notation of [[problems/unit_fractions/E0327/_index|Problem 327]],
$f_2(N)>cN$ for large $N$, so the second question's answer is negative: a
set in which no two distinct members have $a+b\mid2ab$ need not have size
$o(N)$. The first part is immediate, since one of any two integers has at
most as many prime factors as the other; the second is proved by restricting
to integers without very small prime factors and with controlled numbers of
prime factors of each size, and bounding the integers excluded from $A_N$
through a mean-value theorem of de la Bretèche and Tenenbaum, along the
lines of Stef's 1992 argument for integers without two close divisors. The
paper makes no attempt to compute $c$.

**Submission note.** Posted to erdosproblems.com as a proof claim by Will Sawin
(account WillSawin) on 18 July 2026, giving "ChatGPT 5.5 for data analysis and
reference search and ChatGPT 5.6 was used for proofreading. The proof strategy
and writeup are mine." as the AI used:

> My proof gives a negative answer to the second part of the question: More
> precisely, there is a constant $c>0$ such that for large $N$ there is a set $A
> \subseteq \{1,\dots, N\}$ such that if $a,b\in A$ have $a\neq b$ then $a+b
> \nmid 2ab$ and $\lvert A\rvert \geq cN$. This is proved by an explicit
> construction: We take $A$ to consist of all $a \in \{1,\dots, N\}$ such that
> if $b\in \{1,\dots ,N\}$ has $a\neq b$ and $\Omega(b) \leq \Omega(a)$ then
> $a+b\nmid 2ab$. The reason this works is that for a typical $a \in
> \{1,\dots,N\}$, the number of $b \in \{1,\dots, N\}$ with $\Omega(b)\leq
> \Omega(a)$ and $a+b \mid 2ab$ is $O(1)$. Once this is checked, it is not
> difficult to find a positive density set of $a$ on which the average number of
> $b$ satisfying this condition is less than $1/2$, Checking this requires
> expressing the problem in terms of the anatomy of integers, and using
> estimates from analytic number theory.

**Covers.** The second question only. The paper says that its method gives,
for the first question ($a+b\nmid ab$), a lower bound weaker than the odd
numbers' $\lceil N/2\rceil$; through the doubling correspondence recorded on
the problem page, the construction yields even sets of positive density for
the first condition without exceeding the odd numbers. The constant $c$ is
not computed.
