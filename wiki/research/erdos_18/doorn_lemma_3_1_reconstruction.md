## Definitions

A positive integer $n$ is *practical* if every positive integer $m\le n$ is
a sum of distinct positive divisors of $n$. For practical $n$, $h(n)$ is the
least integer such that every positive integer $m\le n$ is a sum of at most
$h(n)$ distinct divisors of $n$. A residue class $c$ modulo $A$ is
*represented* by a sum of divisors $s$ when $s\equiv c\pmod A$; the empty
sum, with value $0$, is allowed.

## Statement

Let $A\ge2$ and let $n$ be practical. Suppose that every residue modulo $A$
is represented by a sum of at most $L$ distinct divisors of $n$, with total
at most $n$ and no summand divisible by $A$. Then $An$ is practical and

$$
h(An)\le h(n)+L .
$$

## Proof

Every divisor of $n$ divides $An$. Let $1\le m\le An$.

If $m\le n$, a representation of $m$ by at most $h(n)$ distinct divisors of
$n$ is a representation by divisors of $An$.

If $m=An$, the single divisor $An$ represents it.

If $n<m<An$, choose one of the prescribed sums $s\equiv m\pmod A$. Then
$0\le s\le n<m$, so $m-s$ is a positive multiple of $A$, and $m-s<An$
because $s\ge0$; hence $0<(m-s)/A<n$. Since $n$ is practical, write
$(m-s)/A=e_1+\cdots+e_r$ with distinct divisors $e_i$ of $n$ and
$r\le h(n)$. Then

$$
m=s+Ae_1+\cdots+Ae_r .
$$

Each $Ae_i$ divides $An$ and is divisible by $A$, and the $Ae_i$ are
distinct; each summand of $s$ divides $n$, hence $An$, and is not divisible
by $A$, and the summands of $s$ are distinct. So the two groups do not
overlap, and $m$ is a sum of at most $L+h(n)$ distinct divisors of $An$.

Every $1\le m\le An$ is thus a sum of distinct divisors of $An$, so $An$ is
practical, and every such $m$ uses at most $\max(h(n),1,L+h(n))=h(n)+L$
divisors, so $h(An)\le h(n)+L$.
