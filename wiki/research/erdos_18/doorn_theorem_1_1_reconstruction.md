## Statement

Let $c_0=14/\log2$, all logarithms being natural (the note fixes this at the
end of its Section 1, physical p. 2). There are infinitely many practical
numbers $n$ with

$$
h(n)\le c_0(\log\log n)^2 ,
$$

where $h(n)$ is the least integer such that every positive integer $m\le n$
is a sum of at most $h(n)$ distinct divisors of $n$.

## Proof

Let $E$ and $x_0$ be as in Proposition 4.1, and fix any odd prime $p_*$,
say $p_*=3$. For each real $x\ge x_0$ the proposition gives a practical $n$
with $x\le n<x^2$ and $h(n)\le c_0(\log\log x)^2-1<c_0(\log\log n)^2$. Taking
$x=x_0,x_0^2,x_0^4,\dots$ produces practical numbers $n$ in the disjoint
intervals $[x_0^{2^i},x_0^{2^{i+1}})$, hence infinitely many distinct ones,
each satisfying the bound.

## What it would settle

The first question of [[problems/divisors/E0018/_index|Problem 18]] asks for
infinitely many practical $m$ with $h(m)<(\log\log m)^{O(1)}$. The site's
definition ranges over $1\le m'<m$ and the note's over $m'\le m$; they differ
only by the one-divisor representation of $m$ itself, so the note's $h$ is
at least the site's. If the claim is correct it answers the first question
with exponent $2$ and the explicit constant $c_0\approx20.2$, improving the
$(\log m)^{1/2}$ of Vose's construction; it says nothing about $h(n!)$,
the subject of the second and third questions.
