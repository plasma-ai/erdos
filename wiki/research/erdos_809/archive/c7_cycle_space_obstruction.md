# Why cycle-space rank does not give the color bound

***

This page preserves an earlier research route and its local standing. The
completed threshold proof is in the
[solution note](../proofs/c7_solution.md).

The examples below obstruct a binary cycle-space method while leaving the threshold conjecture open.

Let $D$ be the binary vertex-edge incidence matrix of $G$, and let
$P:\mathbb F_2^{E(G)}\to\mathbb F_2^r$ send an edge to the basis vector
of its color. Write $Z_1(G)=\ker D$ for the binary cycle space.

## Nonvanishing and rank do not encode the rainbow condition

Since $\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}P=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}$, every odd cycle
vector $z$ satisfies $\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}Pz=1$, for **every**
coloring. The C7-rainbow condition requires the much stronger assertion
$|\operatorname{supp}(Pz)|=7$, not merely $Pz\ne0$.

Moreover, if $c(G)$ is the number of connected components,

$$
 \dim(\ker P\cap Z_1(G))
 =\dim\ker\begin{pmatrix}D\\P\end{pmatrix}
 \ge e-r-n+c(G).
$$

At quadratic edge and color counts near the conjectured extremum, a
quadratic-dimensional color-even cycle space is therefore unavoidable.

## A concrete example where seven-cycles span everything

Use the [three-branch graph](c7_dense_curve_obstruction.md), with its
aligned coloring of the three complete bipartite wings. It is chordal:
first eliminate the vertices in $U_i$, whose neighborhoods $A_i$
are cliques, and then eliminate the wing cliques. Hence its cycle space
is generated by triangles; this also follows by repeatedly splitting a
cycle along a chord.

Every triangle is contained in a clique of order at least seven for
sufficiently large $n$. Triangles through a tip lie in
$\{u\}\cup A_i$, and all other triangles lie in $C\cup A_i$.
In any $K_m$ with $m\ge7$, seven-cycles span its entire cycle space.
Here is a direct verification. Swapping consecutive interior vertices in
a seven-cycle gives, as the binary difference, any prescribed four-cycle:

$$
 (a,b,c,d,e,f,g,a)+(a,c,b,d,e,f,g,a)
 =\{ab,bd,dc,ca\}.
$$

Four-cycles span the even-edge-cardinality subspace of the cycle space.
For example, using the standard triangle basis through a fixed vertex,
differences of triangles whose other endpoint pairs share a vertex are
four-cycles; these connect the entire triangle basis. Adding any odd
seven-cycle supplies the remaining one-dimensional quotient. Thus the
seven-cycles of the three-branch graph span all of $Z_1(G)$.

Nevertheless, the color map has a large kernel on that space. Put
$a=|A_i|$, $u=|U_i|$, and identify the three wings coordinatewise.
All non-wing colors are unique, so a vector in $\ker P$ is supported
on the wings. Such a vector is Eulerian exactly when each branch
restriction lies in $Z_1(K_{a,u})$, and the color condition says that
the three restrictions sum to zero. Consequently

$$
 \ker P\cap Z_1(G)\cong Z_1(K_{a,u})^2,
 \qquad \dim=2(a-1)(u-1).
$$

For the recorded parameters this is
$0.002n^2-0.22n+2$. Its minimum nonzero support size is exactly eight:
at least two branch restrictions are nonzero, each has at least four
edges, and two corresponding four-cycles attain eight.

## Scope of the obstruction

Full span by seven-cycles does not imply that color identification is
injective on that span. Even adding minimum support eight for the
color-even cycle code does not remove its quadratic dimension. This
rules out the direct rank/injectivity argument investigated here; it
does not rule out every possible nonlinear use of cycle spaces or coding
theory.
