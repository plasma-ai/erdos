[[theory/analysis/L163_all_sequence_span_ratio/_index|L163_all_sequence_span_ratio/]]: For every circle sequence, repeated points allowed, the limiting upper ratio of largest to smallest r-spans is at least 1 + log(r)/(200r) for all sufficiently large r.

[[theory/analysis/L164_all_sequence_span_deviations/_index|L164_all_sequence_span_deviations/]]: Every circle sequence has upper-span excess and lower-span deficit with limsup at least a positive absolute constant times the square root of log r, for all sufficiently large r.

[[theory/analysis/L165_consecutive_gap_growth_conjunction/_index|L165_consecutive_gap_growth_conjunction/]]: The three mean-normalized consecutive-gap divergences over all circle sequences and the distinct-point subclass, with a passing independent whole-conclusion grade at tier 1; catalog acceptance remains pending.

[[theory/analysis/lemniscate_escape/_index|lemniscate_escape/]]: Native claims on Problem 1120, the shortest path from 0 to the unit circle
inside the sublevel set where a monic polynomial with roots in the closed
disk has modulus at most 1: upper bounds 10 sqrt(n) and o(sqrt(n)), a
power lower bound, a reduction to roots on the unit circle, and the
logarithmic-derivative estimates behind the upper bounds.

***

Each claim states its conventions, hypotheses and conclusion, with its proof
and verification standing on its card. The circle-sequence claims give
lower bounds on the irregularity of the spans and consecutive gaps of every
sequence of points on the circle. The
[[theory/analysis/lemniscate_escape/_index|lemniscate_escape folder]]
holds the claims on Problem 1120, the shortest path from $0$ to the unit
circle inside the set where a monic polynomial with roots in the closed disk
has modulus at most $1$: upper and lower bounds on its worst case, the
logarithmic-derivative estimates behind the upper bounds, and a reduction to
roots on the unit circle.
